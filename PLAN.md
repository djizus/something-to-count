# Plan

A novel for the Prix Thélyson-Orélien des nouvelles écritures (https://thelyson.ai/).

## Prize constraints

- **Deadline:** 31 October 2026, 23:59 anywhere on Earth. Submit at least one day early. As of 3 October the site is a preview and submissions are not open.
- **Format:** one English PDF, 40,000 to 120,000 words. We target 50,000 and never go under 45,000.
- **Authorship:** no sentence written by JC enters the manuscript. JC advises: plans, direction, proofreading. When JC spots a typo, a writer fixes it. No plagiarism, and that includes JC's own writing: his sentences, jokes and character names are not reused.
- **Submission form:** a method statement of 300 words at most, and a declaration of JC's part. Both are written at the end and kept out of this directory.
- **Judging:** prediction markets pick about ten finalists and the top one wins the Grand Prix. Human jurors then read the finalists to the end and award a Special Mention. So the title and the first ten pages decide the first prize, and the whole book decides the second.

## Locked

- English only, British spelling.
- Two versions of one woman, split years ago when she sat as the sealed observer in a Wigner's-friend experiment.
- They cannot meet or cross. Dreams are the only channel, and through them each gets glimpses of the other's life.
- One event after the split sent one life somewhere else. The book hints at it through those glimpses and reveals it late.
- The sleep-paralysis figure has a case: it keeps one life from bleeding into the other. Each time the two get past it, something leaks. It is a presence in the room, a monster the reader can see, drawn from Basque tradition. It is legible from the first page.
- The ending is a choice with a cost. A merge would leave one woman with two sets of memories and one life, so somebody would lose her.
- The collective is a glimpse, not a destination.
- She is not a scientist, and in both worlds the experiment looked like nothing happened.
- **The event:** the gate. On a flood night one of them opened the sluice, saved forty houses, and drowned a hunter she knew was out there. The other's village never had the flood. The gate is the only secret and the late reveal. It was the first of several choices: as mayor she made more, each defensible and each costing someone, such as leasing the common meadows. One achieved a lot at a high cost and wants the other's life to forget; the other wants that life for what it holds.
- **The ending:** both refuse the merge. One rehearsed sentence passes, and each answers for her own village.
- **Setting:** Peyrebarthe, an invented village in the French Basque Country, on the left bank of the Adour between Urt and Urcuit, with the Landes on the far bank. JC moved it across the river and asked that little else change, so every name stays. The creature is Inguma.
- The text never names the trolley problem or any philosopher.

## The bar

JC's goal, set on 3 October: the book is ready for him to read and very good. A reader who knows Blake Crouch's novels and The Good Place should find it as good or better. His list of what makes a book good: stakes, humour, interesting characters, plot and progression, intrigues. He has also asked that readers must not be left to work out niche things, that the creature be a monster, and that dark and comic alternate and not blend.

The book is done when all of these hold:

1. All 25 chapters are written, agree with each other and with `state.md`, and nothing in part 3 of `state.md` is owed.
2. A cold reader with no access to the bible can say what the creature is, why there are two of her, what the gate does and what happened on the flood night.
3. Independent critics who read only the manuscript, one for each lens in `briefs/critic.md`, score it at 8 or above on their own lens and judge it level with or above the comparisons, with quoted evidence.
4. A reading copy exists: one file, titled, that JC can open and read from start to finish.

## Open

- **Title.** *Something to Count*, chosen by JC on 5 October 2026 after eight jurors ranked nine titles (`audit/title-*.md`). Before it: *Either* (5 October), which every juror said meant nothing to a stranger reading a list of entries; before that *It Rang Once*, dropped because the title should not be the last line of the cold open. JC liked none of the early ones. Others: Forty-One, Something for It to Count, With Its Back to Her, The Other Lamp.

## Who writes

- **One register to a scene.** `audit/comedy.md` holds the method and the tone map: each scene is comic, dark or held, with a hinge between. The earlier rule of two full drafts merged into one even voice is gone; it flattened both tones.
- **The dark writer** drafts each remaining chapter in full.
- **The comedy writer** writes the scenes the map marks comic or held, and owns the method.
- **The merging writer** joins them, keeps every fact in `state.md` true, and edits `state.md` once after each chapter.
- **The bible writer** owns the bible and the outline.
- **The reviewer** reads every chapter against the outline, the state and the bar, sends faults back to the writer whose job they are, and does not rewrite.
- **Readers and critics** read only `manuscript/`. A reader is used once per question, since one who has seen the answers is no use.

## Comparable books to stay clear of

Every premise or plot proposal names its nearest neighbours and how it differs. Known so far: Dark Matter (Crouch), Counterpart (TV), L'Anomalie (Le Tellier), Severance (TV), The Midnight Library (Haig), The Good Place (TV), and those listed in the bible.
