# Scene 2, version X

The post came at eleven, to a mairie that was shut until two. Maylis took it from the box and carried it to the mayor's tray, as she did on Tuesdays and Fridays.

There were two envelopes from the prefecture at Pau. The top one was a reminder. The commune had not yet returned its count of road signs, all of which would need changing in January, and the count was now required by Friday. The one under it was thinner. She could read the heading through the window of the envelope, and it said flood watch, Adour, yellow.

She laid them in the tray in the order they had come.

By then the café had settled Sunday. She had it from the owner's wife at noon, across the crate of bread, in a low voice and at some length. The commune's girl was courting the Castagnet woman. She had gone to the house with chrysanthemums, for want of knowing better, while the husband was at the counter. She had frightened him off his own complaint the week before to clear the way. The girls at Arlette's were down to one, and the one was looking about her. In sixteen years the café had found nothing to say about the two of them, and it was making up the time.

At a quarter to two she was back on the platform. The tide was at the top. The river stood two centimetres over her father's line, brown, and going by faster than a person walks.

Guy Larrère drove into his space at five past two and was in the mairie twelve minutes. He lowered his window the usual amount as he drew level with her, and she told him through it that the river was over the line.

'I've had the yellow,' he said. 'Pau sends a yellow each time it rains on Pau. The gate stays shut. You'll keep your telephone by you at night, as your father did, and you'll do nothing until it rings.'

'And the Port? Does it say to warn them?'

'They live behind a dyke. They know there's a river.' He held a sheet out through the gap. It was a form with four columns. 'The signs. Every panel with the commune's name on it. Pau wants the number by Friday. Your father would have known it without going round.'

She took the form.

Then he put the window down as far as it went. She had not seen that done in eighteen years.

'They tell me you were at the Castagnet house on Sunday,' he said. 'With flowers. Twenty minutes in the kitchen.'

'I took her a pot.'

'A man's wife is no business of the commune's.' His face was not the face he had for the winch. 'He's sat at home every night since, in the kitchen, like a dog in a doorway. Nobody can get near the house. You'll keep away from it. That's not advice.'

The window went up, all the way.
