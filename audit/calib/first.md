# Cold read: first

## 1. Would I hand it to that friend

Yes, as a 2-hour chase, with 3 warnings. Scudder's speech in chapter 1 and one line in chapter 6 are openly antisemitic. Nobody changes and nobody the hero loves is ever at risk. Luck does most of the work.

## 2. By criterion

- **Stakes.** Weak after chapter 4. The book tells us the war "was coming, as sure as Christmas", so the hero is running to save a set of naval papers. Personal stakes are his own neck, and he enjoys the hunt.
- **Tension.** Strong in single scenes. Chapter 5 decided it: "One of the others had been looking at my boots, and a word in German called the speaker's attention to them."
- **Progression.** Episodic. Each chapter is a new disguise and a new helper. Chapter 7 drops the clock: "For the better part of ten days he did all the rough nursing I needed."
- **Comedy.** I smiled about once a chapter. "That's what comes o' bein' a teetotaller." "A highwayman with a marked taste for expensive motor-cars." "It's a wise conspirator that knows his own name."
- **Characters.** One voice and a row of cameos. The hero is good company but ends as he began. The helpers each get one scene. The old man is the only one who stays: "he could hood his eyes like a hawk."
- **The ending.** The bridge game is the best idea in the book, and the fingers tapping on the knees is a fair tell. Then the capture, the yacht and the war take 3 short paragraphs, and the win is reported in one line of dialogue.
- **Clarity.** Read twice:
  - ch. 3, the cypher ("A was J, the tenth letter of the alphabet, and so represented by X").
  - ch. 4, the opening, where chapter 1's story is called "a pack of lies" and then "not quite", with the 14th and the 15th of June both in play.
  - ch. 5, "Alexander Trummle" becoming "Mr Turnbull", and most of the roadman's dialect.
  - ch. 6, the aerodrome paragraph ("he would think it had gone over the hill beyond the trees").
  - ch. 8, the phone call that proves the visitor was an impostor.
  - ch. 9, the two lists, "Fairly certain" and "Guessed".

## 4. Against the comparison

**Below**, for a reader who likes both.

The one thing: the hero is saved by luck and never has to pay. He lists it himself in chapter 6: "The milkman, the literary innkeeper, Sir Harry, the roadman, and the idiotic Marmie, were all pieces of undeserved good fortune." Then he runs by chance into the enemy's own house and is locked in a room with a cupboard of explosives. A Crouch hero loses something on every page he turns. The Good Place makes 4 people become different people. Here a cheerful man has a hard month and comes out the same man, with a captain's commission.
