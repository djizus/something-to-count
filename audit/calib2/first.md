# Cold read: first

## 1. Would I hand it to that friend

Yes, but as a bedside book, not a page-turner. The warning: there is no plot and nobody is ever in danger. Three men and a dog row up a river and the narrator digresses. Also one racial slur (ch. 7, the blazer joke), period jokes about women, and long purple or guidebook stretches to skim.

## 2. By criterion

- **Stakes**: none. The worst on offer is a wet night; in ch. 19 they take the train home and lose nothing.
- **Tension**: brief and always comic. The best is ch. 14, two men shouting for the boat at midnight in the rain, unsure which of four islands it is.
- **Progression**: geographic only. The pineapple tin (12) could sit in ch. 4 or ch. 18 and nothing would change.
- **Comedy**: the book's strength. I smiled at "the only malady I could conclude I had not got was housemaid's knee" (1), the cheeses (4), the train bribed to be the 11.5 (5), the German song (8), the cat's "Yes! You want me?" (13), the plaster trout (17).
- **Characters**: three clear voices and a dog, consistent, changed by nothing. Decided by Harris, "so ready to take the burden of everything himself, and put it on the backs of other people" (3). Montmorency is the best of them.
- **The ending**: right and funny. They vow to finish "if it killed us", then sneak off: "Here's to Three Men well out of a Boat!" (19).
- **Clarity**: read twice:
  - ch. 3: "It seemed a good thought, and we adopted it". What thought?
  - ch. 5: opens "She said". Who?
  - ch. 6: the maze begins mid-speech; "that dog" appears from nowhere.
  - ch. 7: "They were both beautifully got up". Who? The sexton's "Well then" answers a reply that is missing.
  - ch. 11: the policeman's answer is missing; J. is on a branch, then in the river.
  - ch. 13: "Why that!". We never learn what George saw in the water.
  - ch. 15: the punting accident itself is skipped; Harris "regained his feet" from a story that never starts.
  - ch. 17: the trout is admired before it has been shown.
  - ch. 19: opens "But they are not ornamental"; "the man said:" and then nothing.

## 4. Against the comparison

Below. Line for line it is as funny as The Good Place, sometimes funnier. But both comparisons run on a question that has to be answered: who survives, what is really going on. This has none.

The one thing: nothing is ever at risk, so no chapter ending makes you start the next.
