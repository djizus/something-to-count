# Cold read: second

## 1. Would I hand it over

Yes, as a book to dip into, never as a page-turner. The warning: nothing is at stake. Three men and a dog row to Oxford and give up, and long stretches are scenery and history you will skim. There is one racial slur (ch. 7), and this copy has holes where a set-up is missing.

## 2. By criterion

- **Stakes:** none. The worst that happens to them: "It cast a gloom over the boat, there being no mustard" (12). The one death is a stranger's (16).
- **Tension:** only inside anecdotes, and it lasts a minute: "The weir was just fifty yards below" (15).
- **Progression:** by map only. Nobody ends different from how he began, and ch. 15 is almost all flashback.
- **Comedy:** the reason to read it. I smiled in most chapters. Best: "I like work: it fascinates me. I can sit and look at it for hours" (15), the tin of pine-apple (12), the plaster trout (17).
- **Characters:** one voice and three tics. The narrator's self-deception is a delight ("George *fancies* he is ill", 1). Harris and George are close to interchangeable. The dog is the most distinct ("Yes! You want me?", 13).
- **Ending:** they slink off to a train and a restaurant: "I think we did well to chuck it when we did." Honest and funny, but there is no turn and nothing planted pays off.
- **Clarity:** read twice at:
  - 1: "again turned over the pages" (of what?); "he says" at the end (who? the dog)
  - 3: "It seemed a good thought" (what thought?)
  - 6: "that dog" (which?); the maze story starts mid-speech
  - 7: "They were both beautifully got up" (who?)
  - 13: "Why that!": we never learn what George saw
  - 15: the punting accident itself is skipped; a "would-be murderer" arrives from nowhere
  - 17: the washerwoman's bill; a trout nobody introduced
  - 19: "But they are not ornamental" (what?); "the man said:" followed by nothing

## 4. Against the comparison

**Below**, for this reader.

Sentence for sentence the comedy is level with the show at its best, and above it in ch. 12 and 17. But both comparisons run on a question you need answered: who dies, what is this place. This book has none. No chapter ends with a reason to start the next, and I could have stopped at any of five places and lost nothing. It is a very funny book I would never stay up for.
