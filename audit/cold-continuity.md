Read `00` to `17` once, in order, at thriller speed. Chapter numbers are the file numbers; `00` is the night of 11 December 2014.

### 00

**1. Kind 1.**
> From up there the river was on one side of her and the fields were on the other, far below, dark and empty.

Two paragraphs earlier the other side of the bank was forty houses. Now it is fields, and I read back to see where the houses had gone. The whole book depends on the houses and the fields being two different places behind the same bank.

Change: "From up there the river was on one side of her. On the other side, far below and further along than the houses, were the flood fields, dark and empty."

### 01

**2. Kind 1.**
> She put the stone with the rest of her work and told nobody.

The stone is a picture in her head, so I read "put the stone" twice before I took it as a way of speaking.

Change: "She put the picture of the stone away with the rest of her work and told nobody."

### 02

**3. Kind 1.**
> That left what Madame Etcheto gave it herself, which was the stars she could see or the ones behind the cloud.

"What … was the stars" reads like a slip, and the "or" surprised me, because in room twelve the old woman gave it both. I needed the second pass to see that the "or" is there to set up "Either".

Change: "That left the stars, which was what Madame Etcheto gave it: the ones she could see, or the ones behind the cloud as well."

### 03

**4. Kind 3.**
> Lesca was lying on her back in a bed that wasn't hers, listening to the rain on the roof.

Until now "Lesca" has been Maylis's surname in Jeannot's mouth, so for half a paragraph I thought the book had started calling Maylis by it, and "a bed that wasn't hers" made no sense. The coat line at the end of the paragraph put it right.

Change: "Lesca was the mayor of Peyrebarthe, and she was lying on her back in a bed that wasn't hers, listening to the rain on the roof."

### 04

**5. Kind 5: how one woman gets into the other's village. Understood late.**
> She didn't stand up and turn around, because that meant another meeting.

By now I had the crossing as: the one who gets up when the alarm rings goes home, and the one who stays in the chair wakes up inside her (02: "Maylis got up out of the seat. Somebody was still sitting in it."; 03: "That morning she had stayed sitting, and she had woken up over there with a mouth."). So "didn't stand up" stopped me. If Maylis does not get up, how does Lesca ride home with her? I went back to the end of 02. The answer is that standing up early makes a meeting and getting up at the alarm makes a crossing, but this sentence only gives the first half. It was settled for good in 06 ("when the warmth beside her in the chair got up to go, she stayed sitting") and shown plainly in 13.

Change: "She didn't stand up and turn around, because that meant another meeting. She sat with the warmth beside her until Coralie's alarm rang, and got up alone."

### 05

**6. Kind 3.**
> 'That's a day,' Lesca said to Maylis's right hand.

I did not yet know that the right hand was the one part Maylis kept, so I could not see why anybody would talk to a hand. The book says so forty lines later ("Maylis still had her right hand, wrapped in a handkerchief").

Change: "'That's a day,' Lesca said to the right hand, which was the one part of her Maylis still had."

**7. Kind 1.**
> Maylis got that laugh three or four times a year. Lesca didn't know there was another half.

I took "got that laugh" as Maylis laughing, then had to turn it around: Maylis is the one who earns it and laughs back.

Change: "Maylis got that laugh out of her three or four times a year, and always laughed with her. Lesca didn't know she was supposed to."

### 07

**8. Kind 5, with a trip back (kind 2): what the rain has to do with them. Understood from what I was shown, after checking.**
> On Wednesday and Thursday nights Maylis had pushed Inguma off until 4, and Lesca had stayed at home.

This is where the rain made sense: keep Lesca out and the road dries. But "stayed at home" sent me back to 06, because on that Wednesday Lesca was at home all day, at her own desk, and 06 says "At home, in Maylis's bedroom, it was raining." So staying at home is not what stops the rain. I worked out that what counts is whether the two of them sat in the chair together the night before. The same question hangs over the first nights in 01 and 02, when Maylis pushed and it rained anyway.

Change: "On Wednesday and Thursday nights Maylis had pushed Inguma off until 4, and had never reached the chair where Lesca sat."

### 08

**9. Kind 2.**
> She said the rest for her, slowly, across the hall.

"The rest" of what? I went back up the page to find "Inguma, enauk—", which is some twenty-five lines earlier, with "You're the water" and Josiane's bread and water in between.

Change: "She took the words up where Maylis had stopped and said the rest for her, slowly, across the hall."

**10. Kind 1.**
> I don't get into a bed over somebody who can't speak. I've lifted too many of them.

On the first pass "them" was beds.

Change: "I don't get into a bed over somebody who can't speak. I've lifted too many people who couldn't."

### 09

**11. Kind 5, with a trip back (kind 2): why a word will not come out in the steel room. Understood late.**
> 'It's the ti—' Lesca said. She started again, and it stuck on the same word.

I read the five lines that follow twice. I could not tell whether the room was stopping her or she was stopping herself. I went back to 04, where Lesca cuts off "Either" with "Not in here", to see whether that had been the same thing; it was not, and it had pointed me the wrong way. What I took from the page here was a guess: in that room she cannot say what she knows is false. I was only sure at the end of 11 ("In this room it came out"), and 17 confirmed it ("It came out whole").

Change: replace Lesca's "'This isn't your kitchen.'" with "'In your kitchen I could say it. In here it won't come out.'"

### 10

**12. Kind 1.**
> 'Hand,' Coralie said.

The section before leaves Coralie at work at 10 that night, with water in the corridor. Then she is in the kitchen and "They had done this every evening for ten days", so I thought we had gone back to earlier in the evening. Her line about the yard, eleven lines down, put the scene after the shift.

Change: "'Hand,' Coralie said, when she got in from the late shift."

### 13

**13. Kind 5: where a person ends up and why. Never fully understood.**
> Inguma hadn't taken her home. It had put her down where her card was.

Lesca has just said "I'm letting go" and let go of the wheel. Maylis's order to Inguma was "Then you'll take her home." So I expected her own bed, as Lesca did. I do not know why she is set down in the hall of the dead instead. My guess was that she pays for the one ring she held on, or for having said in 09 that she would not be sent home, but the page shows neither. In 15 Inguma then holds her in the dark until nearly 6, and I do not know why that either; I guessed it waits until somebody else has taken the seat. That Madame Etcheto is the one who sits down I understood at once, from "Twelve" and the chair pulled in.

Change (it has to use something already on the page; this is the nearest I can find): "Inguma hadn't taken her home. She had told it she wouldn't be sent home, and it had put her down where her card was."

### 15

**14. Kind 2.**
> Since the night of the plastic bag she had spoken to Lesca only in council meetings, and then only to the wall.

The plastic bag is one clause of Larrère's speech in 06, nine chapters back. I went to find it.

Change: "Since the night she handed back her sash in a plastic bag, she had spoken to Lesca only in council meetings, and then only to the wall."

### 17

**15. Kind 1.**
> 'You told me on Friday not to keep that gate shut for you,' Maylis said. 'I didn't. That wasn't why.'

Two short negatives after "not to keep … shut". I had to work out what she didn't do, and "why" of what.

Change: "'You told me on Friday not to keep that gate shut for you,' Maylis said. 'It stayed shut, but not for you.'"

## 2. Errors

**Total: 6.**

**1. Chapter 01.** One word in two spellings: "window sill" here, "windowsills" and "windowsill" in 14.
> Coralie's tomato plant on the kitchen window sill still had one green tomato on it.

Corrected: Coralie's tomato plant on the kitchen windowsill still had one green tomato on it.

**2. Chapter 04.** Correct, but "her watch" reads first as the thing on her wrist.
> Maylis felt her watch the pot all the way to the fourth house.

Corrected: Maylis felt Marie-Jo watching the pot all the way to the fourth house.

**3. Chapter 06.** Every "she" in the paragraph before is Coralie; this one is Maylis.
> A doctor would put Maylis in an ambulance to Bayonne, and this was the bed she had to come back to.

Corrected: A doctor would put Maylis in an ambulance to Bayonne, and this was the bed Maylis had to come back to.

**4. Chapter 14.** "His" has no owner nearer than two paragraphs back, and Jeannot is the last man named.
> Then his headlights came down the hill again and across the glass.

Corrected: Then Kévin's headlights came down the hill again and across the glass.

**5. Chapter 17.** "That door" could be Sarthou's, the care home's or Madame Darrigrand's, and "her" follows a paragraph that ends on Larrère.
> Maylis measured it in her head, from the trapdoor to that door. It was closer to a kilometre and two hundred metres. She didn't correct her.

Corrected (I took the door to be Madame Darrigrand's; if it is another, name that one): Maylis measured it in her head, from the trapdoor to Madame Darrigrand's door. It was closer to a kilometre and two hundred metres. She didn't correct Madame Darrigrand.

**6. Chapter 15.** Correct, but no bar in the Port has been mentioned, so "its bar" reads as a slip for the café or the reception tent.
> The crowd was leaving, down the road to the Port and its bar.

Corrected: The crowd was leaving, down the road to the Port and the bar in the reception tent.

### Two queries, not counted

- "Noon" and "midnight" are the only clock times not in figures (05, 07, 08, 09, 11, 12). If the rule covers them, they are 12 and 12 at night; if not, they are consistent as they stand.
- The spelling is British throughout. Two words are American in a British text: "dish towel" (01, 05, 11) and "baby carriage" (17, in the same sentence as "pavement").
