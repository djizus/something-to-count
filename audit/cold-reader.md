### Chapter 1

**1.** "She put the stone with the rest of her work and told nobody."
Kind 1. The stone is a picture in her head, so I read "put the stone with" as something she did with her hands, then went back.
Change: "She treated the stone as one more job and told nobody."

### Chapter 2

**2.** "Maylis kept the key to the floodgate, and to the padlock on its platform, so she turned her back on the open doors."
Kind 1. The "so" skips a step. I had to work out that the eels would have been netted at her own gate.
Change: "Maylis kept the key to the floodgate, and to the padlock on its platform. If those boxes were full, they had been filled at her gate. She turned her back on the open doors."

**3.** "That left what Madame Etcheto gave it herself, which was the stars she could see or the ones behind the cloud."
Kind 2. Madame Etcheto gave it both ("The ones behind the cloud as well"), so the "or" sent me back up the page to check what she had said. "Which was the stars" also reads like a slip for "were".
Change: "That left the stars, which Madame Etcheto gave it herself. Maylis could give it the ones she could see, or the ones behind the cloud as well."

**4.** "A long way off, Coralie's alarm rang. Maylis got up out of the seat. Somebody was still sitting in it."
Kind 5: why one of them wakes in the other's bed. **Understood late.** Here I saw that there were two of them in the chair. I did not see that staying seated is what carries the second one across. Chapter 3 gives the other side ("The warmth had got up to go. Lesca had stayed sitting.") and I still took it as two separate facts. It became plain only in chapter 6: "when the warmth beside her in the chair got up to go, she stayed sitting. In the other village Lesca woke up, and Maylis went with her."
Change, in chapter 3, where the scene has already reached it: "The warmth had got up to go, and Lesca, who had stayed sitting, had gone along with it."

### Chapter 3

**5.** "Lesca was lying on her back in a bed that wasn't hers, listening to the rain on the roof."
Kind 3. For most of the first paragraph I took "Lesca" for Maylis under her surname. The last sentence of the paragraph ("when she became mayor") settled it.
Change: "Lesca, the mayor, was lying on her back in a bed that wasn't hers, listening to the rain on the roof."

### Chapter 4

**6.** "She had Maylis's hands too, on the steel arms of the chair, the left one open and the right one closed in a fist."
Kind 2. In chapter 3 it was the left hand she kept closed ("with her left hand closed", "Her left hand went up still closed"), so I went back to see whether the hand had changed. Chapter 5 explains the right fist. Nothing is wrong, but I checked.
Change: "the left one open now and the right one closed in a fist."

**7.** "Jean Castagnet's name was cut above theirs."
Kind 1, brief. There is one name on the stone under his, so "theirs" stopped me.
Change: "Jean Castagnet's name was cut above the one they shared."

**8.** "She didn't stand up and turn around, because that meant another meeting." Then: "At 6 on Monday morning she woke up."
Kind 5: how one woman gets into the other's village. **Understood late.** On the Friday night Maylis got up and Lesca stayed. Here Maylis stays in the chair as well, and then simply wakes with Lesca driving her legs. I could not tell who had left the chair and who had not. Chapter 6 cleared it up.
Change: "At 6 on Monday morning the alarm rang. She got up out of the chair and the warmth beside her stayed where it was. Then she was awake, and her legs swung out of the bed and carried her down to the kitchen."

### Chapter 5

**9.** "'That's a day,' Lesca said to Maylis's right hand."
Kind 3. I read it twice to see why she was talking to a hand. Two scenes later ("said out loud, to the right hand") I understood that the right hand is the part Maylis still has, so that is where Lesca speaks to her.
Change: "'That's a day,' Lesca said, to the right hand, which was still Maylis's."

**10.** "'Still in the box, were you?' the dealer said."
Kind 3, brief. Nobody in this scene has been dealing yet, so for one line I did not know who "the dealer" was or what he dealt.
Change: "'Still in the box, were you?' said the man dealing the cards."

**11.** "Lesca didn't know there was another half."
Kind 1. I had to go back over "the other half of the laugh" and "Maylis got that laugh three or four times a year" to see that the other half is Maylis laughing back.
Change: "Lesca didn't know she was expected to laugh back."

### Chapter 6

**12.** "'I want that,' Maylis said."
Kind 1. I did not know what "that" was: the timer, or being waited for. The secretary's answer told me it was the timer.
Change: "'I want one of those,' Maylis said."

**13.** "It ends: stand at his own gate without the prefecture watching."
Kind 1. The gate cameras had not been mentioned before this speech, and the cut sentence comes in two pieces with the bell between them. I read the speech twice.
Change: "The secretary will find one under the item on the cameras at the floodgate. "A man can no longer—" The timer rang there. I was going to say: stand at his own gate without the prefecture watching him."

### Chapter 7

**14.** "The pulse went down the chart from 64 to 34, beside two columns of ticks headed *eyes* and *turned*."
Kind 1. I stopped on "turned" and went back to the wet cotton to guess what the two columns recorded.
Change: "beside two columns of ticks headed *eyes wetted* and *turned over*."

**15.** "His breath stopped halfway down, and then at his collar."
Kind 1. On first reading the breath stops twice in two places.
Change: "His breath went only halfway down, and then only as far as his collar."

**16.** "When Maylis woke at 6, Lesca was still sitting in the chair. It was Lesca who got her out of bed."
Kind 1. Read as they stand, the two sentences say Lesca is in the chair and at the bed at once. I knew the answer by now, but only on the second pass.
Change: "At 6 the alarm woke Maylis. Lesca had stayed sitting in the chair and came with her. It was Lesca who got her out of bed."

**17.** "On Wednesday and Thursday nights Maylis had pushed Inguma off until 4, and Lesca had stayed at home."
Kind 5: what the rain has to do with either of them. **Understood here, on the second read; said outright in chapters 8 and 9.** I read the paragraph twice to line the nights up with the days. Then "Lesca had stayed at home" sent me back to chapter 6, because on the Tuesday night Lesca had also stayed at home (Maylis went to her) and Wednesday was wet all the same. What the two dry days have in common is that Maylis never reached the chair.
Change: "On Wednesday and Thursday nights Maylis had pushed until 4 and never reached the chair."

### Chapter 8

**18.** "But she was watching Maylis the way she would have watched a child stuck in the middle of a prayer."
Kind 5: why Inguma turns back and Lesca stays the night. **Understood from what I was shown, on the second read.** The "But" made me look for a contrast that is not there. Then I had to stop and work out that the words Madame Etcheto finishes are the ones Maylis had bitten off on purpose, so the old woman has just kept Lesca in.
Change: drop "But". After "'That's till morning,' Madame Etcheto told Coralie, and settled her hands on the duck." add: "The left hand picked up the cloth again."

### Chapter 9

**19.** "'It's the ti—' Lesca said. She started again, and it stuck on the same word."
Kind 5: why a word will not come out in the steel room. **Understood from what I was shown, after reading the exchange twice.** The next line, "'It came out in my kitchen.'", has no speaker, and "'This isn't your kitchen.'" took me a second pass before I saw that the word sticks because it is not true. Chapter 11 ("In this room it came out.") and chapter 17 ("It came out whole.") confirmed it.
Change: "'It came out in my kitchen,' Maylis said." and "'In your kitchen I can say what I like.'"

### Chapter 10

**20.** "'Hand,' Coralie said."
Kind 3. The section before leaves Coralie at the care home at 10 that night. For a few lines I did not know whether this kitchen scene came before it or after it. "The yard came in under our back door tonight" settled it.
Change: "It was after 10 when Coralie got home. 'Hand,' she said."

### Chapter 13

**21.** "Inguma hadn't taken her home. It had put her down where her card was."
Kind 5: where a person ends up and why. **Understood late in outline; one link never understood.** That the hall is where the one who cannot be taken home goes, I had from room twelve and from chapter 9. That somebody else then takes the seat, I understood at "'Twelve. One, two.'" in chapter 15, and chapter 16 says it. What I never understood is why Inguma sets her down there at all, when she has just said "I'm letting go" and let go. I guessed that holding the wheel for one ring was already too long, or that her own body had been left too long. The book does not show which.
Change, if the first guess is right: "Inguma hadn't taken her home. She had held on one ring too long for that. It had put her down where her card was."

### Chapter 14

**22.** "After twenty metres she turned around, unhooked the key from her belt and threw it back to him."
Kind 2. She then shouts "The handle's on", so I did not see what the key was for. I went back to chapter 12 for the padlock on the platform.
Change: "'That's for the padlock,' she shouted. 'The handle's on. It sticks at nineteen. Get your shoulder under it.'"

### Chapter 15

**23.** "The two names on it were level with her right knee."
Kind 2. The stone is "as high as her shoulder" in chapter 1. I went back, then found the stage two sentences further on ("Below her").
Change: "Lesca stood on the stage, on the cross of white tape, with the sash over her good coat."

### Chapter 17

**24.** "At first light on Sunday 13 December Maylis walked down into the Port."
Kind 2. Chapter 16 ends on the Tuesday. I turned back to check the day before I saw that this is the other village, two days earlier.
Change: "At first light on Sunday 13 December, the day after the flood, Maylis walked down into the Port."

**25.** "'I said it was in that house,' she said. 'I only had the one name wrong.'"
Kind 1. "It" points twelve chapters back, to Josiane's speech at the counter. I got there, but not at once.
Change: "'I said there was adultery in that house,' she said. 'I only had the one name wrong.'"

## 2. Errors

**Total: 6.**

**1. Chapter 1.** Spelling not consistent: one word in chapter 14 ("windowsills", "windowsill"), two words here.
"Coralie's tomato plant on the kitchen window sill still had one green tomato on it."
Corrected: "Coralie's tomato plant on the kitchen windowsill still had one green tomato on it."

**2. Chapter 3.** "Ago" in past narration. The book says "Twelve years earlier" and "Sixteen years earlier" elsewhere.
"Before that she hadn't held them since a February eleven years ago."
Corrected: "Before that she hadn't held them since a February eleven years earlier."

**3. Chapter 6.** Pronoun with an unclear owner. The "She" of the paragraph before is the secretary.
"She sat still and waited for the cold."
Corrected: "Maylis sat still and waited for the cold."

**4. Chapter 7.** Pronoun with an unclear owner. "They" can be the legs, two paragraphs up, or the two women.
"They didn't step off."
Corrected: "The legs didn't step off."

**5. Chapter 11.** Pronoun with an unclear owner. The last two uses of "She" are Arlette.
"She had left her own Castagnet there."
Corrected: "Lesca had left her own Castagnet there."

**6. Chapter 14.** Pronoun with an unclear owner. Kévin was last named three paragraphs up, and two other men are in the scene.
"Then his headlights came down the hill again and across the glass."
Corrected: "Then Kévin's headlights came down the hill again and across the glass."

Checked and found clean: Inguma is "it" throughout; every clock time and date is in figures; no American spellings; no doubled words or spacing slips. Three words are American in usage, though not in spelling, and are left to the author: "baby carriage" and "truck" and "dish towel".
