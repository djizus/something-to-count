### Prologue

**1. Kind 3.**
"From up there the river was on one side of her and the fields were on the other, far below, dark and empty."
One paragraph earlier the other side of the bank was forty houses. For a moment I had the gate standing over the houses and did not know where the fields had come from.
Change: "From up there the river was on one side of her. On the other, past the last roof, the fields lay far below, dark and empty."

### Chapter 1

**2. Kind 1.**
"Somebody had put it up straight."
I read "put it up" as hung or posted, and "straight" as directly, before I saw a stone standing upright.
Change: "Somebody had stood it up straight."

**3. Kind 1.**
"It was a cellar dug into the field, under a trapdoor in the grass."
The sentence before ends on "the flood fields, where he shot ducks from his hut", so "It" could be the fields or the hut, and a hut that is a cellar needed a second look.
Change: "The hut was a cellar dug into the field, under a trapdoor in the grass."

### Chapter 2

**4. Kind 5. Understood, after going back.**
"'The thing on your chest is the way in,' Kévin said."
Four lines up he says "I pushed it off both times." I went back to see whether he had ever been in himself or was passing on something he had heard. This is the first time the book says how one gets in, so I wanted to know how far to trust it.
Change: "'They say the thing on your chest is the way in,' Kévin said."

### Chapter 3

**5. Kind 5. Understood late.**
"Lesca had stayed sitting, and had gone with it."
It reads as a contradiction: she stayed, and she went. I read it twice and moved on without it. I had it at the motion later in the chapter ("That the mayor remain seated"), and for certain in chapter 6 ("she stayed sitting. In the other village Lesca woke up, and Maylis went with her").
Change: "Lesca hadn't got up, and the warmth had taken her along with it."

### Chapter 5

**6. Kind 1.**
"'It takes a small m,' Lesca said. Bordenave went over the M and made it a small one."
*Madame le Maire* has two capital Ms. I had to work out which one she meant.
Change: "'Maire takes a small m,' Lesca said."

**7. Kind 1.**
"Then she looked across the table for the other half of the laugh." and, two lines on, "Lesca didn't know there was another half."
I read both twice before I saw that the other half was Maylis laughing back.
Change: "Then she looked across the table for Maylis to laugh too." and "Lesca didn't know she was supposed to."

### Chapter 6

**8. Kind 5. Never understood.**
"She worked out that the count would take it one working day: eight hours from 6, when Lesca woke up."
I understood what she wanted: to be fetched home at 2 in the afternoon. I never understood how it could work. She gives Inguma its count at 11 at night, the way Madame Etcheto does to keep it off until the count is done. Yet it still comes that night to put her in the chair, and the eight hours run from 6. Lesca does it the other way in chapters 5 and 7: she crosses first and gives the count when Inguma comes to fetch her.
Change, in her instruction rather than in the narration: "She told Inguma to come at its usual minute, and after that to count every metre of roadside she had cut in this village since 2008 before it came to take her home."

### Chapter 8

**9. Kind 1.**
"She said the rest for her, slowly, across the hall."
The words were broken off fifteen lines earlier. For a moment I could not tell whether the old woman was finishing what Maylis wanted or undoing it. I read from "Inguma had come in" again to be sure she had sent away the thing Maylis was waiting for.
Change: one line after "The tablecloths came down again, one after another, all the way back to the door.": "It was the one sentence Maylis had kept her teeth shut on all evening."

**10. Kind 1.**
"Ask me tomorrow and I'd have you."
The next sentence starts with "But" and refuses her, so I read it twice to see why tomorrow would be different. I took it to mean anywhere except that bed.
Change: "Ask me tomorrow, downstairs, and I'd have you." The line in chapter 9, "I said I'd have you tomorrow", still answers it.

### Chapter 9

**11. Kind 5. Understood late.**
"'It's the ti—' Lesca said. She started again, and it stuck on the same word."
I read the whole exchange twice. "It came out in my kitchen" / "This isn't your kitchen" told me the room was doing it, but not why. I only had it at the end of chapter 11 ("In this room it came out"), and chapter 17 ("It came out whole") confirmed it: in the steel room she cannot say what she knows is false.
Change: turn the next beat into a second attempt. For "Maylis waited for her to say so again. She didn't." put "Lesca opened her mouth to say so again. That stuck too."

**12. Kind 5. Understood late.**
"'That's where I sit if I stay with you,' Lesca said."
Three paragraphs earlier Maylis has costed the same staying as "a whole life with one hand". I could not tell whether Lesca, if she stayed, would be living in Maylis or sitting with the dead. I went back to Madame Etcheto's words in chapter 2 and still did not have it. I had it in chapter 13, when she holds on too long and is put down "where her card was".
Change: use the old woman's own phrase, so that the chair reads as a place laid for her. "'That's the place they lay for me if I stay with you,' Lesca said."

### Chapter 12

**13. Kind 3.**
"The old wing came first."
I read the paragraph twice to build the building: back door, old wing, ramp, fire door, main corridor, front yard. It held after that, and chapter 14 needs it. What I was missing was which rooms were in the old wing.
Change: "The old wing came first, with rooms nine to twelve."

### Chapter 14

**14. Kind 3.**
"She rang Coralie as she drove."
I had left her at the patch with the van pointing at the Port. Then the lamp over the platform "came up beside her", and I went back to find where she had turned around.
Change: "She turned the van around and rang Coralie as she drove back."

### Chapter 17

**15. Kind 2.**
"At first light on Sunday 13 December Maylis walked down into the Port."
Chapter 16 ends on a Tuesday. I went back to check that I had not lost two days, then saw that chapter 17 steps back to the other village.
Change: "At first light on Sunday 13 December, the day after the bank broke, Maylis walked down into the Port."

## 2. Errors

**Count: 3.** All three are pronouns or pointers with an unclear owner. I found no typo, no grammar error, no punctuation slip, no American spelling, no clock time or date written in words, and no place where Inguma is anything but "it".

**1. Chapter 11.**
"I was in there."
Nothing before it in the scene names the care home. They are in the bedroom, and "in there" could be the spare room across the landing.
Corrected: "I was in the care home."

**2. Chapter 12.**
"Maylis stood on the step with a bag of sand held against her good side and watched Coralie do eleven chairs before she looked up."
"she" can be either woman.
Corrected: "Maylis stood on the step with a bag of sand held against her good side and watched Coralie do eleven chairs before Coralie looked up."

**3. Chapter 14.**
"Lesca had gone out of her the way warmth goes out of a chair, and those words were what she had left behind."
"her" is Maylis and "she" is Lesca, inside one sentence.
Corrected: "Lesca had gone out of her the way warmth goes out of a chair, and those words were what Lesca had left behind."

Not counted, because the spelling is correct: "dish towel" (chapters 1, 5 and 11) and "baby carriage" (chapter 17) are American words in a book with British spelling. The British words are "tea towel" and "pram".
