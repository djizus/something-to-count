# Cold read 2: continuity

Read once, in order, `00` to `17`, at thriller speed. Nothing else was opened.

The five kinds, as numbered in the brief:

1. read a sentence or paragraph twice
2. went back to an earlier page to check a fact, a name or a date
3. did not know for a moment who, whose head, which village or whose hand
4. a word to look up
5. could not tell how or why something happened

### Chapter 1

**1. Kind 1.**
> Somebody had put it up straight.

I stopped to ask what this told me. The line before already stands the stone up.
Change: cut the sentence, or make it "It had been set up straight, on purpose."

**2. Kind 1.**
> She treated the stone as one more job and told nobody.

"Treated as one more job" did not give me a picture. I read on without knowing what she meant to do.
Change: "She decided to deal with the stone herself, like any other job, and told nobody."

### Chapter 2

**3. Kind 1.**
> Maylis kept the key to the floodgate, and to the padlock on its platform, so she turned her back on the open doors.

The "so" carries a step I had to work out: if she sees the boxes, she is part of it.
Change: "Maylis kept the key to the floodgate, and to the padlock on its platform. If she saw what was in those boxes she would have to do something about it, so she turned her back on the open doors."

**4. Kind 1.**
> That left the stars, which Madame Etcheto gave it herself.

I first read "gave it herself" as "gave herself", then had to sort out who gives what to whom.
Change: "That left the stars, the job Madame Etcheto gave it every night."

### Chapter 3

**5. Kind 5. Understood late.**
> The warmth had got up to go. Lesca had stayed sitting, and had gone with it.

This is the line that explains how one woman wakes in the other's bed, and it says she stayed and she went in the same breath. I read it three times and moved on without it. It became clear at the motion (*That the mayor remain seated*) and plain in chapter 6, "So she would do what Lesca had done to her, and stay sitting."
Change: "The warmth had got up to go home. Lesca hadn't got up, and the warmth had taken her along with it."

### Chapter 4

**6. Kind 1.**
> 'Then I'll tell you what it is at work,' she said.

"What it is at work" reads for a second as "what is at work here".
Change: "'Then I'll tell you what we'd call it at work,' she said."

**7. Kind 2.**
> She had Maylis's hands too, on the steel arms of the chair, the left one open now and the right one closed in a fist.

In chapter 3 it was the left hand Lesca kept closed all day ("Her left hand went up still closed"). Here I thought the closed hand had changed sides and went back to check. The "now" belongs to the left hand only, but the sentence lets it cover both. The right fist is explained in chapter 5.
Change: "She had Maylis's hands too, on the steel arms of the chair. The left one was open now. The right one was closed in a fist."

**8. Kind 5. Understood late.**
> 'I can't. Inguma has sat on me every night for twelve years, and I've never kept it off. I stopped trying in the first month.'

Maylis tells her to stay in her own bed and this is the answer. I could not see why being sat on means she has to come. I needed the leaf crumbs from chapter 1 and the cold chair of chapter 10 ("Lesca wasn't in it yet") to be sure that Inguma puts her in that chair every night.
Change: "'I can't. Inguma has sat on me every night for twelve years and put me in this chair, and I've never kept it off. I stopped trying in the first month.'"

### Chapter 5

**9. Kind 1. Understood in chapter 11.**
> In my village there's a lake where the village field was. You'll count the stones the gravel company took out of it.

Stones taken out of a lake stopped me. The gravel pit only arrives in chapter 11.
Change: "In my village a gravel company dug up the village field, and the hole is a lake now. You'll count the stones it took out."

**10. Kind 3.**
> 'Still in the box, were you?' the dealer said.

"The dealer" comes in as if I had met him. For a moment I looked for who he was.
Change: "'Still in the box, were you?' said the man dealing the cards." He can be "the dealer" after that.

**11. Kind 1.**
> The ones who can walk go first, from the front, because that fills a minibus fastest.

"From the front" could be the front of the minibus, the front door or the front rooms. Chapter 12 settles it.
Change: "The ones who can walk go first, from the rooms at the front, because that fills a minibus fastest."

### Chapter 6

**12. Kind 2.**
> The gendarmes had typed the night in three lines.

In chapter 3 the typed part of the file looked like one line: "Under that the file was typed, by the gendarmes", then *First light*. Here there are three, and I went back to see whether I had missed two.
Change, in chapter 3: "Under that the file was typed, by the gendarmes. She went to the last typed line."

### Chapter 7

**13. Kind 1.**
> She meant to have a third before she asked Coralie back.

I could not tell why the asking had to wait for a third night.
Change: one clause of reason, in her terms. For example: "She meant to have a third, to be sure of the bed, before she asked Coralie back."

**14. Kind 5. The link I understood here. One part of it I never understood.**
> On Wednesday and Thursday nights Maylis had pushed Inguma off until 4 and never reached the chair.

This paragraph is where I saw what the rain has to do with them: two nights without Lesca, two dry days. It worked. Then I went back, because Maylis also pushed Inguma off on the first nights (chapter 2, "I pushed"; chapter 4, "You pushed it off, the first nights. I felt you do it") and never reached the chair before Friday the 27th, yet it rained every one of those days. I never found what made 2 and 3 December different from 23 to 26 November.
Change: let her notice the difference herself. For example: "On Wednesday and Thursday nights Maylis had pushed until 4, and nobody had been under Inguma with her."

### Chapter 8

**15. Kind 1.**
> The tablecloths came down again, one after another, all the way back to the door.

I read back from "Lesca raised the voice" to be sure what had just happened: Maylis shut the teeth to stop the words, Madame Etcheto finished them for her, and so Lesca stays. The page shows Inguma leaving but not Lesca staying, and "That's till morning" comes from a woman who does not know what she has done.
Change: add one line after it, to answer "Maylis felt the pull go out of the jaw" a few lines earlier: "The pull came back into the jaw."

**16. Kind 1.**
> 'I'd have the one who answers,' she said. 'Ask me tomorrow and I'd have you.

Yes, then yes tomorrow, then no, in three sentences. "Tomorrow" seemed to come from nowhere until chapter 9 opened.
Change: "'Ask me again tomorrow and I'd have you."

### Chapter 9

**17. Kind 5. Understood late.**
> 'It's the ti—' Lesca said. She started again, and it stuck on the same word.

I saw the word stick and did not see why. I guessed at "It came out in my kitchen" and at "Maylis waited for her to say so again. She didn't." I was sure only in chapter 11 ("In this room it came out") and chapter 17 ("It came out whole"): in the steel room only what is true comes out.
Change, three lines down: "Maylis waited for her to say so again. Lesca opened her mouth, and that wouldn't come out either."

**18. Kind 5. Understood late.**
> 'That's where I sit if I stay with you,' Lesca said.

A minute earlier Maylis says that Lesca staying costs her "a whole life with one hand", so Lesca would be living in her. Now Lesca says that staying puts her at the table of the dead. I could not hold both. Chapter 13 shows which it is.
Change, if I have the meaning right: "'That's where the rest of me sits if I stay with you,' Lesca said."

### Chapter 12

**19. Kind 3.**
> The door opened onto the yard, at the end of the building nearest the bank.

Six lines later the minibus is "in the front yard", and I had to go back up the paragraph to build the map: back yard, old wing, ramp, fire door, corridor, front yard. Chapter 14 depends on that map.
Change: "The door opened onto the back yard, at the end of the building nearest the bank."

**20. Kind 2.**
> Then she came down and locked the padlock on the platform behind her, as she had every night for eighteen years.

Kévin has netted from that platform for four winters, and an hour after dusk he is standing on it. I went back to chapter 2 and chapter 6 to see how he gets past a lock she closes every night. He lifts his boxes "over the rail", so I suppose he climbs, but nothing says so.
Change, at his entrance: "Kévin had climbed the rail onto the platform by the gate. He had a torch in his teeth and was pulling a net up out of the water, hand over hand."

### Chapter 13

**21. Kind 5. Understood late.**
> Inguma hadn't taken her home. She had held on one ring too long for that. It had put her down where her card was.

I took this to mean she was dead, by Madame Etcheto's rule. Then she refuses the seat, walks away and is taken home after all, and I did not know why she was let go. Chapter 15 ("Twelve. One, two." and a chair pulled in) and chapter 16 ("somebody sat down") gave me the reason, and that was the right place to learn who. The exchange itself could be plainer when it happens.
Change, in chapter 15: "Somebody else pulled a chair in to a table, and the voice stopped calling her name."

### Chapter 14

**22. Kind 3.**
> The care home was the last building before the gap.

She has just come down at the gap and passed Sarthou's, which the book calls the last house. "The last building before the gap" turned me around, and I had to place the building again.
Change: "The care home came straight after Sarthou's house."

### Chapter 17

**23. Kind 1.**
> 'The arm is the excuse you've written for me. It isn't mine,' Maylis said.

I had to look back up at "You had the one arm" to find which arm and whose excuse.
Change: "'One arm is your excuse for me. It isn't mine,' Maylis said."

### The six things the book shows and does not explain

| What | How I got it |
|---|---|
| How one woman gets into the other's village | Understood late. Shown at the end of chapter 2 and in chapter 3, not clear to me until the motion, plain in chapter 6. See 5. |
| Why one wakes in the other's bed | From what I was shown: the leaf crumbs, Madame Etcheto's "It comes to your bed", the warm chair. |
| How long she can stay | From what I was shown: twenty minutes, then a count buys a day; the pulse on the pad; the left hand that has to be told twice; the squeeze test. |
| Why a word will not come out in the steel room | Understood late, chapter 9 to 11. See 17. |
| What the rain has to do with them | Understood at the two dry days in chapter 7, before anyone says it. One part never understood. See 14. |
| Where a person ends up and why | Understood late, chapter 15 to 16. See 18 and 21. |

No word sent me to a dictionary. *Inguma, enauk hire beldur* is translated on the spot, and glass eels, the catch and the dip are each explained where they first matter.

Dates, weekdays, clock times, distances, vote counts and letter numbers all agreed wherever I checked them against an earlier page.

## 2. Errors

**7 entries.** Three pronouns, one tense, one correct sentence that reads as a slip, two words that are not British (in nine sentences). I found no typo and no punctuation slip. Inguma is "it" throughout. Clock times and dates are in figures throughout.

**Chapter 7. Tense.**
> Thursday and Friday were the only dry days since the 24th.

Thursday and Friday had been the only dry days since the 24th.

**Chapter 9. Pronoun.**
> Maylis turned her back on her and walked out onto the wooden floor, towards the empty chair.

Maylis turned her back on Lesca and walked out onto the wooden floor, towards the empty chair.

**Chapter 12. Pronoun.**
> Maylis stood on the step with a bag of sand held against her good side and watched Coralie do eleven chairs before she looked up.

Maylis stood on the step with a bag of sand held against her good side and watched Coralie do eleven chairs before Coralie looked up.

**Chapter 15. Correct, but reads as a slip.** The stone is shoulder-high in chapter 1, so names at knee height look wrong until the stage comes back to mind.
> The two names on it were level with her right knee.

From the stage, the two names on it were level with her right knee.

**Chapter 17. Pronoun.** "She" reads first as Coralie.
> Maylis followed her as far as the bedroom door and found she had brought the pad with her.

Maylis followed her as far as the bedroom door and found the pad still in her own hands.

**Chapter 17. Not British: "baby carriage".**
> Two doors up, Madame Darrigrand stood on the pavement beside a baby carriage, with the baby inside her coat.

Two doors up, Madame Darrigrand stood on the pavement beside a pram, with the baby inside her coat.

> The carriage was full of mud up to the hood.

The pram was full of mud up to the hood.

> She wiped the handle of the baby carriage with her sleeve.

She wiped the handle of the pram with her sleeve.

**Chapters 1, 5 and 11. Not British: "dish towel".** The British word is "tea towel".
> She dropped the pad on the table and pulled the dish towel off the oven door.

She dropped the pad on the table and pulled the tea towel off the oven door.

The same change in five more sentences:

- Chapter 5: "Maylis sat in her own chair with her right hand beside the bowl, wrapped in a clean dish towel."
- Chapter 5: "Under the dish towel Maylis closed her fingers on the cut."
- Chapter 5: "The dish towel fell off, which left her bare palm on the wood, cut and all."
- Chapter 11: "I finished my shift at 7, and you came in at 9 in your wet clothes and got onto this bed with your boots on and your hand wrapped in a dish towel."
- Chapter 11: "When she opened it the scar was there, where it had been since the night of the dish towel."
