All forty-six corrections of the first two reviews are in the text. None of the fourteen below was on either list. No chapter is clean. There is no misspelling, no doubled word, no unclosed quotation mark, no American spelling and no clock time or count in the wrong form. Eight of the fourteen are a pronoun, and four of those are beside Inguma. One of the eight (11.4) was made by a correction of the second review.

Line numbers are the line in the chapter file.

## Chapter 7 (3)

**7.1, line 41. Kind 5 (Inguma).**
> It stopped halfway down.

The sentence before is "When it had gone she lay on the floor and took a breath." That "it" is Inguma, which has just come "down the stairs". So for a moment this "It" is Inguma too, stopped halfway down them. Coralie's speech has the noun: "His breath stopped halfway down."
Corrected: The breath stopped halfway down.

**7.2, line 41. Kind 4 (preposition in a rare sense).**
> She got up by the leg of the table, coughed into the sink and looked.

"By" is meant as "holding on to". It reads first as "beside": she stood up next to the table leg.
Corrected: She pulled herself up by the leg of the table, coughed into the sink and looked.

**7.3, line 59. Kind 5 (Inguma).**
> She took what breath there was and held it.

The sentence before is "Inguma came down at its minute." Inguma is its subject and Maylis is not in it, so "She" follows Inguma. This is the pattern both earlier reviews corrected at line 37 ("Inguma pressed down just as hard. She pushed"). Only the first word changes.
Corrected: Maylis took what breath there was and held it.

## Chapter 8 (1)

**8.1, line 79. Kind 4 (verb in a rare sense).**
> The hall clapped him.

"Clap" with a person as its object is correct British English and is rare. It reads as a word left out ("clapped for him"), or as "slapped".
Corrected: The hall clapped.

## Chapter 9 (1)

**9.1, line 155. Kind 5.**
> He kept his back to the car and spoke to Maylis.

His speech then calls her "she" from the first word to the last: "Today she wants my hut filled", "Her mayor got Friday the 4th wrong". A man who speaks to a woman says "you", so the "she" reads as some other woman. He is facing her and speaking to the room about her.
Corrected: He kept his back to the car and his eyes on Maylis.

## Chapter 10 (1)

**10.1, line 9. Kind 1 (agreement).**
> Coralie had rolled every bath towel in the cupboard and packed them along the bottom of the door.

"Every bath towel" is singular and "them" is plural.
Corrected: Coralie had rolled all the bath towels in the cupboard and packed them along the bottom of the door.

## Chapter 11 (6)

**11.1, line 23. Kind 5 (Inguma).**
> 'You wanted a whole day off it, and you had one. I sat by that bed from noon on Saturday till breakfast on Sunday. Your hand was colder than on the Monday. What did you give it to count?'

Inguma has not been named in this chapter. The first "it" follows "a night's sleep", so it reads as a day off sleep. The second follows "Your hand", so it reads as a hand that was given something to count. Arlette uses the name in chapter 3 ("For Inguma she had the grass"). Naming it once, at the first "it", settles both.
Corrected: 'You wanted a whole day off Inguma, and you had one. I sat by that bed from noon on Saturday till breakfast on Sunday. Your hand was colder than on the Monday. What did you give it to count?'

**11.2, line 69. Kind 4 (reads as a word missing).**
> 'The front row belongs to the care home,' Lesca said. 'It has every year.'

"It has" stands for "it has belonged", but the verb before it is "belongs", so "has" reads as "owns" with its object left out. Sixteen lines up Lesca says "as it does every year".
Corrected: 'The front row belongs to the care home,' Lesca said. 'It does every year.'

**11.3, line 81. Kind 5.**
> Monsieur Daguerre, the baker. When the prefect reads out the date, he'll call out "Here".

By the grammar "he" is the prefect, who is the subject of the clause just before it. It is Monsieur Daguerre who calls out.
Corrected: Monsieur Daguerre, the baker. He'll call out "Here" when the prefect reads out the date.

**11.4, line 135. Kind 5 (two women).**
> 'So for twelve years I've had two answers,' she said.

The paragraph before is Coralie's speech, closed. A new paragraph of speech is a new speaker everywhere else in the book, so this "she" reads as Lesca answering, until "you opened it" three sentences on. The second review closed the speech above and added "she said"; it needs the name.
Corrected: 'So for twelve years I've had two answers,' Coralie said.

**11.5, line 149. Kind 6 (one thing named two ways).**
> I've sat beside that bed three times with the phone in my hand, and by now I know what you look like when you've gone.

Coralie has been sitting on the edge of this bed since line 133. At line 115, from the doorway, she called it "this bed". "That bed" reads as another bed in another room.
Corrected: I've sat beside this bed three times with the phone in my hand, and by now I know what you look like when you've gone.

**11.6, line 169. Kind 7.**
> Then she clipped the four sheets back together, as they were typed.

The typing is before the scene and takes "had". In the simple past "as they were typed" reads as "while they were being typed".
Corrected: Then she clipped the four sheets back together, as they had been typed.

## Chapter 12 (2)

**12.1, line 257. Kind 5.**
> She wasn't going to have him turn the handle that sent him there.

"There" has no place to point to. The sentence before has "the six months", and the places named in the paragraph are the gate and Jeannot's hut. Chapter 6 has the word: "That's prison, for eels."
Corrected: She wasn't going to have him turn the handle that sent him to prison.

**12.2, line 319. Kind 5 (Inguma).**
> But the seat under her was still the seat of the van, and there was rain on the windscreen.

The sentence before is "Inguma pressed down just as hard." It is the same sentence the second review found in chapter 7, and again a "her" follows it with Maylis not named since the paragraph began.
Corrected: But the seat under Maylis was still the seat of the van, and there was rain on the windscreen.

## Checked and left

- Chapter 11, lines 119 and 125. "I've known that for twelve years" and "For eleven years I told myself it was a coincidence" still stand side by side. The second review left it to the author as a matter of fact. It is not counted here either.
- Chapter 11, line 133, "I was in there." No building has been named. The next sentence ("I was working nights that week") gives it, and line 135 has "that building".
- Chapter 7, line 69, opens its scene with "Lesca walked her down through the Port". Maylis is the only woman it can be. The second review of chapters 00 to 06 left the same opening twice.
- Chapter 7, line 79, "Last night she had lain still", and chapter 11, line 41, "tomorrow's ceremony". "Last night", "tonight" and "tomorrow" are used in the telling all through the book.
- Chapter 10, lines 59 to 81. Lesca's night is told in the simple past with no "had". The line before it gives the reason: the night "came to Maylis", and she is shown it as a scene. The "had" comes back at line 81 for what is earlier still.
- Chapter 10, line 107, "four turns less than he was told". Strictly "fewer". It is Jeannot's speech.
- Chapter 8, line 77, "On the Monday she forbids me". Present for past, in Jeannot's speech to the hall, and he goes on in the present.
- "Spelled", "leaned", "around", "towards", "onto", "until" in the telling, "trapdoor", "doorstep", "care home", "the Port" and "the State" are each written one way in all six chapters. "On to" appears only where "on" belongs to the verb ("holding on to that stone").
- Quotation marks, italics and the dash at a broken-off word are in pairs and in one form in every paragraph of the six chapters.
