## Chapter 7 (2)

**7.1, line 37. Kind 5 (Inguma).**
> She pushed until the church clock struck 4, when Inguma got down and left the way it had come.

The sentence before is "Inguma pressed down just as hard." So "She" follows Inguma, and "pushed" follows "pressed". The first review named Maylis two sentences up, for the same reason. This one was left.
Corrected: Maylis pushed until the church clock struck 4, when Inguma got down and left the way it had come.

**7.2, line 97. Kind 5.**
> The legs took her up onto it and stopped on the trapdoor.

"It" is the hut, which is four sentences back in the paragraph before. The nouns just before "it" are the van and the smoke.
Corrected: The legs took her up onto the hut and stopped on the trapdoor.

## Chapter 8 (2)

**8.1, line 3. Kind 5 (two women).**
> Lesca was walking her back along the track when Jeannot's van came down off the bank towards them.

This is the first sentence of the chapter, and "her" has no owner. Maylis is not named until the bar, eight lines down.
Corrected: Lesca was walking Maylis back along the track when Jeannot's van came down off the bank towards them.

**8.2, line 145. Kind 5 (two women).**
> At midnight Lesca walked Maylis up her own stairs, to the open door of the spare room.

The first review put the name in. "Her own" now answers to Lesca, the subject, and the section before opens "In Lesca's own bedroom". It reads as Lesca's house. It is Maylis's.
Corrected: At midnight, in Maylis's house, Lesca walked her up the stairs, to the open door of the spare room.

## Chapter 9 (3)

**9.1, line 17. Kind 3 (a mark that changes the meaning).**
> 'She got three words out at that bar,' she said. 'You're the water. The hall thought she was thirsty.

Coralie is quoting Maylis. With no marks around the three words, it reads as Coralie telling the woman across the table that she is the water. Chapters 1 and 6 put double marks around words quoted inside a speech ("I'll tell him.", "A man can no longer.").
Corrected: 'She got three words out at that bar,' she said. '"You're the water." The hall thought she was thirsty.

**9.2, line 97. Kind 5 (two women).**
> 'This isn't your kitchen.' Her fist closed.

Two women are standing in the room, the two lines before have no "said", and nobody is named in this paragraph. Line 233 has the same beat with the name ("Lesca's fist closed").
Corrected: 'This isn't your kitchen.' Lesca's fist closed.

**9.3, line 155. Kind 4 (rare sense).**
> Jeannot came around on his stool with his glass.

"Came around" for "turned" is rare. It reads as if he moved across the room on the stool. Chapter 7 has "Jeannot turned around on his stool".
Corrected: Jeannot turned around on his stool with his glass.

## Chapter 10 (1)

**10.1, line 95. Kind 5 (two women).**
> Her phone was on the chest of drawers.

The sentence before is "Lesca had done hers in that order." Lesca's phone, with the same number in it "because of the gate", is a page back. For a moment this phone is Lesca's too.
Corrected: Maylis's phone was on the chest of drawers.

## Chapter 11 (4)

**11.1, line 37. Kind 5 (two women).**
> She went around the new stage with her face turned away from the names on the stone.

The sentence before is "Lesca watched her cross the square towards the church." "She" follows Lesca.
Corrected: Arlette went around the new stage with her face turned away from the names on the stone.

**11.2, line 81. Kind 6 (quoted word marked two ways).**
> When the prefect reads out the date, he'll call out Here.

A capital in the middle of a sentence, with no marks, reads as a slip. Chapters 1 and 6 use double marks for a word quoted inside a speech.
Corrected: When the prefect reads out the date, he'll call out "Here".

**11.3, line 85. Kind 6 (the same).**
> He was asked in 2019, and he said Here.

Corrected: He was asked in 2019, and he said "Here".

**11.4, lines 133 and 135. Kind 3 (a mark that opens and does not close).**
> By 4 it had stopped coming, and at breakfast they told me what had been done.
>
> 'So for twelve years I've had two answers. Either you opened it for forty houses, and I live with the mayor.

Coralie's speech runs over a paragraph break, so the first paragraph ends with no closing mark. That is the printer's rule and it is correct. It is also the only place in the book where it happens, and it looks like a mark left off.
Corrected: By 4 it had stopped coming, and at breakfast they told me what had been done.'

'So for twelve years I've had two answers,' she said. 'Either you opened it for forty houses, and I live with the mayor.

## Chapter 12 (4)

**12.1, line 93. Kind 4 (reads as a word missing).**
> On red Sarthou turns his siren, and I move them.

Nothing in the book says the siren has a handle, so "turns his siren" reads as "turns his siren on" with the last word dropped.
Corrected: On red Sarthou sounds his siren, and I move them.

**12.2, line 139. Kind 4 (reads as a verb missing).**
> It had gone the colour of the sky, and very wide.

"Had gone" has to serve "very wide" as well, and a river does not "go wide". The last three words hang.
Corrected: It had gone the colour of the sky, and it was very wide.

**12.3, line 309. Kind 4.**
> If she was down there at 2:51, nobody was at the gate.

It is her thought set back into the past ("if I'm down there, nobody's at the gate"). On the page the second "was" reads as a wrong tense.
Corrected: If she was down there at 2:51, nobody would be at the gate.

**12.4, line 309. Kind 4 (word order).**
> She got in again wet and rolled the window down to let the cold in.

"Again wet" reads as "wet again".
Corrected: She got in again, wet, and rolled the window down to let the cold in.

## Checked and left

- Chapter 11, lines 119 and 125. Coralie says "I've known that for twelve years", and six lines on, "For eleven years I told myself it was a coincidence." Both may be meant. It is a matter of fact and not of grammar, so it is for the author and is not counted.
- Chapter 10, line 89, "On her night Lesca had forty houses to open the gate for." The first review took out "had had" and put the time phrase in. With the phrase the simple past stands.
- Chapter 9, line 215, has "till morning" in the telling. The other five "till" are in speech and the telling has "until" elsewhere. Both are correct.
- Bordenave reads from his pad in plain type (chapter 7, line 49, and chapter 9, line 115). Coralie reads from the speech in italics (chapter 11, line 111), where the italics part the mayor's words from her own inside one speech. Left.
- Clock times, counts, pulse readings and the times shown on a screen hold to the rule in all six chapters, as the first review found.
