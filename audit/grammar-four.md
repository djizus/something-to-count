## 00

Clean.

## 01 (1)

**1.1** Kind 4, the clause attaches to the wrong person.
> On Monday the mayor, Guy Larrère, had rung her to raise the floodgate down in the river bank.

"He rang her to raise the gate" says that he raised it. A reader stops to work out who turned the handle.

Corrected: On Monday the mayor, Guy Larrère, had rung and told her to raise the floodgate down in the river bank.

## 02 (2)

**2.1** Kind 4, regional idiom.
> 'My mum says you've done a rib, and you've got nine strings of lights to hang. Does it hurt? Can I go up in the bucket?'

"Done a rib" is British slang for "hurt a rib". It reads as a sentence with a word missing. Everywhere else the rib is "cracked".

Corrected: 'My mum says you've cracked a rib, and you've got nine strings of lights to hang. Does it hurt? Can I go up in the bucket?'

**2.2** Kind 5, "her" changes owner between two lines.
> 'You said it comes to her bed. I'm in that bed too,' Coralie said. 'When has it ever sat on me?'

The line before is "They lay a place for her at their table", where "her" is the dead sleeper. Here "her" is Maylis.

Corrected: 'You said it comes to Maylis's bed. I'm in that bed too,' Coralie said. 'When has it ever sat on me?'

## 03 (2)

**3.1** Kind 6, a pulse in words.
> 'Ninety, and coming down.'

Every other pulse in the book is in figures, in speech as well: 112, 130, 64, 58, 48, 47, 36, 34, "under 40". The first review saw this one and left it because it opens a sentence. It is still the only one.

Corrected: 'It's 90, and coming down.'

**3.2** Kind 5, "it" points at the wrong thing.
> At that table she had never said anything except that she abstained. She opened her mouth to say it.

The nearest thing "it" can be is "that she abstained". It is meant to be the verb she has just announced.

Corrected: At that table she had never said anything except that she abstained. She opened her mouth to say the verb.

## 04 (1)

**4.1** Kind 4, word order.
> All she had to tell him with was a dream.

The first six words read as "the only thing she needed to tell him". Then "with" arrives and the reader has to start the sentence again.

Corrected: The only reason she could give him was a dream.

## 05 (2)

**5.1** Kind 5, two women in one body.
> Lesca had the rest ready. Maylis felt the chest fill for it, as deep as the cracked rib would let it. She got to the mouth first.

Lesca is the one who is about to speak, so "She" can be Lesca until the next line.

Corrected: Lesca had the rest ready. Maylis felt the chest fill for it, as deep as the cracked rib would let it. Maylis got to the mouth first.

**5.2** Kind 5, the same.
> Maylis knew what Lesca wanted. She had lain still for the village's sake. It was going to cost her Coralie.

"She" follows "Lesca", and Lesca also lies under Inguma every night.

Corrected: Maylis knew what Lesca wanted. Maylis had lain still for the village's sake. It was going to cost her Coralie.

## 06 (3)

**6.1** Kind 5, whose door.
> On Sunday this same woman had come to her door in a blue dressing gown, with a potato in her hand.

The scene is seen through Maylis, so "her door" reads as Maylis's door, with Maïté visiting in a dressing gown. It was Maïté's own door.

Corrected: On Sunday this same woman had come to her own door in a blue dressing gown, with a potato in her hand.

**6.2** Kind 5, "her" after Lesca's name is Maylis.
> Lesca's hand came away and left her the mouth.

Maylis has not been named for two paragraphs. Maïté has just spoken and Lesca is the last woman named.

Corrected: Lesca's hand came away and left Maylis the mouth.

**6.3** Kind 5, "it" with nothing before it.
> She had been in room twelve when Madame Etcheto told them what happens to the one it can't take home.

Inguma is not named in this section. The nouns before "it" are the chart, the phone, the number and the thumb. The first review named the creature in two other sections of this chapter for the same reason.

Corrected: She had been in room twelve when Madame Etcheto told them what happens to the one Inguma can't take home.

## Seen and left alone

Checked, not counted.

- 03, "a Peyrebarthe that had its flood in 2014. Afterwards it made her mayor." Two events before the scene without "had". The strict form is "had had its flood", which reads as a word typed twice. The year carries the tense.
- 01 and 03, "That was twelve years ago", "A minute ago", "eleven years ago" in past-tense narration, beside "Sixteen years earlier". Normal in fiction told this close to one person.
- 02 and 03, "I've left you on that landing eleven years", with no "for". It is how the villagers count years in speech ("Eleven years I've been timed like an egg", "Twelve winters the mayor has been up at that house"). The narration always has "for".
- 05, "'Ei—' it said. Lesca cut it off. 'You have three minutes.'" Two speakers in one paragraph, but one mouth, and the sentence between them says who has it.
- 05, "At 8 Lesca walked her down through the Port" and "At noon Lesca walked her into the café" open a section on "her". Maylis is the only woman it can be.
- 05, "'Guy, she calls me, in my own square.'" "She" is "the village's girl" from the sentence before, not Madame Castagnet.
- 05 and 06, "The village was sent the bill", "she had been given one of Lesca's afternoons", "I'm told I wave at the camera". Passives of the everyday kind.
- 06, "It was five hours late." The line before has "something dry" and the line after names Inguma.
