## Chapter 13 (2)

**13.1 (4, reads as a mistake).**
"The palm of that hand was bandaged."
The sentence before is "Her right forearm was pressed across her side." No hand has been named, so "that hand" points at nothing.
Correction: "The palm of her right hand was bandaged."

**13.2 (4, reads as a mistake: a stray article).**
"Lesca waited for the Either."
The word takes no article anywhere else in the book: "to say Either" (chapter 4), "Either is not a person" (chapter 6), "That was Either" (chapter 17). Here "the Either" reads as a word left in by accident.
Correction: "Lesca waited for Either."

## Chapter 14 (5)

**14.1 (6, inconsistency: written words not in italics; two places).**
"The screen said Castagnet, house."
"The phone on the seat began to ring again. The screen said Castagnet, house."
Everywhere else, words that somebody reads off a page, a card, a sign or a phone are in italics: "*Etcheto back in 12.*", "*Caution. Wet floor.*", "*No. 42*". Without them the comma reads as part of the sentence, and "house" as a stray word.
Corrections: "The screen said *Castagnet, house*." and "The phone on the seat began to ring again. The screen said *Castagnet, house*."

**14.2 (4, reads as a mistake).**
"She had told Coralie both."
It is Maylis's one-word answer on the phone, set down without quotation marks. After "told Coralie", "both" reads as "both of them" with the noun missing.
Correction: "She had told Coralie she wanted both."

**14.3 (4, reads as a mistake: regional idiom).**
"'Little Lesca,' he said. 'You've gone off your head.'"
"Off your head" is British and informal. A reader who does not know it will look for a missing word.
Correction: "'Little Lesca,' he said. 'You've gone mad.'"

**14.4 (4, reads as a mistake: idiom).**
"Maylis wanted to let go of the chair and run for it."
"It" is the fire door. But "to run for it" is a set phrase that means to run away, so the sentence says that Maylis wanted to flee.
Correction: "Maylis wanted to let go of the chair and run to it."

**14.5 (5, pronoun).**
"He took the next one from Jeannot and the one after from her."
"Her" is Maylis. The last woman named is the one in the wheelchair, three paragraphs back, who "had taken hold of her sleeve". Coralie and Nadine are in the corridor too.
Correction: "He took the next one from Jeannot and the one after from Maylis."

## Chapter 16 (1)

**16.1 (5, pronoun).**
"That evening Maïté let her in without asking why she had come."
It is the first sentence of a new scene, and Lesca is not named in that scene until its fifth paragraph. The last "her" before the break is Madame Etcheto: "until the chair had turned her away."
Correction: "That evening Maïté let Lesca in without asking why she had come."

## Chapter 17 (1)

**17.1 (5, pronoun).**
"'I paid to register forty-one of them, to get his name on a card, and I never got it once.'"
"His" is Larrère's. Jeannot has not named him, and Larrère is not in the cemetery. Two men have been named in the scene, both by the priest before Jeannot arrived: Monsieur Etcheto, whose grave is beside them, and Monsieur Larrère. In chapter 1 the line was "that man's name", said with Larrère's car in the square.
Correction: "'I paid to register forty-one of them, to get Larrère's name on a card, and I never got it once.'"
