## 01 (6)

**1.1** Kind 7, tense.
> That was twelve years ago, in June 2014, when the village council rented the barn to a team from a university. Inside it the team built a steel room the size of a garden shed.

The flashback opens without "had", then uses it two lines later ("had told her"). The other flashback in the chapter opens with it ("Coralie had found her").

Corrected: That was twelve years ago, in June 2014, when the village council had rented the barn to a team from a university. Inside it the team had built a steel room the size of a garden shed.

**1.2** Kind 4, verb in an odd sense.
> 'He had that gate raised to empty my water.'

A container is emptied, water is not.

Corrected: 'He had that gate raised to let my water out.'

**1.3** Kind 4, reads as an agreement error.
> 'Eggs is what Either gets in this house.'

Corrected: 'Eggs are what Either gets in this house.'

**1.4** Kind 3, speech inside speech not marked.
> 'He said, I'll tell him. That's forty-one Jeannot's paid for. He's spent more on stamps than the association has spent on its gate.'

It reads for a moment as Maylis promising to tell him. Chapter 06 marks speech inside speech with double quotation marks.

Corrected: 'He said, "I'll tell him." That's forty-one Jeannot's paid for. He's spent more on stamps than the association has spent on its gate.'

**1.5** Kind 4, reads as "kept away from work".
> She took the pan off the heat and went to get the first-aid box she kept from work.

Corrected: She took the pan off the heat and went to get the first-aid box she had brought home from work.

(The same phrase is in 17.)

**1.6** Kind 5, "it" beside the creature.
> This time somebody was under it with her. It wasn't Coralie. Coralie was asleep by the wall.

The first "it" is the creature. The second can be read as the creature too.

Corrected: This time somebody was under it with her. Not Coralie. Coralie was asleep by the wall.

## 02 (4)

**2.1** Kind 4, regional idiom, and two sentences joined by a comma.
> 'I pushed it off both times, I was that scared.'

Corrected: 'I pushed it off both times. I was so scared.'

**2.2** Kind 5, "her" points at the grandmother.
> 'He got his Basque from his grandmother, and in his first week he said the words along with her, through the door, to be friendly.'

Corrected: 'He got his Basque from his grandmother, and in his first week he said the words along with Madame Etcheto, through the door, to be friendly.'

**2.3** Kind 6, one door with two names.
> 'She's the one who repaired the lamp over the yard door, and she'll tell you she didn't.'

It is "the back door" everywhere else (00, 02 twice, 05, and on through the book).

Corrected: 'She's the one who repaired the lamp over the back door, and she'll tell you she didn't.'

**2.4** Kind 5, "it" changes owner between two lines.
> In room twelve she had given it the leaves in the square.

The line before is "It sounded like something she was reading off a label", where "it" is the words.

Corrected: In room twelve she had given Inguma the leaves in the square.

## 03 (9)

**3.1** Kind 4, "take" reads as "bear".
> Two words were all Maylis could take before Lesca had the jaw back.

Corrected: Two words were all Maylis could get out before Lesca had the jaw back.

**3.2** Kind 7, tense.
> It had been needed for Larrère, who was mayor before her.

Corrected: It had been needed for Larrère, who had been mayor before her.

**3.3** Kind 5, two women.
> She hadn't spoken to Lesca directly since 2018, so she said it to the wall above her head.

"Her head" can be Marie-Jo's own. Chapter 06 has "the wall above Maylis's head".

Corrected: She hadn't spoken to Lesca directly since 2018, so she said it to the wall above Lesca's head.

**3.4** Kind 4, "reads that" is rare.
> 'It reads that he was saved, by her.'

Corrected: 'It reads as if he was saved, by her.'

**3.5** Kind 4, the same.
> 'It reads that he was opened.'

Corrected: 'It reads as if he was opened.'

**3.6** Kind 5, "them" with no plural before it.
> Now she read them with a finger under each.

The sentence before has "that line" (the red one) and "one line a day", both singular.

Corrected: Now she read those lines with a finger under each.

**3.7** Kind 5, two "it"s side by side, one of them the creature.
> 'She gave it that, blade by blade, and it was at it till daylight.'

Corrected: 'She gave it that, blade by blade, and it was counting till daylight.'

**3.8** Kind 5, "she" reads as Coralie.
> 'Coralie tells me she rings for the night staff at 3.'

Corrected: 'Coralie tells me the old woman rings for the night staff at 3.'

**3.9** Kind 4, old or regional form.
> 'I've the minutes of this morning's meeting to read.'

Everywhere else it is "I've got" ("I've got lights to hang", "I've got a registered letter").

Corrected: 'I've got the minutes of this morning's meeting to read.'

## 04 (6)

**4.1** Kind 5, "it" is the finger she has just lifted.
> Coralie took her finger off *You* and left it on *Somebody else*.

She had a finger on each box. The one she took off cannot be the one she left.

Corrected: Coralie took her finger off *You* and left the other on *Somebody else*.

**4.2** Kind 4, a quotation mark that opens and does not close.
> 'You're looking at her. The second item is how this works. There are three points, and I'll say them once.
>
> 'In this chair, what one of us knows, the other gets. That's how I had your rib.'

This is the correct convention for a speech that runs over two paragraphs. It is used only here in these seven chapters, and it looks like a dropped quotation mark.

Corrected, as one paragraph: 'You're looking at her. The second item is how this works. There are three points, and I'll say them once. In this chair, what one of us knows, the other gets. That's how I had your rib.'

**4.3** Kind 5, "it" beside the creature.
> 'I can't. It has sat on me every night for twelve years, and I've never kept it off.'

The last "it" in the exchange was the flood ("I don't know why it comes").

Corrected: 'I can't. Inguma has sat on me every night for twelve years, and I've never kept it off.'

**4.4** Kind 5, whose look, on whose face.
> Maylis could feel the look on it, the one people give a woman beside a grave.

After "Maïté looked up from the flowers at Maylis's face", the look reads as Maïté's.

Corrected: Maylis could feel the look on her own face, the one people give a woman beside a grave.

**4.5** Kind 4, "turned around" reads as "turned to face the other way".
> She took the pot and turned around the kitchen once with it.

Corrected: She took the pot and walked once around the kitchen with it.

**4.6** Kind 4, unusual passive.
> 'I was looked at like that at my mother's funeral.'

Corrected: 'People looked at me like that at my mother's funeral.'

## 05 (3)

**5.1** Kind 5, three women and two pronouns.
> Maylis got that laugh three or four times a year. She had always laughed with her.

Corrected: Maylis got that laugh three or four times a year. She had always laughed with Coralie.

**5.2** Kind 1, grammar: the first clause is attached to nothing.

Corrected: 'Everything I asked, I got an answer to.'

**5.3** Kind 4, "stand somebody up" means to fail to meet them.

Corrected: Maylis felt her legs lift her out of the chair.

## 06 (7)

**6.1** Kind 5, "it" with nothing before it but the wardrobe.
> She gave it every metre of roadside she had cut in this village since 2008, to count before it came to take her home.

Inguma is not named anywhere in this section. The sentence before ends "to the top of the wardrobe".

Corrected: She gave Inguma every metre of roadside she had cut in this village since 2008, to count before it came to take her home.

**6.2** Kind 5, "her" twice for two women.
> Maylis lay beside her on her back, with her eyes open.

Corrected: Maylis lay on her back beside her, with her eyes open.

**6.3** Kind 1, wrong preposition.
> *02:50. Water through the bank at the repair beside the last house, on the width of a door. The keyholder gave the bank ten minutes.*

Chapter 05 has "across the width of a door".

Corrected: *02:50. Water through the bank at the repair beside the last house, across the width of a door. The keyholder gave the bank ten minutes.*

**6.4** Kind 4, word order.
> He was crossing the square in front of the stone, pulling a plastic sheet piled with plane leaves by one corner.

"By one corner" attaches to the leaves.

Corrected: He was crossing the square in front of the stone, pulling a plastic sheet by one corner. It was piled with plane leaves.

**6.5** Kind 5, "it" with nothing before it.
> That gave it somewhere to sit.

Inguma has not been named since the section began. The nearest nouns are the floor and the filing cupboard.

Corrected: That gave Inguma somewhere to sit.

**6.6** Kind 5, "it came" used for the creature and then for the mayor's life.
> If it never came, all of this was hers: the desk, the stone, a square that said good morning. That morning, standing in front of the stone, she might have said yes to it. But it came with Kévin's cap and one pillow.

The last sentence reads as Inguma arriving with a cap and a pillow.

Corrected: If Inguma never came, all of this was hers: the desk, the stone, a square that said good morning. That morning, standing in front of the stone, she might have said yes to it. But all of it came with Kévin's cap and one pillow.

**6.7** Kind 6, a motion worded two ways.
> 'So I move that on the 12th the prefect speaks alone.'

The other motions use the bare verb: "That the mayor remain seated" (03), "I propose that the stone be given a verb" (03, the same speaker).

Corrected: 'So I move that on the 12th the prefect speak alone.'

## Seen and left alone

These were checked and are not counted.

- 05, "Lesca walked her down through the Port", "walked her up onto the bank", "walked her into the café", "walked her along the passage". Active, and clear each time. Only the passive form is a problem.
- 06, "with effect from." The sentence stops on purpose and the next line explains it.
- 01, "a hand of water". Jeannot shows the depth with his hand in the same breath. The phrase comes back in 10 and 12.
- Present tense for a past event in speech: "This morning somebody asks me" (04), "yesterday she's up at his wife's" (05), "this morning she laughs in the minute" (06). Three speakers, one habit of speech.
- Present tense for a general truth in the narration: "the kind the council votes on", "A motion is supposed to say what it is for" (03), "what happens to the one it can't take home" (06).
- 24-hour times (06:00, 06:20, 18:40, 22:00, 02:50, 03:11) appear only where a clock radio or the file is being read. Everything else is in the 12-hour form.
- 03, "Ninety, and coming down." A pulse in words, at the start of a sentence. The others are in figures.
- 05, *Madame le Maire* on Bordenave's pad. The capital is the joke.
