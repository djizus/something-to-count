No misspelling, doubled word, missing word, unbalanced quotation mark or American spelling is left in these chapters. Names, capitals, compound words, dates and clock times are written one way throughout. Inguma is "it" everywhere, and no "she" or "her" can be taken for it.

## 00

Clean.

## 01 (1)

**1.1** Kind 4, a phrase that is not English.
> On Sunday night I had a hand of water on those fields.

"A hand of water" reads as a sentence with a word missing. The gesture in the next line explains it, but the reader has already stopped. Chapter 5 has the ordinary form, "a hand's width".

Corrected: On Sunday night I had a hand's width of water on those fields.

## 02 (2)

**2.1** Kind 4, a bare participle that reads as the wrong tense.
> And Maylis, come to take me home.

"Come" stands for "who has come". A fluent reader takes it for "came" misspelled, or for an order given to Maylis.

Corrected: And Maylis, who's come to take me home.

**2.2** Kind 6, the day is one out.
> Somebody had been under there with her since Tuesday, and she wanted to know who.

The book calls the first visit Tuesday: the heading of chapter 1 is "Tuesday 24 November 2026, 2:51 a.m.", and Coralie says "on Tuesday at 3 in the morning". On that visit Maylis was alone under it. Somebody was under it with her for the first time on the second visit, twenty-four hours later.

Corrected: Somebody had been under there with her since Tuesday night, and she wanted to know who.

## 03 (3)

**3.1** Kind 7, a present tense inside a past-tense sentence.
> When something had to be decided, the only form she had for it was a motion, the kind the council votes on.

Every other explanation of this kind in the book is in the past: "the baby eels that came up the river", "the steel tooth that stopped the chain running back", "Any member could speak for three minutes".

Corrected: When something had to be decided, the only form she had for it was a motion, the kind the council voted on.

**3.2** Kind 4, the sentence seems to stop one word early.
> She wrote the motion in.

"Wrote in" is meant as "filled in under the heading". Standing alone as a paragraph, with the heading three sentences back, "in" reads as the start of a phrase that was cut.

Corrected: She wrote the motion under the heading.

**3.3** Kind 6, one document quoted two ways. Open since the first review.
> *12 December 2014. Body of CASTAGNET Jean recovered at first light from the cellar of his hut. Watch stopped at 03:11.*

Chapter 6 gives the same typed line as "*First light. CASTAGNET Jean recovered from the cellar of his duck hut. Trapdoor locked on the inside. Watch stopped at 03:11.*" The words differ in five places: the date, "Body of", where "first light" stands, "hut" against "duck hut", and the trapdoor.

Corrected: *First light. CASTAGNET Jean recovered from the cellar of his duck hut. Watch stopped at 03:11.*

This makes chapter 3 the chapter 6 line word for word, with the trapdoor sentence left out. The date is not lost: her own line just above is dated 11 December, and the stone has the 12th. If the locked trapdoor is not being held back for chapter 6, put that sentence into chapter 3 as well.

## 04

Clean.

## 05 (1)

**5.1** Kind 5, two women in one body.
> The patch she repaired every spring was in the bank above his roof.

The sentence before is "At 8 Lesca walked her down through the Port", so "she" follows Lesca. Lesca also repaired that patch every spring, in her own village. The woman meant is Maylis. Naming her here also settles "By her count" two paragraphs later.

Corrected: The patch Maylis repaired every spring was in the bank above his roof.

## 06 (3)

**6.1** Kind 4, an idiom that reads as a slip.
> That made twice he had asked her about that kitchen, and he still hadn't asked about the river.

"That made twice" is a spoken British turn. A fluent reader sees a word missing after "twice".

Corrected: That was the second time he had asked her about that kitchen, and he still hadn't asked about the river.

**6.2** Kind 3, a missing comma.
> Next to her Lesca's right hand tapped its fingers on the floorboards.

Without the comma the eye joins "her Lesca's", and the reader has to start again.

Corrected: Next to her, Lesca's right hand tapped its fingers on the floorboards.

**6.3** Kind 4, word order.
> She had worked the roadsides out at the speed of the village tractor. Inguma was doing them on foot.

"Worked the roadsides" is what Maylis does for a living, so the sentence is read that way until "out" arrives. It means she had calculated.

Corrected: She had worked out the count at the speed of the village tractor. Inguma was doing the roadsides on foot.

## Seen and left alone

Checked, not counted.

- 01, "Beside her Coralie slept on, turned to the wall." The same shape as 6.2, but nothing possessive follows "her", and the line is read once.
- 01, "The roof came up to her shoulder. So had the stone." Short for "so had the stone come up". Correct, and the picture of the stone is earlier than the car.
- 01, "Eighteen days was five trailers." A length of time takes a singular verb.
- 03 and 06, "A motion is supposed to say what it is for" and "what happens to the one Inguma can't take home". Each is a rule stated whole, not an explanation tucked into a past-tense sentence as in 3.1.
- 03 and 06, "I propose that the stone be given a verb", "*That the mayor remain seated*", "I move that on the 12th the prefect speak alone". The form of a motion. It is only ever used for motions.
- 04, "All Saints' Day" in the narration and "All Saints" in Marie-Jo's mouth. The short spoken name of the feast.
- 04, "The getting away was hers." It answers Coralie's "which one was you?", so it can only be Maylis.
- 04, "That floodgate is opened on Friday afternoon." Said on a Saturday, thirteen days before the Friday meant. Lesca has pinned it to "Friday the 11th" earlier in the same scene.
- 05, "Its tractor pulled your van out of the track". A van is pulled out of mud, but Jeannot's answer, "And left two wheel tracks in it", needs the track.
- 05, "the State's office". The only capital S, and the only use of the word.
- 06, "with effect from." The sentence stops where Larrère stops her, and his next line says so.
- 06, "The church clock struck 2, the hour on her pad. She sat still and waited for the cold." The secretary is the last woman named, but the pad and the cold are Maylis's.
