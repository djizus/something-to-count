## Chapter 13 (1)

**13.1 (4, reads as a mistake: word order).**
"On Sunday Lesca had tried to call it the tide, standing, and the word hadn't come."
"Standing" refers to Lesca on her feet in the steel room in chapter 9. Set after "the tide", between two commas, it reads as a stray word or as the tide standing.
Correction: "On Sunday, standing in the steel room, Lesca had tried to call it the tide, and the word hadn't come."

## Chapter 14 (3)

**14.1 (5, pronoun: "she" and "it").**
"She walked towards it through the water, counting."
The last woman to act was Coralie, two sentences back: "At 10 Coralie had walked Madame Etcheto back to it." The same verb follows, so "She walked" can be taken for Coralie. The "it" before this one was room twelve. This "it" is the front yard, and the nearest nouns are plural ("two vehicles", "the glass doors").
Correction: "Maylis walked towards the yard through the water, counting."

**14.2 (1, grammar: agreement).**
"Whatever had given in her side at the trapdoor now let her have a quarter of a breath at a time, so she took them fast."
"Them" has no plural to stand for. The only noun is "a quarter of a breath".
Correction: "Whatever had given in her side at the trapdoor now let her have a quarter of a breath at a time, so she breathed fast."

**14.3 (4, reads as a mistake: idiom).**
"A man sat in the last chair but one."
"The last but one" is British and uncommon elsewhere. To a reader who does not know it, "but one" reads as a missing word. The next line, "The last chair was empty", then seems to contradict it.
Correction: "A man sat in the second chair from the end."

## Chapter 17 (2)

**17.1 (4, reads as a mistake: word order).**
"In the kitchen she got out the first-aid box she kept from work and strapped the rib where Maylis sat, with three turns of the wide bandage, pulled tight."
"The rib where Maylis sat" reads as if "where Maylis sat" says which rib.
Correction: "In the kitchen she got out the first-aid box she kept from work and strapped Maylis's rib where she sat, with three turns of the wide bandage, pulled tight."

**17.2 (5, pronoun: "its" beside Inguma).**
"It went out over the step of the door, and the door shut behind it on its rubber seal."
"It" is Inguma twice, and "its" three words later is the door. The seal can be taken for a part of Inguma, which is already steel and rivets.
Correction: "It went out over the step of the door, and the door shut behind it on the rubber seal."
