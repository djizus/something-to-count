No chapter is clean. There are no misspellings, no doubled or missing words, no unclosed quotation marks and no American spellings in these five chapters. Inguma is "it" everywhere; no "she" or "her" is attached to it.

## Chapter 13 (5)

**13.1 (5, pronoun).**
"The wall on her right was gone again."
The sentence before ends "Lesca stood up after her", where "her" is Maylis. Both women are standing and both are called Maylis Lesca, so "her right", "she could see" and "the name on the card was hers" have no owner until Maylis is named again two sentences later.
Correction: "The wall on Lesca's right was gone again."

**13.2 (5, pronoun: "it").**
"'I asked you not to come,' Maylis said. 'You came, and you brought it with you. But my bank's still standing. If I get you out of me now, it may hold. You're not staying.'"
The first "it" is the river, but the river has not been named in the chapter, and the only "it" a reader is carrying is Inguma. The second "it" is the bank.
Correction: "'I asked you not to come,' Maylis said. 'You came, and you brought the river with you. But my bank's still standing. If I get you out of me now, it may hold. You're not staying.'"

**13.3 (4, reads as a mistake).**
"Far off, the phone had rung itself out."
"To ring itself out" is not a set phrase. "Rung … out" reads as a misspelling of "wrung out".
Correction: "Far off, the phone had rung until it stopped."

**13.4 (3, a comma that changes the meaning).**
"She had wanted both herself, the bed and Castagnet."
It reads as a list of three things she wanted: herself, the bed and Castagnet.
Correction: "She herself had wanted both, the bed and Castagnet."

**13.5 (7, tense).**
"She had held the wheel against it for a whole ring, and this was where that put her."
The putting is before the scene.
Correction: "She had held the wheel against it for a whole ring, and this was where that had put her."

## Chapter 14 (9)

**14.1 (4, reads as a mistake).**
"She had put Lesca out, and it had come too late: the river was through the bank already."
"To put somebody out" usually means to annoy or inconvenience them. "It" has no noun to stand for.
Correction: "She had got Lesca out of her, but too late: the river was through the bank already."

**14.2 (4, reads as a mistake).**
"Under the bank was Coralie, who had said 7, and be in the house."
"Be in the house" is Coralie's own order from chapter 12, set down without quotation marks. It reads as a missing word.
Correction: "Under the bank was Coralie, who had said 7, and told her to be in the house."

**14.3 (5, pronoun).**
"She had given the bank ten minutes, and it had held for sixteen."
The sentence before ends on "Lesca's file", and in that file (chapter 6) Lesca also "gave the bank ten minutes". "She" can be taken for Lesca.
Correction: "Maylis had given the bank ten minutes, and it had held for sixteen."

**14.4 (1, grammar: misplaced clause).**
"He was holding a child above his head, wrapped in a blanket."
"Wrapped in a blanket" attaches to his head.
Correction: "He was holding a child wrapped in a blanket above his head."

**14.5 (4, reads as a mistake).**
"Monsieur Daguerre was sitting in it with his cap on over his pyjamas and both hands on the arms."
A coat goes on over pyjamas. A cap does not.
Correction: "Monsieur Daguerre was sitting in it in his pyjamas, with his cap on and both hands on the arms."

**14.6 (5, pronoun).**
"Her mouth opened and shut again."
The "her" just before it, in "the water running out of her raincoat", is Maylis. This "her" is Coralie.
Correction: "Coralie's mouth opened and shut again."

**14.7 (4, reads as a mistake).**
"He walked him across the yard in his rubber boots."
Monsieur Hourcade is on Jeannot's back. "To walk somebody" means to go beside them while they walk, as in "Coralie had walked Madame Etcheto back to it" earlier in the chapter.
Correction: "He carried him across the yard in his rubber boots."

**14.8 (6, inconsistency: time).**
"It was 4 a.m."
The only "a.m." in the book outside the date line that opens chapter 1. Elsewhere the hour stands alone or takes "in the morning": "at 3 on Saturday morning", "nearly 6 on Saturday morning".
Correction: "It was 4 in the morning."

**14.9 (4, reads as a mistake).**
"Over the bed stood the lift they used for getting a resident out of it."
In British English a lift is the cabin that goes between floors. The machine over a bed is a hoist.
Correction: "Over the bed stood the hoist they used for getting a resident out of it."

## Chapter 15 (2)

**15.1 (5, pronoun: "it").**
"Then it called a number, first whole and then in parts."
"It" is the voice. But the chapter opens "Inguma didn't put Lesca down. It held her in the dark", so this "it" can be taken for Inguma.
Correction: "Then the voice called a number, first whole and then in parts."

**15.2 (5, pronoun).**
"Somebody has to stack them."
"Them" is the two hundred chairs, two sentences back. The nearest plural is "the loudspeakers". It is the last line of the chapter.
Correction: "Somebody has to stack the chairs."

## Chapter 16 (3)

**16.1 (5, pronoun).**
"She still had her black coat on."
The sentence before is "That evening Maïté let her in without asking why she had come", where "she" is Lesca. This "she" is Maïté.
Correction: "Maïté still had her black coat on."

**16.2 (4, reads as a mistake; two places).**
"'The member concerned may speak to it.'"
"He didn't ask to speak to it."
"To speak to an item" is correct in a council, and rare outside one. It reads as a wrong preposition, or as talking to the item.
Corrections: "'The member concerned may speak on it.'" and "He didn't ask to speak on it."

**16.3 (3, punctuation: speaker).**
"'Against.'"
The line has no speaker and follows "Larrère kept both of his on the book", so it reads as Larrère voting against. It is the deputy mayor calling for the votes against.
Correction: "'Against,' the deputy mayor said."

## Chapter 17 (7)

**17.1 (6, inconsistency: British English; three sentences).**
"Two doors up, Madame Darrigrand stood on the pavement beside a baby carriage, with the baby inside her coat. The carriage was full of mud up to the hood."
"She wiped the handle of the baby carriage with her sleeve."
"Baby carriage" is American. The rest of the book's vocabulary is British (torch, pavement, windscreen, rubbish bin).
Corrections: "Two doors up, Madame Darrigrand stood on the pavement beside a pram, with the baby inside her coat. The pram was full of mud up to the hood." and "She wiped the handle of the pram with her sleeve."

**17.2 (6, inconsistency: time).**
"At 02:57 she left it."
In this book "02:57" is what a screen shows, and a spoken time has no leading zero. Sarthou himself says "3:11" in chapter 14. The one other spoken "02:51", in chapter 11, is an entry read out of a file, not a sentence.
Correction: "At 2:57 she left it."

**17.3 (6, inconsistency: one thing with two names).**
"Josiane took a loaf out of the box, laid it on the counter and counted the change into Maylis's left hand one coin at a time."
Eight lines earlier the bread "stood in its crate on the counter", as in chapter 4.
Correction: "Josiane took a loaf out of the crate, laid it on the counter and counted the change into Maylis's left hand one coin at a time."

**17.4 (5, pronoun).**
"Maylis followed her as far as the bedroom door and found she had brought the pad with her."
"She" can be Coralie or Maylis. It is Maylis, as the next paragraph shows.
Correction: "Maylis followed her as far as the bedroom door and found the pad in her own hands."

**17.5 (4, reads as a mistake: word order).**
"'Her daughter wants the words she said each night said over her.'"
"Said each night said" reads as a word typed twice.
Correction: "'Her daughter wants the words she said each night to be said over her.'"

**17.6 (5, pronoun).**
"'She left on the bus.'"
The line before is about "who had rung her, and from whose kitchen", where "her" is Maylis. "She" is Maïté, who has not been named since the bus scene.
Correction: "'Maïté left on the bus.'"

**17.7 (5, pronoun).**
"A second shovel was leaning against the wall of the tool hut, where her father had kept it."
The "her" just before it, in "'She gave my mother her first communion dress'", is Madame Etcheto or Jeannot's mother. This "her" is Maylis.
Correction: "A second shovel was leaning against the wall of the tool hut, where Maylis's father had kept it."
