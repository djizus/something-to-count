No chapter is clean. There is no misspelling, no doubled word, no unclosed quotation mark and no American spelling in these six chapters. Two thirds of the list is one fault: a "she", "her" or "it" that follows the wrong noun. Six of those are near Inguma.

Line numbers are the line in the chapter file.

## Chapter 7 (8)

**7.1, line 5. Kind 5.**
> Coralie sat on the edge of it in her uniform, with a square of wet cotton in one hand and her phone at her ear.

The noun just before "it" is the roof.
Corrected: Coralie sat on the edge of the bed in her uniform, with a square of wet cotton in one hand and her phone at her ear.

**7.2, line 27. Kind 5.**
> He was eighty-one.

It follows "I've seen one do it", where "one" is a rib. No man has been named, so "He" has no owner.
Corrected: The man was eighty-one.

**7.3, line 37. Kind 5 (Inguma).**
> She pushed, the way Coralie had told her not to.

It follows "Inguma came down off the wardrobe and sat on her chest." For half the sentence "She" is the one that sat down.
Corrected: Maylis pushed, the way Coralie had told her not to.

**7.4, line 37. Kind 5 (Inguma).**
> It pressed down just as hard.

It follows "Every breath went through the crack in the rib." The "It" reads as the rib or the breath. Chapter 12 has the same three sentences with the name in ("Inguma pressed down just as hard").
Corrected: Inguma pressed down just as hard.

**7.5, line 39. Kind 5 (Inguma).**
> At 2:51 it came down the stairs, with the sound of a sack being lowered down a step, and it found her.

Inguma has not been named in this paragraph. The nouns before "it" are the blanket, the floor, the table and the stove.
Corrected: At 2:51 Inguma came down the stairs, with the sound of a sack being lowered down a step, and it found her.

**7.6, line 79. Kind 1 (agreement).**
> On Wednesday and Thursday night Maylis had pushed Inguma off until 4.

Two nights.
Corrected: On Wednesday and Thursday nights Maylis had pushed Inguma off until 4.

**7.7, line 83. Kind 5 (two women).**
> She had nobody to say it to and no mouth to say it with.

The line before is "The rain came when Lesca came." "She" reads as Lesca until "no mouth".
Corrected: Maylis had nobody to say it to and no mouth to say it with.

**7.8, line 105. Kind 5 (two women).**
> She looked back the way they had come.

It follows "Maylis held out her right hand for the watch and Lesca gave it to her." Lesca is the last one named, and Lesca has the eyes in this scene.
Corrected: Maylis looked back the way they had come.

## Chapter 8 (2)

**8.1, line 13. Kind 4 (word order).**
> The woman next to them marked hers with her tongue between her teeth.

It reads as if she marked the card with her tongue.
Corrected: The woman next to them marked hers, with her tongue between her teeth.

**8.2, line 145. Kind 5 (two women).**
> At midnight Lesca walked her up her own stairs, to the open door of the spare room.

This opens a section. The section before it is in Lesca's bedroom with Arlette, where every "her" is Lesca or her mother, so this "her" has no owner. The name fixes it and makes "her own stairs" clear as well.
Corrected: At midnight Lesca walked Maylis up her own stairs, to the open door of the spare room.

## Chapter 9 (6)

**9.1, line 43. Kind 5 (two women).**
> Maylis let her have the mouth.

The paragraph before is Coralie speaking and looking, so "her" reads as Coralie.
Corrected: Maylis let Lesca have the mouth.

**9.2, line 61. Kind 5.**
> Then she leaned across the bread, took the left wrist in both of hers and pushed it down flat on the table, with all her weight on it.

"Both of hers" answers to "wrist", so it reads as both of her wrists.
Corrected: Then she leaned across the bread, took the left wrist in both hands and pushed it down flat on the table, with all her weight on it.

**9.3, line 83. Kind 5 (two women).**
> She wanted to tell her not to go.

The paragraph before is Coralie's, ending on "she said". "She" reads as Coralie.
Corrected: Maylis wanted to tell her not to go.

**9.4, line 85. Kind 5 (two women).**
> 'It's twenty minutes to Bayonne,' she said.

The two sentences before it are about Coralie ("She had waited since March to tell anybody"), so "she said" reads as Coralie answering herself.
Corrected: 'It's twenty minutes to Bayonne,' Maylis said.

**9.5, line 137. Kind 4 (idiom).**
> Kévin was up a ladder at it, in the rain.

"At it" for "working on it" is a rare use and reads as a word missing.
Corrected: Kévin was up a ladder mending it, in the rain.

**9.6, line 207. Kind 5 (Inguma).**
> That night Maylis tried the words the way Madame Etcheto used them, to keep it off her.

This opens a section. "It" has no noun, and "her" reads as Madame Etcheto.
Corrected: That night Maylis tried the words the way Madame Etcheto used them, to keep Inguma off.

## Chapter 10 (6)

**10.1, line 9. Kind 4 (rare sense).**
> Coralie had never seen one beaten.

"Beaten" reads as "hit" before it reads as "defeated".
Corrected: Coralie had never seen one fail.

**10.2, line 45. Kind 5 (two women).**
> Coralie's free hand was flat on her right side before the laugh got there.

It reads as Coralie's own side.
Corrected: Coralie's free hand was flat on Maylis's right side before the laugh got there.

**10.3, line 57. Kind 5 (Inguma).**
> Its arms were cold.

It follows "Maylis lay still under Inguma, and then she was in the chair." Inguma is the book's "it", so the arms read as Inguma's. The refrain elsewhere is "The arms of the chair were warm."
Corrected: The arms of the chair were cold.

**10.4, line 59. Kind 4.**
> The floodgate stayed shut.

This is Larrère's order, reported. As it stands it reads as a fact of the night: the gate stayed shut. Lesca opens it a page later.
Corrected: The floodgate was to stay shut.

**10.5, line 71. Kind 5.**
> It rang once and nobody answered.

She has taken out her phone and read the screen, but nothing says she called. "It rang" reads as her own phone ringing with a call coming in, and Larrère has just rung her in the same scene. Line 97 does state the step ("she pressed Jeannot's number").
Corrected: His number rang once and nobody answered.

**10.6, line 89. Kind 4 (reads as a word twice).**
> Lesca had had forty houses to open the gate for. She had also had Coralie's car in that yard.

"Had had" is correct and looks like a doubled word. A time phrase does the work of the first "had".
Corrected: On her night Lesca had forty houses to open the gate for. She also had Coralie's car in that yard.

## Chapter 11 (2)

**11.1, line 15. Kind 5.**
> I don't like the man, he drives through my yard as if it was the road, but I voted for him, and I'll say so while I'm standing in it.

"It" is meant to be the office. The nouns before it are the yard and the road.
Corrected: I don't like the man, he drives through my yard as if it was the road, but I voted for him, and I'll say so while I'm standing in here.

**11.2, line 27. Kind 1 (dangling clause).**
> She had counted her ponies up the ramp of that truck, to make room for the hole.

The clause hangs on "counted": she counted them to make room. It was the ponies that went to make room.
Corrected: She had counted her ponies up the ramp of that truck. They had gone to make room for the hole.

## Chapter 12 (6)

**12.1, line 203. Kind 4.**
> Her rib let her walk, and no faster.

"And" where the sense is "but". A reader stops to work it out.
Corrected: Her rib let her walk, but no faster.

**12.2, line 233. Kind 4.**
> *Where were you? I ring again tonight. Be in that van.*

Present for future. Larrère uses the future everywhere else ("You'll hear from me"), so here it reads as a foreigner's slip.
Corrected: *Where were you? I'll ring again tonight. Be in that van.*

**12.3, line 239. Kind 5.**
> I'm bringing them in.

One net has been shown and it is called "the net" twice. "Them" reads as the eels.
Corrected: I'm bringing my nets in.

**12.4, line 297. Kind 5.**
> At 11 she rang his number.

The last men named are Sarthou and old Bordenave. Jeannot is three sentences back.
Corrected: At 11 she rang Jeannot's number.

**12.5, line 321. Kind 4 (idiom).**
> She took one to a ring and pushed.

"One to a ring" for "one breath for each ring" is a rare turn and reads as a word missing.
Corrected: She took one breath for each ring and pushed.

**12.6, line 323. Kind 5 (Inguma).**
> Three. Over its back she could see the lamp on the platform and the handle where she had left it.

The paragraph before is about the phone ("The phone rang a second time"), and a phone has a back. Inguma was last named two paragraphs up.
Corrected: Three. Over Inguma's back she could see the lamp on the platform and the handle where she had left it.

## Checked and left

- Pulse readings are in figures (34, 44, 64). So are they in chapters 1 to 6. It is a rule of the book, not a slip.
- Times read off a screen keep the screen's form (02:51, 23:55, 17:50). Times in the telling and in speech are 2:51 and "at 5". The split is held in all six chapters.
- Coralie's text messages use figures for a room and an age ("back in 12", "a woman of 94") where the telling has "room twelve" and "eighty-one". Read as how a message is typed.
- Not one of the seven kinds, so not counted: chapter 9, line 249, "At each place somebody sat with their head down over a card", and two lines on, "One chair was empty."
