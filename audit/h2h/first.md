# Head to head: first

Read X first, then Y.

## 1. Which one I go on reading

**X, slightly.**

## 2. By criterion

- **Hook: X.** Three reasons to turn the page in 1,250 words: "Ten more minutes, she thought. Then it would break", the thing that "sat down on her chest and turned its back to her", and "She counted the days. Eighteen." Y has one, and the narrator hands it to me: "tonight is the end of all of this." Nothing has happened yet.
- **Who I care about: level.** Y gets there faster: three people in a kitchen, "it's just more fun to drink and do nothing." X gets there more quietly but it lands: she wants the duck and writes *Either*.
- **Voice: X.** Dry and its own: "where she had once sat for an afternoon and been paid for it", "gave her a sandwich and went back to their university." Y is warm and easy but falls back on stock: "dark, Spanish eyes", "It still slays me", "what makes tragedy so tragic."
- **Clarity: Y.** I read nothing twice. In X I went back three times: who and when the opening "she" is; "whose stone it was", when her own name is cut on it; and the last two paragraphs of bank, gate, fields, tide, association and mayor.
- **Promise: X.** A date, a flood, a choice at a gate, and something that came out of a steel room. Y promises a man will lose a family he gave up a career for; a good promise, but stated twice in the same words rather than shown.

## 3. The writing alone

**X better.** Its sentences are concrete and each carries a fact: "felt the clay move under her palm", "like a full sack dropped off a trailer." Y's dialogue moves well, but the narration explains ("A multidisciplinary award given for achievements in...") and announces feelings ("He means everything to me"). X only drops to Y's level in its final section, which is a site plan.

## 4. What each should learn from the other

- **X from Y:** let two people talk for a full page. Y's kitchen is one room, one line of feeling, no effort to follow; X stacks a flood, a creature, an experiment and a stone, then ends on drainage instead of on a line.
- **Y from X:** put the threat on the page instead of promising it. One thing going wrong in the first 1,250 words would do more than saying "the end of everything" twice.
