# Head to head: second

Read Y first, then X.

## 1. Which one I go on reading

**X, slightly.** Y is the easier read and the better company. X gives me four concrete questions I want answered, and Y gives me a narrator telling me to be worried.

## 2. By criterion

- **Hook: X.** "12 DECEMBER", then "She counted the days. Eighteen." Plus a phone that "rang once". Y's hook is announced, not shown: "tonight is the end of all of this", said twice.
- **Who I care about: Y.** Daniela, on "It would be stupid not to" and "belligerently kind". Maylis writing "Either" on the pad is good, but it is the same beat as the wrist a page earlier, and she never gets to talk.
- **Voice: Y.** It speaks to me and it smiles: "No, it's just more fun to drink and do nothing." X reports. Its best joke is dry and buried: "gave her a sandwich and went back to their university."
- **Clarity: Y.** Nothing read twice. In X I reread "find out whose stone it was" (her name is on it), the van turned round on top of the bank, the leaves over rivets, and the paragraph on gate, fields and tide.
- **Promise: X.** A countdown, a flood, a choice at a gate, and "whether, somewhere, you also see the other one". Y promises a lost family and a road not taken, but nothing strange has happened yet.

## 3. The writing alone

**X better, narrowly.** X's sentences are exact and seen: "a sheet as wide as a door", "like a full sack dropped off a trailer". Y leans on stock phrases: "sucker punch", "dark, Spanish eyes", "It still slays me", "what makes tragedy so tragic", and a paragraph on fatherhood that could belong to any father. But Y flows and X does not. X is one short declarative after another, and it explains what I had already understood: "She was scared.", "She knew what a name and a date on a stone meant."

## 4. What each should learn from the other

- **X from Y:** give the two women a page of talk and let them be funny together, vary the sentence length, and end the section on a pull instead of a job description.
- **Y from X:** cut the narrator's warnings and put one strange, concrete thing on the page in their place.
