# Head to head: third

Read Y first, then X.

## 1. Which one I go on reading

**X, slightly.** Y is the easier company, but X has given me three things I need answered and Y has given me one, which the narrator had to tell me twice.

## 2. By criterion

- **Hook: X.** "Ten more minutes, she thought. Then it would break", then "It rang once." Y's hook is an announcement: "the end of everything I know, everything I love", said at the start and again at the end, with nothing yet happening.
- **Who I care about: Y, narrowly.** Three people talking to each other: "No, it's just more fun to drink and do nothing." Maylis I like for what she does not ask (the wrist, then *Either* on the shopping pad), but she is alone on the page for most of it.
- **Voice: Y.** "It would be stupid not to." It talks to me. X is deadpan and I enjoy it where it bites ("gave her a sandwich and went back to their university"), but it keeps one pace throughout.
- **Clarity: Y.** I read nothing twice. In X I reread "whose stone it was" (her own name is on it, so whose?) and the paragraph laying out the bank, gate, track and tide.
- **Promise: X.** A date on a stone, eighteen days, a thing on the wardrobe, a lamp she may have seen twice, a gate over a track where someone lives. Y promises loss and a road not taken, but not yet what kind of book.

## 3. The writing alone

**X better, narrowly.** X's images are exact and its own: "a sheet as wide as a door", "like a full sack dropped off a trailer", "She lifted sacks of cement for a living, but never off her own chest." Y leans on stock: "what makes tragedy so tragic", "dark, Spanish eyes", "liberate the cork", "my gaze alighting". But Y's sentences vary and flow; X's are nearly all the same length and shape, and by the third section that flatness shows.

## 4. What each should learn from the other

- **X from Y:** let two people talk for a page, and vary the sentence; the reader needs warmth and air between the strange things.
- **Y from X:** show the threat instead of declaring it; one concrete thing going wrong beats two paragraphs saying everything will.
