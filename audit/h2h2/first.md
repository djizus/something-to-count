# Two openings: first

Read Y first, then X.

## 1. Which one I go on reading

**Y, clearly.** Things happen to someone in Y. In X the narrator tells me twice that something is going to happen.

## 2. By criterion

- **Hook: Y.** "Under hers was a date. That she could read: 12 DECEMBER." then "She counted the days. Eighteen." A clock I can count. X's hook is an announcement: "tonight is the end of all of this."
- **Who I care about: Y, slightly.** "so she wrote *Either* across the bottom" told me who Maylis is without a word of explanation. X's family is warm, but the feeling is stated: "I love my son. He means everything to me."
- **Voice: level.** X is the easier company, a man talking to me in his kitchen. Y is drier and funnier: "The tenth, we ring the priest." / "Don't. He'd only ring me to dig the hole."
- **Clarity: X.** I read nothing twice. In Y I went back over the opening: which side the fields are on, what the handle does, why she points the van down the track. I also reread "whether, somewhere, you also see the other one."
- **Promise: Y.** A grave-digger with her own name on a stone, eighteen days, a river about to come through. X promises a happy man will lose his family, and so far nothing more particular than that.

## 3. The writing alone

**Y better.** Its sentences are concrete and none is wasted: "She lifted sacks of cement for a living, but never off her own chest." X leans on stock phrases ("a sucker punch that comes at you out of nowhere", "dark, Spanish eyes", "It still slays me") and explains its own effect ("maybe that's what makes tragedy so tragic"). X does read more smoothly. Y's run of short sentences starting with "She" gets stiff in the opening section.

## 4. What each should learn from the other

- **Y from X:** stay in one room long enough for me to settle. X holds one kitchen and three people for the whole stretch. Y gives me a river bank, a bedroom, a barn in 2014, a stone and a square, with four mysteries open at once.
- **X from Y:** put the threat on the page instead of promising it. One odd thing in that kitchen would do more than two paragraphs of warning.
