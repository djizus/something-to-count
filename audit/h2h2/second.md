# Two openings: second

## 1. Which one I go on reading

**Y, slightly.** Y gives me three things to chase and a joke that landed. X gives me a family I like and a promise it only states. Y loses the margin it should have had by making me work in its first page.

## 2. By criterion

- **Hook: Y.** "Under hers was a date. That she could read: 12 DECEMBER." A deadline I can count, against X's "tonight is the end of all of this", which is the writer telling me to be worried, twice.
- **Who I care about: level.** X: "I stare into the cabinet and wait for the ache in my throat to go away." Y: "so she wrote *Either* across the bottom". Both show love by what somebody holds back.
- **Voice: Y.** "The tenth, we ring the priest." / "Don't. He'd only ring me to dig the hole." Dry and its own. X is warm but anyone's: "one of those smiles that seem architecturally impossible".
- **Clarity: X.** I read nothing twice. In Y I re-read the floodgate page to place the bank, the river, the fields and the track, and still don't know why she turned the van. I also stopped on "The square was two trailers of leaves a week."
- **Promise: Y.** A gravedigger with 18 days to explain her own stone, a flood coming, a steel room with two lamps. X promises that a happy man loses everything, but not how, or what kind of book it is.

## 3. The writing alone

**Y better, narrowly.** Y's sentences are concrete and carry weight: "She lifted sacks of cement for a living, but never off her own chest." X reads faster and never snags, but leans on stock: "a sucker punch that comes at you out of nowhere", "what makes tragedy so tragic", "It still slays me". X wins on ease, Y on every sentence earning its place.

## 4. What each should learn from the other

- **X from Y:** show the threat instead of announcing it; one strange concrete thing in the kitchen would do more than both "end of everything" lines.
- **Y from X:** let me in without effort; one clear scene with people talking before the geography and the three mysteries, and say plainly what the woman at the floodgate is choosing between.
