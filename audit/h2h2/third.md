# Two openings: third

Read X first, then Y.

## 1. Which one I go on reading

**Y, clearly.** Things happen in Y. X tells me twice that something will.

## 2. By criterion

- **Hook: Y.** "She counted the days. Eighteen." A date on a stone, a bank with ten minutes left, a phone that "rang once". X's hook is the narrator's word for it: "tonight is the end of all of this", said at the top and again as the last line, with nothing yet on the page to back it.
- **Who I care about: Y, slightly.** Maylis wants Coralie's fingers kept on her wrist and doesn't ask; she wants the duck and writes *Either*. X's family is warm, but the love is declared: "I love my son. He means everything to me."
- **Voice: level.** X is easier, a man talking to me in his kitchen. Y is drier and funnier: "The tenth, we ring the priest." / "Don't. He'd only ring me to dig the hole."
- **Clarity: X.** I read nothing twice in X. In Y I reread the floodgate paragraph to place river, bank and fields and to work out why she turns the van, and the lamps line ("whether, somewhere, you also see the other one").
- **Promise: Y.** A countdown, a flood, a choice between forty houses and whoever is on that track, and a thing made of leaves and rivets. X promises a happy man will lose his family and regret the road not taken, which I have read before.

## 3. The writing alone

**Y better.** Its sentences are plain and concrete and carry a second job: "She lifted sacks of cement for a living, but never off her own chest." "The square was two trailers of leaves a week." X runs smoothly but leans on stock: "everything I know, everything I love", "a sucker punch that comes at you out of nowhere", "dark, Spanish eyes", "It still slays me", and a paragraph explaining why tragedy is tragic.

## 4. What each should learn from the other

- **X from Y:** show one piece of the threat instead of announcing it twice; let a gesture carry the feeling, as the wrist and the shopping pad do.
- **Y from X:** stay in one place long enough to settle. X spends all its pages in one room on one feeling and I never lose my footing. Y opens four threads (the bank, the thing, the lamps, the stone) and then restarts on Tuesday's rain, where the pace drops.
