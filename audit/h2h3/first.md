# Two openings: first

Read X, then Y.

## 1. Which one I go on reading

**Y, slightly.** X is the faster read and I finished it without effort, but its scene is over when the pages end. Y left me with a date, a person and a village I wanted more of.

## 2. By criterion

- **Hook: X.** "I have FMS" and "My son has been erased" give one clear question in one scene, and it ends on a fall. Y has a better question ("12 DECEMBER … Eighteen") but spreads it over a flood, a thing on the wardrobe and a steel room, then ends on wet ink.
- **Who I care about: Y.** Maylis wants the duck and writes *Either*; she wants the fingers left on her wrist and "didn't ask". Barry is a cop with a dead daughter, and the woman I cared about in X has just jumped.
- **Voice: Y.** "Then you're bad at it." "He'd only ring me to dig the hole." "Is the man who works for the village around?" X has no humour and no voice of its own beyond pace.
- **Clarity: X.** I read nothing twice. In Y I went back over "before she turned anything" (the handle, not the van), over who Jeannot Castagnet was and whose eyes read *No. 41*, and over the Monday floodgate paragraph, which arrives sideways in the middle of Tuesday.
- **Promise: Y, narrowly.** X promises a quick mystery about memories of lives that never happened. Y promises a countdown inside a comic village with a river about to break, which is the rarer book, if it keeps its threads in hand.

## 3. The writing alone

**Y better.** Its sentences are plain and exact: "felt the clay move under her palm", "like a full sack dropped off a trailer". X leans on stock phrasing ("mystical in their luminous shrouds of fog", "Tears carve trails through her eyeliner", "It's just a chapter") and on speeches that brief the reader rather than sound like a woman on a ledge.

## 4. What each should learn from the other

- **X from Y:** show a person by what they hold back, as with the duck and the wrist, instead of having them explain their grief in paragraphs.
- **Y from X:** one scene, one question, and stop on an act. Y's last scene, the leaves and the postman, is funny but lets the eighteen days go slack, and the opening on the bank needs X's ease of geography.
