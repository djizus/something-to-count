# Two openings — second

Read Y first, then X.

## 1. Which one I go on reading

**Y, slightly.** X has the harder punch, but it ends by killing the only person I had started to care about. Y leaves me with two women I want more of and a date 18 days away.

## 2. By criterion

- **Hook: X.** "I have FMS", then "Barry resists the urge to run": a memory illness you can catch, and a jump on the last line. Y has three hooks (ten minutes until the bank breaks, the thing on the wardrobe, her name on a stone dated 12 December) and they pull against each other. The last page, with the rake and the registered letter, lets the pressure drop.
- **Who I care about: Y.** "What she wanted was for Coralie to stay awake and keep her fingers on that wrist." Also *Either* written on the shopping pad. Barry is a kind policeman with cold feet and a lost daughter, no more yet.
- **Voice: Y.** "Then you're bad at it." "He'd only ring me to dig the hole." "'That's me,' Maylis said." X has no voice of its own; it reads like any competent thriller.
- **Clarity: X.** I read nothing twice. In Y I reread the paragraph of leaves over riveted steel, the line "She needed to see the end of that track before she turned anything", and "He numbered his letters" (which he?). Y also runs five time frames in 1670 words; X runs one scene.
- **Promise: level.** X promises a fast mystery: who is erasing lives, and can it be caught. Y promises a village comedy with a countdown and a flood. X's promise is clearer; Y's is the one I have not read before.

## 3. The writing alone

**Y better.** Its sentences are plain and exact: "It hit the floor like a full sack dropped off a trailer." "Larrère lowered his window a few centimetres, which was as far as it went for anybody." X leans on stock phrases: "luminous shrouds of fog", "Tears carve trails through her eyeliner", "Life has broken my heart too", "she visibly braces herself against the emotion". Ann's speeches explain the premise more than they sound like a woman on a ledge.

## 4. What each should learn from the other

- **Y from X:** stay in one scene with one question, and stop on an act that cannot be undone, not on ink running.
- **X from Y:** show a person through one small choice instead of having her explain her own grief in paragraphs.
