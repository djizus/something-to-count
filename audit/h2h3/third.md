# Two openings: third reader

Read X first, then Y.

## 1. Which one I go on reading

**Y, slightly.** X grabbed me faster, but when I put both down it was Maylis and Coralie I wanted more of. X gave me a situation and Y gave me people.

## 2. By criterion

- **Hook: X.** "And with a casual grace, she eases herself off the edge." One scene, one question, and it ends on a blow. Y has a good clock ("She counted the days. Eighteen.") but stops on ink running in the rain.
- **Who I care about: Y.** "What she wanted was for Coralie to stay awake and keep her fingers on that wrist." And *Either* on the shopping pad. Barry is a cold-footed cop with a dead daughter, and Ann is a case history who speaks in paragraphs.
- **Voice: Y.** "Then you're bad at it. I've got people of ninety who can lie on their backs all night without waking anybody." It is dry and I smiled three times. X has no voice of its own, only pace.
- **Clarity: X.** I read nothing twice. In Y I went back over three things: why she turns the van to see the end of the track, "She put it with the rest of her work", and the registered letter (who Castagnet is, and that the mayor is hiding in plain sight).
- **Promise: Y, narrowly.** X promises a fast thriller about memories of a life that never happened, and I believe it. Y promises that plus a village I would enjoy living in for 300 pages. The risk is that it opens six threads in six pages: flood, thing on the wardrobe, steel room, stone, rib, letter.

## 3. The writing alone

**Y better.** Its sentences are plain and exact: "It hit the floor like a full sack dropped off a trailer." X leans on stock phrases ("luminous shrouds of fog", "Tears carve trails through her eyeliner", "This low point isn't the book of your life. It's just a chapter") and on dialogue that explains the premise to the reader, not to Barry.

## 4. What each should learn from the other

- **X from Y:** show the person through what she does and jokes about, not through a speech on a ledge.
- **Y from X:** stay in one scene long enough to land a hit, and end the sample on it. The last page of village business spends the tension that the stone bought.
