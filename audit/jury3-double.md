# Literary read: the double

## 1. Where it belongs

This is a village tragicomedy about two versions of a woman who would each like the other's life, until borrowing it exposes what neither will admit about her own. Its nearest company is Saramago's *The Double*, Dostoevsky's *The Double* and Atkinson's *Life After Life*: identity theft, social displacement and the temptation to repair a disastrous choice by living it again.

## 2. Against those books

**Saramago, *The Double*.** Tertuliano discovers his likeness in a film; the investigation becomes a contest over identity, sex and possession, with fatal consequences for women caught in it. This novel matches that conversion of curiosity into appropriation. Lesca's motion, "take the other one's bed, and Coralie Mounet in it" (3), makes the desire brutally legible. It has less of Saramago's argumentative range and the disturbance produced by his recursive ending. Its distinctive advance is to let the woman being appropriated judge the transaction: "I don't get into a bed over somebody who can't speak" (8). Coralie has professional knowledge, desire and the authority to refuse. This is a substantial variation, not a derivative plot.

**Dostoevsky, *The Double*.** Golyadkin's socially adept double takes the place his awkward original cannot occupy, exploiting the rituals of the office. Lesca similarly knows how to command a room that treats Maylis as equipment. The reciprocal visit is equally sharp: "She liked it. She slowed down to get more of it" (6). Maylis wants the deference she also discovers is fear. The novel matches Dostoevsky's understanding of status as bodily pleasure, but cannot match the fever of his repetitions or the uncertainty about what Golyadkin perceives. Its own achievement is shared bodily control: a subordinate can seize a wrist or stop a van while losing the mouth. The office comedy has familiar ancestry; the anatomy of resistance is its own.

**Poe, "William Wilson".** Wilson's namesake repeatedly intervenes in his wrongdoing; killing the double culminates in the recognition that he has destroyed himself. Here the double also forces a withheld truth into speech: "You rang once" (13). The confrontation has comparable accusatory force, especially because the accusing voice is Lesca's own. It is less disturbing at the level of narration: the reader receives reliable access to both women, rather than a compromised man's account. It does something Poe's moral double cannot: Maylis saves the man Lesca killed and still fails somebody else. "She wanted it not to be her" (14) prevents the original from becoming an innocent conscience. There is no derivative borrowing beyond the tradition's shared situation.

**Atkinson, *Life After Life*.** Ursula's repeated lives test whether foreknowledge can avert deaths, from domestic accidents to wartime catastrophe. The flood replay here has comparable urgency: "Then I want both" (9). Practical knowledge is insufficient to repair a life. The narrower design cannot offer Atkinson's accumulation of different possibilities or her historical breadth; one repeated crisis carries almost all the burden. Its distinct contribution is simultaneous disagreement between the lives. Lesca cannot simply revise her past, because Maylis owns the body and has claims on its future. The resemblance is conceptual, not derivative.

## 3. What is its own

The municipal worker's body becomes disputed civic territory. Her right hand can refuse access to her partner and later hold a handbrake against the rest of herself. Consent and political obedience share an actual muscle.

The university's steel chamber survives inside a Basque night visitor: tobacco, rivets and plane leaves belong to one creature. Counting links a folk prohibition to flood measurements, votes, pulse charts and bingo, then becomes a dreadful task when Coralie counts forty-one survivors.

The other life is alluring in detail and ugly in consequence. Maylis wants her name in granite; Lesca's advancement has cost Kévin his freedom and Arlette her ponies. I have not encountered this precise combination of double story, municipal labour and the ethics of nursing.

## 5. Five faults, worst first

1. **The vocabulary of choice becomes too complete an explanation of Maylis.** "Either" prevents her choosing a counting task even when keeping Lesca out matters desperately (9). Dinner, authority, supernatural defence and rescue repeatedly diagnose the same inhibition. Keep the dinner refrain and its final return, but let her fail the nightly defence through a different, particular fear. Give the habit less power to explain everything.

2. **The climax's timing is conspicuously arranged.** "Maylis had given the bank ten minutes, and it had held for sixteen" (14). The extra six minutes deliver Jeannot to safety while putting the fatal breach at the other world's exact death time. The coincidence is powerful, but the machinery shows. Establish the uncertainty of her estimate earlier, through a repair that outlasts or defeats her prediction, so the reprieve feels physically possible as well as symbolically appointed.

3. **The narration sometimes explains its strongest actions.** "But all of it came with Kévin's cap and one pillow" (6) summarizes consequences already vividly encountered. Similar explanatory landings give many scenes the same shape. Cut some of these closing interpretations and trust the cap held in Kévin's hands, the unsigned question and the pillow to carry their meanings.

4. **Some villagers remain useful repeating devices.** "How do I put that?" (8) makes Bordenave an excellent recorder of misunderstood speech, but his pad often performs the same service. Give him one consequential refusal to record or one private reason for recording; shorten a repeat to make room. His flooded cellar provides the beginning of a person beyond the joke.

5. **The supernatural closure is too clean.** "One lamp went out. Then the other" (17), followed by Inguma's disappearance, neatly confirms that the women have learned what the haunting required. Preserve their mutual refusal to sit, but leave a small unexplained residue. The human losses remain open; the uncanny could bear remaining open too.

## 6. Three things it must not lose

- Coralie's independent ambition. Bayonne precedes the disaster, and leaving fulfils a life she already wanted.
- The accusation against Maylis inside the rescue: she prefers a death she can call the river's doing to one her hand visibly causes.
- The second shovel. Jeannot helps without thanking, forgiving or agreeing that he needed saving. The final act gives them work together without settling the argument.

## 7. The verdict

Yes, it would stand on that shelf, particularly beside Saramago and Atkinson. I would put it forward for a prize judged by readers of those books, though the prose and repeated choice motif would keep me from claiming it as their equal. The decisive achievement is that correcting the double's fatal choice produces another failure, and the ending allows neither woman to purchase innocence through confession or service.
