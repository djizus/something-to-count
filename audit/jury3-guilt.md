# Guilt, choice and confession

## 1. Where it belongs

This is a comic village novel whose supernatural machinery subjects two versions of one woman to an inquiry into responsibility. It stands nearest to Camus's *The Fall*, Conrad's *Lord Jim* and Ishiguro's *The Remains of the Day*: saving others, serving an office and confessing a wrong all become ways of arranging what one can bear to know about oneself.

## 2. Against those books

**The Fall.** Clamence's recollection of the woman falling into the Seine undermines the benevolence on which he has built his standing; his subsequent confession recruits his listener into a universal guilt that preserves his superiority. Lesca's annual "No one came to tell him" achieves comparable force through a sentence that is literally true and deliberately evasive. Her timer protects her from questions as effectively as Clamence's eloquence. The novel does worse when it supplies the interpretation itself: "She wanted it not to be her." Camus lets confession remain an exercise of power; here Lesca's public confession has a more settled ethical direction. The shared body gives the evasion a distinct physical form: the woman seeking absolution can commandeer another woman's mouth and bed. This is kinship, not derivation from *The Fall*.

**Lord Jim.** Jim's jump from the *Patna* becomes an event he repeatedly tries to outlive; the rescue of his reputation in Patusan ends in another fatal failure of judgment. Lesca similarly tries to repair one death in a place where nobody has yet suffered it. "It's twelve years too late for anybody here" is as good a challenge to that project as this novel contains. It also earns Conrad's distinction between admirable intention and disastrous consequence: Maylis's rescue leaves a care-home resident dead. It has less of Conrad's uncertainty about who possesses the story. Marlow's witnesses and conjectures make Jim recede under examination; these alternating viewpoints generally settle what happened. Its own achievement is to give the people who pay for heroic self-repair a sustained practical presence in wheelchairs, vans and soaked beds. It does not borrow Conrad's plot.

**The Remains of the Day.** Stevens attends to a diplomatic gathering while his father dies upstairs; professional service provides the language in which he suppresses grief and desire. Here work performs the same evasion. Asked where she has been during thirteen terrifying hours of bodily absence, Maylis answers, "At work." The soup eaten standing beside council minutes is worthy of Ishiguro's shelf. But Stevens's circumlocutions disclose contradictions he cannot recognise; this narrator repeatedly recognises Maylis's contradictions for us, and "Either" becomes an overly dependable signal. The novel adds something Ishiguro does not attempt: Coralie can distinguish two claimants to the same face through the resistance of a wrist. Her care is also an ambition independent of loving Maylis. There is no substantial evidence of derivation.

**The Visit.** Dürrenmatt shows collective murder becoming respectable through credit purchases and public professions of justice. This village likewise processes moral violence through minutes, votes and ceremony. "The chair votes once, Madame le maire" makes a comic procedural correction out of two selves fighting for authority. The objects carry social judgment as effectively as the new shoes in *The Visit*. The collective pressure is less thoroughly developed: the petitioners accept a vote and largely disappear until the disaster requires their accusation. Dürrenmatt makes us watch the town's conversion. Here the division between drowning one man and flooding many homes produces incompatible injuries rather than a unanimous bargain. The resemblance is structural, not derivative.

## 3. What is its own

The combined precision of bodily possession and municipal employment is unusual. One hand signs letters while its displaced occupant lies on the office floor trying to summon the creature home. Inguma's counting can consume a working day because Maylis estimates supernatural labour at tractor speed. These inventions belong to this particular worker's imagination.

The living village's bingo hall becoming the hall of the dead preserves the duck prize, competitive neighbours and the coffee smell. Death continues village habits without acquiring grandeur. Most distinctive of all is the last collaboration: the rescued man helps dig the grave of the woman whose rescue failed. Gratitude cannot settle the account.

## 5. Five faults, worst first

1. **The supernatural causation narrows the moral inquiry.** "You're the water" becomes an established explanation, and the final cessation follows an explicit promise. The bodily and meteorological machinery can make culpability look externally certified. Keep the observable coincidences and the catastrophe, but remove some declarations that settle their meaning. Let the connection remain more troubling than a proved accusation.

2. **The narration explains its strongest actions.** "If she went on, whatever the river did behind her was the river's doing." The next sentence interprets the interpretation. Cut "She wanted it not to be her" and comparable glosses. The turn away from the gate, followed by the death, permits the reader to recognise the evasion.

3. **The comedy becomes predictable through repeated verbal badges.** "Three against three. It stays shut" repeats the oven imitation after its first excellent appearance. "Either", numbered complaints, timers and measurements increasingly announce their thematic jobs. Cut repetitions that add no new pressure, especially explanatory responses to jokes. Preserve the failed oven imitation after the flood, when repetition changes meaning.

4. **The villagers' passivity is too convenient at the decisive moment.** "A vote was a vote" disposes of the nine petitioners when Maylis needs help. A baby, wet doorsteps and an orange warning give them reasons to resist that settlement. Show one unsuccessful attempt to organise independent help, or give their withdrawal a concrete cost they fear. The catastrophe should expose choices beyond the central pair's.

5. **The person who dies is more fully a supernatural instructor than a bereaved family's loss.** "Madame Etcheto's daughter wants the words her mother said each night to be said over her." The daughter arrives as a reported funeral request. Give her one encounter with Maylis that cannot be converted into advice or absolution. The woman in twelve deserves an aftermath beyond the protagonists' moral education.

## 6. Three things it must not lose

- Coralie's refusal to make competent care synonymous with unlimited forgiveness. Her departure was an ambition before it became a consequence.
- The incompatible claims of the gate and the hut, including Maylis's culpable wish to escape authorship of harm.
- The final grave-digging, with Jeannot still obstinate and the letter expressly withholding thanks.

## 7. The verdict

It would stand on that shelf, with less psychological freedom and more visible construction than its strongest neighbours. I would put it forward for a prize judged by readers of those books, though I would not yet argue that it equals them. The deciding achievement is that both rescue and confession leave obligations behind. Nobody's improved account of herself restores the woman in room twelve.
