# The literary read: the double

## 1. Where it belongs

A short novel (about 42,000 words) of the double as the life not lived: one woman split by a single night, each half crossing into the other's body, told as village comedy until it stops being one. It stands nearest to Saramago's *The Double* (the self who comes for the other's bed), Stevenson's *Jekyll and Hyde* (two wills, one body, the tell-tale hand) and Atkinson's *Life After Life* (the night run again), with Conrad's "The Secret Sharer" behind all three.

## 2. Against those books

**Saramago, *The Double*.** The same engine: an identical self, down to the scar ("The same hand and the same line"), who decides to take the other's woman. Lesca minutes it: "*That the mayor remain seated, and take the other one's bed, and Coralie Mounet in it.*" Saramago does the vertigo better. His reader never knows who is the original or whether the world will hold; here nobody doubts for a page. What this novel does that Saramago does not: the woman is not fooled. Maria da Paz and Helena are deceived for a night. Coralie takes a pulse and refuses both women: "I don't get into a bed over somebody who can't speak. I've lifted too many of them." That scene corrects the tradition.

**Stevenson, *Jekyll and Hyde*.** Jekyll wakes and sees Hyde's hand on the bedclothes. This novel turns that one image into a law, "The owner keeps her right hand", and gets a stair post, a handbrake and a joke from it: "They had one nose between them, so now neither of them could breathe." It is as well worked as Stevenson and funnier. It is worse at dread: the double is not the worse self, only the guiltier and more decisive one, so nothing frightens. Its own move is that the host becomes the double's conscience, which reverses "William Wilson".

**Atkinson, *Life After Life*; Le Tellier, *The Anomaly*.** Ursula Todd practises a life until it comes right. This book's best idea is the refusal of that. With the date, the hour and the file in hand, the second night comes out worse: "Maylis had given the bank ten minutes, and it had held for sixteen." And the cause is the rescuer: "You're the water." Neither Atkinson nor Le Tellier makes the returning self the catastrophe. Both do scale better. Le Tellier shows a whole world reacting to duplicates; this has a café and a score pad, though it uses them well ("She's got a mayor in her. Put that at the top").

**Conrad, "The Secret Sharer".** The other self who has killed a man, hidden in the captain's own quarters and at last let go. This ending is Conrad's, by consent instead of by murder: "'Don't come again.' 'I won't.' It came out whole." Conrad's sentences are out of its reach.

**Derivative?** Of none of these. The skeleton of the first third, a box experiment and a self from another branch who wants your partner, is Blake Crouch's *Dark Matter*, and the divided body is the film *All of Me*. By the bingo night it has left both behind.

## 3. What is its own

- Inguma, a Basque night-weight that ferries the visitor home and can be delayed with a counting job: "You'll count the fingers of my right hand. Then you'll take her home."
- The dead at bingo, calling a name surname first, and a mayor who declines her seat in committee language: "You can put that in the minutes as apologies received."
- The double as weather.
- A village that can read the uncanny only as adultery: "A woman takes flowers to the wife because she's after the husband or because she's after the wife."
- Both arms of the lever problem lived by one person, neither acquitted, and the motive left unknowable: "That sounded no more true, and no less."
- The grammar of a shared body: "Lesca told the mouth to open. It was stiff, but it was hers to use."
- A book in which everyone counts (pulses, letters, turns, heads) and which ends on a count.

## 5. Five faults, worst first

1. **The uncanny is administered.** The rules arrive as an agenda, "The second item is how this works. There are three points, and I'll say them once", and by the end of chapter 4 "She believed the date." Dostoevsky and Saramago live on doubt; this book spends none. Keep the meeting, which is Lesca to the life, but cut every point the reader has already watched happen (the right hand has been shown twice), and give Maylis or Coralie one scene in which the ordinary explanation is believed.

2. **The hinge turns on the catchphrase.** At 5 on Friday, with the handle fitted and daylight left: "'Either,' Maylis said. 'Then it's shut,' Jeannot said." The forty-first death hangs here, and it reads as reflex. Kévin has just released her from the blackmail, so the reader cannot see what she is refusing. Make it a choice: one beat of what she weighs, with Larrère's car in view, before the word. Madame Darrigrand's accusation will then cut as it should.

3. **The flaw is explained before it is shown.** "What she wanted was for Coralie to stay awake and keep her fingers on that wrist. But Coralie had to be up before 6, so Maylis didn't ask." Chapter 1 uses the formula three times (the wrist, the duck, the car window). One is enough; the shopping pad does the rest.

4. **Everything rhymes.** Nineteen days of rain, nineteen turns, nineteen sandbags, Coralie at nineteen; twelve; 2:51; "Jean's for my gravestone." One sees the watchmaker. "'Nineteen,' Madame Etcheto said. 'That's not many.'" Let some numbers be only numbers, and cut the gravestone line.

5. **The box.** "They shut you in a box once, to see whether there were two of you. Would the other one know what she wants for dinner?" This is the theme handed over in dialogue, and the one piece of furniture from another shelf. The two cards in the last scene earn its place, so keep it at this size, but cut that line and never explain it further.

## 6. Three things it must not lose

1. **Coralie on the landing**, choosing neither woman, and leaving at the end for her own reasons.
2. **No acquittal.** Lesca never learns which she opened it for. Maylis saves a man who "was in no danger" and whose letter "doesn't thank you". Do not let anyone soften either.
3. **The folk machinery played straight and funny**: Inguma's counting, the corn, the bingo of the dead, and the committee voice carried into that hall.

## 7. The verdict

Yes, it would stand on that shelf, at the short end, beside "The Secret Sharer" and "The Jolly Corner", and nearer Le Tellier in temper than Dostoevsky. It has what the shelf asks for: a use of the double that the tradition has not had. The second chance makes things worse, and the woman both selves want sees through them.

I would put it forward. I would expect it on a longlist, and I would expect a jury that prizes the sentence to stop it there. A jury that prizes design and feeling would take it further, provided faults 1 and 2 are mended first.
