# The literary read: the speculative novel a prize jury reads

## 1. Where it belongs

A short novel, about 43,000 words, of the life not lived. In a river village on the Adour, a labourer who never chooses shares her body with the self who chose once, opened a floodgate, drowned a man and was made mayor for it. It is a thought experiment run in a comic parish, and it stands nearest to Ted Chiang's "Anxiety Is the Dizziness of Freedom", Kate Atkinson's *Life After Life* and Le Tellier's *The Anomaly*, with Ishiguro in its manners.

## 2. Against those books

**Chiang, "Anxiety Is the Dizziness of Freedom".** The device is his. Chiang's prism lights a red or a blue lamp and connects you to the self who saw the other one. Here: "One of these two lamps will come on... What we want to know is whether there is another you, somewhere, who sees the other one." On the device the novel is derivative, plainly, and of this story. It asks Chiang's question as honestly as he does: does one decision make a person? It is worse on rigour. Chiang states one rule and derives everything; this book adds a rule when the plot wants one. What Chiang does not do: his other selves trade information and the story ends in absolution. Here the other self is weather. "You're the water."

**Atkinson, *Life After Life*.** Ursula gets the night again until she gets it right, and practice makes perfect. This novel is the rebuttal. Lesca rang once and one man drowned. Maylis lets it ring 9 times, says "Both", and the count in the hall stops at "Forty-one." Atkinson has decades, a family and a music in the snow that this prose never tries for. But she never makes the second chance cost what the first did, and that is the harder thought.

**Le Tellier, *The Anomaly*.** His best pages are two Joannas and one husband. This novel plays that scene inside one body and wins it: "I don't get into a bed over somebody who can't speak. I've lifted too many of them." Le Tellier is cleverer, wider and colder, with eleven lives and a president. This has one kitchen and more feeling. His doubles do not cause the catastrophe; this one does.

**Ishiguro, *Never Let Me Go*.** The same people who do not ask. Kathy's passivity is that book's horror. Maylis's "Either" is a joke for eleven chapters and then kills someone, which is Ishiguro's lesson properly learned. What it does worse is voice. Kathy's evasions are a mind talking; here evasion is reported from outside and the mind is carried by a shopping pad.

In passing: the investigator who turns out to be the anomaly is the turn of Mandel's *Sea of Tranquility*. This novel reaches it at the midpoint and uses it as a moral lever, the better use. Coralie, taking the pulse of an absent body, is Niffenegger's Clare, allowed to leave.

## 3. What is its own

- **Inguma.** A sleep-paralysis spirit of plane leaves and riveted steel as the ferry between worlds, held off by giving it things to count, and written into a care plan: "Staff to wait. Staff not to join in."
- **The body as a committee.** "The owner keeps her right hand." One hand on the stair post against the rest of her; one nose between two women.
- **The dead playing bingo** with grains of corn, and the exit from it: "The mayor of Peyrebarthe sends her apologies."
- **Metaphysics in the register of the parish pump.** Motions, a kitchen timer, registered letter 42, a stone that needs a verb: "Castagnet, Opened. It reads as if he was opened."
- **The village misreading the tragedy as adultery**, in the same room, at the same moment.
- **An ending in which neither world is the better one.**

## 4. Scores

- **Originality: 7.** A borrowed box, an unborrowed village. "You'll count the fingers of my right hand. Then you'll take her home."
- **The central idea and how far it is taken: 8.** Taken to the end without flinching. "I waited for the river to decide, so that it wouldn't be me."
- **The prose: 7.** Exact, dry, one register. At its best: "out on the fields the dark ground began to shine." At its usual: "She liked it. She slowed down to get more of it."
- **The people: 8.** Coralie is the book. "There's a key, and a handle, and Larrère, and a van. And then there's me, as a distance."
- **The comedy: 8.** I smiled often. "Either is not a person. I cannot summon Either to the station."
- **What the ending does: 8.** It pays a debt and forgives nothing. "It's four pages, and it doesn't thank you."
- **Whether I will remember it in a year: 8.** The car window going "all the way down, into the door"; the thing on the wardrobe; 41.

## 5. Five faults a literary reader would hold against it

1. **A rulebook, read out.** "The second item is how this works. There are three points, and I'll say them once." Then a fourth, then the weakening body, then the rain. The keystone rests on two dry days: "The rain came when Lesca came." Chiang needs one rule; Ishiguro explains nothing. I would let each rule be found in action, as the right hand is at the gate, cut the recital to its jokes, and leave the rain unprovable. The book is stronger if Maylis can never be sure.

2. **The centre is thin and sometimes told.** Two women are told apart by a fist, a sash and a pad, and where the book goes inside it states its theme: "At the gate I thought I could do better than you. I drove past the handle to be the one who did." Also: "It was going to cost her Coralie." Cut every sentence that says what a scene has shown. The last meeting in the steel room needs only "Forty-one", the question about the house, and "I won't."

3. **The author's hand at chapter 3.** "The other Maylis Lesca lived in a Peyrebarthe that had its flood in 2014. Afterwards it made her mayor. Call her Lesca." Three sentences hand over what *Never Let Me Go* would have withheld for a hundred pages. I know why it is there. I would still open on the bed with one pillow and let the reader count.

4. **Too many levers on one choice.** A blank file, an undated resignation, "That's prison, for eels", a merger, an adultery, a call from the widow's kitchen. Each makes Larrère more guilty and the two women less. The idea wants a clean choice; at 5 on Friday "Either" should be hers alone. Keep the letter and the kitchen clock; drop a lever.

5. **One sentence length, and tags worked hard.** "I'll be brief", "Here", "That's my door", "a hand's width", the numbered points. Most pay off, the oven door above all. But by chapter 12 the pattern shows, and the prose never risks a long sentence, even in the hall of the dead. Ration each tag to set, turn and payment, and let the sentence lengthen twice.

## 6. Three things it must not lose

1. **Forty-one.** The second try costs what the first cost, and the book says so in a headcount.
2. **Coralie**: the pad, the pulse, the pillow, and her leaving.
3. **The flat voice at the worst moments.** "You can put that in the minutes as apologies received." "There was a hatbox on it. Nothing else."

## 7. The verdict

It would stand on that shelf, at the short, odd, European end of it, beside Le Tellier rather than Ishiguro, and it would not look out of place. It is not one of those books: the box is Chiang's and the prose has one gear. I would put it forward to a jury of human readers who know them, for a longlist with confidence and a shortlist with an argument, because it does the one thing none of them does. It gives a woman her night again and makes her pay the same price for it.
