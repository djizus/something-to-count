# The literary read: tradition 5, the speculative novel a prize jury reads

## 1. Where it belongs

A short many-worlds novel played as village farce: one woman split by a two-lamp experiment into a mayor who opened a floodgate and drowned a man, and a road-mender who has never chosen anything. It stands nearest to Ted Chiang ("Anxiety Is the Dizziness of Freedom", "The Merchant and the Alchemist's Gate"), Mandel's *Sea of Tranquility* and Le Tellier's *The Anomaly*, with Ishiguro as the standard its restraint invites.

## 2. Against those books

**Chiang.** The steel room is the prism from "Anxiety Is the Dizziness of Freedom": one lamp per branch, and "whether there is another you, somewhere, who sees the other one". That device is borrowed, and the woman from the university who says "that was the question" does not disguise it. Where it ends up is "The Merchant and the Alchemist's Gate": you may cross, you cannot undo, you can only come to say it. The novel says this as well as Chiang does, in "I rang once", and it withholds the forgiveness Chiang grants. What it does worse is rigour. Chiang states one rule and tests it. This book has the box, the demon, the counting, the finger test, the limbs and the weather, and it explains them in dialogue. What Chiang does not do is make the thought experiment cost a marriage: "I don't get into a bed over somebody who can't speak. I've lifted too many of them."

**Mandel, *Sea of Tranquility*.** The turn is the same: the investigator is the anomaly. Gaspery is the disturbance he was sent to explain, and Lesca is the flood she came to prevent: "You're the water." Mandel gives it as a late revelation. This novel does better, because it gives it at a bingo bar where the hall takes it for thirst, and then makes the heroine act on it with one hand. It does worse on air. Mandel's worlds have weather, light and distance. Lesca's Peyrebarthe is a stone, a timer and a landing.

**Le Tellier, *The Anomaly*.** Both books set a metaphysical event in front of officials and let procedure be the joke. Both ask which duplicate keeps the lover. Le Tellier has eleven cases and none is deep. This has one and goes to the bottom of it, and its people are better than his. Its civil service is as funny: "Then it should have gone last Monday." It lacks his nerve about what the event means, and it has none of his play with form. It is not derivative of him.

**Ishiguro, *Never Let Me Go* and *Klara and the Sun*.** "Either" is Kathy's not-asking turned into one word, and Inguma set to count grass is Klara bargaining with the Sun. Ishiguro lets the reader find what the narrator will not say. This narrator says it: "What she wanted was for Coralie to stay awake and keep her fingers on that wrist." *Klara* keeps the Sun uncertain to the last page. Here the tablecloths lift by chapter 8, and the doubt has been spent. What Ishiguro never attempts is this kind of joke: "Castagnet, Opened. It reads as if he was opened."

Atkinson's *Life After Life* shares the belief that foreknowledge will save someone. This novel is harder on it: "Your mayor's thirty centimetres wrong."

## 3. What is its own

- A sleep-paralysis demon as the door between worlds and also as its bailiff, put off with counting jobs out of a municipal ledger: "Each load is weighed, so the loads are in a book."
- The afterlife as an endless village bingo, and a death announced by a number: "'Twelve. One, two.' Somebody pulled a chair in to a table."
- Two selves in one body fought as slapstick with consequences: "They had one nose between them, so now neither of them could breathe."
- The trolley problem run twice on the same woman, with the rescued man refusing the premise: "I was in no danger, and I'll say so to anyone."
- A marriage kept on a shopping pad with boxes.
- A room where a false sentence will not come out of the mouth.

## 5. Five faults, worst first

1. **One idea, four machines.** The box, Inguma, the lucid-dream test and the hall of the dead each have rules, and the rules are handed over as instruction: "The thing on your chest is the way in... Lie still under it and you're in the dream, wide awake." Ishiguro explains nothing and Chiang explains one thing. I would cut every line in which a character states a rule. Maylis would find stillness because she is too tired to push. The university would be reduced to the envelope and the two cards.

2. **It glosses its own symbols.** "It had never been true, and it wasn't true now. She did want one of them, badly." Later: "That was Either, with the river in it." The pad had already said it. I would strike the commentary wherever an object is doing the work.

3. **The prose has no voice, and the two women are narrated identically.** "She was the one driving." "In this village she ran the care home in the Port." The exception shows what is missing: "*That the mayor remain seated.*" I would write Lesca's chapters in her idiom of items, motions and minutes, so the page tells me whose world I am in before the weather does.

4. **Every motif is rung off on schedule.** The window, the timer, the oven door, the verb, forty-two, five fingers and four steps each get their closing chime, and chapter 16 is three of them in a row: "'I'll be brief,' he said... He said nothing." A jury reads that as engineering. I would leave two unanswered. The council scene could go, so that Lesca ends on "Somebody has to stack the chairs" and the landing.

5. **The chorus is one tic each, and the villain takes the blame.** Josiane's syllogism is repeated word for word in both villages. Three pages after Lesca's confession she is given a co-defendant: "You stood in my kitchen that night and rang her from my phone." Larrère has one human line: "Nobody else in this village has ever opened one before I knocked." I would give him a second, and I would let Maïté's accusation cost Lesca something. As it stands it relieves her.

## 6. Three things it must not lose

- **Coralie**, with every refusal intact: the pulse chart, the two steps of four, the pillow in the car.
- **The hall of the dead**, with twelve called before we see room twelve.
- **The unthanked ending**: letter forty-two, "The arm is the excuse you've written for me. It isn't mine", and the second shovel. Nobody is told which woman was right.

## 7. The verdict

It would stand on the shelf at the *Anomaly* and *Sea of Tranquility* end, and it would hold its place there on people and on comedy, where it beats both. It would not stand beside *Never Let Me Go* or Chiang. Its prose has no voice of its own, and its fantastic apparatus is four devices where their books need one. I would put it forward for a longlist and argue for it in the room on the strength of Coralie and chapter 14. I would not expect it to win as it stands. A juror who has read Chiang will name the prism in the first meeting. The answer to that juror is Inguma, and Inguma currently shares the stage with three other mechanisms.
