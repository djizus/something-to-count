# The literary read: guilt, choice and confession

## 1. Where it belongs

A short moral novel in comic dress. One woman chose in the dark and has lived twelve years inside the official account of it; her double is given the same night and tries not to choose. It stands nearest to *Lord Jim* (the single act, the public version, the second chance that goes wrong), *The Fall* (the cry not answered, the confession) and *The Remains of the Day* (office language as armour, love lost across a corridor), with Dürrenmatt's village arithmetic behind all three.

## 2. Against those books

**Conrad, *Lord Jim*.** Jim jumps, and the inquiry wants facts that do not hold the truth. This novel has the same gap between record and act. The file says "No order had been given to raise it." The speech says "No one came to tell him." The act is "It rang once." It matches Conrad in pricing the second chance: Jim is undone again in Patusan, and the mayor who crosses for "a man who hasn't drowned yet" brings her river with her. It does worse at turning the act over. Marlow worries at the jump for half a book; this novel shows the night once, in chapter 10, and otherwise stays outside its people. What it does that Conrad does not: it runs both answers. Jim never meets the self who stayed on board.

**Camus, *The Fall*.** The one ring is Clamence's bridge. He hears the cry and walks on; she rings and "didn't wait for the second ring." Camus's confession is a strategy: Clamence accuses himself in order to judge everyone. This confession is plainer, and more honest about what follows it: "I opened the gate, and I drowned him", then a medal asked back "until things are clearer", then "Somebody has to stack the chairs." It has little of Camus's intelligence about the penitent's vanity, though it touches it: the mayor crosses "to stay", for a bed with two pillows, and calls that a rescue.

**Ishiguro, *The Remains of the Day*.** The nearest in feeling. Stevens has dignity; Lesca has the motion, the item and the three-minute timer. When she decides to take her double's life, she minutes it: "That the mayor remain seated, and take the other one's bed, and Coralie Mounet in it." The landing, four steps wide, is Stevens outside Miss Kenton's door. Ishiguro does it better because the armour is the voice: every sentence of Stevens's lies a little. Here a plain third person tells the truth throughout, so the self-deception has to live in props. What this novel adds is Coralie, who has known for twelve years and chosen not to ask: "Because you'd have told me." Miss Kenton never has that power.

**Dürrenmatt, *The Visit* and *The Pledge*.** Forty houses against one man is Güllen's sum, and the commemoration, with its prefect, camera and front row, is Güllen's ceremony. Dürrenmatt is harder: his town does it for money and cheers. From *The Pledge* comes the correct prediction the world declines to honour: Bordenave's cross against Friday the 4th, Jeannot's hand going up. This comedy is gentler, and its people are liked.

The crossing is also Briony's last draft in *Atonement*, the version in which the man lives. But this rewrite kills someone.

It is not derivative of any of them. Its debts are to the tradition, not to a book.

## 3. What is its own

- Both branches of the choice in one body, fought over a jaw, a wrist and a handbrake. The dilemma is farce before it is argument.
- The penitent as the cause: "You're the water." Going back to put it right is the second offence.
- The second woman's choice is to keep her hands clean, and the book says so: "If she went on, whatever the river did behind her was the river's doing. She wanted it not to be her."
- Guilt as a weight on the chest that can be bought off with something to count. Every count in the book, leaves, stones, corn, forty houses, is a way of not looking.
- An afterlife of bingo, declined in committee language: "You can put that in the minutes as apologies received."
- The rescued man who is not grateful: "It's four pages, and it doesn't thank you. I was in no danger."

## 4. Scores

- **Originality: 8.** "You're the water."
- **The central idea and how far it is taken: 8.** It follows the idea to where a softer book would stop, and makes the second choice no better than the first: "I know what you picked. I was the other one."
- **The prose: 7.** Exact and well timed, capable of "The loudspeaker on the bank road said 'drowned him' after the square had finished hearing it", but it has one register for everything.
- **The people: 7.** Coralie alone is an 8 or better: "I don't get into a bed over somebody who can't speak. I've lifted too many of them." The two Maylises are, by design, half a person each.
- **The comedy: 7.** "Either is not a person. I cannot summon Either to the station."
- **What the ending does: 8.** No absolution, and some company: "Then he stepped over the string in his rubber boots and began to dig."
- **Memory in a year: 8.** "Then the water rose up the door and covered it, and the red light went out."

## 5. Five faults, worst first

1. **In the middle the machinery outweighs the conscience.** "Lesca had the rest: the legs, the left hand and the mouth." Chapters 5 to 9 keep a ledger of who holds which limb and what Inguma has been given to count, and a reader of this tradition waits until chapter 10 for the act itself. I would trim the rule-keeping and add more moments like the trapdoor, where the shared body confesses for her: "The legs didn't step off."

2. **One prose for two minds.** Chapter 3 opens in the mayor's own idiom, "Pillows: two. At home she had one", then lets it go. By chapter 11 her pages read like the roadmender's. I would keep Lesca's inventory-and-agenda voice running through all her chapters, so that when she looks up from the typed sheet in the square, the style breaks with her.

3. **Both guilts are cushioned.** The mayor is handed a guiltier man, late: "You stood in my kitchen that night and rang her from my phone." The roadmender's dead is ninety-four and walked back to her room herself, and Coralie says "That part is mine." Together they leave neither woman as alone with it as Jim or Clamence. The book half knows, since Maylis refuses the excuse: "At 3 I never tried the handle." I would keep that refusal, and plant Larrère's knowledge earlier and smaller, so it does not arrive as relief on the evening of the confession.

4. **The pattern is fastened too tight.** 2:51, nineteen turns, the reflector, the suitcase, five fingers, four steps: everything returns and clicks. A watch "stopped at 03:11" in one village is answered by a bank that "broke, at 3:11" in the other, and "Either" is rung in most chapters. The tradition trusts a loose end, and coincidence here does moral work. I would drop the 3:11 match and two or three Eithers.

5. **The last chapter is a round of exits.** Larrère, Kévin, the bus, the café, Marie-Jo, Coralie, the steel room, the priest, Jeannot: nine closings in under 3,000 words. "'I said it was in that house,' she said. 'I only had the one name wrong.'" A good line, but Coralie's bag and the grave need the room. I would cut the bus to a sentence; Jeannot's "I saw the wardrobe" carries it.

## 6. Three things it must not lose

1. **The motive left unsettled.** "That sounded no more true, and no less." And Coralie's twelve years of knowing half and refusing the rest.
2. **Confession without absolution.** The sentence travelling back through the square "at walking pace", the medal handed across the tape, the chairs, and Maïté's verb: "*Left*."
3. **The refusal to make the second choice the right one.** "She wanted it not to be her", the forty-one, and a rescued man whose letter does not thank her.

## 7. The verdict

Yes, it would stand on that shelf, at the end where Dürrenmatt's short novels and McEwan sit. Not beside Conrad or Camus: its prose reports where theirs thinks. Its idea is as good as theirs, and it is funnier.

I would put it forward for a prize judged by readers of those books, expect a longlist, and argue for more on the strength of chapters 10 to 15. What decides it is whether a juror forgives the limb-by-limb middle long enough to reach the one ring, the square and the dip.
