Read once, 00 to 17, in order. Kinds are the five in the brief: 1 read twice, 2 went back to check, 3 lost the speaker, head, place or hand, 4 a word to look up, 5 could not tell how or why.

### Chapter 1

**1. Kind 1.** "'It's my water. One. A duck comes down onto standing water."

This is the first time Jeannot numbers his points. "One." comes after his first sentence, so I read it as one of something to do with the water.

Change: "'I'll tell you about my water. One. A duck comes down onto standing water."

### Chapter 2

**2. Kind 1.** "There was still the barn, where the smell came from."

I expected her to go to the barn. In the next line she is in bed counting her fingers, so I read back to see what I had missed.

Change: "That left the smell of the barn. The thing that carried it came at 2:51."

**3. Kind 2.** "A long way off, Coralie's alarm rang."

She lay down at 2:50 and seems to spend a minute in the chair. I went back to check the time, because Coralie's alarm is the 6 o'clock one. Chapter 3 tells me she slept in the chair. Chapter 2 doesn't.

Change: add "She slept there." before the sentence.

### Chapter 3

**4. Kind 3.** "Lesca lay on her back and listened to the rain on the roof."

For four sentences I took Lesca for Maylis under her surname, in her own room. "No rain had fallen on her own roof for two weeks" then looked wrong. "When she became mayor" settled it.

Change: "Lesca lay on her back and listened to the rain on a roof that wasn't hers."

### Chapter 4

**5. Kind 5, understood late.** "The first half had come out and the rest had stuck, the way the handle stuck at nineteen turns."

I could not tell why "Either" would not come out. I only had it in chapter 9, at "'This isn't your kitchen.'": nothing untrue can be said in the steel room, and "Either" was untrue because she wanted one of the two. The clue is two lines down ("Tonight she wanted one of the two, badly.") and I read past it.

Change, in that later line: "Tonight it wasn't true. She wanted one of the two, badly."

**6. Kind 1.** "I was going to say that nobody has looked after your bank for twelve years."

The sentence has just stuck twice. Here she says all of it. I read back to see what was different.

Change: "It was about your bank and who looks after it."

### Chapter 5

**7. Kind 4.** "The skin was gone in one straight line, from the bottom of the first finger to the heel of the hand."

"First finger" could be the thumb or the one next to it. "Heel of the hand" I guessed. Chapter 13 says "forefinger" for the same scar, which is clearer.

Change: "The skin was gone in one straight line, from the base of the forefinger to the heel of the hand."

**8. Kind 1.** "Maylis had lain still for the village's sake."

Read twice to get the point, which is what she had not lain still for.

Change: "Maylis had lain still for the village's sake, not for this."

### Chapter 6

**9. Kind 5, understood on the second read.** "She gave Inguma every metre of roadside she had cut in this village since 2008, to count before it came to take her home."

In chapter 2 the count is what keeps Inguma away from the bed. So I could not tell how she still reached the chair that night, and I went back to Madame Etcheto. The count is for the trip home. The words are there, but I missed them.

Change: "She let it come for her as usual. For afterwards she gave it every metre of roadside she had cut in this village since 2008, to count before it came to take her home."

**10. Kind 5, understood late.** "When she went to shut the folder, the left hand was slow."

I did not join this to the next line about the body left at home. I had it in chapter 9, when Lesca's left hand is slow in the same way and she says her own body is colder each time.

Change: after "She had to tell it twice." add "Her hold on this body was weaker than it had been at 10."

### Chapter 7

**11. Kind 1.** "When Maylis woke at 6, Lesca was still sitting in the chair."

The next sentence has Lesca getting her out of bed. For a moment that was Lesca in two places.

Change: "At 6 Maylis got up out of the chair. Lesca stayed sitting. It was Lesca who got her out of bed."

### Chapter 8

**12. Kind 1.** "I'd have you tomorrow."

I read it twice to decide whether "have" meant take, and whether "tomorrow" meant the day or "at once". Chapter 9 ("It's tomorrow") shows she meant the day.

Change: "I'd say yes to you tomorrow." The echo in chapter 9 changes with it.

### Chapter 9

**13. Kind 5, never settled.** "The tide had brought the river to her father's line."

In chapter 7 I understood, from the two dry days, that the rain comes over with Lesca. After Sunday the 6th the book stops showing whether she is in Maylis by day. Yet on Tuesday the river is back on Lesca's file to the day. I could not tell whether she was still crossing, or whether the water already in the river was enough.

Change: "It hadn't rained since Sunday, but the tide had brought the river to her father's line." Or the opposite, if it was still raining. I could not tell which was meant.

**14. Kind 5, never fully understood.** "One night. That's all I have to keep you out for."

Lesca has just said she is coming to stay. Nothing shown stops her trying again on Saturday. I assumed Friday counts because it is the night on the file.

Change: "Friday is the night your river comes through. That's the one night I have to keep you out for."

### Chapter 11

**15. Kind 2.** "Behind her the secretary had the visitors' book open and her pen ready."

It is 9 in the morning. I went back to chapter 6, where the secretary opens the town hall at 2 and Maylis is alone in it until then.

Change: "Behind her the secretary, in early for the man from Pau, had the visitors' book open and her pen ready."

### Chapter 12

**16. Kind 1.** "'She had a van at the end of the track,' Maylis said. 'It wasn't yours.'"

I read it twice. I could not see why it would have been Kévin's.

Change: "'She had a van at the end of the track,' Maylis said. 'Not yours. His.'"

Where she ends up, and why. At the time I took it that she had held the wheel one ring too long and was paying Madame Etcheto's price. That she can refuse the seat, and that "twelve" then fills it, I only had in chapter 15 ("Somebody pulled a chair in to a table.") and chapter 16 ("'It was you.'"). I never knew why Inguma then held her until nearly 6.

Change: "Inguma hadn't taken her home. She had held that wheel one ring too long."

### Chapter 14

**19. Kind 2.** "The voice had been her own, only lower than she used it."

I went back one page. Chapter 13 ends on the same five words and says Lesca "heard them come out in Maylis's voice".

Change: "The voice had been her own."

### Chapter 15

**20. Kind 1.** "In this village he had retired, and he hadn't been in the square since."

He is standing in the square, in uniform, in the sentence before.

Change: "In this village he had retired, and this ceremony was the only thing that brought him back to the square."

### Chapter 17

**21. Kind 5, understood late.** "As it went, the steel under its leaves knocked against the steel of the frame, like one part of the room touching another."

What Inguma is. I read the sentence twice. Then the rivets, the tobacco leaves and the barn smell from chapter 1 added up to the steel room itself. I am fairly sure, not certain.

## 2. Errors

Four. I found no typo, no American spelling, no clock time or date written in words, and Inguma is "it" everywhere.

**1. Chapter 3. Pronoun with an unclear owner.**

"In June 2014 the woman from the university had asked whether, somewhere, she also saw the other lamp."

"She" reads first as the woman from the university.

Corrected: "In June 2014 the woman from the university had asked whether, somewhere, another Maylis Lesca saw the other lamp."

**2. Chapter 5. Pronoun with an unclear owner.**

"By her count he had half a wheelbarrow there."

Lesca is the last woman named and she is the one walking.

Corrected: "By Maylis's count he had half a wheelbarrow there."

**3. Chapter 6. Pronoun with an unclear owner.**

"His mother was her deputy, and she handed her sash back across this table in a plastic bag."

Three feminine pronouns and two women. "Her sash" reads first as the mayor's.

Corrected: "His mother was the mayor's deputy, and she handed her own sash back across this table in a plastic bag."

**4. Chapter 16. Verb form.**

"'That the council asks the prefect to remove the mayor,' the deputy mayor read, with a finger under the line."

Every other motion in the book uses the bare form: "That the mayor remain seated", "that the stone be given a verb", "that on the 12th the prefect speak alone".

Corrected: "'That the council ask the prefect to remove the mayor,' the deputy mayor read, with a finger under the line."

Not counted here because they are listed in part 1: items 7, 15, 19 and 20 are also slips of wording or continuity.

Not counted, because they are word choice and not spelling: "baby carriage" (ch. 17) and "dish towel" (ch. 1, 5, 11) are American words in a British text. The British words are "pram" and "tea towel".

## 3. Who is against her

**Against Maylis, and how strongly I felt it (1 to 5)**

- Her own "Either": 5. On the platform at 5 on Friday (ch. 12) she loses the gate to nobody but herself.
- Lesca: 4. From "and Coralie Mounet in it" (ch. 3) to the handbrake (ch. 13) she wants the body, the bed and Coralie. She also brings the river.
- Larrère: 3. The undated resignation and Kévin's name on the blank sheet are a real hold (ch. 6, 9, 12). On the night he is a voice from a kitchen.
- Jeannot: 3. The man she is trying to save votes shut and locks his trapdoor.
- The river and the bank: 3.
- Inguma: 1. It carries people. It isn't against anyone.

**Wanted somebody against her, and nobody was**

Chapter 14. Lesca lets go at 2:53 and Larrère hangs up a minute later. From there to 3:11 she is up against mud and a clock.

**Does the book lack an antagonist?**

No. Lesca is one for ten chapters and Larrère holds the folder over her all the way, but both are gone before the bank breaks, so the last choice is Maylis against her own "Either".
