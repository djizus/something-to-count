## 2. Errors

**Count: 10.** No spelling error and no typo found. Inguma is "it" everywhere. Clock times and dates are in figures everywhere.

1. **Ch 2.** Two different "it"s in six words.
   "It means she's not afraid of it."
   → "The words mean she's not afraid of it."

2. **Ch 3.** "she" can be the woman from the university.
   "In June 2014 the woman from the university had asked whether, somewhere, she also saw the other lamp."
   → "In June 2014 the woman from the university had asked her whether, somewhere, she also saw the other lamp."

3. **Ch 5.** "her" can be Lesca or Maylis.
   "By her count he had half a wheelbarrow there."
   → "By Maylis's count he had half a wheelbarrow there."

4. **Ch 7.** "she" follows a sentence about Lesca.
   "Last night she had lain still."
   → "Last night Maylis had lain still."

5. **Ch 11.** Comma splice. It reads as a slip, not as her way of talking.
   "I don't like the man, he drives through my yard as if it was the road, but I voted for him, and I'll say so while I'm standing in here."
   → "I don't like the man. He drives through my yard as if it was the road. But I voted for him, and I'll say so while I'm standing in here."

6. **Ch 12.** "her" twice, for two women.
   "Maylis stood on the step with a bag of sand held against her good side and watched her do eleven chairs before Coralie saw her."
   → "Maylis stood on the step with a bag of sand held against her good side and watched Coralie do eleven chairs before she looked up."

7. **Ch 14.** Comma splice.
   "Look at that floor, there isn't a centimetre of water on it."
   → "Look at that floor. There isn't a centimetre of water on it."

8. **Ch 14.** "her neck" is Nadine's and "her own" is Coralie's.
   "She took the torch out of Nadine's fist, lifted the list from around her neck and hung it on her own."
   → "She took the torch out of Nadine's fist, lifted the list from around Nadine's neck and hung it around her own."

9. **Ch 16.** The other two motions in the book use the bare verb ("That the mayor remain seated", "that on the 12th the prefect speak alone"). This one does not, so it looks like a slip.
   "'That the council asks the prefect to remove the mayor,' the deputy mayor read, with a finger under the line."
   → "'That the council ask the prefect to remove the mayor,' the deputy mayor read, with a finger under the line."

10. **Ch 17.** American word in a British text, three times.
    "Two doors up, Madame Darrigrand stood on the pavement beside a baby carriage, with the baby inside her coat."
    → "Two doors up, Madame Darrigrand stood on the pavement beside a pram, with the baby inside her coat."
    "The carriage was full of mud up to the hood."
    → "The pram was full of mud up to the hood."
    "She wiped the handle of the baby carriage with her sleeve."
    → "She wiped the handle of the pram with her sleeve."

Not counted, for a decision: "truck" (ch 11 twice, ch 14) where British usage has "lorry"; "the dump" (ch 17) for "the tip"; "dish towel" (ch 1, 5, 11) for "tea towel". "Noon" and "midnight" stay in words throughout, which read as right.

## 3. Who is against her

**Who or what stands against Maylis**

- Her own "Either": 5. It shuts the gate at 5 on Friday, with Jeannot above ground beside her.
- Lesca: 4. She takes the legs, the mouth and nearly Coralie, and she brings the river. Strongest in chapters 5, 8 and 9. She lets go in one paragraph in the van.
- Larrère: 4 until Friday night, 2 after. The undated resignation and Kévin's name on the blank sheet are a real threat. On the night he is a voice on a phone.
- Jeannot: 3. His vote, his locked trapdoor, "Then it's shut."
- The river and the clock: 3.
- Inguma: 2. Frightening for two chapters, then a tool that does her job for her.
- The café: 1. Comic.

**Where I wanted somebody and nobody was**

Chapter 12, at 5 on the platform. Larrère sits a hundred metres back with his window up and lets Jeannot do his work for him. Then chapter 14 from 2:53: once Lesca is gone, all that is against Maylis is mud, a rib and minutes.

**Does the book lack an antagonist?**

No. It has two, Larrère and Lesca, but both have left the stage before the bank breaks, so the last hour is fought against time and her own habit.

Not asked for, since the answer is no: if the gap in chapter 12 matters, the cheapest fix is Larrère getting out of his car on the bank at 5.
