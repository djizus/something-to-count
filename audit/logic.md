# The "why doesn't she just" read

Seventeen places, worst first.

Kinds: **1** nobody would · **2** why doesn't she just · **3** a rule does not bind · **4** a verdict contradicts a fact · **5** a rule arrives on cue · **6** who wanted what.

---

**1. Ch 11, five o'clock on the platform. Kind 2. *Structural.***
> 'Either,' Maylis said.

Reader: At ten past five that morning she decided to turn that handle tonight herself. So Kévin's sheet goes to Bayonne at three exactly as it would at five. The only thing five o'clock changes is that Jeannot is standing beside her. Open it.
Fix: the price must differ between the two hours. Before "Either": *At three, with the river coming through the bank, nobody could hold that sheet against a raised gate. At five the bank was still standing, and he could.*

**2. Ch 13, 02:55. Kind 1. *Structural.***
> Jeannot's van had gone down it at dusk, and if his van could do it, hers could.

Reader: He told her not to bring that van ("You'd sit there till Easter"), she waded that dip to her shins, and she is two hundred metres from a winch with the handle on. Nobody forgets that with forty houses behind her.
Fix: move, don't add. Bring "Jeannot had warned her about the dip, and at dusk she had waded through it" up to replace this sentence, then: *On foot it was twenty-five minutes. The van was the only way to have both.* She bets knowing, which is her.

**3. Ch 11, Kévin at the gate. Kind 2. *Structural.***
> 'No,' Maylis said. 'Take the van down to the care home.

Reader: That morning she needed a second pair of hands. Here they are, offering to sit on the hatch and able to take the winch, and she sends them where, in his next line, "nobody's moving anybody".
Fix: cut his offer ("I'll sit on his hatch all night… I put him in the seat") and "She wanted to say yes"; his "He wouldn't for me either" then closes the hatch. Close the winch with: *She would not put the boy on that sheet at the handle the sheet was about.*

**4. Ch 16. Kind 4. *One line.***
> 'You never raised that gate. You left it, which comes to the same thing.'

and Maylis: "Turn the handle first, and everyone behind the bank lives."

Reader: Jeannot, with two arms and a shoulder, hit a wall at nineteen. A woman with one arm and a broken rib would have stopped there too. Larrère, the Port, Coralie and Maylis all convict her of not raising a gate the page showed would not rise, and the one witness says nothing.
Fix: one sentence in letter 42: *It says the handle stopped at nineteen under a man with two arms, and who had been written to about it.*

**5. Ch 13, the handbrake. Kind 6. *One line.***
> Her right hand was still locked on the handbrake, which she had pulled up to keep this van at her gate.

Reader: She fought for eight rings to keep the van at the gate, and the moment it is hers she drives it where Lesca was driving. Was she fighting for the gate, or to get Lesca out?
Fix: replace the clause: *which she had pulled up to stop another woman driving her. Nobody was driving her now.*

**6. Ch 11, the call to Hontaube. Kind 2. *One line.***
> So she rang the town hall at Hontaube, the next village

Reader: Nine people in the café signed for this gate on Tuesday. Lafitte lives under the bank and his pad proved her right. Sarthou is Jeannot's cousin, with water on his own step. She asks strangers for a form.
Fix: *She had tried the café first. The nine had watched Jeannot's hand go up, and a vote was a vote.*

**7. Ch 11, at the hatch. Kind 2. *One line.***
> 'I was told.'

Reader: Say by whom: the one who gave Lafitte Tuesday's sentence eight days early. It is her only proof, and she doesn't use it.
Fix: let Jeannot shut it first: *'And don't give me Lafitte's pad. Larrère has said that sentence at every high water since 2008.'*

**8. Ch 12, the hall of the dead. Kind 3. *One line.***
> 'she will not be taking this seat.'

Reader: The one rule with a death in it comes due. Her card is called, and she declines it like a meeting. Then twelve is called and somebody else sits. Was that a trade or a coincidence?
Fix: plant it in Madame Etcheto's speech in ch 3: *A place that has been laid is filled by morning, by her or by another.*

**9. Ch 13, the caller. Kind 1. *One line.***
> under the time it said Castagnet, house.

Reader: A man who parks with his lights off rings the keyholder on the husband's landline, then six minutes later on his own mobile.
Fix: make the 02:59 call "Castagnet, house" as well, and add: *No mobile had ever worked on the terrace side.*

**10. Ch 11, the van. Kind 3. *One line.***
> At nine minutes to three something heavy landed on the roof of the van.

Reader: "It comes to that bed" (ch 3, again ch 9). Lie on your side and it has nowhere to sit (ch 10). She is upright, awake, in a van. Kévin's "doesn't need you asleep" turns up five pages before it is needed.
Fix: use what ch 9 found: *It came at that minute for the woman who made the call, and tonight she was the one at the gate with the phone.*

**11. Ch 7, the register. Kind 2. *One line.***
> 'Decision of the fourth of May 2015, moved by the mayor.

Reader: She risks being found dead in her bed to learn who can order a gate. The answer is a rule Lesca wrote herself, after the villages split, so it has no force at home. And Lesca stands facing her every other night. Ask her.
Fix: date it before the split: *'Statutes of the association, 1962. The word "written" added on the fourth of May 2015, moved by the mayor.'* Then in ch 5 Lesca can say "the prefect" instead of "I'll find out whose", and the trip is for the wording.

**12. Ch 13 and 16, the list. Kind 4. *One line.***
> It was your mayor's list. I pinned it up, and we did what was pinned up.

Reader: Maylis told her "start at the bank end" by phone a quarter of an hour before the water. In ch 16 Coralie owns ten o'clock and not three.
Fix: in ch 16, after "That part is mine": *And at three you said the bank end, and I read the list.*

**13. Ch 10. Kind 5. *One line.***
> It can't come and fetch me home while I'm sitting on its back.

Reader: Who told her? It is stated by the one who needs it, a chapter before it works.
Fix: move the seed to the end of ch 8, when Inguma takes the chair: *It fetched from that chair. It had to get up to do it.*

**14. Ch 5. Kind 5. *One line.***
> When we're on our feet we can talk, and neither of us can lie.

Reader: She has been on her feet for one minute. How does she know?
Fix: cut the clause, and show it at "I want somebody to blame": *She had not meant to say it. In that room it came out.*

**15. Ch 16. Kind 3. *One line.***
> 'There is no other sheet,' he said.

Reader: The threat that bought five o'clock was already tripped three times (the letter to Pau, the hour away from the van, a gate up by a hand). It vanishes in five words, and Maylis doesn't blink.
Fix: *She had kept a gate shut for a sheet that one look at a gendarme could unwrite.*

**16. Ch 7, the tie. Kind 3. *One line.***
> In a tie the chair's vote decides. The motion fails.

Reader: In ch 2, "The chair has an interest, and doesn't vote." This motion is about her own speech, and Larrère, who moved it, lets her break the tie.
Fix: cut "and doesn't vote" in ch 2.

**17. Ch 8, the bar. Kind 6. *One line.***
> Lesca began the words.

Reader: Two women fight over one jaw before I know that saying the words again sends it off to count and keeps her in the body.
Fix: one sentence before it: *If she gave it another job it would go off and count, and she would keep the body.*
