# The sentence that says the obvious

Read of `manuscript/00.md` to `16.md` as they stand on 4 October 2026.

**60 sentences** of the five kinds, 3 comma-and joins, and 34 words (26 with a plainer option, 8 without). Chapter 13 has the most (9), then chapter 1 (8).

Each entry gives the line number in the file, the kind (1 to 5, as numbered in the brief), the sentence as it stands, and the fix. 36 are plain cases. The 24 marked *judgment call* are sentences a patient reader would let pass; they are listed because the read was meant to be impatient.

Left alone, as the brief asks: reasons that are jokes ("because soup had been ticked", "They had one nose between them", "That gave it somewhere to sit"), reasons that tell the reader something new, and everything said aloud. Also left alone: the sentences that restate the rules of the chair or the sum of ten minutes against twenty-five at the moments where the plot turns on them (11:311, 13:71 to 13:75).

## Two unrelated facts joined with a comma and "and"

About 300 of these joins remain in the book. Nearly all are in speech, or tie an action to the next action or to its result. Three join facts that have nothing to do with each other.

> She's ninety-four, and she says that every night to a thing that sits on her chest.

"She's ninety-four. She says that every night to a thing that sits on her chest."

### No plainer word; the first use has to carry it

- **landing** (03 to 11, 15, 16). The book turns on it. Its first use, 03 line 11, is bare: "on the far side of a landing". The explanation comes at 03 line 215. Bring it forward: "For eleven years Lesca had heard that woman get up in the room across the landing, at the top of the stairs."
- **gutter** (03, 04, 06, 15). First use, 03 line 133, is "cleared the gutter". "Cleared the leaves out of the gutter along the roof" would carry it.
- **sash** (02, 03, 14, 15). The first use, "the mayor's sash, blue, white and red", nearly carries it. "Across her chest" would finish the job.
- **rivets** (01, 09, 12). The same word in French; "a row of rivets" in grey steel is clear enough to leave.
- **plane trees** (01, 02, 06, 14). A species name. "The leaves of the plane trees" tells the reader it is a tree; leave.
- **eels** (02, 06, 11). 02 explains them; leave.
- **rake, raked** (01, 02, 03) and **wheelbarrow** (01, 06, 11, 13). Leaves and clay stand beside them each time; leave.
