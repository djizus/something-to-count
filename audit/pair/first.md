# Pair read: first

Read A, then B. Pages are 250 words.

## 1. Which one

**A, slightly.**

## 2. By criterion

- **Stakes: A.** "A place that has been laid is filled by morning, by her or by another." B's chapter 3 leaves that rule out, so when twelve is called in the hall of the dead, nothing ties it to Lesca refusing her seat.
- **Tension: A.** Chapter 13: "You told her the bank end. She hung this on me and went... Then the lights went out." Coralie is missing down a flooded corridor while Maylis has to keep pushing wheelchairs. In B, Coralie is safe in the doorway.
- **Progression: A.** B's chapter 8 packs the track, bingo night, the landing, the letter, the vote and the note into one chapter and summarises half. A plays each one out.
- **Comedy: A.** Bingo night: the coffee runs "off the end, onto Josiane's shoes", then "'Tooth,' Marie-Jo said into the microphone. 'Castagnet,' Josiane said." B summarises the scene, then calls back to a grass trimmer it never showed.
- **Characters: A.** "I waited for the river to decide, so that it wouldn't be me." B hands Maylis a kinder reason and tells me what she feels: "It hurt to hear."
- **The ending: A.** See 5.
- **Clarity: B.** B never made me reread. A did, twice: the chapter 3 paragraph that starts "On Thursday Lesca went home" (envelope, crossing, card and river gauge in one block), and chapter 12, where both women say they will go to the blind and then fight over the wheel.

## 3. The first fifty pages

B, sooner. In B it was page 19, the end of chapter 2: "That village had not been spared its flood. It hadn't had it yet." By then I had two villages, a date and a man to save.

A hooked me earlier, on page 17 ("'Come back across,' her mouth said. Maylis hadn't said it"), but I did not yet know what I was looking at. Chapter 3 then rewinds "four nights earlier", and the same flood line arrives on page 24. That was the point in A.

B's order (her, the mayor, her, the mayor) got me there five pages sooner and without a rewind.

## 4. The night of the flood

A's.

In A she drives past the handle because "she wanted the man and the gate, both, and she wanted to be the one who got them." At the dip she takes three steps back towards the lamp, then: "She wanted it not to be her." That is the woman who wrote *Either* on a shopping pad so that the duck would not be her doing. Then Jeannot raises the gate at twenty past three, so it "would have gone up at three", and she can only say "At three I never tried it."

B gives everyone a way out. She keeps the gate shut at five for Kévin, the winch sticks for Jeannot anyway, and room twelve is last because of the mayor's list. I believed each step and blamed nobody, so the choice weighed less.

## 5. The last two chapters

A ends better, though B's chapter 15 has the better single scenes.

A's order is right: the silt, the café ("Had the key and went for a walk, did you?"), Coralie leaving, and only then the two women in the steel room. "Is yours in the house?" "Across the landing. Four steps. Yours?" "She took the pillow." B opens chapter 16 with that meeting, and it costs less there.

A also has "Nobody emptied hers", and "Lesca, Maylis. Surname first."

B is warmer: its mayor gets the pillow back. But with one couple mended, the book reads as the right woman rewarded. A leaves both landings open, and I kept thinking about it.

## 6. What the loser does better

Passages from B that A should take back:

1. "'I'd fly. Wouldn't you? Straight up over the church. Then I'd do my mum's gutter.'" And "writing won't hold in a dream." A pays both off ("I flew"; reading 03:31 twice) without planting either.
2. "She had a winch that had been sold for scrap, and somebody was turning it... Lesca thought she had the answer now. Somewhere was Peyrebarthe."
3. "'One ring is an attempt,' Lesca said. 'It's also all there was of it. The form needs a third box.' 'It has two.'" She ticks *No*.
4. "'I've hidden behind that sum for twelve years, and I'm not having you behind it as well.'" And: "'What was the verb?' 'Drowned.'"
5. "'In seventy-one it was a sheep.' He leaned out over the water, which was brown. 'It'll have sunk.'"

## 7. Against the comparison

**Below**, for a reader who likes both.

The one thing: how much I had to hold in my head. Crouch gives me one rule and then runs. The Good Place gives me one twist at a time. A gives me the chair, who drives whom, the right hand, the fetching, the counting and the laid place, mostly inside one meeting in chapter 4, and I reread twice to keep up. The flood chapter is as tense as Crouch, and the trolley problem is sharper than the show's. I had to work to reach them, and a friend might not.
