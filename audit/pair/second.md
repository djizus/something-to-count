# Pair read: second

B read first, then A. Pages counted at 250 words.

## 1. Which one

**A, clearly.** B is better in chapters 2 to 4. A is better from chapter 5 to the end, and by more.

## 2. By criterion

- **Stakes: A.** A gives the flood a cause and the dead a price: "You got my rib … And I get your river", then "a place that is laid is filled by morning, by her or by another". In B the flood just arrives ("Then it's the winch").
- **Tension: A.** Coralie is down the dark wing on Maylis's word while Maylis has to keep pushing chairs: "Each time she came back in, she looked down the corridor before she looked for a chair."
- **Progression: A.** Maylis ends the fight in the van with the rule, and without her word: "Lesca waited for the other word, the one this mouth put after everything. It didn't come." In B Lesca simply lets go.
- **Comedy: A.** Maylis flat on the mayor's floor telling Inguma to hurry, while Madame Ducasse "set the next letter on the floor beside the right hand, and the hand signed it". B summarises bingo night; A plays it.
- **Characters: A.** Lesca meets the dead man alive: "He had gone grey. The man on the stone hadn't had the time." B tells that scene from Maylis's head and loses it.
- **The ending: A.** "'Had the key and went for a walk, did you?' Then he looked up." B has Marie-Jo report the same thing.
- **Clarity: B.** A's chapter 3 opens "Four nights earlier" and packs the envelope, the chair, the second card and the gauge into one paragraph. I read that page twice. In B I re-read only the page in chapter 5 where the flood's cause is argued and dropped.

## 3. The first fifty pages

A, sooner. In A it was the end of chapter 2, about page 17: "'Come back across,' her mouth said. Maylis hadn't said it." In B that line comes on page 28. B's earlier hook is page 19, "It hadn't had it yet", which sets the date but is a thought, not an event.

B's fifty pages are the more comfortable. A's are faster, and chapter 3 is rushed: the first crossing deserves B's chapter 4 length.

## 4. The night of the flood

A's.

In B Maylis keeps the gate shut at five because of Kévin's name on a sheet, and at the dip she reasons like a decent woman: "Coralie had a list, a minibus, Kévin's van and ten minutes' warning." Then the winch sticks for Jeannot anyway, so the night is half an accident.

In A it is the woman from page 3, who would not tick the duck because "it would be Maylis's doing": "If she turned that handle, a man drowned, and her hand had done it. If she went on, whatever the river did behind her was the river's. She wanted it not to be her." Then the gate goes up under Jeannot: "It would have gone up at three." I believed it because she fails in the exact way she has failed all book, and she says so afterwards: "I waited for the river to decide, so that it wouldn't be me."

One wobble in A: "Go to the blind first" changes nothing, since Maylis has just said she will drive down. In B those five words turn her.

## 5. The last two chapters

A.

Chapter 15: the living Madame Etcheto learns what her seat cost. "Then it wasn't Inguma that came for her. It was you." And: "Nobody emptied hers." B plays that meeting for a joke. A's council ends on "Lesca, Maylis. Surname first", the card of the dead said aloud.

Chapter 16: A shows the café's new saying happen, and lets Maylis refuse the excuse in letter 42 ("That one's yours. At three I never tried it").

B is kinder to Lesca, and partly better for it. The pillow carried back gives one mended couple against one broken. A's "Good night, Maylis" is right but thin.

## 6. What B does better

1. "Maylis had asked for Coralie, once, and got her, and after that she hadn't wanted to push her luck by asking for anything else." A has the flashback without the reason.
2. "I'd have taken both. One for the village, and one for the washing-up."
3. "'There's something in the slot. In seventy-one it was a sheep.' He leaned out over the water, which was brown. 'It'll have sunk.'"
4. "'The form needs a third box.' 'It has two.' Lesca did not leave boxes empty. She took the pen and ticked *No*".
5. "I chose you, Maylis, over somebody who asked."

## 7. Against the comparison

Below both.

The one thing: nothing pulls the floor out. By page 25 I know the date, the place, the man and the choice, and the flood night delivers that, with the victim changed. A Crouch novel and The Good Place each turn into a different story at least once. This one pays exactly what it promised. It pays well, and the village is funnier than anything in Crouch, but I never had to wonder what kind of book I was in.
