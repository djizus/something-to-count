# Pair 10 — first

Read B in full, then A. Eight chapters (0, 1, 2, 6, 9, 11, 13, 16) are word for word the same, so in A I read those once, as B. Of the other nine I read A's chapter 15 in full and the changed passages of the rest against B. A page is about 250 words.

## 1. Which one

**B, slightly.** The two differ by about 700 words. Most of those are better in B, and the one large difference, chapter 15, is clearly better.

## 2. By criterion

- **Stakes: level.** Same text. "The trapdoor was twenty-five minutes away on foot. The bank had ten."
- **Tension: level.** The ring count in the van and the van sinking at the dip are the same in both.
- **Progression: B.** Ch. 4: "The first item is who I am. Maylis Lesca, mayor of Peyrebarthe." The premise is handed over in the scene where I needed it.
- **Comedy: B.** Ch. 8: "They had one nose between them, so now neither of them could breathe." I smiled. A has only "Maylis's right hand went over the mouth, and the left came up to drag it off." Also Josiane in ch. 7: "I got none for a coffee, and he gets two."
- **Characters: B.** Ch. 15: "'I abstain,' Larrère said." He has waited eleven years for that item and cannot vote for it. A does not have the line.
- **The ending: B.** See 5.
- **Clarity: B.** Pages read twice: **B 5, A 7.**
  - Both, same text: ch. 3, the file ("Tuesday had been the 24th"); ch. 6, the tied vote with two hands up; ch. 7, who has won when Madame Etcheto finishes the words; ch. 8, "only the last night counts"; ch. 12, why Lesca lands in the hall of the dead.
  - A only: the first page of ch. 3 and the first page of ch. 14. Reading A second, these are the two I actually stopped on. A carries a helper line on two of the shared pages (see 6).

## 3. The first fifty pages

Same place in both: ch. 3, about page 23, "That village hadn't been spared its flood. It hadn't had it yet." B reaches it sooner in effect. A opens ch. 3 with "Lesca was lying on her back in bed" and "It was her own bedroom," and the next three sentences say it is not. I went back to the top. B names her in one line, so the pull from "'Come back across,' her mouth said" carries straight through.

## 4. The night of the flood

Level. Chapters 9, 11 and 13 are identical, and 12 differs by two sentences. I believed the choice in both: at the dip, "She wanted it not to be her." That is the woman who writes *Either* on the shopping pad, and it costs her room twelve. In the van, Lesca letting go reads cleaner in B. A adds "It had come for Lesca, and not for the woman it was sitting on," which I already knew.

One beat I only half believed, in both: at 5 on Friday, with Jeannot standing on the bank, she says "Either" and takes the handle off.

## 5. The last two chapters

B. Chapter 16 is the same. In 15, A reports the council in one paragraph: "On Tuesday the council gave Lesca three minutes to speak against her own removal, and she turned the timer back to nothing herself." The book has been setting that table since ch. 3 (the timer, the sash in a plastic bag, Marie-Jo talking to the wall), and A pays it off in a summary. B plays it: the sash folded in three with the keys on top, Marie-Jo saying it "to Lesca this time, not to the wall." In B "Lesca, Maylis. Surname first" echoes the card in the hall of the dead.

## 6. What the loser does better

- Ch. 5: "All day she had been waiting for Inguma to finish counting its lake and come to take Lesca away." B's "that cold to come back to the kitchen door" made me work out what she was waiting for.
- Ch. 7: "Inguma would come for Lesca tonight, and Lesca wasn't going to give it anything to count." It sets up why the clenched teeth matter. In B I went back for that at "Inguma, enauk—".
- Ch. 12: "On Wednesday night she had told Maylis whose chair that was." A reminder on a page I read twice.
- Ch. 14: "under a blanket with the hospital's name on it." B dropped the detail. Keep it beside B's "her own Coralie."

## 7. Against the comparison

B is **below** both, not by much.

Below Blake Crouch on drive. He gives one rule and runs. Here I counted eight (lie still, shared knowledge, stay sitting, the right hand, the counting, truth when standing, she brings the river, the hall), and four of my five second readings were rule pages. Above him on people: "Outgoing. Castagnet J., mobile. Not answered. 02:51."

Below The Good Place on how often I smiled, though the best are at its level: the oven door, "I cannot summon Either to the station," Jeannot's six points.

The one thing that decides it: the rule load.
