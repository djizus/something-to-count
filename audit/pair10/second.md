# Pair 10: second reader

Read A in full, then B. Nine chapters are the same in both (0, 1, 2, 6, 9, 11, 13, 16, and 10 but for one word). The difference is about 600 words. B was read second, so it had the easier ride.

## 1. Which one

**B, slightly.**

## 2. By criterion

- **Stakes: level.** Chapter 13 is the same text in both, down to "Forty-one."
- **Tension: A, by a hair.** At the bingo A tells me what the shut mouth is for: "Lesca wasn't going to give it anything to count." B cuts that, so the fight over the jaw has no stated prize until Lesca starts the words.
- **Progression: B.** Chapter 15 plays the removal vote as a scene. A reports it in one sentence.
- **Comedy: B.** "They had one nose between them, so now neither of them could breathe." Also "He sits in his car." / "In mine he sits at my table, and I time him." And Josiane: "I got none for a coffee, and he gets two." I smiled at all three. None is in A.
- **Characters: B.** Larrère waits eleven years for the item, then: "I abstain." Marie-Jo speaks "to Lesca this time, not to the wall."
- **The ending: B.** See 5.
- **Clarity: B.** Pages read twice in A: **3**. Chapter 3, page 1 ("It was her own bedroom", and then it is not, and I do not yet know who Lesca is). Chapter 14's opening (Coralie's fingers on a wrist, straight after the other Coralie counting heads: which one?). Chapter 8, the steel room ("only the last night counts"). In B: **2**. The same steel-room page, made a little worse by "This isn't your kitchen" where A has "I was sitting down". And the first page of the bingo, for the missing reason above. That second one is a guess, since I already knew the answer.

## 3. The first fifty pages

The same line in both, about page 21: "That village hadn't been spared its flood. It hadn't had it yet." The first hook is earlier and shared: "'Come back across,' her mouth said. Maylis hadn't said it."

B got me there sooner. Its chapter 3 starts "Lesca was the other Maylis Lesca, the one who had become mayor". In A I spent a page working out whose head I was in, and the hook arrived while I was still checking.

## 4. The night of the flood

Level. The choice is the same text in both. I believed it: "If she went on, whatever the river did behind her was the river's doing. She wanted it not to be her." That is Either turned into an act. Chapter 16 makes her own it: "I waited for the river to decide, so that it wouldn't be me."

Lesca letting go of the wheel differs by two sentences, and A's are the better. "It had come for Lesca, and not for the woman it was sitting on" tells me who is resisting and what it costs her. B makes me work that out in the middle of the fastest scene in the book.

## 5. The last two chapters

B. Chapter 16 is identical. In 15, B gives the sash folded in three, the timer that is "louder from here", and the bell: "Now it had rung on hers before she began one." That pays off the timer, the wall and Larrère at once, so "Lesca, Maylis. Surname first" lands as the name on the card of the dead. In A the line ends a summary and I nearly missed it.

## 6. What the loser does better

1. Chapter 7: "Inguma would come for Lesca tonight, and Lesca wasn't going to give it anything to count."
2. Chapter 8: "'You said it in my kitchen.' / 'I was sitting down.'" It restates the standing rule as a joke.
3. Chapter 12: "It had come for Lesca, and not for the woman it was sitting on."
4. Chapter 5: "All day she had been waiting for Inguma to finish counting its lake and come to take Lesca away." B's "that cold to come back to the kitchen door" is prettier and tells me less.
5. Chapter 12: "On Wednesday night she had told Maylis whose chair that was."

## 7. Against the comparison

**Below, though not by much, and only in the middle.**

Chapters 11 to 13 are level with Crouch: I could not stop. The last chapter is better than his endings usually are. The comedy is smaller than The Good Place and I smiled often: the oven door, "It reads that she opened him", being sworn at in six points.

What decides it is the number of rules. The chair, sitting and standing, the right hand, the counting, the hall of the dead and "You're the water" arrive in six instalments, and chapters 6 to 8 ask me to keep the ledger. The rule the climax rests on, that the flood comes only if Lesca is there, is settled by a chart and one line about the river making up two days in an afternoon. I accepted it without feeling it. Crouch gives one rule and runs. The Good Place tells its rules as jokes.
