# Pair 11 — first

## 1. Which one

**B, slightly.** One device separates them: in B, nobody standing in the steel room can say an untrue thing. It buys comedy, a firmer threat and a better last scene. It costs a tenth rule and two more re-reads.

## 2. By criterion

- **Stakes: B.** Ch 8: "If I'm not, it holds." / "Nothing had stopped a word of that." In A it is only a confession: "It's me. I bring it."
- **Tension: level.** Ch 11 and 13 are word for word the same. "On the sixth ring the rib caught and held."
- **Progression: B.** Lesca goes from "it won't come out" (ch 4) to "'To stay,' she said. 'That's the one that comes out.'" (ch 8) to saying it unforced in the square. In A she is candid from ch 8.
- **Comedy: B.** "'Tell me it's Tuesday.' 'It's—'" and "'It came out in my kitchen.' 'This isn't your kitchen.'" Four extra smiles.
- **Characters: A.** "'I look after that bank,' Maylis said. 'It gets four wheelbarrows of clay every spring.'" Maylis wins with her own work. In B the room wins for her.
- **The ending: B.** "'I won't.' It came out whole. They both heard it."
- **Clarity: A.** Pages read twice: A five, B seven.

**Pages read twice, both versions**

1. Ch 3 opening: whose bed, which Coralie, which eleven years.
2. Ch 6 council vote: Maylis drives Lesca's body, so the hands swap owners.
3. Ch 7 bingo: "Maylis had won", then "Maylis had lost".
4. Ch 8: "Your river made up two days in an afternoon, so only the last night counts."
5. Ch 12: how Lesca leaves the hall of the dead alive, when ch 2 said she would die.

**B only**

6. Ch 4: "Because nobody has looked after that bank for twelve years… and a river finds—". Three false clauses get out, then the retry sticks at "Because".
7. Ch 8: "I won't be sent home", said standing, and she is sent home. So the rule means what she believes, not what is true.

**Rules I had to hold, both versions**

1. In the chair, each gets what the other knows.
2. Stay sitting when the other wakes and you drive her body.
3. The owner keeps the right hand and can take the jaw for a few words.
4. Inguma takes the visitor home when it finishes counting; the words set the count.
5. It comes at 2:51. Push until 4 and it leaves; lie still and you are in the chair.
6. The visitor's own body lies at home with its eyes open.
7. Lesca brings her 2014 river with her, day for day; only the last night counts.
8. The one it cannot take home goes to the bingo of the dead.
9. Five fingers means awake.

**B adds**

10. Standing in the steel room, a woman can only say what is true.

## 3. The first fifty pages

Level. Same place in both, about page 21: ch 3, "That village hadn't been spared its flood. It hadn't had it yet." The one early difference comes around page 28, in ch 4: B's Tuesday test is more fun and a hundred words longer.

## 4. The night of the flood

B. The night is the same text; what differs is what I knew going in. B has shown that Maylis cannot say "Either" where only true things come out ("It had never been true"). So "Either" on the platform and "Both" down the phone read as lies, and "She wanted it not to be her" follows.

Lesca too. In B she tries to say "to get—", cannot, then tells herself the story again in her office. "There was no man in it" is her catching up with the room. In A she calmly said "I'm coming to stay" two days earlier, so "This time the man came first" contradicts her and 02:53 teaches her nothing.

## 5. The last two chapters

B, by one line. Ch 15 is identical. In ch 16, A's "I won't" is a promise from the woman who said "I'm coming". B's has been tested.

## 6. What A does better

1. Ch 4: "'And a handle that sticks doesn't make it rain. That's a reason to blame Larrère. It isn't a reason for a river.'"
2. Ch 8: "'I said that for her.' Lesca's fist closed. 'She'd told me she would have me, and I wanted her to. There's nobody in here to say it to.'" B never says why she lied to Coralie.
3. Ch 10: "'There's a man I can get out this time.' … It was a reason she could give across this desk."
4. Ch 12: "Maylis was the one person she had stopped lying to."

## 7. Against the comparison

Below, narrowly. Ch 11 to 13 stand with Crouch's set pieces, and the dip is as good a moral trap as The Good Place sets. What decides it is rule load: ten rules from two systems, a physics box and a folk demon, most of them by page 50. Crouch runs on one. The Good Place hands them out one at a time, each with a joke on it.
