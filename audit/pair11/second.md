# Pair 11, second reader

Read B first, then A. They are word for word the same except in chapters 4, 8, 10, 12 and one line of 16. The difference is one rule: in B, a woman standing up in the steel room can only say what is true. In A, Lesca tells Maylis the truth because she decides to.

## 1. Which one

B, slightly.

## 2. By criterion

- **Stakes:** level. Chapters 3, 9, 11 and 13 carry them and are identical.
- **Tension:** B. Ch. 8: "'I w—' Lesca's fist closed. 'I can't stand here and say I won't.'" She cannot even promise, so Friday is settled.
- **Progression:** B. Ch. 4, "I don't know why it comes. That I can say." Then ch. 8, "Now I've got both."
- **Comedy:** B. Three smiles A lacks: "'Tell me it's Tuesday.' 'It's—'"; "It won't slow me down", just before it does; "'It came out in my kitchen.' 'This isn't your kitchen.'"
- **Characters:** level. B has the better Lesca: "'To stay,' she said. 'That's the one that comes out.'" A has the better Maylis: "I look after that bank."
- **The ending:** B, by one line (see 5).
- **Clarity:** level. Pages read twice in B: 3 (ch. 1, what raising the gate does to Jeannot's water; ch. 3, first page, two bedrooms and two Coralies; ch. 6, the tied vote with two hands up). In A: the same three, unchanged, plus 1 of its own (ch. 8, "There's nobody in here to say it to. You gave her that chart yourself."). B's new rule cost no second read, because it is shown by a test.

Rules I had to hold, in both versions:

1. Inguma comes at 2:51 to a sleeper on her back. Lie still and you are in the chair.
2. In the chair, each gets what the other knows.
3. Stay sitting when the other wakes, and you wake in her body and drive it.
4. The owner keeps the right hand.
5. Inguma takes the visitor home when it has finished counting what the words gave it.
6. Refuse to be taken home and you get the empty chair at the bingo of the dead.
7. Lesca brings her river. Only 2:51 on the last night counts.
8. The sum: ten minutes for the bank, twenty-five on foot to the trapdoor, four from gate to hut, the handle sticks at nineteen.

B adds a ninth: standing in the room, only what is true comes out. A 8, B 9.

In B the stuck mouth has three causes: the room, Lesca refusing "Either", and two drivers on one jaw.

## 3. The first fifty pages

The same place in both: ch. 3, about page 23, "That village hadn't been spared its flood. It hadn't had it yet." B holds better at about page 31, where a woman who cannot lie says she doesn't know why the flood comes. A's page ends on "It's what I've got".

## 4. The night of the flood

Maylis's choice at the dip is the same text in both, and I believed it: "She wanted it not to be her." The difference is Lesca in the van, and I believed B's. The room had refused her "I'm coming to get—". So when her waking mouth says "I can get this one out. Let me drive", I know she is lying to herself, and "There was no man in it" is her catching up with the room. A says "Maylis was the one person she had stopped lying to", and a page later she pleads the man to Maylis.

## 5. The last two chapters

Ch. 15 is identical. Ch. 16 differs by one line after "'I won't.'": "It came out whole. They both heard it." B ends better. The same words stuck in ch. 8, so here they are proof. "They both heard it" could go.

## 6. What the loser does better (A)

1. Ch. 4: "'I look after that bank,' Maylis said. 'It gets four wheelbarrows of clay every spring. And a handle that sticks doesn't make it rain.'" B drops it. Worse, B lets "nobody has looked after that bank for twelve years" come out whole in the room that stops untrue sentences, and ch. 1 says it is false.
2. Ch. 8: "'It's me. I bring it.'" B never has her say it. In B's room it would come out whole.
3. Ch. 8: "'I said that for her.' Lesca's fist closed. 'She'd told me she would have me, and I wanted her to.'" B trades the reason for the tide lie for the kitchen joke.
4. Ch. 12: "Maylis was the one person she had stopped lying to." The best line about the two of them.
5. Ch. 8: "'I could tell you I'm coming to get yours out, and it would sound better.'" Lesca choosing, where B's Lesca is made to.

## 7. Against the comparison (B)

Below both. Chapter 13 is level with Crouch. The oven door and Jeannot's numbered letters are level with The Good Place for smiles. The rule load decides it. Crouch asks me to hold one rule and then runs. This asks for nine, and chapters 4 to 8 go on learning whose hand is whose.
