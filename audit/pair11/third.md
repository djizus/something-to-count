# Pair 11 — third

Read A first, then B. Twelve of the seventeen files are word for word the same. The rest differ by about thirty lines: B adds a fourth rule ("Standing up in this room, a woman can only say what's true"). B came second, so its new pages got more of my attention than a cold reader would give them.

## 1. Which one

A, slightly.

## 2. By criterion

- **Stakes:** level. Chapters 9, 11 and 13 carry them and are identical.
- **Tension:** A. Wednesday night: "I could tell you I'm coming to get yours out, and it would sound better." A woman who can lie and doesn't is harder to face than one who sticks on "I w—".
- **Progression:** A. Lesca goes from "A river finds a bank like that" to "It's me. I bring it" to the microphone, each step chosen. In B the room takes the first two for her.
- **Comedy:** B. "'Tell me it's Tuesday.' 'It's—'" I smiled, and again at "'It's Saturday,' Maylis said."
- **Characters:** A. "Maylis was the one person she had stopped lying to." Also Maylis answering back: "That's a reason to blame Larrère. It isn't a reason for a river."
- **The ending:** B, by one line: "It came out whole. They both heard it."
- **Clarity:** A. Pages read twice: 4 in A, 6 in B.
  - Both: ch. 3 opening (whose bed, which Coralie); ch. 3 the file (2014 dates laid over this year's); ch. 6 the vote (seven, six, a chair with two hands up); ch. 7 bingo (why the old woman finishing the words means "Maylis had lost").
  - B only: ch. 4 steel room (why standing up; why "nobody has looked after that bank for twelve years" comes out whole and the next clause sticks; "It's Saturday", said at about 3 on Sunday morning in a room that passes only what is true); ch. 8 Sunday night ("Now I've got both", just before Maylis's "Both" means something else).
  - Rules held in A (10): 1 the box made two of her; 2 lie still under Inguma and you reach the chair, push and you stay out; 3 in the chair each gets what the other knows; 4 stay seated when the other wakes and you drive her body; 5 the owner keeps her right hand, and the jaw for a few words; 6 Inguma takes the visitor home when its count is done, and anyone with the words sets the count; 7 it comes at 2:51 and gives up at 4; 8 a visitor's own body lies with its eyes open; 9 Lesca brings her river, and only the last night counts; 10 refuse to go home and you sit at the bingo of the dead.
  - Rules held in B (11): the same, plus: standing in the steel room, only the truth comes out.

## 3. The first fifty pages

Same place in both: ch. 3, about page 21 at 280 words a page, "That village hadn't been spared its flood. It hadn't had it yet." The versions do not differ until about page 26, so neither gets there sooner. After that, A's ch. 4 reaches the date faster. B stops to demonstrate its rule, and the stop is funny.

## 4. The night of the flood

A's. The night is the same text. What differs is the Lesca who arrives at it. In A she has told Maylis the bed is the reason and the man is the one that sounds better, so both are alive in her. When she drives past the handle, then sees "the same thing, done with a steering wheel", I believe each step. In B a room that cannot be wrong has refused her "I'm coming to get—". Then ch. 12 says "She had wanted both herself, the bed and the man." The room says one thing and the book says the other. And the fourth rule does nothing on the night: no event turns on it.

## 5. The last two chapters

B, slightly. Ch. 15 is identical. In ch. 16 B's "I won't" answers the "I w—" that stuck on Wednesday, the one place the fourth rule pays. A's ending still works: Inguma going out over the step says the same.

## 6. What the loser does better

Only the first of these moves across without the rule.

- "It had always come when she needed it. It had never been true, and it wasn't true now."
- "'Tell me it's Tuesday.' / 'It's—' Maylis stood with her mouth open on it."
- "'So that's a fourth point, and I didn't have it on my list.'" And: "'It won't slow me down.'"
- "'I don't know why it comes. That I can say.'"
- "'I won't.' / It came out whole. They both heard it."

## 7. Against the comparison

Below both. Chapters 13 and 14 are level with the best of either: the corridor of chairs, "Forty-one", "and I drowned him". What decides it is the load of rules in chapters 5 to 8. Crouch gives me one rule and runs. The Good Place states a rule and gets a joke out of it in the same scene. Here I carried ten and stopped four times to work out whose hand or whose village.
