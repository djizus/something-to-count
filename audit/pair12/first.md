# Pair 12 — first

Read A in full, then B, then compared them. They differ in 11 short passages, about 150 words in all. Chapters 2, 5, 9 to 11 and 13 to 16 are word for word the same. Pages are counted at about 275 words.

## 1. Which one

B, slightly. B was read second, which flatters its clarity. I have allowed for that.

## 2. By criterion

- **Stakes:** level. One shared line carries them: "what I'd have had is a river over forty-two beds."
- **Tension:** B. Chapter 12, the hall of the dead. A gives the outcome before the scene is played: "As it was, Inguma would have to take her home from here." B says only "this was where that put her", so at "Her knees bent and she sat down" I did not know she would get up.
- **Progression:** B. Chapter 3, the ladder. A explains the idea ("If the dates went on matching, she could read every line of the file as this year's, over there") and then shows it at the desk. B goes from "Tuesday had been the 24th" straight to the file.
- **Comedy:** level. I smiled at the same places in both: the oven door, "Either is not a person. I cannot summon Either to the station", "And six. Bugger off", "apologies received". A has one more smile at the vote. B's bingo ends faster: "Inguma was gone. Maylis had lost." then "How's that spelled?"
- **Characters:** A. Chapter 8, Lesca in the room where only true things come out: "It comes with me." B has "Now I've got both", which admits it without saying it.
- **The ending:** level. Same text.
- **Clarity:** A, slightly.
  - Read twice in A: ch. 12, about p. 111, the van, rings two to six (which hand is whose). Ch. 12, about p. 113, the hall: the narrator says she "had let go before she was sitting in it", and a few paragraphs later she sits in it. Ch. 13, about p. 119, the dip (600 metres, 400 metres, six minutes, four minutes).
  - Read twice in B: ch. 8, about p. 73, "Your river made up two days in an afternoon". I first took "made up" as "invented". Ch. 6, about p. 58, the vote: two hands go up and nothing says which way they count until the secretary does.
  - The van and the dip are shared text, already known on the second read. They separate nothing.

## 3. The first fifty pages

The same place in both: chapter 3, about page 22. "That village hadn't been spared its flood. It hadn't had it yet." The last line of chapter 2 got me there.

A gave me the idea 150 words sooner, on the ladder, as an explanation. In B it came with the file and hit harder. Neither is sooner by a page.

## 4. The night of the flood

Level. Chapters 11 and 13 are identical. I believed the choice in both.

Why: at 5 p.m. Jeannot asks her open or shut, and she says "Either". At the dip the reason takes one line: "She wanted it not to be her." It is the dinner answer from page 4 at the size of a river. Lesca letting go is as true: "This was the same thing, done with a steering wheel."

The only difference is upstream: A states the rule for that night more plainly (see 6.1).

## 5. The last two chapters

Level, identical. They end well: "Lesca, Maylis. Surname first", "the car door shutting on a pillow", and Jeannot stepping over the string with the second shovel.

## 6. What the loser does better

From A, for B to take back:

1. Ch. 8: "Now I know. It comes with me. Keeping me out for a night or two changes nothing, because the river catches up the day I'm back. You saw it do that on Saturday. One night matters."
2. Ch. 6: "The mayor's vote had made the tie. Then it had broken it."
3. Ch. 4: "It's Saturday night." Only true things can be said in that room, and by the clock it is Sunday. "Saturday night" still holds. B's "It's Saturday" does not.
4. Ch. 1: "On Monday the river was low. You put the gate up, and my water ran out through it into the river." The gate is new to me here, and A says which way the water went.
5. Ch. 3: "She had just woken up in Maylis's bed, in Maylis's body, beside Maylis's Coralie." It places me in one line. I smiled.

## 7. Against the comparison

Below, by a little.

As comedy B is level with The Good Place. I smiled on most pages up to chapter 8, and the jokes come from rules applied to people (the timer, the motion, the shopping pad), as they do in that show.

It does not run like a Blake Crouch novel. What decides it is the number of rules. Crouch gives one and runs with it. Here I carry six by chapter 8: the chair, staying seated, the right hand, the counting, the truth standing up, the river that comes with her. The van scene needs all six at once, and that is where I slowed down.
