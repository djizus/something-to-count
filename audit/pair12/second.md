# Pair 12, second reader

Read B first, then A. They differ in twelve sentences across chapters 1, 3, 4, 6, 7, 8 and 12. Everything else is word for word the same. A explains; B leaves the reader to it. A came second, so its explanations reached a reader who already knew the answers, and may look more redundant here than they are cold. Pages are counted at 275 words.

## 1. Which one

B, slightly.

## 2. By criterion

- **Stakes: B.** Ch. 12, the hall of the dead. B: "She had held the wheel against it for a whole ring, and this was where that put her." A adds "As it was, Inguma would have to take her home from here", which tells me she gets out before she sits down.
- **Tension: B.** Ch. 3, the ladder. A says "If the dates went on matching, she could read every line of the file as this year's, over there" two paragraphs before "That village hadn't been spared its flood. It hadn't had it yet." B keeps the turn for the file.
- **Progression: A.** Ch. 8: "Now I know. It comes with me." and "One night matters." The rule changes in plain words. B's "Now I've got both. Your river made up two days in an afternoon" made me stop to work out what "both" was.
- **Comedy: level.** A gains a smile in ch. 6 ("The mayor's vote had made the tie. Then it had broken it.") and loses one at bingo, where two rule sentences sit between Bordenave's "It's got no date" and Madame Etcheto settling her hands on the duck.
- **Characters: B.** Ch. 3: "For eleven years Lesca had heard that woman get up in the room across the landing, at the top of the stairs." A has "At home that alarm rang on the other side of the landing", and opens with "Call her Lesca", which is the author talking.
- **The ending: level.** Same text.
- **Clarity: A, slightly.** Pages read twice:
  - B: p. 57 (ch. 6, the tie vote: which way the two raised hands counted). p. 72 (ch. 8, "Now I've got both"). p. 6 (ch. 1, Jeannot's points one and two: whose water, running where), a sentence more than a page.
  - A: p. 114 (ch. 12, the four sentences on why she is in the hall. She "let go before she was sitting in it", then sits in it).
  - Both: p. 146 (ch. 16, Sarthou: "I do not record that I would have stayed").
  - Not reread in either: bingo. "All Maylis had to do was keep the mouth shut until it came" already carries it.

## 3. The first fifty pages

The same place in both: about p. 22, ch. 3, the flood file, then the motion that closes the chapter ("and Coralie Mounet in it"). B gets there harder, not sooner. A announces the turn on the ladder first, and opens the chapter by handing over the landing and the eleven years, which B lets me find from two pillows.

## 4. The night of the flood

Level: chapter 13 is identical. I believed the choice in both. "She wanted it not to be her" is Either with a river in it, and the book has earned it (the pad, the handle at 5, "Say which, Maylis. For once."). What differs is the approach. A's ch. 8 makes "She had put Lesca out, and it had come too late" easier to accept. B's ch. 12 makes Lesca's letting go cost more, because nothing has promised her a way home.

## 5. The last two chapters

Level, same text. They end well: "Lesca, Maylis. Surname first," and Jeannot stepping over the string with her father's shovel.

## 6. What the loser does better

From A, to take back into B:

1. Ch. 6: "Maylis put up the left hand, against. Lesca's right hand let go of the timer and went up beside it, against as well. That was two hands for the mayor speaking on the 12th, and only one mayor."
2. Ch. 6: "The mayor's vote had made the tie. Then it had broken it."
3. Ch. 8: "Now I know. It comes with me." Lesca says standing what she could not say in ch. 4. In B she never says it.
4. Ch. 4: "It's Saturday night." At 2:51 a.m., in a room where only true things come out, "It's Saturday" is false.
5. Ch. 1: "On Sunday night I had a hand of rainwater on those fields." and "my water ran out through it into the river."

Leave behind: "Call her Lesca", the ladder paragraph, both bingo sentences, the ch. 12 paragraph.

## 7. Against the comparison

Below, by a little. The Good Place side holds: a flaw as small as "Either" punished by the plot, an afterlife run as bingo. What decides it is the rule load in the middle. From ch. 4 to ch. 8 I carry six or seven rules (the chair, the right hand, the counting, the truth standing up, 2:51, the water, the empty place) while the body changes hands half a dozen times. Crouch gives one rule and a chase. From ch. 9 to the end this book is level with him, and harder on its heroine than he is on his heroes.
