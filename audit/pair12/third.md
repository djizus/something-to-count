## 7. Against the comparison

**Below, by a short distance. What decides it is the effort the middle asks for.** A Blake Crouch novel and The Good Place never make me go back up the page. This one did, nine times, and eight of the nine fall between chapter 6 and chapter 12.

The cause is the number of rules. I counted seven: stay sitting and you wake in the other body; the owner keeps her right hand; in the chair each gets what the other knows; standing in the steel room you can only say what is true; Inguma takes the visitor home when it has finished counting; Lesca brings the river with her; hold out against Inguma for one ring and a place is laid for you among the dead. Crouch gives one rule and then spends the book on its consequences. Here the seventh rule is explained in the paragraph after it has decided the climax of chapter 12.

Where it is level or above:

- **The hook.** I could not stop from the end of chapter 3: "That village hadn't been spared its flood. It hadn't had it yet." That is about twenty-five pages in, later than Crouch but in time.
- **Chapters 9 to 13.** As good as Crouch. Clock times, one kilometre of track, a van on its belly in the dip. I read chapter 13 without looking up.
- **The choice.** "That was Either, with the river in it" is the best line in the book. She saves a man who was sitting on a dry floor while room twelve fills. The Good Place played the trolley problem for laughs; this plays it straight and makes her pay. On nerve, it is above.
- **The ending.** Nobody is forgiven. Jeannot picks up the second shovel. That is the warmth a Good Place reader needs, and it is enough.

Where it is below:

- **Chapters 6 to 8.** The danger is a date eleven days off. Page by page, the stake is who has the jaw. I was waiting for Friday.
- **The one big turn** ("You're the water") is shouted across a bingo hall, misheard, and only explained the next morning on a three-column chart. The idea is strong. I had it explained to me; it did not hit me.
- **The idea does not grow** after chapter 8. Crouch keeps opening his out. The second village is seen in four short visits.
- **The cause is asserted.** Why the river follows her is never said, though the proof is good: two nights kept out, a dry road, a cross on Bordenave's pad.

**Comedy** (funny meaning I smiled). I smiled often up to chapter 8 and in chapter 11, and it is the right kind for a Good Place reader: procedure treated as farce.

- Smiled: the postman and the man in the car; "Eggs is what Either gets in this house"; the oven door; Inguma given two lots of stars and arriving at 1; "There's nobody in the chair"; "You've got a shed, Maylis. It has a lawnmower in it"; "It takes a small m"; "Either is not a person. I cannot summon Either to the station"; the mayor signing letters lying on the floor; "timed like an egg"; "Tooth"; "Then it should have gone last Monday"; Jeannot's six points; "Then they'll want the bins out"; "I've got nothing on the sheet for this."
- Did not smile: Josiane's husband-or-wife theory on its second and third outing (chapters 6 and 7). Its payoff in 16, "I only had the one name wrong", did work.
- The Good Place varies its jokes more. Here three engines run throughout: the window gap, the numbered points, the timer.

**Pages I had to read twice**

1. Ch. 3, the ladder: from "On the 24th. It's in the file" to "It hadn't had it yet." The step from rain on a roof to reading a 2014 file as this year's.
2. Ch. 6, arrival in the other village: which hand is now whose, then the paragraph that packs in the cameras, Kévin's six months and the sash in the plastic bag.
3. Ch. 6, Larrère's motion: he moves that the prefect speak alone, then describes the mayor speaking.
4. Ch. 6, the vote: seven, six, two hands up, a tie made and broken by the same vote.
5. Ch. 7, 4 and 5 December: the forecast is thirty centimetres out, then the post matches the file again. No reason given until chapter 8.
6. Ch. 7, end of bingo: "Maylis had won" and "Maylis had lost" ten lines apart; who gains when Madame Etcheto finishes the words.
7. Ch. 8, kitchen: Lesca says "Friday", the door stays shut, Inguma is on the chest anyway.
8. Ch. 8, last page: the empty chair, "Nil", and whose card LESCA, MAYLIS is.
9. Ch. 12, after the break: "that ring had laid a place for her here. But she had let go before she was sitting in it."
