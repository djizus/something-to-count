# Pair 13 — first

Read B first, then A. Same story and ending; the differences are in the sentences and a few facts.

## 1. Which one

**B, clearly.** Sure of the direction; the gap is line-level, not story-level.

## 2. By criterion

- **Stakes: level.** Same river, same forty-one. B states the rule exactly ("your bank goes, as mine was going"; A's "as mine did" is wrong, hers never broke).
- **Tension: B.** Ch 12, "This time Castagnet came first" against "This time the man came first". A hides people I know: "A man came in from the street… It was Jeannot."
- **Progression: B.** Ch 7, "By 6:20 Lesca had given Inguma her lake to count again." Without it, A never says why she is still driving at bingo.
- **Comedy: B, narrowly.** I smiled in the same places in both: eggs for Either, the oven door, "I cannot summon Either to the station", the six-point "Bugger off", "apologies received". Only B has the resignation written "on the roof of his car, bent over the sheet to keep the rain off it"; A moves it into an office and loses the gap. Only A has "counted one of her" and "Neither of them liked it".
- **Characters: level.** B calls people by their names; A has the better single touches (see 6).
- **The ending: B.** Ch 15, "when they called twelve, somebody sat down."
- **Clarity: B.** Pages read twice:
  - *Both:* ch 3, the flood file (two calendars, "hadn't had its flood yet"); ch 5, Larrère's window (why "Ei—" sticks outside the steel room, and who "her" is); ch 8, last page (whose empty chair, and "Nil"); ch 12, arrival in the hall of the dead ("this was where that put her"); ch 14, first page ("Twelve. One, two").
  - *B only:* ch 6, the vote (seven, six, two hands).
  - *A only:* ch 1, the square ("the man in the car", "the sender", "the man behind the glass"); ch 2, Bixente ("two lots of stars… got through them early"); ch 4, "Because nobody has looked after that bank" (she says the false thing whole, then is stopped); ch 5, "That wasn't enough any more"; ch 6, Larrère in an office at 9 when the town hall opens at 2; ch 9, "She used the bandage alone this time"; ch 11, minibus and Madame Etcheto at the back door, then everything at the front in ch 13; ch 11, "'How many seats has your van got?' Maylis said"; ch 16, the café ("Then he looked up.").
  - *Voice:* B's sentences sound like a person telling it. A person says "Jeannot", "Your pulse is 112", "Lesca waited for the Either". A says "the man who wrote the letters", "the woman this body belonged to", "the other word, the one this mouth put after everything".

## 3. The first fifty pages

The same place in both: the last line of ch 2, about page 15 ("'Come back across,' her mouth said. Maylis hadn't said it."), and locked by the end of ch 3 ("and Coralie Mounet in it"). Sooner in B, because ch 1 gets me there without working out who is in the car and whose letter it is.

## 4. The night of the flood

The choice is the same in both: the van sinks, she walks on to the hut, "She wanted it not to be her." I believed both, B more. In B I could see what it cost: the minibus at the front, room twelve at the bottom of the ramp, Lesca's own night "in the dark, with the dip under water". And "Lesca waited for the Either" names the word, so "Both" down the phone to Coralie reads as the old word again. A's one advantage is "She was scared.", which echoes the opening.

## 5. The last two chapters

B. In ch 15 "somebody sat down" pays off the chair pulled in at the start of ch 14; A's "it came into that room" does not. In ch 16 the dealer "looked up and saw her at the counter", and the envelope comes from behind the fruit bowl. A keeps two lines B should have: the fare (see 6) and "Now it had rung on hers before she began one."

## 6. What A does better

1. "The fare had gone up twice since she counted it out." (ch 16)
2. "Eighteen years of cutting roadsides had come down to six lines and a gap." (ch 6)
3. "They were the same height. Neither of them liked it." (ch 4)
4. "She was scared." (ch 13, at 02:55)
5. "The mayor's vote had made the tie. Then it had broken it." (ch 6, the page I re-read in B)

## 7. Against the comparison

**Below, not by much.** The village comedy stands beside The Good Place for smiles, and ch 10 and ch 13 would hold their own in a Crouch novel. What decides it is the rules of the crossing. There are five or six, and nearly every page I read twice in B is a rule page, the worst at the climax (ch 12, why holding the wheel for one ring puts her in the hall). Crouch gives one rule and accelerates. Here I slowed down exactly where the book should run.
