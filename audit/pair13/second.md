# Pair 13 — second

## 1. Which one

B, clearly. It is the same book scene for scene, but in B I rarely had to go back.

## 2. By criterion

- **Stakes: B.** Ch. 5, after the soup: "It would come and take Lesca home. But Coralie would still want her back." A has "That wasn't enough any more".
- **Tension: B, slightly.** Ch. 13, the rescue. B's ch. 11 puts the back door under the bank and the minibus in the front yard, so I knew where Coralie had gone. A's minibus is at the back door in ch. 11 and at the glass doors in ch. 13.
- **Progression: B.** Ch. 7: "By 6:20 Lesca had given Inguma her lake to count again." A never says why Lesca is still in her all Saturday.
- **Comedy: level.** B has the resignation written on the roof of Larrère's car in the rain (ch. 6). A keeps "They were the same height. Neither of them liked it." and "counted one of her". I smiled at all three.
- **Characters: A.** "The fare had gone up twice since she counted it out." It says how long Maïté's suitcase has been packed.
- **The ending: B, slightly.** Ch. 16, the café: "Then he looked up and saw her at the counter."
- **Clarity: B.** Lists below, by chapter, since the files have no page numbers.

Read twice in A:

1. Ch. 1, the square: "the president of the association", "the man in the car" and "the mayor" are one man.
2. Ch. 2, Bixente: "given two lots of stars, and it got through them early". Two lots should take longer.
3. Ch. 3, the bed: "The woman this body belonged to", "The other one". Whose jaw?
4. Ch. 4: Lesca says the false answer in full, then cannot say it.
5. Ch. 5, the last kitchen line: "That wasn't enough any more."
6. Ch. 6: Larrère in his office at 9, when ch. 1 keeps him in his car until 2.
7. Ch. 7, bingo: why Maylis keeps the mouth shut.
8. Ch. 11 and 13: which door of the care home is which.
9. Ch. 14: "have the case back", for the medal.
10. Ch. 16, the café: "Then he looked up." At what?

Read twice in B:

1. Ch. 6: "Every half hour she put two fingers on the wrist". "She" reads as Maylis.
2. Ch. 10: "the flood fields were a lake now". Ch. 5 says the lake is where the village field was.
3. Ch. 14: "Her mother left the church wall". B has cut the line that put her there.
4. Ch. 15: "Then it wasn't Inguma that came for her." Her?

Read twice in both: ch. 4, the three points of how the chair works.

**Whose sentences sound like a person telling it: B.** A person telling a story says Larrère and Jeannot, not "the man behind the glass" or "a woman with a cracked rib". But B loses some of the teller's asides, and a nurse says "A hundred and twelve", not "Your pulse is 112".

## 3. The first fifty pages

The same place in both: the last line of ch. 2, "'Come back across,' her mouth said", about page 15, then "hadn't had its flood yet" in ch. 3, about page 21. B got me there sooner. In A I had gone back three times before the second line.

## 4. The night of the flood

B. "If she turned that handle, Jeannot drowned and her hand had done it." A says "a man drowned". That morning he swore at her in six points, so I believe she cannot drown Jeannot. "A man" is a principle. B also makes Lesca's own choice fair: "twenty-five minutes away, on foot, in the dark, with the dip under water".

## 5. The last two chapters

B, slightly. In ch. 16 the dealer's new joke lands on her and the envelope comes from behind the fruit bowl where ch. 4 left it. A's ch. 15 is better at Madame Etcheto's wheelchair (below).

## 6. What A does better

1. "They opened the door, counted one of her, paid her, gave her a sandwich and went back to their university." (ch. 1)
2. "They were the same height. Neither of them liked it." (ch. 4)
3. "'...and last night it came into that room.' 'With me in it.' 'Yes.'" (ch. 15) B's "somebody sat down" is neater and hurts less.
4. "The fare had gone up twice since she counted it out." (ch. 16)
5. "She was scared." at the patch (ch. 13). It repeats the opening word for word, on the other woman.

## 7. Against the comparison

Below, though the last third (ch. 11 to 13, and 16) is level with either.

The middle decides it. From ch. 4 to ch. 8 I was learning rules: who drives, who keeps which hand, what counting buys, what can be said standing up. Meanwhile the flood is a date on a pad. Crouch gives one rule and has someone in danger by page 10. The Good Place gets a laugh out of each new rule. Here the bingo of the dead is as good as anything in that show, but the smiles come from Larrère and Jeannot, not from the rules.
