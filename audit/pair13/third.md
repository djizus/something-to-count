# Pair 13: third

Read order: B first, cold, then A, already knowing the story.

## 1. Which one

**B, slightly.** Same book, told twice. B is the one I would read aloud.

## 2. By criterion

- **Stakes: level.** One sum carries both: "The trapdoor was twenty-five minutes away… The bank had ten."
- **Tension: level.** A tells me the score ("Now she was more afraid of her own stairs", "Maylis had lost"). B turns the page faster.
- **Progression: B.** The resignation written "on the roof of his car, bent over the sheet to keep the rain off it" keeps Larrère behind glass until he walks through mud in 16. A sits him at a desk at 9, in a town hall that opens at 2.
- **Comedy: A, slightly.** B lost three smiles: "counted one of her", "They were the same height. Neither of them liked it", "Whether the prefect might have the case back". It gained one, the car roof.
- **Characters: B.** People have names. A's "a man was sitting in a cellar" holds him at arm's length. B's "Jeannot was sitting in his cellar" does not.
- **The ending: A, slightly.** See 5.
- **Clarity: B, clearly.**

**Pages read twice in B (6)**

- Ch 1, Jeannot's One and Two: how raising a gate drains a field.
- Ch 6, the cut to the bedside: "Every half hour she put two fingers on the wrist." Whose she.
- Ch 6, the tie vote: which hand counts, and what "against" keeps.
- Ch 8, "the river catches up the day I'm back."
- Ch 11, "up the ramp inside the back door, through the open fire door and down the corridor."
- Ch 14 into 15: "Somebody pulled a chair in to a table", then "somebody sat down", then "came for her". I had to work out who.

**Pages read twice in A (10)**

- Ch 2, "you're in, and you've still got your head."
- Ch 2, "given two lots of stars, and it got through them early."
- Ch 3, "The woman this body belonged to", "a woman with a cracked rib… That woman had the key."
- Ch 4, Lesca says "Because nobody has looked after that bank for twelve years" in full, a page after the room stopped a false sentence.
- Ch 4, "One of the men in it lived in the fourth house."
- Ch 6, Larrère at a desk at 9.
- Ch 9, "She used the bandage alone this time."
- Ch 10, "a house where nobody had ever picked up that phone."
- Ch 11 into 13: the minibus is "parked at the door" at the bank end, then in a front yard.
- Ch 16, "A man came in out of the rain… Then he looked up." At whom.

**Which sounds like a person telling it: B.** A teller says "Larrère paid her wages", not "the man behind the glass paid her wages". A sounds written: "worn as smooth as a stone step", "lit up like a stage". A is the better teller only in its asides. B goes flat twice: "It was heavy", "Her voice was strong".

## 3. The first fifty pages

Same spot in both: chapter 3, from "Today was the 28th" to "and Coralie Mounet in it". B reached it sooner, because A makes me translate "that woman" and "the man who wrote the letters" while the idea is landing.

## 4. The night of the flood

B. "If she turned that handle, Jeannot drowned and her hand had done it" is about the man who swore at her in six points. A's "a man drowned" is about a principle. And "She had just sent Coralie to the bank end" only hurts if I know where the bank end is. B drew the building in 11. A moved the minibus.

A does one thing better here: "She was scared", alone on its line at 02:55, the prologue's words.

## 5. The last two chapters

A, by two lines. In 15, "last night it came into that room." / "With me in it." / "Yes." is plain and it lands. B's "somebody sat down" is a riddle where the book should speak straight. In 16, "The fare had gone up twice since she counted it out" tells me how long that suitcase has been packed. B's 16 is tidier, but that is housekeeping.

## 6. What the loser does better

1. "They opened the door, counted one of her, paid her, gave her a sandwich and went back to their university."
2. "They were the same height. Neither of them liked it."
3. "She tried to look behind the sum for the woman who had made it, but all she found was a handle, turning."
4. "'…and last night it came into that room.' 'With me in it.' 'Yes.'"
5. "The fare had gone up twice since she counted it out."

## 7. Against the comparison

**Below, by a little.** The choice at the dip and the bingo hall of the dead are level with both. What decides it is the number of rules I carry: whose hand, what standing does, what counting buys, what brings the river. The one that matters, "It comes with me", arrives in chapter 8 of 16. Crouch gives one rule early and runs.
