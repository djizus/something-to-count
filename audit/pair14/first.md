# Pair 14, reader: first

Read A in full, then B. Only chapters 5 to 8 differ. The rest is word for word the same, so every verdict comes from those four chapters and what they do to the chapters after them.

## 1. Which one

A, slightly.

## 2. By criterion

- **Stakes: A.** "Monsieur Darrigrand was on the stairs with a mattress on his back." In A the forty houses have people in them before the night. In B they are a number.
- **Tension: A.** "She wrote 47 under 48. At work she rang the doctor for any pulse under 40." The day as mayor has a clock in A. In B she is only late.
- **Progression: A.** "Your pulse was 34 when I rang him" makes Coralie's move to the spare room a consequence.
- **Comedy: B, slightly.** "'The timer?' the secretary said. 'You have it, Madame le maire. It's in the inventory.'" A cuts that and two more smiles from the council scene. A adds Sarthou's "I have removed it from my gate twice since 7."
- **Characters: A.** "She didn't ring it. A doctor would put Maylis in an ambulance to Bayonne. And Madame Etcheto had said it was this bed." A trained woman trusts the old woman's rule over her own.
- **The ending: level.** Same text. It lands a little harder in A (see 5).
- **Clarity: A, slightly.** No page read twice in either. B stopped me once: "In the other village it was raining", other from where. B's cut to "'Madame le maire will sit down,' Coralie said" doesn't say which Coralie. A says "Lesca's Coralie".

## Afraid for anybody, chapters 5 to 8

**A**
- 5: For Jeannot, that the warning itself sends him to the hut ("I'm giving it one now"). For Maylis, of Lesca on the stairs. A first unease for the last house, with the patch dark above Sarthou's roof.
- 6: For Maylis, of dying in her own bed, with figures: 64, 58, 47, 36. For Coralie, with her thumb over the number. Here I read faster.
- 7: For Jeannot, after his speech with the ham. For Bordenave and the care home: water over his ankles, the drain sending the rain back.
- 8: For Coralie, who puts herself on nights. For Kévin, his name against the glass. For the Darrigrands and the baby. For whoever the card is for.

**B**
- 5: The same, minus the last house.
- 6: For Kévin only. I was told Coralie might find her dead and did not feel it. Five hours late played as a joke.
- 7: For Jeannot. I was afraid of the date, not for anyone in the houses. The river is a figure on a post.
- 8: For Coralie, Kévin and the card. Not for the Port.

## 3. The first fifty pages

Same place in both, because the text is the same: chapter 3, "That village hadn't had its flood yet", about page 21. A's one change inside the fifty, Sarthou's house in chapter 5, comes after it.

## 4. The night of the flood

Identical chapters. I believed the choice more in A. Her sum at the bank ("Three minutes to get there. Two to get him up the ladder") is the kind of sum that left her at 34 in chapter 6, so I knew it was wrong before she did. And when she turns her back on the lamp, I knew whose stairs and cellars were under it.

## 5. The last two chapters

Level on the page. A ends better for what comes before: "You had the key" is said by a woman whose husband I had watched carry a mattress upstairs, and Coralie packs her bag after watching a pulse go to 34 in that bed.

## 6. What B does better

1. "'I want that,' Maylis said. She hadn't meant to say it out loud." Then the inventory line. Maylis wants something, and it is funny.
2. "Nobody at that table had heard him finish a sentence since 2015. They put their pens down to watch."
3. "Madame Etcheto had said it came to that bed. This wasn't that bed. At 2:51 it came down the stairs, with the sound of a sack being lowered down a step, and it found her."
4. "The river hadn't moved since Wednesday night. […] The boards of the platform were dry under her boots, for the first time since the rib." Maylis gets the clue with her own eyes.
5. "The brown water climbed the last bare centimetres, with sticks turning in it. Then it lay along the paint from end to end."

## 7. Against the comparison

Below, not by much. Chapter 13 is level with Crouch. The oven door and Jeannot's numbered points are level with The Good Place.

The middle decides it. From 4 to 8 the book adds rules faster than danger: the chair, the right hand, standing and not lying, the counting, the water that comes with her. Crouch runs one rule and tightens it every chapter. A's pulse chart closes part of that gap.

The chart also opens a question: Maylis is at 34 after thirteen hours away, yet Lesca is away from 6 on Saturday to Sunday breakfast, and nobody counts her pulse.
