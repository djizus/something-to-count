# Pair 14 — second

Read B first, then A. They are word for word the same outside chapters 5 to 8.

## 1. Which one

**A, slightly.** Sure of the direction, not of the size: A made me afraid in the middle, and it pays for that with one rule the later chapters do not keep.

## 2. By criterion

- **Stakes: A.** "The mayor's body wasn't tired. Maylis was the one getting weaker, and she knew why." The old woman's saying becomes a number on a chart.
- **Tension: A.** "The last line on the chart said 36," with Coralie's thumb over the doctor's number, cut against Larrère talking with no bell. In B the same eleven minutes are only funny.
- **Progression: A.** Bordenave in his cellar, "This floor was dry yesterday," then on Tuesday "It had come up a step a day since." B's Port does not change until the shoelace of water.
- **Comedy: B.** "'The timer?' the secretary said. 'You have it, Madame le maire. It's in the inventory.'" A's mayor is too weak to enjoy the chair. A does add Sarthou: "That is the village's clay. I have removed it from my gate twice since 7."
- **Characters: A.** Coralie: "At work she rang the doctor for any pulse under 40, without asking anybody first." Then: "She didn't ring it." It is her first real decision, and chapter 16 answers it: "I wouldn't wake her. That part is mine."
- **The ending: level.** Same words. It weighs more after A, because the saying about dying in your own bed has already been tried on Maylis.
- **Clarity: B.** A sent me back once. Maylis is at 34 after 13 hours away. Lesca is away 15 hours on Monday and from 6 on Saturday to Sunday breakfast, and chapter 14 says "Bayonne has had you for three hours and found nothing." B's rule holds. B's one stumble: "In the other village it was raining," which I read as Lesca's.

## 3. The first fifty pages

Same point in both: chapter 3, about page 22, "That village hadn't had its flood yet." Neither got me there sooner. The only difference inside fifty pages is A's stop at the last house in chapter 5, where the patch has "turned dark across the width of a door." A shows the opening's door of water a chapter before B does.

## 4. The night of the flood

Same text. I believed A's. When she turns her back on the lamp, A has already put me under that bank: jam jars in the rain, a mattress going up the Darrigrands' stairs. So "She wanted it not to be her" reads as a failing I understand. In B the Port is forty shutters and a sum, and the choice costs less than it should.

## 5. The last two chapters

Level, same words. Both end well, on "Lesca, Maylis. Surname first" and on Jeannot stepping over the string with the second shovel.

## 6. What the loser does better

Five from B that A should take back:

1. "'I want that,' Maylis said. She hadn't meant to say it out loud." Her first want out loud, and it is a kitchen timer.
2. "Maylis had never kept anybody waiting in her life, so she took her time over it."
3. "Nobody at that table had heard him finish a sentence since 2015. They put their pens down to watch."
4. "At 2:51 it came down the stairs, with the sound of a sack being lowered down a step, and it found her." Proof that it is not the bed, before the van.
5. "The boards of the platform were dry under her boots, for the first time since the rib." She sees it herself. In A the café tells her.

## 7. Chapters 5 to 8: was I afraid, and of what

**A**
- 5: for Coralie on the fourth stair, of Lesca getting into that bed. A little for the Port, at the patch over Sarthou's roof.
- 6: yes, for Maylis, of her dying in her own bed while she sits in the mayor's chair. For Coralie too, with the phone. The strongest fear in the four chapters.
- 7: for Jeannot, when the hall claps "On Friday the 11th I'll be in my hut." For Bordenave's cellar.
- 8: for Coralie at "I'm on nights from Friday," for the Darrigrands, and for whoever the card is for.

**B**
- 5: the stair, the same.
- 6: no. I worried about her job and Kévin's six months. "Coralie finding her dead" named a danger I did not feel.
- 7: for Jeannot, the same. Nothing for the Port.
- 8: for Coralie and the card, the same. The Port is one line of water and nine signatures.

## 8. Against the comparison

**Below both, and not far.** The one thing: the middle. The best idea in the book, "You're the water," lands in chapter 7, and her answer to it is to sit up in a van and stay awake. A Crouch hero turns a new rule into a move by the next page, and The Good Place would have got three reversals out of a room where nobody standing can lie. The flood night and the speech at the stone are as good as either.
