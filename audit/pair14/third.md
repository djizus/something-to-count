# Pair 14 — third

Read A in full, then B. The two differ only in chapters 5 to 8; 00–04 and 9–16 are word for word the same, so sections 3, 4 and 5 turn on what 5–8 set up.

## 1. Which one

**A, slightly.** The gap is small in words and all one way: in A the danger in the middle of the book has a body and a street.

## 2. By criterion

- **Stakes: A.** Ch. 6, Coralie's chart: "It had been 64 at 6. At 10 it was 58", then "She wrote 47 under 48", then "The last line on the chart said 36." B says it once: "and Coralie finding her dead."
- **Tension: A.** "She didn't ring it. A doctor would put Maylis in an ambulance to Bayonne. And Madame Etcheto had said it was this bed the thing came to." A thumb over a number means something only when I know why she can't press it.
- **Progression: A.** The Port gets wetter house by house: Sarthou's clay (5), Bordenave "in water over his ankles" (7), "Monsieur Darrigrand was on the stairs with a mattress on his back" (8). In B the river is a reading on a post.
- **Comedy: level.** B has "It's in the inventory." A has "That is the village's clay. I have removed it from my gate twice since 7." I smiled at both.
- **Characters: A.** Coralie choosing the old woman's rule over her training (above).
- **The ending: level.** Same text. A's Port makes Madame Darrigrand's "You had the key" land a little harder.
- **Clarity: A, just.** In B I read the 18:40 paragraph twice ("Every half hour she put two fingers on the wrist": whose?). In A I stopped once on logic, not prose: Maylis fades in thirteen hours over there, but Lesca stays a day, then a day and a night, at no cost anyone counts.

## 3. Chapters 5 to 8: was I afraid, and of what

**A**
- 5: For Maylis's place in her own kitchen ("I'd have this one again"), and of Lesca on the stairs. A first unease for the Port: the patch dark "across the width of a door" over Sarthou's roof.
- 6: Yes, for Maylis's life. The falling pulse, the left hand that has to be told twice, Coralie one press from the ambulance that would kill her. The strongest fear in the four chapters.
- 7: For Jeannot ("On Friday the 11th I'll be in my hut", and the hall claps). For Bordenave's house. Of Lesca taking Coralie on the landing.
- 8: For the care home (Coralie on nights, thirty metres from the patch), for Kévin (his name against the glass), for the Port (pump, mattress), and for whichever Maylis the card is laid for.

**B**
- 5: Only for the marriage.
- 6: No. I enjoyed it as comedy. Five hours late read as embarrassment, not danger.
- 7: One fear A lacks: the kitchen floor, "and it found her". Nowhere to hide. Jeannot as in A. The Port, not yet.
- 8: Care home, Kévin and the card as in A. The Port, hardly: I had not seen inside one house.

## 4. The first fifty pages

Level, and the same place: chapter 3, "That village hadn't had its flood yet." The only difference inside fifty pages is A's Sarthou scene, which helps.

## 5. The night of the flood

Same text; I believed it more in **A**. When Maylis turns her back on the lamp I knew what was under it: a cellar, a mattress, a baby two doors up, a drain already running backwards. "She wanted it not to be her" reads as a failing with a price. In B, forty houses is still Lesca's number. B does plant the vanity that chapter 16 confesses ("I want that"), and A should take it.

## 6. The last two chapters

Level. 15 is the weaker chapter in both. 16 ends well: "Then he stepped over the string in his rubber boots and began to dig."

## 7. What B does better

1. "'I want that,' Maylis said. She hadn't meant to say it out loud. 'The timer?' the secretary said. 'You have it, Madame le maire. It's in the inventory.'" (She can want it and still be too weak to stand.)
2. "Nobody at that table had heard him finish a sentence since 2015. They put their pens down to watch."
3. "At 2:51 it came down the stairs, with the sound of a sack being lowered down a step, and it found her."
4. "Its signature was a single stroke that came back and crossed itself."
5. "The brown water climbed the last bare centimetres, with sticks turning in it. Then it lay along the paint from end to end."

## 8. Against the comparison

**Below, slightly.** Chapters 9 to 13 are level with Crouch: one clock, one choice, and it costs. The comedy is level with The Good Place for smiles (the oven door, "Either is not a person"). What decides it is the middle: for four chapters the book manages its rules (chair, right hand, counting, standing truth, the river that comes with her) where Crouch would be spending them. A is the version that has started to fix this.
