# Pair 15 — first

Read B, then A. Chapters 0–4, 9 and 11–16 are word for word the same. The versions differ in 5–8 and in one line of 10.

## 1. Which one

**B, clearly.** The difference is about 500 words, and it all falls one way. I read B first, so A's chapter 6 was a second pass; the pulse numbers are missing from A's page all the same.

## 2. By criterion

- **Stakes: B.** Ch. 6, "She wrote 47 under 48." In A, staying over costs Maylis a late council. In B it is killing her.
- **Tension: B.** Ch. 6: Larrère talks for 11 minutes off the timer while "The last line on the chart said 36."
- **Progression: B.** The water climbs one rung a chapter: clay on Sarthou's road (5), Bordenave's cellar "over his ankles" (7), "a step a day" and Darrigrand's mattress (8). In A the Port stays offstage until the petition.
- **Comedy: B, slightly.** Same jokes, plus two that made me smile: "That is the village's clay. I have removed it from my gate twice since 7." and "The cross stays. Friday was a metre."
- **Characters: B, slightly.** Coralie with the doctor's number up and not ringing, because "it was this bed the thing came to", is the best thing she does before the flood.
- **The ending: level.** Same text.
- **Clarity: B, slightly.** No page read twice in either. A's "In the other village it was raining" stopped me for a line: for five chapters the other village has been Lesca's. One wobble in B: Maylis is at 34 after 13 hours, yet Lesca's body lies empty from Saturday 6 to Sunday breakfast and she only has a slow hand.

## 3. Fear, chapters 5 to 8

**B**
- 5: Not for a life. Afraid Maylis loses Coralie to the one who answers ("I'd have this one again"). The dark patch over Sarthou's roof made me uneasy for the row.
- 6: Yes, for Maylis's life: 64, 58, 47, 36, while Inguma does the roadsides on foot. Also afraid Coralie would ring, and an ambulance would take the body from the bed the thing comes to.
- 7: For the Port (the cellar, the drain "sending it back up") and for Jeannot (25 minutes against 10, then "I'll be in my hut").
- 8: For Lesca, for the first time ("colder each time I go back to it"), which turns the card with her name into a threat. For the houses, for Jeannot, a little for Kévin.

**A**
- 5: Same as B for Coralie. Nothing for the Port.
- 6: No. She is 5 hours late and it plays as embarrassment. "Coralie finding her dead" is a clause with no number behind it, and the one bedside cut comes at 18:40.
- 7: For Jeannot only. The pulse chart is shown once she is safe.
- 8: For Jeannot, and for whoever the card is for. The Port is a shoelace of water and 9 signatures.

## 4. The first fifty pages

Level, same point in both: ch. 3, "That village hadn't had its flood yet", about page 22. The only difference inside fifty pages is Sarthou and his shovel, after the hook.

## 5. The night of the flood

Same text. B's choice I believed; A's I accepted. Two reasons. "No job on that bank had ever taken her the time she gave it": in B I had already watched her bad sum (roadsides at tractor speed) nearly kill her, so the 8-minute plan is the same fault and I knew it would fail. And in B I knew who lived under the bank, so "She wanted it not to be her" costs what it should. Lesca letting go of the wheel also weighs more: "She knew what that cost" has a cold body behind it.

## 6. The last two chapters

Level. Ch. 16 is the stronger of the two in both: "I know what you picked. I was the other one." and a letter 42 that "doesn't thank you". B arrives better prepared for Madame Darrigrand's "You had the key", because we saw her husband carry the mattress upstairs.

## 7. What A does better

- "Its signature was a single stroke that came back and crossed itself."
- "It hadn't rained since then either." (States the clue. B leaves it to dry boards.)
- "She wrote on the roof of his car, bent over the sheet to keep the rain off it, while he dictated."
- "He turned to face her." (Before Larrère's order: the turn from bluster to charge.)
- "The doctor's number was written across it and underlined twice."

## 8. Against the comparison

Level with The Good Place: a trolley problem with a real handle, a village that gets everything wrong out loud, and an ending that makes her pay.

Below Blake Crouch, and one thing decides it: in the middle of the book the danger is a date. Between the council in 6 and the van in 11, nothing is closing on the heroine on the page; what she stands to lose is Coralie's good opinion. Crouch puts a body at risk in every chapter. B's chart and cellar close part of that gap, not all of it.
