# Pair 15 — second

Chapters 0 to 4, 9 and 11 to 16 are word for word the same. B differs in 5, 6, 7, 8 and one sentence of 10.

## 1. Which one

B, clearly. The margin is five chapters wide, but they are the five where the book was weakest.

## 2. By criterion

- **Stakes: B.** 'The last line on the chart said 36.' In A the same day costs Maylis nothing I could count.
- **Tension: B.** 'She didn't ring it. A doctor would put Maylis in an ambulance to Bayonne.'
- **Progression: B.** Bordenave's cellar: dry, then 'water over his ankles', then 'a step a day since'. A reports the river from a marker post.
- **Comedy: level.** B adds two smiles: 'That is the village's clay. I have removed it from my gate twice since 7', and Bordenave ankle-deep, defending his pad: 'The cross stays. Friday was a metre.' A's day as mayor is lighter and keeps its cleanest beat (see 6).
- **Characters: B**, for Coralie. 'At work she rang the doctor for any pulse under 40, without asking anybody first.' Her move to the spare room is earned in B. In A it is abrupt.
- **The ending: level.** Same text. It lands harder in B, because I had already watched the water reach Bordenave and the Darrigrands.
- **Clarity: A, slightly.** A never sent me back. B did once, to count hours. Maylis is at 34 after thirteen hours away. Lesca was across fifteen on Monday 30th and pulled at full strength on the stairs, and by Sunday breakfast she has been across twenty-seven with only a slow left hand. One line has to square that.

## Chapters 5 to 8: was I afraid for anybody

**A**

- 5: No. I worried for Maylis's job, and on the stairs for what Lesca would take from her.
- 6: No. 'Coralie finding her dead' is said once and the day plays as a treat. The five hours late are a punchline.
- 7: Yes, for Jeannot, from 'On Friday the 11th I'll be in my hut.' The warning put him there.
- 8: For Coralie, at 'Thirty metres from the patch', and for Maylis at the card with her name. The forty houses were still a number.

**B**

- 5: A first small fear for the Port. The patch is 'dark across the width of a door' above Sarthou's roof on the Monday, in the opening's own words.
- 6: Yes, for Maylis: that she dies in her bed while she is enjoying herself as mayor. 64, 58, 47, 36. And for Coralie, that she picks wrong between the doctor and the bed.
- 7: Jeannot, as in A. Still Maylis for a page: 'The hand was cold and it stayed open.'
- 8: The Port, which now has a mattress going up a staircase and a pump in a cellar. And Lesca, for the first time: 'It's colder each time I go back to it.' The empty chair at the bingo table becomes a fate with a rate.

## 3. The first fifty pages

The same point in both, because the pages are the same: chapter 3, about page 21, at 'That village hadn't had its flood yet'. Neither gets there sooner. B holds on better up to page fifty, because chapter 5 opens on the patch.

## 4. The night of the flood

The words are identical. I believed Maylis's choice in both: 'She wanted it not to be her' is the Either of chapter 1 with a river behind it. I believed B's more. What she turns her back on has faces, and when Lesca holds the wheel for one ring, 'She knew what that cost' rests in A on a card and in B on a body I had watched go cold.

## 5. The last two chapters

Level, word for word. They end well in both: 'Lesca, Maylis. Surname first', 'I won't' coming out whole, Jeannot stepping over the string.

## 6. What A does better

1. 'Maylis had never kept anybody waiting in her life, so she took her time over it. When she did sit, she sat up straight.' B follows the first sentence with 'She couldn't have stood as long as they could', which contradicts it and kills the smile.
2. 'He turned to face her.' B goes straight into Larrère's order speech and loses the turn.
3. 'Its signature was a single stroke that came back and crossed itself.'
4. 'She still sat on the council. When she had something to say to Lesca, she said it to the wall. At home Maylis put out the folding tables with her every July.'
5. 'It hadn't rained since then either.' Without it, the chart in chapter 8 leans on Bordenave's dry road alone.

## 7. Against the comparison

Below, slightly. The middle decides it. Crouch sets one clock and tightens it every chapter. From the stairs to the bingo card this book teaches rules, and the danger is to a marriage and a job. B's pulse chart is the only such clock in those four chapters. From chapter 9 on it is level with him, and chapter 13 is above. Against The Good Place it is level on the smile.
