# Pair 16: second

Read B first, then A. The versions differ only in chapters 7, 8, 10 and 12, where A is about 330 words longer. The other thirteen files are word for word the same.

## 1. Which one

**A, slightly.**

## 2. By criterion

- **Stakes: A.** "From the chair she couldn't hear the breath any more, so she got up. She had to bend down until her ear was over the mouth. She stayed there." (ch. 7). B only reports the cost.
- **Tension: A.** That passage sits just before the landing, so Lesca asks for Coralie's bed while her own breath is stopping.
- **Progression: A.** The cost climbs: a cold hand at noon, no breath at midnight, "She's the colour of this tape" on Friday (ch. 10).
- **Comedy: level, A by one smile.** Same scenes. I smiled at "Either is not a person. I cannot summon Either to the station", at "She's got a bar full of coffee", and at "And six. Bugger off." A adds "I've had the sandwich. I've told you those curtains want washing, twice, and you let me."
- **Characters: A.** Arlette: "You never made a sound, even as a baby. I used to get up to see."
- **The ending: level.** Chapters 13 to 16 are identical.
- **Clarity: B.** A made me reread twice: "If she stays the whole of Friday night, there may be no hand to come back to" (she has just survived a day and a night, which is longer), and Darrigrand's "You never had to." In both I reread the dealer's "Had the key and went for a walk, did you?" (ch. 16).

## 3. The first fifty pages

Same text in both (00 to 05), so level. I wanted to go on at the end of chapter 2: "'Come back across,' her mouth said. Maylis hadn't said it." (about page 16). I could not stop from chapter 3: "That village hadn't had its flood yet." (about page 23).

## 4. The night of the flood

Maylis's choice is the same words in both, and I believed it: "She wanted it not to be her." It is Either with a river in it.

The versions differ by one sentence at the handbrake. I believed A's Lesca more. When she holds the wheel against Inguma I knew what it cost, because I had watched her stop breathing. I believed B's brake more: "both of the same strength, and the one on the brake had got there first." A's "It was the weak one, and it held" is the better line, but it asks a hand that could not squeeze two fingers to beat a strong one.

A overall.

## 5. The last two chapters

Level, identical. One mark against A: it told me the right hand might not come back, and chapter 16 never says whether it did.

## 6. What B does better

1. Ch. 12: "It was one hand against the other, both of the same strength, and the one on the brake had got there first."
2. Ch. 7: "Maylis read it with her. Yesterday Bordenave had found a metre there, and his cellar had been dry." This is the clue behind "You're the water", and B makes Maylis the one who reads it.
3. Ch. 8: "Maylis took her hand off her mouth. She had the whole of herself back." B goes from the fight straight to Bayonne.

## 7. Against the comparison

**Below, narrowly.** The comedy and the rules (no untrue word said standing, the dead at bingo) are level with The Good Place. What decides it is fear in the middle. Across chapters 5 to 10 it moves between five people, and the man the plot is aimed at is dry and scornful until 3 a.m., so I never felt his danger. Crouch pins the fear to one body on every page.
