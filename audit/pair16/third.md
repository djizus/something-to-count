# Pair 16 — third

I read B in full first, then A. Thirteen of the seventeen files are word for word the same. The versions differ only in chapters 7, 8 and 10, and in one sentence of 12. In A I reread those four and not the rest. A's new beats were the only new thing on the page, so discount my liking for them a little.

## 1. Which one

A, slightly.

## 2. By criterion

- **Stakes: A.** Ch 8: 'If she stays the whole of Friday night, there may be no hand to come back to.' Friday gets a second thing to lose besides the bank.
- **Tension: A.** Ch 7, Arlette at 23:55: 'She had to bend down until her ear was over the mouth. She stayed there.' The next line is Lesca on the landing, asking for the bed.
- **Progression: A, just.** Lesca's body gets worse in three steps: warmer at noon, no breath to hear, 'the colour of this tape'.
- **Comedy: A, just.** 'I've had the sandwich. I've told you those curtains want washing, twice, and you let me.' I smiled. B has no funny line that A lacks.
- **Characters: A.** Ch 10: told to sit down on the edge of the stage, 'Lesca stayed standing.' That is the woman from the soup scene.
- **The ending: level.** Same text.
- **Clarity: B, just.** Ch 7: 'Yesterday Bordenave had found a metre there, and his cellar had been dry' makes the river clue plainer. In A I stopped once in ch 8: why is the right hand slow, when it is the one she always keeps? In both I read twice the part of ch 7 where the river is a metre, then seventy, then fifty-five.

## Chapters 5 to 10: was I afraid for anybody?

**B**

- **5.** Yes, for Maylis, of her being replaced. Lesca's foot on the stair, and 'I'd have this one again.'
- **6.** Yes, for Maylis, of her dying in her bed. The pulse from 64 to 36. The strongest fear of the stretch.
- **7.** For Jeannot, but only as a sum: twenty-five minutes against ten, and 'I'll be in my hut.' Not for Lesca. Her empty body is not on the page.
- **8.** A little, for the Port: the shoelace of water at Sarthou's step. 'A whole life with one hand' is a cost I understood and did not feel.
- **9.** For Jeannot, while the phone rang nine times. Then I smiled.
- **10.** No. I was moved, not afraid. Lesca says she won't be brought back, and she looks well.

**A** (5, 6 and 9 are the same text and the same fears)

- **7.** Yes, for Lesca, of her dying at home while she asks for somebody else's bed.
- **8.** Yes, for Maylis herself, of nothing of her being left to come back. The van key through her fingers kept it going.
- **10.** Yes, for Lesca. She holds on to the stone, she is grey, and she stays standing. 'I won't be brought back' now sounds like someone who knows.

After ch 6, B's fear is for Jeannot and the Port, on a date. A's is also for the two women, now.

## 3. The first fifty pages

Same text, same place, so level. It was the last line of ch 2: ''Come back across,' her mouth said. Maylis hadn't said it.' The end of ch 3 locked it, when she adds 'and Coralie Mounet in it' to the motion.

## 4. The night of the flood

Maylis's choice at the dip is the same text, and I believed it both times: 'She wanted it not to be her.'

The difference is Lesca's choice in the van, and I believed A's more. In A she arrives already spending her life, so holding the wheel against Inguma is a death I had watched coming. 'It was the weak one, and it held' is also better than B's 'both of the same strength, and the one on the brake had got there first', which explains. One cost: with a weak hand on the brake I half expected Lesca to prise it open, as she nearly did in the kitchen.

## 5. The last two chapters

Level. Same text in both.

## 6. What B does better

- Ch 7: 'Maylis read it with her. Yesterday Bordenave had found a metre there, and his cellar had been dry.'
- Ch 10: 'When she looked up once more at the top of the wardrobe, nothing was sitting on it.' Telling Coralie gets a result.
- Ch 8: B goes straight from the fight to Coralie's question. 'Coralie let go of the wrist. She tore the chart off the pad'. A puts the squeeze test in between. Keep the test, but shorter.

## 7. Against the comparison

Below both, and level with Crouch on the night itself, chapters 11 to 13.

The one thing: from ch 7 to ch 10 the danger is mostly a date in a file and a sum. Crouch would have somebody at risk on the page in every chapter. A's added beats go the right way, but the weak hand is shown twice in ch 8 and not again until ch 12. The bandaging in ch 9 is where it should show.
