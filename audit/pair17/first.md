# Pair 17: first

Read B in full, then A. They differ only in chapters 7, 8, 9 and the last page of 11.

## 1. Which one

**B, slightly.** Sure of the direction; the margin is small because thirteen of seventeen files are identical.

## 2. By criterion

- **Stakes: B.** "She got up by the leg of the table, coughed into the sink and looked. It was pink." (7). A has "the rib caught and held", a limit and not a threat.
- **Tension: B.** "'That's my door.' Coralie picked the slippers up and stood them on the radiator." (9). The river is at room twelve a night early.
- **Progression: level.** Same beats, same order. A plants the dry boards on the 4th; B's flood memory runs from the boot sole straight to the priest.
- **Comedy: B, by one smile.** "Bixente had stood the yellow sign in the middle of it all. *Caution. Wet floor.*" The rest I smiled at is in both: the oven door, "I cannot summon Either to the station".
- **Characters: B.** Madame Etcheto gets one more scene alive: "Water goes out by the door it came in at. Open it." Her death costs more.
- **The ending: level** on the page. B has earned it better (see 5).
- **Clarity: A, slightly.** In B I turned back once: pink in the sink on Thursday, then a two-kilometre walk on Saturday with no word about her chest. A never sent me back.

## 3. The first fifty pages

No difference: those pages are the same text. In both I could not stop from the last line of chapter 2, near page 14: "'Come back across,' her mouth said. Maylis hadn't said it." Chapter 3 locked it, near page 20: "That village hadn't had its flood yet."

## 4. The night of the flood

B's. The sentences at the handle and the dip are the same, but B changes what Maylis knows and has left.

- She knows the price. Coralie has told her "The yard came in under our back door tonight", and I have stood in that corridor. So driving past the handle is an evasion with the cost in view, and "She wanted it not to be her" reads true. In A the care home is a list and a distance, and the drive reads closer to a miscalculation.
- She has spent herself. In B she pushes at 2:51 knowing what a push does, as far as "Five. Her mouth tasted of coins." Taking the van is a body's choice as well as a character's.

One debt in B: the lung is never collected. "Something gave in her side. She heard it go" made me expect her to die in the corridor. Nobody looks at her mouth again.

## 5. The last two chapters

Same text. B ends better by inheritance. "I walked her back to her room on my arm ... That part is mine" answers a night when Coralie had already tried to move her and was refused. In A it answers a text message.

## 6. What the loser does better

From A, for B to take back:

1. "The river hadn't moved since Wednesday night. The boards of the platform were dry under her boots, for the first time since the rib."
2. "The brown water climbed the last bare centimetres, with sticks turning in it. Then it lay along the paint from end to end."
3. "Coralie waited in the doorway with the pillow under her arm. Then she crossed the landing." B hangs an eight-sentence case history on that doorway. The warning is needed, but not as her exit.
4. "Every December his widow stood beside it in a black coat and held her face still to be kissed."
5. "the way she had slept before the rib".

## 7. Against the comparison

**Below, narrowly.** The middle decides it. Crouch puts a body at risk on the page in front of you. Here, between the pulse chart in 6 and the van in 11, the danger is mostly a date. B repairs 7 and 9; 8 and 10 still run on a vote, a rehearsal and a marriage. The comedy is level with The Good Place, and chapters 12 to 16 beat most Crouch endings, because nothing is undone.
