# Pair 17 — second

## 1. Which one

B, slightly. They differ only in chapters 7, 8, 9 and three lines of 11.

## 2. By criterion

- **Stakes: B.** Ch. 7, the kitchen floor: "Her mouth tasted of coins. She got up by the leg of the table, coughed into the sink and looked. It was pink." In A pushing only hurts.
- **Tension: B.** Ch. 11, in the van: "Five. Her mouth tasted of coins." A has "the rib caught and held", which is a part failing.
- **Progression: B.** The water climbs in three steps: Sarthou's doorstep on Tuesday, under the back door at room twelve on Thursday, the breach on Friday. A has no middle step.
- **Comedy: B.** "A pad was made to hold a litre. Coralie had never seen one beaten." Then the *Caution. Wet floor.* sign standing in river, and "that boy's mopping it the wrong way." Three smiles in half a page.
- **Characters: B.** "She ran the tap until the sink was white again." She hides blood the way she hides wanting the duck.
- **The ending: level.** 14 to 16 are the same words.
- **Clarity: A.** Ch. 7: "The river hadn't moved since Wednesday night." In B I first read Bordenave's cross as the mayor being wrong, and went back a paragraph after "You're the water". One page read twice in both: the hand against hand in the van in 12.

## 3. The first fifty pages

Same place in both. The last line of ch. 2 made me turn the page: "'Come back across,' her mouth said. Maylis hadn't said it." I could not stop from ch. 3: "That village hadn't had its flood yet."

## 4. The night of the flood

B's. The choice at the dip is the same sentence in both: "She wanted it not to be her." I believed it more in B. She has just pushed in the van knowing what a push can do. So she will spend her own lung and still not put her hand to that handle. In A she runs out of breath, then out of time. In B she also walks away from the lamp having heard the night before: "The yard came in under our back door tonight."

Lesca's night in ch. 9 is better in B too: it stops on the boot and the priest.

## 5. The last two chapters

Level on the page; B lands a little harder. "That part is mine" weighs more when I have watched Coralie offer the dry bed in room three and take "That's my door" for an answer.

B also carries a debt into 16. The nurse who said "dead before the doctor had his coat off" never hears about the pink, and straps the rib for grave-digging. After "Something gave in her side. She heard it go", B owes me one line: blood, or a reason there is none.

## 6. What the loser does better

1. Ch. 7: "The river hadn't moved since Wednesday night. The boards of the platform were dry under her boots, for the first time since the rib."
2. Ch. 8: "The brown water climbed the last bare centimetres, with sticks turning in it. Then it lay along the paint from end to end."
3. Ch. 7: "Coralie waited in the doorway with the pillow under her arm. Then she crossed the landing." B puts ten sentences of case history between "you'll have to ask" and the pillow. There the warning reads as a plant.
4. Ch. 9: "Every December his widow stood beside it in a black coat and held her face still to be kissed."
5. Ch. 8: "I've tried for three nights to keep you out, and I can't. So I'm asking." B's "A third puts this rib through my lung" tells me what I saw in the sink.

## 7. Against the comparison

Below both. The one thing: fear for somebody's body comes late. Crouch has a person I think may die by page thirty. Here nobody is in danger until ch. 6, and the chapters around it run on who gets the pillow. B closes about half that gap. From 11 to 13 it is level with Crouch. The bingo of the dead is level with The Good Place, with fewer jokes to the page.

## Afraid, chapters 5 to 10

Same in both:

- **5.** For Maylis, of being replaced, not hurt: "I'd have this one again." For Jeannot: "I'm giving it one now."
- **6.** Yes, properly. For Maylis, of dying in her own bed while she sits in the wrong council room, the pulse going from 64 to 36. For Kévin, of prison.
- **10.** For Lesca, mildly: "I won't be brought back."

Different:

- **7.** A: nobody's body. I feared Coralie taking the fourth step to the wrong woman. B: that, and Maylis pushing a third night and bleeding, with Coralie upstairs and not told.
- **8.** A: for the Port in general, and of the card with her name. B: the same, and her lying still is now fear, not a rib that won't work.
- **9.** A: for Jeannot, until he swears at her in six points. B: for Madame Etcheto by name, from the slippers standing in water, and for Coralie, who will be at that door.
