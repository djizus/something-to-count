## 7. Against the comparison

**Slightly below Blake Crouch, level with The Good Place. Taken together: slightly below.**

The one thing that decides it is when I am first afraid for somebody. Crouch gives a named person a dated danger inside ten pages. Here it comes at the end of chapter 3 ("That village hadn't had its flood yet"), about 25 pages in. Before that, chapters 1 and 2 offer a nightmare, a puzzle and village business. The opening page has a flood, but it is another woman in another year and I could not yet connect it. A friend with my taste gets through on the comedy. One who wants Crouch's grip on page one may not.

From chapter 3 it holds with Crouch: a dated clock, and rules that cost something (the falling pulse in 6, the rib in 7). I could not put down 11 to 13. The ending is above him. Nothing is reset, forty-one is counted, and Coralie takes the pillow.

Against The Good Place it is level on what that show is loved for. The rules are invented with the same pleasure and every one is used: a room where nobody standing can say a thing she knows is untrue, a demon kept busy with counting, the dead at bingo. And it is a trolley problem whose heroine pays for not pulling the lever: "She wanted it not to be her."

Comedy: I smiled in most chapters up to 12. The window that goes down "a few centimetres". Coralie doing Larrère through the oven door. "It reads that she opened him." "And six. Bugger off." "Apologies received." The smiles are drier than the show's and fewer to the page, and the cast is less warm company.

Clarity: I read two passages twice, the blackmail chain that opens chapter 6 and the sum of minutes and metres at the dip in 13.
