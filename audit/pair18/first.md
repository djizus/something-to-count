# Pair 18: first

Read A, then B. Pages are counted at 300 words. From the steel room on the flood night to the last line the two texts are identical. I read B knowing A, so I could not unknow what B holds back.

## 1. Which one

**A, slightly.**

## 2. By criterion

- **Stakes: A.** "Under hers was a date. That she could read: 12 DECEMBER." By p. 3 the stake has her name and a day on it. B keeps the stone and drops the date.
- **Tension: A.** "Maylis stood on his trapdoor in the rain Lesca had brought. She couldn't step off it until Lesca did." I walk the track knowing that the woman timing the rescue is the flood. In B the walk is a measurement.
- **Progression: B.** That Lesca brings the river lands once, in three words at the bar, and is proved once, on Coralie's chart. A says it three times.
- **Comedy: A.** "'She wants water,' one of the Lassalle sisters said. 'She's got a bar full of coffee,' said her sister." I smiled because I knew what the three words meant and the hall did not. In B the reader is as lost as the hall.
- **Characters: level.** A's Maylis works out the water herself, from Bordenave's cross. B's Lesca gets the best new line in either: "Her own Castagnet hadn't lived long enough to go grey."
- **The ending: level.** Same text. A has one echo B cannot have: the date Maylis feared on p. 3 comes back in Larrère's handwriting.
- **Clarity: A, narrowly.** A made me read the river sum twice. B left two holes: "She had nobody to say it to", with no "it" on the page, and on the flood night nothing says why she doesn't give Inguma something to count.

## 3. The first fifty pages

First fear, in both, is on p. 1: for the forty houses asleep behind the bank, of the bank going ("Ten more minutes, she thought"). On p. 2 it is for whoever is at the end of the track.

First fear for someone with a name: in A, p. 3, for Maylis, of dying on 12 December, eighteen days off. In B, p. 3 has her name on a stone and no date, so I was uneasy with nothing to count. B's first dated fear is on p. 20, for Jeannot, of drowning in his hut on the 11th.

Could not stop: A at p. 3, held by "Seventeen days" under the comedy of the car window and the eggs. B at p. 14, "'Come back across,' her mouth said." A's clock is a false one, and it lets go cleanly: "It isn't a grave. It's for a night" (p. 26) comes after the real date has taken over on p. 21. I did not feel cheated.

## 4. The night of the flood

The night is the same text. What differs is how each woman reaches it. I believed A's.

Lesca: A lets me watch her decide. She lies on her side as her mother told her, Coralie's lamp stays on, and "Then she straightened her legs and turned onto her back." B tells me she "still meant to go", and why.

Maylis: in A she has pushed, slept on the kitchen floor and tried the words ("it came straight across the floor"). The van is all she has left, so "She wanted it not to be her" is a woman who has run out of ways not to choose. In B she never tries the words, and in the van I was asking why not.

## 5. The last two chapters

Level. They are identical, and they work in both: Maïté's verb, "Surname first", "I was the other one", the second shovel.

## 6. What B does better

1. "'The thing that sits on you doesn't need you asleep,' Kévin said. 'I was wide awake, both times.'" Her vigil is lost before it starts.
2. "His hair had gone grey. Her own Castagnet hadn't lived long enough to go grey."
3. "What had come out was that she was going over to stay, in a house where nobody knew about 2:51." Keep it, then end on A's turn onto her back.
4. "Friday the 11th is the last page of her file. That's the night the bank goes, at the patch."
5. "Yesterday Bordenave had found a metre there, and his cellar had been dry." One line where A has a paragraph of arithmetic. A should keep its conclusion and take this.

## 7. Against the comparison

**Below both by a little, and level in the last third.**

The one thing is the number of rules. The van scene needs the chair, the owner's right hand, the count, the trip home and the hall of the dead, all at once. Dark Matter runs on one box. The Good Place changes its rules, but each change comes as a joke. Here I worked to keep them straight from chapter 4 to chapter 9.

What it has that neither has: the care home corridor from 3:11 to 4:16, and a trolley problem Chidi would know, set twice with no right answer. On comedy it passes: I smiled often ("It reads that she opened him", "Either is not a person", "And six. Bugger off.").
