# Pair 18 — second

Read B first, then A. Pages are 250 words: A runs 175, B 173. Most of the text is shared word for word, including everything from the phone call at 2:53 to the last line. They differ in five places.

## 1. Which one

A, slightly.

## 2. By criterion

- **Stakes: A.** p.4: "Under hers was a date. That she could read: 12 DECEMBER." Then: "She counted the days. Eighteen." B's stone has a name and no day.
- **Tension: A.** Chapter 7 stops on Jeannot's trapdoor (p.72): "She couldn't step off it until Lesca did." B walks straight back up the track.
- **Progression: A.** p.90, Maylis tries the words and they fail: "The count tells it when to take the visitor home. It doesn't keep it off." In B I kept asking why she didn't give it the roadsides on Friday.
- **Comedy: level.** Same jokes, and I smiled at the same ones: the oven door, "I cannot summon Either to the station", "And six. Bugger off." One edge to A: at the bingo I smiled at "She wants water" on the line, because I already knew what the three words meant.
- **Characters: level.** A shows Lesca choose; B tells me why, in the best paragraph only one version has (see 6).
- **The ending: level.** Identical text.
- **Clarity: A.** One page read twice, in B: p.69, "She had nobody to say it to and no mouth to say it with." I didn't know what "it" was until "You're the water" on p.74, and I went back. A does the sum on the bank (p.70): "The rain came when Lesca came."

## 3. The first fifty pages

They end on the same line in both, "Are you coming up?" Only the date on the stone differs.

**First afraid for somebody.**
A: p.4, for Maylis, that she would be dead on 12 December. I read A second and knew what the date was, so what I felt was her fear.
B: p.17, for Maylis, when her mouth speaks without her. I was afraid she was being pushed out of her own bed. Nobody's death frightened me in B until p.24: Jeannot, in his cellar, thirteen days off.

**Could not stop.**
A: p.17, the end of chapter 2, with fifteen days already on the clock.
B: p.24, "That village hadn't had its flood yet." In A (p.25) that line explains a count I was already keeping.

One seam in A: the date never appears on the stone again (pp.21, 55).

## 4. The night of the flood

The night is the same text in both. I believed A's, because of what each woman carries into it.

Lesca. In A she lies on her side as her mother told her, watches Coralie's lamp for an hour, and then (p.109) "straightened her legs and turned onto her back." She had a way to stay home and I watched her not take it. So "I'm letting go" in the van undoes a choice I saw. In B I never see her lie down.

Maylis. In A she has used up pushing and the words, so sitting up in the van is the last thing she has. In B Kévin tells her beforehand that it doesn't need her asleep. She doesn't answer him, and sits there anyway.

Her choice at the dip, "She wanted it not to be her", I believed equally in both.

## 5. The last two chapters

Same words. A ends better only through what it set up. "Then it wasn't Inguma that came for her. It was you." / "Yes." weighs more when I have seen her turn onto her back.

## 6. What the loser does better

1. Lesca on her bed, Friday night (B p.107): "None of that had got her ready for the back of his neck. He had mud drying on his boots. His hair had gone grey. Her own Castagnet hadn't lived long enough to go grey." It belongs in A, before she lies down.
2. Same place: "What had come out was that she was going over to stay, in a house where nobody knew about 2:51." That is why she turns onto her back with this Coralie's door open. A leaves me to guess.
3. Kévin at the gate (B p.120): "The thing that sits on you doesn't need you asleep. I was wide awake, both times." Good dread, if Maylis answers it.
4. Maylis to Coralie in the kitchen (B p.80): "Friday the 11th is the last page of her file. That's the night the bank goes, at the patch." A halves that answer.

## 7. Against the comparison

Below, narrowly.

The flood night (A pp.131–150) is level with Crouch. The trolley problem is answered more honestly than The Good Place answers its own: she goes to the man who was in no danger, "so that it wouldn't be me."

What decides it is the number of rules. Dark Matter has one: the box opens on every choice. This book has the chair, the right hand, the count, no lying on your feet, the body at home going cold, the hall of the dead, and the water that follows the visitor. A has an eighth on p.90. A reader of those two wants the rules finished by page 60, and only consequences after.
