## 7. Against the comparison

**Below, narrowly.** I would still hand A to the friend, with one warning: give it until page 20.

**The one thing that decides it: for the first half, the person who wants something and goes after it is the visitor.** A Crouch lead wants one thing by page 10 and runs at it, and the running is why you can't stop. Here that is Lesca. She crosses, drives the body, gives the mayor three minutes, goes up the stairs. The chapters she drives (3, 5, 13) move at Crouch speed. Maylis's acts until then are refusals: she holds the stair post, she shuts her teeth. She first says what she wants on about page 76 ("Then I want both") and first acts on it on page 82, the 5 a.m. call. Before that the clock is a date on a pad, and I waited for it more than I feared it. That is the book's subject, and the ending pays for it in full. It still costs the middle its pull.

What comes with it is the rules. I counted nine: lie still, stay sitting, the right hand, the count, no lies standing up, the empty body weakens, the visitor brings the rain, the seat among the dead, 2:51. One is still being corrected in chapter 9. Crouch gives one rule and its consequences. I read five pages twice: the opening of 3 (who "Lesca" is), Larrère's speech in 6, the steel room in 9, Lesca landing in the hall in 13, and "when they called twelve, somebody sat down" in 16.

Where it is level or above:

- **The two turns.** "That village hadn't had its flood yet" (page 20) and "The rain came when Lesca came" (chapter 7). The second is as good as Crouch's.
- **The climax.** Chapters 12 to 14 are level with him: "Either" on the platform, the van in the dip, "She wanted it not to be her", sixteen minutes instead of ten, the door of twelve.
- **The moral question.** The Good Place played the trolley problem for a joke. This plays it straight, twice, and makes both answers cost somebody. Above, on that point. "2:51. I've known that for twelve years" is the best scene in the book.
- **The ending.** Harder than either comparison would dare, and it holds. Jeannot stepping over the string is the right last picture.

**Comedy.** I smiled in 14 of the 18 files. The surest: the window that goes "all the way down, into the door"; "It takes a small m"; Coralie doing Larrère through the oven door; "Staff to wait. Staff not to join in"; "It reads that she opened him"; the hall hearing "You're the water" as thirst; Jeannot's six points; "apologies received" at the table of the dead; the deputy turning the sheet over to see if there was more on the back. Josiane's husband-or-wife line is told three times and I smiled once. By that measure it is level with The Good Place for the warmth of its people and below it for how often.
