# Pair 19: first

Read B first, then A. Outside chapters 1, 2 and 5 the two are the same text word for word, and the three chapters differ by under 200 words. Pages are counted at 250 words.

## 1. Which one

B, slightly.

## 2. By criterion

- **Stakes:** level. Both are decided at the dip in 14: "If she turned that handle, Jeannot drowned and her hand had done it."
- **Tension:** A, by a hair. Its stair in 5 goes straight from "Maylis knew what Lesca wanted." to "Lesca put the left foot on the first stair."
- **Progression:** B. In 2 she goes looking: "She wanted to know how he had got out from under it." "Maylis wanted to hear what was said to it." In A, Kévin turns up unasked and Madame Etcheto is overheard on a lift home. Two lucky meetings in one chapter hand her the two things she needs.
- **Comedy:** level, same lines. I smiled at "In my tray," said to the oven; "Staff to wait. Staff not to join in."; "It reads that she opened him."; "And six. Bugger off." B has one more: "She put it with the rest of her work and told nobody."
- **Characters:** B. Maylis hunts and still can't ask. Her "Either" to Inguma is funnier once she has driven to the Port for the words.
- **The ending:** level, same text.
- **Clarity:** B, slightly, for "Five, so she wasn't dreaming." I knew the steel room was real a chapter early. Read twice in both: the rain sum in 7 (seventy, a metre, fifty-five, forty-five) and the two-handed vote in 6. B alone tripped me once: "whose stone it was", when her name is cut on it.

## 3. The first fifty pages

The same line in both: "That village hadn't had its flood yet." (3, p.25). After it I did not stop. B had me pulling towards it from p.5. A's chapter 2 read as errands until Madame Etcheto says "That's Inguma" on p.15. I read A second, so it had no surprise left; weigh that.

## What Maylis wants, first forty pages (to the end of 4)

- **Both:** Coralie's fingers kept on her wrist, the duck, and to be no trouble to anyone. I knew it on p.3: "What she wanted was for Coralie to stay awake and keep her fingers on that wrist... so Maylis didn't ask." The first want she says out loud is on p.32, in both: "This morning at 6. I want that first."
- **B:** on top of that, to find out what the stone is and who is under the thing with her, before the 12th. I knew it on p.5: "That was how long she had to find out whose stone it was."
- **A:** the same thing, but I first knew she was after it on p.17: "she wanted to know who." From p.5 to p.17 she counts days and is handed the answers.

## 4. The night of the flood

Same text. I believed it in both: she drives past the handle for "Both", and at the dip "She wanted it not to be her." It is Either with a river in it, and Coralie says so. B a shade more, because by then it has shown a woman who will walk a long way for an answer and will not sign one. One strain in both: at 3 she hauls him out by both collars; at dusk she knocked three times and walked home.

## 5. The last two chapters

Level, same text. "Lesca, Maylis. Surname first." and Jeannot stepping over the string both land. In B, "I want to dig the grave myself" closes something opened on p.5: her wants come out as jobs.

## 6. What the loser does better

A is tighter wherever B explains. B should take back:

1. Chapter 5, first line: "Maylis's mouth spoke without her." B's "let" plus "She had chosen that at 2:51, lying still under the weight" repeats the end of 4, ten lines later.
2. Chapter 5, the stair: "The eyes went up the stairs to Coralie and stayed on her. Maylis knew what Lesca wanted." B adds "It was going to cost her Coralie." The hand on the post says it.
3. Chapter 1: "It was 24 November. She counted the days. Eighteen." Stop there, or fix "whose stone".
4. Chapter 2: "She cut the envelope open with her pocket knife and pulled out the card." B says which lamp three times in four lines.
5. Chapter 2: "Nobody had asked him to come." The same sentence returns at the sand bin in 12. B keeps only the second.

## 7. Against the comparison

Below, by a little. Chapters 12 to 14 are as hard to put down as Crouch, and the rules meeting in 4 ("in a tie the chair decides." "There's nobody in the chair.") is Good Place comedy about a Good Place question. What decides it is the steel room. The box with two lamps is the most Crouch thing in the book, and it stays a doorway. Nobody comes back from the university, why the rain follows her is left at "I don't know why it comes", and the card rides in her chest pocket to the last page without being used.
