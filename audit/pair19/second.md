# Pair 19, reader: second

Fifteen of the eighteen files are word for word the same. B differs in chapters 1, 2 and 5 only, by about 250 words, all about why Maylis does what she does. I read A first, so B's additions reached me as repeats. Discount my complaints about them a little.

## 1. Which one

B, slightly.

## 2. By criterion

- **Stakes: B.** "That was how long she had to find out whose stone it was. She put it with the rest of her work and told nobody." A's "Eighteen." is dread. B's is a deadline.
- **Tension: A.** "Maylis's mouth spoke without her." B's "Maylis let her mouth speak" takes the jolt out of the best chapter opening in the book.
- **Progression: B.** Wednesday in B is a search: she stops at the Labats' door, she drives down to hear room twelve. In A, Kévin turns up unasked with the way in, and that night Coralie happens to be outside the right door.
- **Comedy: A, barely.** "Inside, an old woman was speaking slowly to somebody." In B I had already been told she talks to her wardrobe. The jokes ("Staff not to join in") are untouched and I smiled both times.
- **Characters: B.** "which gave her a reason to stop at the Labats' door" is Maylis exactly: she cannot just ask, she needs the Christmas lights as cover.
- **The ending: level.** Same text.
- **Clarity: level.** I read the same pages twice in both: the river sum in 7 (a metre, seventy, fifty-five) and the correction in 9 that the count sends the visitor home but does not keep Inguma off. B removes one doubt ("Five, so she wasn't dreaming") and adds two: "whose stone it was" when the stone has her own name on it, and "That left the barn", which made me think she was driving there.

## 3. What Maylis wants in the first forty pages

**A.** Page 3: Coralie's fingers kept on her wrist, and not to have to ask. She counts the days to the stone but does nothing about them. First plot want, page 17, last lines of 2: "she wanted to know who." First goal, page 38: "She wanted that gate opened on Friday afternoon as much as Lesca did", and she hands it to Lesca in the same breath. Through page 40 the woman who wants things is Lesca.

**B.** Same private want on page 3. On page 4 she wants to know what the stone means before the 12th, alone. I knew it at that line, and every scene of 2 then follows from it. By page 39 she has chosen to let Lesca speak.

## 4. The first fifty pages

Same place in both, page 24: "That village hadn't had its flood yet." B gets there a third of a page later. But in B I was being pulled from page 4. In A I was carried by the creature and the village until the end of 2.

## 5. The night of the flood

Same text, and I believed it in both. "She wanted it not to be her" is the truest line in the book, and "That was Either, with the river in it" is the right verdict on it. B adds one distant set-up in 5, "She had lain still for the village's sake. It was going to cost her Coralie." It rhymes with the dip, and it is the book explaining itself.

## 6. The last two chapters

Level, same text. They end well: Lesca giving her own name surname first, and Jeannot stepping over the string.

## 7. What the loser does better

Passages from A that B should take back:

1. "Maylis's mouth spoke without her." And drop B's next addition, "She had chosen that at 2:51, lying still under the weight: she would rather the village was told by the one who signed things." The last page of 4 says it in nearly the same words.
2. "Maylis knew what Lesca wanted." Alone. The stair post does the rest.
3. "Kévin Labat was waiting at the door of the village shed when she came to unlock it. Nobody had asked him to come." Chapter 12 repeats the second sentence at the sand bin. B keeps only that one, so the echo is gone.
4. "Inside, an old woman was speaking slowly to somebody."
5. "She cut the envelope open with her pocket knife and pulled out the card." B says the lamp matched before and after the card.

## 8. Against the comparison

Below both, not by much. The one thing: the rules. Crouch gives a rule by hitting the hero with it, and she uses it on the next page. Here there are six (the right hand, no lies standing up, the count, the cold body, the rain, the one night), most delivered in meetings, one amended later. The middle asks to be studied where Crouch asks to be raced. The comedy is level with The Good Place at its best: "The mayor of Peyrebarthe sends her apologies" to the hall of the dead is as good as anything in it. The ending is harder than either would dare.
