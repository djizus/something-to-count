## 7. Against the comparison

**Slightly below both. I would still hand it to the friend.**

The one thing that decides it: for the first third the engine is Lesca's, not Maylis's. A Crouch hero wants something by page 10 and runs at it. Eleanor wants not to be found out. Maylis wants not to be the one who asks or decides. That is the book's subject, and it pays in full at the dip ("She wanted it not to be her"). But until chapter 10 the person pulling me forward is the visitor.

### What Maylis wants in the first forty pages

Pages at 275 words. Page 40 is Larrère's window going all the way down, in chapter 5.

- **Page 3, chapter 1. I knew it here.** "What she wanted was for Coralie to stay awake and keep her fingers on that wrist. But Coralie had to be up before 6, so Maylis didn't ask." She wants Coralie, and she wants to cost nobody anything. The duck and *Either* confirm it on page 5.
- **Page 4.** A task: eighteen days to find out whose stone it is. It is a puzzle, not a want, and Lesca solves it for her on page 29.
- **Page 32.** "She wanted that gate opened on Friday afternoon as much as Lesca did. What scared her was Monday." Her first want with a date on it, and it is borrowed.
- **Page 45, the stair post.** The first time she fights for something. She became the lead for me there, five pages after the forty.

So it is clear from page 3 and never in doubt, but it is a want to avoid. Lesca's is written down on page 26, in her motion, and it is the stronger of the two.

**Could not stop** from page 23: "That village hadn't had its flood yet." Crouch gets there about as fast.

### Against Crouch

Level on the hook, the clock and the climax. Below on rules. Crouch gives one rule and raises its price. Here I counted about ten: lie still, stay sitting, the right hand, the count, no lies standing up, shared memory, the rain, 2:51, the empty body dying, the hall. Three are revised mid-book. Each is clean alone. Together they cost me chapter 9.

### Against The Good Place

Level on the moral problem. It is the trolley problem with a village on the rails, and it refuses the comfortable answer. Maylis saves the man and loses room twelve, and nobody tells her she was right. Jeannot's letter 42 "doesn't thank you". Below on the size of the ensemble's warmth: Coralie carries nearly all of it.

Comedy: I smiled often and never laughed, which is the right setting for this book. Where I smiled:

- "It reads that she opened him."
- "Either is not a person. I cannot summon Either to the station."
- "It takes a small m."
- "Then it should have gone last Monday."
- Larrère chairing the association from his car.

One that did not land: the bread-and-water gloss after "You're the water". It explains a joke the Lassalle sisters had already made.
