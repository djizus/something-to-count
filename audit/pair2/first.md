# Pair 2 — first

Read B first, then A, so A's surprises were not fresh. Chapters 0, 1, 2, 6 and 14 are word for word the same.

## 1. Which one

**B, clearly.**

## 2. By criterion

- **Stakes — B.** The flood file in ch. 3: "*Body of CASTAGNET Jean recovered at first light from the cellar of his blind. Watch stopped at 03:11.*" A only says the file "was typed, by the gendarmes".
- **Tension — B.** Ch. 7: Maylis keeps Lesca out for two nights and the gauge stops at a metre; then the rib gives and the river is "back on the file, to the centimetre". A does it in two sentences, and "the river gauge kept to the file anyway".
- **Progression — B.** Those two nights cost her the vote: "Her mayor said seventy under for Friday the fourth, and it read a metre." In A her pushing changes nothing.
- **Comedy — A, slightly.** Ch. 3: "The timer rang while he was in the middle of the word hydraulic. He finished the word".
- **Characters — B.** Lesca comes on Friday knowing what she brings: "I can't stand here and say I won't." A's Lesca carries a flood nobody could stop, so she is less to blame and less interesting.
- **The ending — B.** "'Don't come again.' 'I won't.' It came out whole, and they both heard it."
- **Clarity — B, slightly.** The only page I read twice was in A: the block paragraph in ch. 3 that runs envelope, chair, card and gauge together. In B I re-read two sentences: "So that's what it fetches me home for" (ch. 8) and "She wasn't hoping" (ch. 11). B's ch. 3 also brings the kitchen timer on without stating the rule.

## 3. The first fifty pages

At about 275 words a page, fifty pages ends with ch. 5. The end of ch. 2 turned the page in both; same text.

- **B: about page 22**, the stopped watch in the file, then "the eleventh fell on a Friday, two weeks away". I had a body and a date.
- **A: about page 32**, ch. 4: "'You got my rib,' Maylis said. 'And I get your river.'" I had met the line in B, so discount me a little. But A spends its best secret there and leaves nothing to find out about the flood for a hundred pages. B says "I don't know why it comes" and makes me wait.

## 4. The night of the flood

**B.** Ch. 13 is nearly identical; what I believed was how she got there.

At five on Friday the man is on the bank in daylight and her hand is on the handle. In A she knows the river is coming whatever she does, and still says "Either". I didn't believe it. In B she has a reason: "If it didn't get her into that chair before four, nobody crossed tonight, and the bank held." So she sits up counting her fingers, and Inguma lands on the roof "an hour and nine minutes short of four". A plan fails, not only her nerve.

Also, A's Maylis tells Lesca "I'll drive down for him myself", so "Go to the blind first" tells her nothing. In B the five words do push her, and Lesca answers for them in ch. 16.

## 5. The last two chapters

**B.** Ch. 15 is a draw (see 6.5). Ch. 16 decides it. A's Lesca says "I brought it", and Inguma walks out for no reason I could name. B's says "it was my own night I was putting right", promises not to come, the room lets her say it, and then Inguma leaves. The rule that only true things can be said in there finally does a job.

## 6. What A does better

1. Ch. 3: "The rule applied to every member and had been needed for one." And: "'A gate is a door in a bank, Madame le maire. It is not a tap.'"
2. Ch. 3: "'It's soup. It doesn't need a vote.' 'It isn't getting one.'"
3. Ch. 8: "It's a whole life with one hand. You eat at my table, and I tip the spoon. […] I won't pay that for a gate. One night."
4. Ch. 12: "The hand locked on the brake was the hand that had held the stair post against her". B swaps it for an explanation.
5. Ch. 15, shorter: "'Yes. She'd walked back to it to say her words.' 'Then it wasn't Inguma that came for her. It was you.'" B's two extra sentences make Lesca pass the blame before the old woman speaks.

## 7. Against the comparison

**Below, not by much.** Chapters 11 to 13 are level with Crouch; I read the flood night without looking up. The comedy (Larrère through the oven door, "Eggs is what Either gets in this house") is warm, quieter than The Good Place, and gone by ch. 13.

What decides it is the load. Two villages, over twenty named people, five rules for the chair, and a dozen objects that each come back. Every one pays off. But Crouch gives me one rule and The Good Place six people, and both let me read fast. This one made me read carefully.
