# Pair 2 — second

Read A in full, then B.

## 1. Which one

**B, clearly.** Most of the text is shared, but B changes the one thing that matters: the flood can be held off, and we watch it not be.

## 2. By criterion

- **Stakes: B.** Ch. 7, after Maylis keeps Lesca out two nights: "The water hadn't moved since Wednesday night, and it hadn't rained since then either." The flood is no longer weather, it is the visit. In A she pushes two nights and "the river gauge kept to the file anyway", so nothing she does counts until the last night.
- **Tension: B.** Ch. 11, in the van: "If it didn't get her into that chair before four, nobody crossed tonight, and the bank held." A only has her hoping Inguma "wouldn't find her under it".
- **Progression: B.** The cross on Lafitte's pad ("That's her first") comes back as Jeannot's third point at the vote: "So I go by the gauge." A's middle runs in a straight line; B's has a reversal that costs her.
- **Comedy: A.** Ch. 3, Larrère explaining sluice gates to the woman who opened one: "It is not a tap. You don't turn it on because the sink's full," and the timer ringing "in the middle of the word hydraulic". B cuts it.
- **Characters: level.** A has Maylis's best speech (ch. 8, "a whole life with one hand"). B has her on the kitchen floor pushing till four so that she can ask Coralie back.
- **The ending: B.** "'I won't.' It came out whole, and they both heard it."
- **Clarity: B.** In A I read two things twice: the Thursday paragraph of ch. 3 (card, chair, envelope and gauge in one block) and the "point one again" answer in ch. 4. In B, no page twice.

## 3. The first fifty pages

B, by about six pages. Both hook on the same last line of ch. 2. The point of not stopping, in B, is the file in ch. 3, about page 22: "Body of CASTAGNET Jean recovered at first light from the cellar of his blind. Watch stopped at 03:11." In A it is the motion that ends ch. 3, about page 28, after a longer council scene. B then starts a clock in ch. 4 ("your rib will tell you how many nights you've got"); A hands over the explanation there and leaves nobody anything to do about it.

## 4. The night of the flood

The three o'clock pages are nearly identical. The set-up differs, and I believed B's.

- At five on the platform, A's "Either" is nerve failing with the answer already known. B gives it a reason: "If the gate went up now and Lesca never came tonight, the flood on the meadows was Maylis's alone." She has seen the river stop once.
- So "She wanted it not to be her" at the dip is the same woman making the same mistake a second time, at a higher price. In A it arrives new.
- Jeannot staying under his hatch is easier to accept when her number has already been wrong once.
- Lesca letting go of the chair: B says why ("that woman had just said it"). A leaves it to the stair post and the reflector, and I was guessing.

## 5. The last two chapters

B. Ch. 15 differs by one line: B's Lesca hedges about the eight rings, and Madame Etcheto goes straight through it ("It was you"). In ch. 16, B's Lesca owns "Go to the blind first", and her promise closes the rule opened in ch. 4, so I know the river is finished. A stops at "She took the pillow" and leaves that open. The grave is the same in both, and it is the right last page.

## 6. What A does better

1. Ch. 3: "The rule applied to every member and had been needed for one." Then the tap and "hydraulic". B keeps the timer and loses the reason for it.
2. Ch. 3: "'It's soup. It doesn't need a vote.' / 'It isn't getting one.'"
3. Ch. 8: "It's a whole life with one hand. You eat at my table, and I tip the spoon. You answer Coralie, and I've got the wrist she holds for my pulse, to pull away with. You write my letters, and I cross a t. I won't pay that for a gate. One night."
4. Ch. 12, the pause in the middle of the words: "'Inguma, enauk hire beldur.' / Lesca waited for the other word, the one this mouth put after everything. It didn't come." B moves it after the spell and explains it.
5. Ch. 3, Maïté at the ladder: "I had his waders drying by the stove every evening after that."

## 7. Against the comparison

Below both, not by much. Ch. 13 is level with a Crouch set piece. What decides it is ease. Crouch and The Good Place can be taken at speed by a tired reader. This asks me to keep count of whose hand, whose jaw, a pad, a timer, a folder, a file and some twenty named villagers, and the laughs come as slow smiles, not one a page. B lowers that load. It does not remove it.
