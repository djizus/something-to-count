# Pair 20: first

Read A in full, then B in full. They differ in five places, all on one thread: the steel room and the two cards.

## 1. Which one

**A, slightly.** On those five passages I prefer A clearly. They come to about 110 words in a book of 44,000, so for a friend the gap is small.

## 2. By criterion

- **Stakes: A, by a hair.** Ch 9, A only: "What one of us has in her head, the other gets, through that chair. … You're getting my flood." It turns Friday at 2:51 into a rule. B has "It comes with me" and no reason.
- **Tension: level.** The ring count in the van, ch 12: "Five. Her mouth tasted of coins."
- **Progression: level.** Ch 7: "The rain came when Lesca came."
- **Comedy: A, slightly.** Ch 4, A only: "'The left,' Maylis said. … 'You're looking at her.'" I smiled. The rest of what made me smile is in both: "Either is not a person. I cannot summon Either to the station"; "It reads that she opened him"; the oven door.
- **Characters: level.** Coralie, ch 9: "And then there's me, as a distance."
- **The ending: A.** "Maylis put the two cards in together. … One lamp went out. Then the other."
- **Clarity: A.** Read twice in both: the centimetre sum in ch 7 (a metre, seventy, fifty-five). In B only: the last page, where "with the card from the steel room" sent me back to ch 2 to find out what the card was. In A only, half a beat: in ch 17 she is in bed, so how is the card in her hand in the steel room?

## 3. The first fifty pages

The same place in both: "That village hadn't had its flood yet" (ch 3, about page 22). The first pull is the end of ch 2, "'Come back across,' her mouth said" (about page 16). In A the premise clicks a page before, at "Somewhere was Peyrebarthe", which answers ch 1's "Maylis asked where somewhere was."

## 4. The night of the flood

Level. Chapters 12 to 14 are identical, and I believed the choice in both. Maylis doesn't pick Jeannot over the Port. She picks not being the hand: "If she went on, whatever the river did behind her was the river's doing. She wanted it not to be her." Sixteen years of Either pay for that, and the "Either" on the platform at 5, in daylight, makes it worse in the right way.

## 5. The last two chapters

Ch 16 is identical. Ch 17: **A.** "Don't come again." / "I won't." closes the two women in both. Only A closes the room.

**What the steel room and the cards are for, by the last page.**

*In A.* The steel room is where one woman became two in June 2014, one lamp each, and its chair is the way across: what is in one head reaches the other, the flood included. The cards are each woman's own record of her lamp, in the same handwriting. On the last page both go into one envelope, the lamps go out and the door shuts. The pocket that held the card now holds letter 42.

*In B.* The steel room is where they meet, where the visitor stays sitting, and where a woman on her feet can't lie. That it is where they split I get only from ch 1 and 2 ("to see whether there were two of you") and have to infer. Lesca never says which lamp she saw. *left* rides in Maylis's pocket to the grave. *right* is seen once in ch 2 and never again. By the last page I can't say what the cards are for. Nothing puts the lamps out, which sits oddly beside "I won't."

## 6. What the loser does better

1. Ch 3, the join: "Lesca lay in the middle of the bed with her left hand closed, as if those fingers were still in it. / So she could cross." In B the crossing lands on Coralie's fingers. A puts the lamp between them, and should move its lamp sentence above the hand.
2. Ch 9: "'Now I know. It comes with me. Keeping me out for a night or two changes nothing…'" B is right to lose "I got your rib that way", which ch 4 already said. A needs only "You're getting my flood."
3. Ch 17: "She took the letter and buttoned it into her chest pocket, with the card from the steel room." As a sentence it beats A's "The card from the steel room wasn't in there any more", which tells me what I saw a page ago. A could stop at the pocket.

## 7. Against the comparison

**Below, narrowly.** The flood night is level with a Crouch set piece, and the oven door and the kitchen timer are level with The Good Place. What decides it is the rules. Both comparisons state their rule early and loudly, then break it. Here the crossing has five, arriving one at a time up to ch 9, and I kept accounts when I should have been afraid. A says two of them out loud. B takes those two away.
