# Pair 21 — first

Read B first, cold, then A. Chapters 0–4 and 11–17 are word for word the same in both, so everything turns on 5–10, where A runs about 2,000 words longer. A was my second pass, so its explanations were bound to look more redundant than they would cold.

## 1. Which one

**B, slightly.**

## 2. By criterion

- **Stakes: level.** A shows the Port filling ("It had come up a step a day since"). B gets there with the shoelace of water at Sarthou's step.
- **Tension: B.** Ch. 7: "Those were the two days without rain. […] The rain came when Lesca came." One paragraph and a line. A works the sum over five paragraphs and I was ahead of it.
- **Progression: B.** Ch. 6: A sends Maylis across to read the flood file, which ch. 3 already showed me and ch. 10 gives in full. B sends her for one reason: "She wanted one day as the woman who signed things."
- **Comedy: A.** More smiles: Sarthou's "the village will receive a bill for my labour", "in the wrong kitchen". B's shared jokes land cleaner ("Larrère was both. 'Either,' she said."), but A has more of them.
- **Characters: A.** Lesca's Coralie is alive in the middle of A (the soup at 6). In B she is offstage from ch. 3 to ch. 11.
- **The ending: level.** Same text.
- **Clarity: level.** No page read twice in either. B: two lines, once, the "Ei—" at Larrère's window, where I did not know who was trying to say the word or why. A: never lost, often told twice.

## 3. The first fifty pages

Level, same text. Hooked at the end of ch. 2 ("Maylis hadn't said it"). Could not stop from ch. 3, "That village hadn't had its flood yet", about page 28. Fifty pages ends with ch. 5, the only part that differs: B opens moving, A stops to give the hour, the weather and what ch. 4 had just said.

## 4. The night of the flood

The night is the same words. The run-up differs, and I believed B's, slightly. The choice hangs on "She wanted it not to be her." B prepares that with one undiluted want, a day as the woman who signs, which fails her at the dip. A's Maylis has read the file like a job sheet and done the sum twice ("It came out the same both times"), so her "Both" reads as ignoring her own arithmetic, not as hope. A's one real gain here is "That's not a no."

## 5. The last two chapters

Level, same text. A's 16 lands a hair better: "Good night, Maylis" has the wrong-kitchen soup behind it.

## 6. What A does better

1. Ch. 5: "One Either, and Lesca could pass for her until noon, when the café would be full." It makes "Ei—" a plan that fails, and fixes B's only snag.
2. Ch. 6: "'Do you know what I'd give for a woman at this table who sat down when she was told? I'd carry my pillow back across that landing tonight.' For sixteen years Maylis had sat down and said nothing, in the wrong kitchen." Through to "'It's good soup,' Maylis said." The one cut I would reverse, though it interrupts the pulse countdown.
3. Ch. 6: "At home she got him a few centimetres at a time."
4. Ch. 9: "'When it was you, did you know he was out there?' 'No one came to tell him.' 'That's not a no.'"
5. Ch. 9: "'I have water at my doorstep,' he said. 'The village keeps sand in its shed. I shall require it. I shall lay it myself, and the village will receive a bill for my labour.'" It also explains why ch. 12 finds no sand in the shed.

## 7. Against the comparison

**Below, by a little.** Below Crouch on pace, level with The Good Place on the moral problem, above both at "Forty-one." What decides it is rule load. Crouch gives one rule and a deadline. Here I carry six (the chair, the right hand, the count, the minute, the standing lie, the rain) and am still being taught one in ch. 9, two chapters from the flood: the count sends her home, it doesn't keep it off.

## Chapters 5 to 10: where I would have put it down

**B: nowhere.**

- 5, 7, 8, 10: no.
- 6: a wobble when Maylis decides to cross. The reason is thin, but the chapter moves before I mind.
- 9: the nearest. The association vote at the café door. The book has promised me a night on the platform, so the vote can only fail, and only Jeannot's reason holds it.

**A: in chapter 9.**

- 5, 8, 10: no.
- 6: skimmed from the flood file to the town hall floor. The pulse chart held me.
- 7: no, but at the sum on the platform I waited for the book to catch up.
- 9: here. After "Jeannot kept his hand up until the car had left the square", A goes to Arlette at the yard tap. It is the third scene of village procedure in a row and nothing pulls forward. I would have closed it for the night there.
