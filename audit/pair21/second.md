# Pair 21: second

Read A in full, then B. Chapters 0 to 4 and 11 to 17 are the same text in both (checked file against file, not reread in B). The versions differ only in chapters 5 to 10, where B is A less about 2,000 words. I read A first, so I knew what B had lost and could not test B's clarity cold.

## 1. Which one

A, slightly.

## 2. By criterion

- **Stakes: A.** Ch 6, on the mayor's floor: "And at home Coralie would be sitting on the edge of the bed, watching her die." B stops a sentence earlier.
- **Tension: B.** Its ch 6 goes from pulse 47 to pulse 36 with only the council between.
- **Progression: B.** Ch 9 asks "don't come on Friday" once. A asks it twice in one chapter and gets the same silence.
- **Comedy: A.** Ch 5: "Maylis brought her right hand across in its dish towel and tipped the spoon." Then "'It won't keep still,' Lesca said." Also Sarthou billing the village for laying its own sand. I smiled at both; B has neither.
- **Characters: A.** Ch 6, soup with the other Coralie: "For sixteen years Maylis had sat down and said nothing, in the wrong kitchen."
- **The ending: level.** Same words.
- **Clarity: A, slightly.** No page read twice in either. A's glosses at bingo ("Inguma had gone to count the corn, so nothing would take Lesca home tonight") did work B leaves to me. B's ch 9 never says why Maylis still asks for the gate open once she knows Lesca is the water.

## 3. The first fifty pages

Same point in both: ch 3, about page 24, "That village hadn't had its flood yet", then the motion with Coralie Mounet in it. Those chapters are identical, so neither gets there sooner. Fifty pages ends near the close of ch 5, and B's ch 5 starts faster because it doesn't repeat the last paragraph of ch 4.

## 4. The night of the flood

Chapters 12 to 14 are identical, so the belief comes from what I carried in. A's. B cut three things I needed: the sum on the walk back ("Twenty-five minutes was a trailer of leaves to the dump and back. Ten was the time it took to hook the trailer up"), the typed page ("Trapdoor locked on the inside"), and "That's not a no." With them, driving past the handle is a woman who has done the sum twice and refuses it, so "She wanted it not to be her" is earned. In B her "Both" has only stubbornness under it. A also has her say "Open" a second time with her own job held against the glass, and go quiet only at Kévin's name. That is the choice at the dip, rehearsed.

## 5. The last two chapters

Level, same words. They land a little harder after A: at "Across the landing. Four steps." I had already eaten soup with that Coralie.

## 6. What B does better

1. Ch 7, the turn in one line: "Those were the two days without rain. Last night she had lain still and Lesca had come across. By noon it was raining again. / The rain came when Lesca came." A states it three times.
2. Ch 5 opens straight on "Coralie had just come down in her uniform." A stops for the date and for why she lay still.
3. Ch 9, first chair scene, ends on "If I'm not, it holds." A adds a refusal the second chair scene plays again.
4. Ch 6: "Larrère was both. / 'Either,' she said." A puts her motive between the two and loses the laugh.
5. Ch 5, the soup ends on "Under the dish towel Maylis closed her fingers on the cut." A adds three sentences saying what that meant.

## 7. Against the comparison

Below, slightly. The flood night is level with Crouch, and the book treats its trolley problem as well as The Good Place does: the third option is the failure. What decides it is the rule load of chapters 5 to 9: which hand, whose mouth, what is being counted and until when, what can't be said standing up. Crouch gives a rule and breaks it inside the chapter. Here I kept a ledger for five chapters before the book ran.
