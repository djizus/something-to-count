## 7. Against the comparison

**Below, slightly. Level from chapter 10 to chapter 15.**

**The one thing that decides it: the rulebook.** A Crouch novel gives me one rule and squeezes it. This gives me about a dozen by chapter 9: lie still to reach the chair, stay sitting to cross, the owner keeps the right hand, nobody standing can lie, the count sends the visitor home, the count does not keep it off, 2:51, the rain comes with Lesca, the empty body cools, the hand weakens, the seat at the dead's bingo. Each is good. Together they cost me nine rereads, and the one the climax hangs on is the muddiest. Chapter 9 says the bank goes if Lesca is there at 2:51. Chapter 13 puts her out at 2:53. Chapter 14 says "it had come too late" in one line. I was working that out while Maylis was driving past the handle.

**Against Crouch.** Level on the hook: "It rang once", then "That village hadn't had its flood yet." Level on the run from Jeannot's six points to "I drowned him"; I read chapters 12 to 15 without getting up. Below on drive in the first half: until chapter 9 Lesca wants things and Maylis refuses them, so the engine is the visitor. A friend who has read Dark Matter will name it at the last line of chapter 3: a box, a second self, and she wants your partner. What is this book's own is the body shared hand by hand, and the other self bringing the weather.

**Against The Good Place.** Level on the moral. That show did the trolley problem as a gag; this does it straight, twice, and gives the harder answer: "I waited for the river to decide, so that it wouldn't be me." Below on comedy rate. I smiled often and never laughed, which meets the bar set, but the show lands three in the time this lands one. The repeated jokes pay off when they break (Larrère's "I'll be brief" followed by nothing). The one that wears is Josiane's husband-or-wife theory, used three times.

**Where I smiled.** "Eggs is what Either gets in this house." "Staff to wait. Staff not to join in." "You've got a shed, Maylis. It has a lawnmower in it." The window going all the way down. "I cannot summon Either to the station." "Eleven years I've been timed like an egg." "She wants water." "And six. Bugger off." "Then it should have gone last Monday."

**The ending.** Works. "What do you want, Maylis?" gets a straight answer at last, wrong for Coralie and right for the book.
