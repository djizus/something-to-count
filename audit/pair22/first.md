# Pair 22 — first

Read A in full, then B. Chapters 0–4, 8, 12 and 14–17 are the same text. The versions differ by a sentence or two in 5, 6, 7, 10, 11 and 13, and in three passages of chapter 9. B was read second, so its re-reads are undercounted.

## 1. Which one

**B, slightly.**

## 2. By criterion

- **Stakes: B.** Ch. 9: "If she gets in again on Friday night, there may be no hand to come back to."
- **Tension: B.** Ch. 13, the handbrake: "The one on the brake was the weaker, and it had got there first." A has "both of the same strength".
- **Progression: B.** Ch. 9 teaches something new: "The count tells it when to take the visitor home. It doesn't keep it off. 2:51 is my minute." A replays chapter 2's beat.
- **Comedy: A.** Ch. 9: "The clock radio went to 02:52. Inguma was still waiting. 'Either,' Maylis said." I smiled. B explains instead.
- **Characters: A.** "Lesca waited for the Either. It didn't come" (ch. 13) lands harder when Maylis has said Either to Inguma two nights before. In B she has already given it a firm order.
- **The ending: level.** Same words.
- **Clarity: B on the mechanism, A on the sentences.**

Rules I had to hold in both:

1. Inguma sits at 2:51 on whoever lies on her back in that bed. Push it off, or lie still and reach the chair.
2. In the chair each gets what the other knows.
3. Whoever stays sitting wakes in the other's body and drives. The owner keeps the right hand and can snatch the jaw.
4. Inguma takes the visitor home when its count ends. Anyone with the words sets the count.
5. Standing in the steel room, nobody can say what she knows is false.
6. The body left behind cools. A visitor who can't be taken home dies and gets a seat at the dead's bingo.
7. The river comes with Lesca, day for day with her 2014 file. Only 2:51 on the Friday counts.
8. Five fingers means awake.

A only: 9. A count given as it lands would keep it off Maylis till 4, and she can't pick one.

B only: 9. No count keeps it off Maylis, because 2:51 is Lesca's minute. 10. The owner's right hand weakens the longer the visitor stays.

## 3. The first fifty pages

The same point in both: "That village hadn't had its flood yet" (ch. 3, about page 20). The only difference inside fifty pages is B's extra sentence in ch. 5, and it changed nothing.

## 4. The night of the flood

B's. Chapter 14 is identical, so belief depends on why she is sitting awake in a van with no other move. In B the words don't keep it off, so the vigil is all she has, and "You'll count the fingers of my right hand. Then you'll take her home" uses the rule exactly. In A the same night follows from her failing to pick between two equal jobs while coughing blood. All through ch. 12 I asked why she didn't say "the corn" at 2:50, and nobody in ch. 17 asks it either.

## 5. The last two chapters

Level, word for word. One mark against B: the weakening hand is never mentioned after ch. 13, a loose thread A doesn't have.

## 6. What the loser does better

1. Ch. 9: "It stopped where it had landed and waited for its work." Then: "'Either,' Maylis said." B can keep this and still have Lesca say no count would have kept it off.
2. Ch. 9: "'What did you give it to count?' Maylis didn't answer."
3. Ch. 6: "and this was the bed she had to come back to."
4. Ch. 7: "She took what breath there was and held it. Then she let it go and lay still." B's added sentence repeats the pink in the sink.
5. Ch. 11: "on her side with her knees up." B says "nowhere to sit" twice in that chapter, after ch. 7 had said it.

## 7. Against the comparison

**Below, narrowly.** By the smile test the comedy is level with The Good Place (the oven door, Josiane's theory, "She wants water"), and chapters 14–17 hold up against either. What decides it is the rule load. There are ten rules to carry, and the deadline is announced by Lesca, never tested: "If I'm in your village at 2:51 on Friday night, your bank goes." A Crouch novel gives one rule and lets the reader watch it bite.
