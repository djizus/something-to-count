# Pair 22 — second

Read B first, then A. Eleven of the eighteen chapters are word for word the same (00–04, 08, 12, 14–17). Only chapter 9 differs by more than a sentence.

## 1. Which one

**B, slightly.**

## 2. By criterion

- **Stakes: level.** Forty houses, forty-two beds and one man in both. B's 'there may be no hand to come back to' (ch. 9) is paid off in one clause.
- **Tension: B.** Ch. 9: she gives the count properly and 'It crossed the room, jumped onto the bed and sat down on her chest.'
- **Progression: B.** Her side, pushing, the words: each fails for a reason, so the van is what's left. In A the third is lost to a joke.
- **Comedy: A.** 'It stopped where it had landed and waited for its work.' Then 'Either.' I smiled.
- **Characters: A, slightly.** 'Lesca waited for the Either. It didn't come.' (ch. 13) means more when Either cost her a night three nights before.
- **The ending: level.** Same text. 'I know what you picked. I was the other one.'
- **Clarity: B.** A sent me back from ch. 9 to ch. 6. B over-explains but never lost me.

## 3. The first fifty pages

Level. The only difference inside them is one sentence at the top of chapter 5 in B, 'Maylis had her right hand and nothing else', which helped. In both I couldn't stop from chapter 3, about page 25: 'That village hadn't had its flood yet.'

## 4. The night of the flood

B's. The choice at the dip is the same text in both, and I believed it in both: 'She wanted it not to be her.'

What differs is why she has to stay awake in that van. In A it is because on Tuesday she couldn't pick between roadsides and corn, and she never tries again. I kept asking why she didn't say 'the corn'. In B she has tried, and Lesca tells her: 'The count tells it when to take the visitor home. It doesn't keep it off.' The vigil is forced on her.

Against B: 'The one on the brake was the weaker' made me doubt Lesca couldn't open it.

## 5. The last two chapters

Level. Chapters 16 and 17 are identical, and they end well: Jeannot steps over the string with the second shovel.

## 6. What the loser does better

From A:

1. Ch. 9: 'It stopped where it had landed and waited for its work. […] Inguma was still waiting. "Either," Maylis said.' B could keep it: she says Either, it waits, she names the roadsides, it comes anyway.
2. Ch. 6: 'A doctor would put Maylis in an ambulance to Bayonne, and this was the bed she had to come back to.' B names Madame Etcheto here and again forty lines on.
3. Ch. 13: '"I've come to stay," Lesca said.' and 'Lesca sat back down in the chair.' B adds a reminder to each, at the climax.
4. Ch. 7: 'She took what breath there was and held it. Then she let it go and lay still.'
5. Ch. 13: 'It was one hand against the other, both of the same strength, and the one on the brake had got there first.'

## 7. Against the comparison

**Below, slightly.** What decides it is the number of rules between chapters 4 and 9. Crouch gives one rule and runs. Here I was keeping accounts (whose jaw, whose hand, whose body is at home going cold) while the flood waited. Chapter 14 is as good as his best. It has The Good Place's trolley problem and makes it cost somebody, but its smiles (the oven door, the six points, Josiane) come further apart.

## Rules I had to hold

Both versions, ten:

1. On her back at 2:51, Inguma sits on her; lie still and you are in the chair.
2. In the chair, each gets what the other knows.
3. Stay sitting when the other wakes and you drive her body; she keeps her right hand.
4. The words give Inguma a count; it takes the visitor home when it finishes.
5. Standing in the steel room, nobody can say what she knows is false.
6. The visitor's own body goes cold at home.
7. Pushing keeps Lesca out, and costs the rib.
8. The river comes with Lesca; only 2:51 on Friday counts.
9. The one Inguma can't take home sits at the bingo of the dead.
10. Five fingers means awake.

A only: a count keeps Inguma off. Chapter 6 says otherwise: she gave it the roadsides and was in the chair that night.

B only: the count doesn't keep it off, which settles rule 4. The owner's right hand weakens the longer the visitor stays.

## Pages I read twice

Both versions:

- Ch. 3, the first crossing: who has the left hand, who has the wrist.
- Ch. 7, 'The rain came when Lesca came.' In ch. 6 it rains at home on the Wednesday, with Lesca away.
- Ch. 15–16, 'Twelve. One, two.': whose chair that was.

A only: ch. 9, 'Both would get her past 4', against ch. 6.

B only: ch. 9, 'no hand to come back to', against 'a whole life with one hand' a page later.
