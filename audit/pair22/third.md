## 7. Against the comparison (A alone)

**Below, slightly.** Above Crouch on people and sentences, level with The Good Place on its moral problem, below both on how easily it reads.

**The one thing that decides it: the rule count.** Dark Matter runs on one rule and one want. The Good Place adds a rule only as a joke or a reversal. A asks me to hold eleven, and it is still issuing them past halfway: the rain rule in 7, "one night matters" in 9, the empty chair in 9. By then a Crouch book has stopped explaining and is only running. The late ones read as amendments, not discoveries.

Level or better:

- The turn in 3, "That village hadn't had its flood yet", is a Crouch turn. I couldn't stop from there.
- The flood (14) is as tight as anything he writes. I believed the dip: "She wanted it not to be her."
- The trolley problem is lived twice and both answers cost. The Good Place played it as a sketch; this earns it.
- Comedy: I smiled often and never laughed. The oven door; "I don't cook Either"; "Staff not to join in"; "It reads that she opened him"; "Either is not a person. I cannot summon Either to the station"; "They had one nose between them"; Jeannot's six points; "apologies received". The oven door that breaks off in 17 is the best use of a running joke in the book.

Below:

- Maylis asks for nothing until the doorstep in 12. That is the subject and it works, but for eleven chapters the urgency is borrowed from Lesca and the calendar.
- Nobody wins. Right for this book; a friend with this taste will respect the ending more than enjoy it.

### Rules I had to hold

1. Lie still under Inguma and you reach the chair; push it off and you don't.
2. In the chair each gets what the other knows.
3. Whoever stays seated when the other wakes rides in her body and drives.
4. The owner keeps her right hand, and can take the jaw for a few words.
5. Inguma fetches the visitor when its count ends; anyone with the words can set the count.
6. Standing in the steel room, nobody can say what she knows is false.
7. The visitor's own body lies empty and its pulse falls.
8. Pushing costs the rib; a third night means the lung.
9. Lesca brings the rain.
10. Only 2:51 on the Friday counts.
11. The one it cannot take home gets a seat at the dead's bingo.

Plus a timetable: eighteen days, ten minutes for the bank, twenty-five on foot to the trapdoor, four from open gate to drowned hut.

Rules 1 to 6 cost nothing, because each is acted (the stair post, "Tell me it's Tuesday"). Rules 9 to 11 I had to stop and fit.

### Pages read twice

- 12, last page: I couldn't tell whether her plan failed because she dozed (23:34 to 01:12) or because sitting up never stops it.
