# Pair 23 — first

Read B in full, then A. Same scenes, same order, about 130 lines changed. A was read second, so it never had a cold read.

## 1. Which one

**B, slightly.**

## 2. By criterion

- **Stakes: B.** Ch. 4: "You can keep me out. Push it off and you never get to this chair. I've felt you do it. That rib will tell you how many nights you can push." The price of keeping the flood out is stated. A's "You push. I've felt you do it" leaves it to be guessed.
- **Tension: level.** The van at 2:51 and the run down the track are word for word the same.
- **Progression: level.** Same beats, same order.
- **Comedy: A.** Ch. 4, Marie-Jo on the chrysanthemums: "Somebody should go in and tell him." I smiled. B finishes the joke for me: "tell him he's dead."
- **Characters: level.** A has the better Sarthou, B the better Lesca ("It would have been true of both names. I left him.").
- **The ending: B.** Ch. 17: "The arm is the excuse you've written for me. It isn't mine. At 3 I never tried the handle." A has "That one's yours. At 3 I never tried it." It is the book's last moral beat and in A I could not parse it.
- **Clarity: B, clearly.** Pages read twice:
  - **B, cold, 4 places.** Ch. 3, first page (whose bed, whose body, which Coralie). Ch. 7, "The rain came when Lesca came" (which nights she pushed, which days were dry). Ch. 9, Lesca's rules, "The words keep it off the old woman… The count only decides when it comes back", which I had to square with ch. 7. Ch. 14, first page (van, gate, patch, track: which way she is driving).
  - **A, story already known, 12 places.** Ch. 2, "'I did a rib.' 'That's the way in'" (reads as if the rib is the way in). Ch. 5, "'Ei—' Lesca said" (wrong speaker) and "What did you say to her?" (who). Ch. 6, "you're the one who puts the date on it", "In her own bedroom" (whose) and "She was five hours late" (who). Ch. 9, "It doesn't keep it off" and "I told them no". Ch. 11, first line, "Her mother" (whose). Ch. 12, "the platform gate" (a second gate?) and "she wasn't going to hand them to him". Ch. 17, "At 3 Sarthou carried her out" and "That one's yours".

## 3. The first fifty pages

B, about six pages sooner.

In B it was the last line of ch. 2, about page 16: "'Come back across. I've left you on that landing eleven years,' her mouth said. Maylis hadn't said it." That line has a question in it. A ends the chapter on "'Come back across,' her mouth said", which is only a strange voice. There the point came in ch. 3, about page 22: "That village hadn't had its flood yet."

This is a judgment on the two lines, not a measurement.

## 4. The night of the flood

The choice is the same in both and I believed it in both. "She wanted it not to be her" is the truest line in the book: it is Either turned into an act.

B, slightly, because it shuts two doors I would otherwise have pushed on. Why not put Kévin at the handle: "She wasn't going to have him turn the handle that sent him there." A's version took a second read. And what the choice cost: "If she had stopped at the handle instead of driving past it, the gate would have been up at 3."

Lesca's own night in ch. 10 is identical.

## 5. The last two chapters

B. In ch. 16 Lesca says "I left him" to Maïté, which she owes her. Ch. 17 turns on Maylis refusing the excuse Jeannot has written for her, and only B lets me read that refusal. A keeps one better line, Sarthou's.

## 6. What the loser does better

From A. In each, B explains what A let me get on my own.

1. Ch. 4: "'With him sitting in the café? Somebody should go in and tell him.'"
2. Ch. 6: "'It rang there. He can no longer stand at his own gate without Pau watching. I'm told I wave.'"
3. Ch. 8: "'One. It's Jeannot. Two. Jean's for the stone.'" B's "my gravestone" loses the stone I had already seen his name on.
4. Ch. 13: "This was the same thing, done with a steering wheel."
5. Ch. 17: "'That is recorded. I do not record that I would have stayed.'"

## 7. Against the comparison

**Below**, for B.

What decides it is the rules. Crossing runs on six of them: stay seated, the owner's right hand, the count, 2:51, no lie told standing, the rain. From ch. 6 to ch. 9 I was keeping them straight when I should have been dreading Friday or smiling. Crouch gives one rule and keeps raising its price. The Good Place spends that attention on jokes.

Chapters 12 to 14 and the last page are level with Crouch. Larrère's window, Jeannot's numbered points and "Either is not a person. I cannot summon Either to the station" would hold their place in The Good Place. The middle does not.
