# Pair 23, second reader

I read A in full, then B. They are the same book, scene for scene. B differs by about 150 line changes, nearly all adding a name, a reason or a reminder. B had the advantage of being read second.

## 1. Which one

**B, slightly.** The margin is small because nothing in the plot differs.

## 2. By criterion

- **Stakes: B.** Ch. 9, at the empty chair: "The one it can't take home dies in her own bed, and they lay a place for her." A has only "told you who it's for", which leans on a line from ch. 2.
- **Tension: level.** The ring count in the van at the end of ch. 12 is the same text.
- **Progression: B.** Ch. 8, after the corn: "Inguma had gone to count, so Lesca was still there. Maylis had kept her teeth shut all evening to stop that." In A I only learned who had won bingo night on the landing.
- **Comedy: A.** Ch. 4: "With him sitting in the café? Somebody should go in and tell him." B adds "he's dead" and I smiled less.
- **Characters: level.** B gives Coralie "That's what you want… I want that floor." A gives Sarthou "I do not record that I would have stayed."
- **The ending: B.** Ch. 17: "The arm is the excuse you've written for me. It isn't mine." A's "That one's yours" I had to decode.
- **Clarity: B.** Ch. 9: "The words keep it off the old woman in room twelve. They won't keep it off you."

**Read twice in A.** Three on the first pass, noted before I opened B:

- ch. 9, "The count tells it when to take the visitor home… It doesn't keep it off";
- ch. 13 into ch. 15, how Lesca leaves the hall of the dead alive;
- ch. 14, the minutes from 02:55 to 03:11, and "No job on that bank had ever taken her the time she gave it".

Seven more I guessed through, and saw as hitches only when B fixed them:

- ch. 1, Jeannot's water running out through the gate;
- ch. 2, "I did a rib" / "That's the way in";
- ch. 5, "'Ei—' Lesca said";
- ch. 11, "Her mother" (whose?);
- ch. 12, "She wouldn't put him at that handle";
- ch. 14, "number thirty" (I took it for a house);
- ch. 17, "carried her out" (who?).

**Read twice in B.** None, but I knew the story by then, which flatters B. The hall of the dead and the minutes in ch. 14 are the same text as A and would stop a cold reader too. One new hitch: ch. 5 calls the catch "the steel tooth", then ch. 12 has "the catch dropped into its first tooth".

## 3. The first fifty pages

The same place in both: the last line of ch. 2, about page 17, sealed around page 24 by "That village hadn't had its flood yet." B got me there with less effort (the two-way gate, "The thing on your chest is the way in"), and its ch. 2 last line already carries the landing and the eleven years. B, by a little.

## 4. The night of the flood

B's. The choice is identical and I believed it in both: "She wanted it not to be her." But B answers what I was asking in A, why Kévin isn't put at the handle: "She wasn't going to have him turn the handle that sent him there." B also states the cost flatly: "If she had stopped at the handle instead of driving past it, the gate would have been up at 3." A keeps one better line, for Lesca: "This was the same thing, done with a steering wheel."

## 5. The last two chapters

B. "It would have been true of both names. I left him." makes Lesca say it to the widow. A's "right for both lines" leaves the reader to finish it. Coralie's "That's what you want" answers sixteen years of her own question. B also walks Maylis from the café to Marie-Jo's chair, where A jumps.

## 6. What the loser does better

1. Ch. 4: "Somebody should go in and tell him."
2. Ch. 3: "Opened what? There's a name over it. It reads that she opened him."
3. Ch. 6: "It rang there. He can no longer stand at his own gate without Pau watching. I'm told I wave."
4. Ch. 9: "The gate stays shut. The Port knows it has a river."
5. Ch. 17: "'One,' she said, in the voice she called the numbers in. 'On its own. Sit down on it.'"

In each, B explains what A let me finish myself.

## 7. Against the comparison

**Below, narrowly.** Chapters 12 to 15 are level with either. The van, the corridor of wheelchairs and the speech would hold a Crouch reader, and the dead at bingo is a Good Place idea. What decides it is the rule load. Those two give me one rule, let me trust it, then break it. Here I carry six (the minute, the count, the chair, the right hand, the truth standing up, the rain), and the count is revised in ch. 9. Even B asks for work there.
