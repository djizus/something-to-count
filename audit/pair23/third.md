## 7. Against the comparison

**Slightly below.** Below a Blake Crouch novel through the middle, level with one on the flood night, level with The Good Place on the idea.

**The one thing that decides it: the rules of the crossing are learned by correction.** Chapter 4 gives them as a meeting, the best scene of the first half. Chapter 7 then changes them (she brings the rain). Chapter 9 changes them again ("The count only decides when it comes back to take the visitor home"). Chapter 13 adds one more (hold on for a whole ring and you land among the dead). Every page I read twice was about mechanics: who is in which body, and what the thing on the chest does when. Five of the seven are in chapters 6 to 9, where a Crouch book speeds up. I read Dark Matter without going back once.

Level or better:
- **Chapters 12 to 14.** The clock, the distances and the handle are exact, so at the dip I could do the sum with her: 600 metres behind, 400 ahead, six minutes. "She wanted it not to be her" is the truest line in the book. I did not stop between the sandbags and "Forty-one".
- **The cost.** Crouch gives the man his family back. This book takes Coralie and Madame Etcheto and makes both women say what was theirs.
- **The moral design.** It is the trolley problem answered both ways: one woman turns the handle, one refuses to, and neither is let off. The room where nobody standing can say a false thing is a Good Place device, and "I won't" coming out whole is its payoff.

Where a reader of both pushes back:
- The premise is Dark Matter's: a box, a second self, a crossing to take the bed and the wife. The book's own turn, "You're the water", comes two fifths of the way in. Until then I was reading a smaller Dark Matter with better jokes.
- Lesca leaves the hall of the dead by sending her apologies. I smiled, but chapter 2 promised that the one it cannot take home dies in her bed, and she wakes on a hospital stretcher. The chair pulled in for twelve pays that debt, and I only understood it in chapter 16.
- "Either" on the platform at 5, with the handle fitted and Jeannot above ground, takes three lines. I believed it, because the shopping pad had set it up. A death turns on it and it passes faster than the phone call to Hontaube.

**Comedy.** I smiled in every chapter from 1 to 15 except 7. Best: "It reads that he was opened"; the kitchen timer and Larrère "timed like an egg"; "I cannot summon Either to the station"; Jeannot's six points down the phone; Hontaube posting the form; Daguerre's "Here". The best comic move is the jokes going sour: "I'll be brief" with nothing after it in 16, and the oven impression Coralie cannot finish in 17.

## Pages read twice

1. **Ch 3**, the flood file ("Tuesday had been the 24th" to "That village hadn't had its flood yet"). I had to work out that both villages are in 2026 and the other one is replaying her 2014 rain.
2. **Ch 6**, the council vote. Which hand is whose, what the motion was, why seven against seven defeats it.
3. **Ch 7**, "The rain came when Lesca came". The paragraph that sets pushed nights against dry days.
4. **Ch 8**, the start and end of the bingo. Why Maylis keeps her teeth shut, and that Madame Etcheto's corn is what keeps Lesca in.
5. **Ch 9**, Lesca in the chair on what the words do and do not do. It overturned what chapters 2 and 7 had taught me.
6. **Ch 9**, the last page. The card says LESCA, MAYLIS and they share the name; for a moment the seat was Maylis's.
7. **Ch 13**, "Holding this wheel was the same thing". Why she lets go, and why one ring of holding sends her to the hall.
