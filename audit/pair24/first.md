# Pair 24: first

Read A in full, then B. Fourteen of the eighteen chapters are word for word the same. The versions part in four places: the opening of 3, one sentence in 6, the night Maylis tries the words in 9, one paragraph in 15.

## 1. Which one

B, slightly.

## 2. By criterion

- **Stakes:** level. Same text; "Watch stopped at 03:11" does the work in both.
- **Tension:** B. Ch. 9, Lesca: "It sits on you at 2:51 whatever you say to it." That shuts the easy way out before the flood. In A I sat through the van asking why she didn't name a count.
- **Progression:** B. A's ch. 9 replays ch. 2 ("Either", and it sits). B moves on: she says Either, then the roadsides, and learns what the words are for.
- **Comedy:** B, by one smile: "Inguma went on waiting. It couldn't count Either." Every other smile is in both ("Either is not a person. I cannot summon Either to the station"; "timed like an egg"; "And six. Bugger off").
- **Characters:** A. "Maylis didn't answer. Lesca didn't ask twice." In B she accounts for herself, which is less like her.
- **The ending:** level. Same words.
- **Clarity:** B where it matters, A in the sentences. Lists below.

**Rules I had to hold, both versions (13):** 1 two villages, split at the lamps in 2014; 2 lie still under Inguma and you reach the chair; 3 in the chair each gets what the other knows; 4 stay seated and you wake in the other's body; 5 the owner keeps her right hand, and can take the jaw for a few words; 6 Inguma takes the visitor home when its count is done; 7 standing in the steel room, nobody can say what she knows is untrue; 8 the body left behind cools, and the one it can't take home dies and joins the bingo; 9 Lesca brings the river, day for day with her file, and only 2:51 on the Friday counts; 10 pushing keeps her out and costs the rib; 11 five fingers means awake; 12 the gate: eighteen turns, sticks at nineteen, ten minutes for the bank, four for the hut; 13 Larrère's undated letter and the sheet with Kévin's name.

**B only (14):** the words keep Inguma off Madame Etcheto but not Maylis, because 2:51 is Lesca's minute.

**A only:** no fourteenth rule, but a question it never answers: why can't she give it its work in 9 when she did in 6?

**Pages read twice, both:** ch. 4, the three points and the fourth; ch. 6, the motion and the tied vote; ch. 7, "The rain came when Lesca came" (checking the dry days); ch. 13, "Then she saw what she was holding" down to "the same thing"; ch. 14, who is how far from what at the dip.

**A only:** ch. 9, "I can't push, and I can't give it its work." I went back to 6.

**B only:** ch. 3, the first sentence; ch. 6, "Maylis walked her across the square" (a sentence, not a page).

## 3. The first fifty pages

Same point in both: the last line of ch. 2, about page 16, "her mouth said. Maylis hadn't said it." It locked at about page 22: "That village hadn't had its flood yet." A got me over the join sooner. Its ch. 3 opens clean; B's first sentence there stopped me on the line that has to deliver the premise.

## 4. The night of the flood

B. Chapters 12 to 14 are identical, so the difference is what I carried in. In B she has tried the words, named the roadsides and been told they won't keep it off. Staying awake in the van is the last thing she has, and Inguma landing on the roof at 2:51 is what Lesca said would happen. "You'll count the fingers of my right hand" then reads as the rule turned round. In A I kept thinking: say the roadsides. The choice at the dip I believed in both, on "She wanted it not to be her."

## 5. The last two chapters

Level. 16 and 17 are the same words, and they end well: "I know what you picked. I was the other one."

## 6. What A does better

1. Ch. 3: "The other Maylis Lesca lived in a Peyrebarthe that had its flood in 2014. Afterwards it made her mayor."
2. Ch. 3: "She lay on her back and listened to the rain on the roof. The room could have been her own."
3. Ch. 6: "Maylis walked Lesca's body across the square from the church."
4. Ch. 15: "It had taken her home, to her own body. But the body wasn't in her bed."
5. Ch. 9: "Maylis didn't answer. Lesca didn't ask twice." B could keep this and go straight to Lesca's "The words keep it off the old woman in room twelve."

## 7. Against the comparison

Below, narrowly. The one thing is the rule load. Crouch gives one rule and runs. Here I held fourteen before the bank broke, and from ch. 6 to ch. 9 I was keeping accounts more than turning pages. The flood night and the ending match either, and I smiled as often as at The Good Place.
