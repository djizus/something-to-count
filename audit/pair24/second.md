# Pair 24 — second

Outside four passages the two versions are word for word the same: the first paragraphs of chapter 3, one sentence in chapter 6, the Either night in chapter 9, and one paragraph at the top of chapter 15. Everything below rests on those. I read B first, so A had the advantage of a second acquaintance.

## 1. Which one

**B, slightly.**

## 2. By criterion

- **Stakes: level.** Same words. "That village hadn't had its flood yet."
- **Tension: B.** Chapter 9. In B she names a job, it sits on her anyway, and Lesca says: "It sits on you at 2:51 whatever you say to it." The van is then all she has left. In A she says "Either", gives up ("I can't give it its work"), and all through the flood night I asked why she hadn't simply named one job.
- **Progression: A.** "Lesca waited for the Either. It didn't come." lands harder in A, where her last try with Inguma died on that word. In B she got past it in chapter 9 already, and nothing rode on it.
- **Comedy: B.** "Inguma went on waiting. It couldn't count Either." I smiled. A cuts from "Either" straight to the weight on her chest, which repeats chapter 2.
- **Characters: level.** A has the better silence: "Maylis didn't answer. Lesca didn't ask twice."
- **The ending: level.** Same words.
- **Clarity: split.** A is clearer line by line (chapters 3 and 6). B is clearer about how the thing works (chapter 9).

**Rules I had to hold, both versions:**

1. Inguma sits on her at 2:51. Pushing gets it off by 4 and costs the rib.
2. Lying still under it leads to the chair.
3. In the chair each gets what the other knows.
4. Stay sitting when the other wakes and you drive her body. The owner keeps her right hand and can snatch the jaw.
5. Inguma takes the visitor home when it has finished counting. The words and a job delay it.
6. Standing in the steel room, nobody can say what she knows is untrue.
7. The visitor's own body lies cold with its eyes open. The one it can't take home dies and gets a seat at the bingo.
8. The owner weakens the longer the visitor stays.
9. Lesca brings her river. The rain follows her.
10. Only one night counts: in at 2:51 on the 11th, the bank goes.
11. Five fingers means awake.

**B only:** 12. The words don't keep it off Maylis. The count only sets when the visitor goes home.

**A only:** no twelfth rule, but an open question in its place: would the words have worked if she had picked one?

**Pages read twice:**

- B only: the first page of chapter 3 ("the one whose village had its flood in 2014 and made her mayor"; "It could have been her own bedroom"). Chapter 6, "Maylis walked her across the square", to check whose body.
- A only: the Either night in chapter 9, to work out whether the words would have worked.
- Both: chapter 7, "The rain came when Lesca came", for the count of dry days. Chapter 9, "Keeping me out for a night or two changes nothing... One night matters", which comes two pages after two nights out dried the road. Chapter 13, "Holding this wheel was the same thing".

## 3. The first fifty pages

The same place in both. Hooked at the end of chapter 2 ("her mouth said. Maylis hadn't said it", about page 17). Unable to stop at "That village hadn't had its flood yet" (about page 25). A gets there with less friction, because its chapter 3 opens cleanly. So A, by a paragraph.

## 4. The night of the flood

Chapters 12 to 14 are identical, and I believed the choice in both: "She wanted it not to be her." I believed it more in B. I arrived knowing the vigil in the van was her last resort, and not a method she had dropped after one bad try.

## 5. The last two chapters

Level. They are the same words. They end well: a letter 42 that "doesn't thank you", and Jeannot stepping over the string.

## 6. What the loser does better

B should take back from A:

1. "The other Maylis Lesca lived in a Peyrebarthe that had its flood in 2014. Afterwards it made her mayor."
2. "She lay on her back and listened to the rain on the roof. The room could have been her own."
3. "There was nothing wrong with her own ribs."
4. "Maylis was in here too, and she was about to say it."
5. "Maylis walked Lesca's body across the square from the church."

Not chapter 15. B's "home, but not to her bed" is the better one.

## 7. Against the comparison

**Below Crouch, level with The Good Place. Below overall, narrowly.** What decides it is the load of rules. Crouch gives one rule and runs. Here I carried eleven or twelve, and the one the climax hangs on, that only Friday's 2:51 counts, is told to me by Lesca and never shown.
