# Pair 3: reader "first"

## 1. Which one

**A, slightly.** Most pages are the same, so the margin is small, but the direction is not in doubt.

## 2. By criterion

- **Stakes: A.** Ch. 8, Lesca: "Every night I cross, I bring a day of my flood with me." Then ch. 12: "You're the water." B says it as bookkeeping: "One day of my file for every night I sit in that chair with you."
- **Tension: A.** Ch. 11 ends inside the fight, one breath to a ring for six rings, then "The arms of the chair were warm." B stops at the first ring.
- **Progression: A.** One man can raise the gate ("No other order raises it"), so every scene leans on Larrère. B's prefect thread (ch. 4, 6, 7, 11) ends in an envelope that "said nothing about a gate".
- **Comedy: B, narrowly.** "'The keyholder has no preference,' Larrère said through the gap, and the member who kept the minute book wrote that down." Also the road salt.
- **Characters: A.** Maylis crosses because "She wanted one day as the woman who signs things", and Kévin tells her "I'd do them, Maylis. Don't keep a gate shut for me." Both make the failure hers. B has the best Arlette (see 6).
- **The ending: A, narrowly.** See 5.
- **Clarity: A.** In B I reread three pages: the rules in ch. 4 ("four points", which become five), the statute line in ch. 6 with two fingers on it, and ch. 15's "Who filled it was settled out on a track, by the other one." In A I reread one: Lesca arrives on the Saturday having given Inguma nothing, yet it does not come until bingo. B covers that ("It's done my signatures. Every one since 2015").

## 3. The first fifty pages

The same line in both: "'Come back across,' her mouth said. Maylis hadn't said it." End of ch. 2, about page 15 in A, a page later in B. A kept me from there. B let me go again in ch. 4, during the numbered rules, and got me back at the stair post, end of ch. 5, just inside page fifty.

## 4. The night of the flood

A's. The chapter is the same text; what differs is what she carries into it. In A, Kévin has taken the eel sheet away as an excuse by five o'clock, so "Either" on the platform is hers alone, and "she thought she could do better than that" at the winch is a wish I had watched grow since her day at the mayor's desk. In B the sheet is still under Larrère's hand ("His name was on the sheet") and her day as mayor was an errand about a statute, so the same sentences arrive with less behind them. Lesca letting go is also easier to believe in A: she has been told she is the water, and the reflector is "no nearer than it had been twelve years ago". B's reason is neater than it is convincing.

## 5. The last two chapters

A, narrowly. Ch. 16 is nearly identical, and A's line to Kévin is the cleaner one: "You told me on Friday not to keep that gate shut for you. I didn't." In ch. 15, A tells Madame Etcheto plainly, "I took my river over there with me, and last night it came into that room", where B explains a rule about laid places. B has the scene's best line, though (6.4).

## 6. What the loser does better

1. Ch. 2: "'The keyholder has no preference,' Larrère said through the gap, and the member who kept the minute book wrote that down."
2. Ch. 10, Arlette: "You'll count my ponies on the common land. That won't take long. They went up a ramp, and I counted them as they went. Then you'll go to where my daughter has sat down, and you'll fetch her back."
3. Ch. 11, the grit bags: "It has to be given something to count, and I've got no stars in here. Put those down one at a time." Then: "That's not many. It counts quickly." In A she counts the bags for no stated reason.
4. Ch. 15: "'What did you have last night?' 'Nothing. I didn't want to sit down.'"
5. Ch. 12: "She had come to take this life from a woman who never said what she wanted, and that woman had just said it. *That the mayor remain seated.*"

## 7. Against the comparison

**Slightly below both.** Ch. 13 is level with Crouch at his best, and the ending is braver than his: she pulls a man off a dry floor and loses room twelve and the pillow. The moral design is level with The Good Place; the laughs per page are below it.

The one thing that decides it: for eight chapters the heroine's trait is not choosing, so the drive comes from Lesca, and when Lesca is off the page (ch. 6 to 8: a folder, a statute, a vote) the book coasts. Crouch's lead wants something by page twenty and runs. Maylis starts with the five o'clock phone call in ch. 9, and from there I did not put it down.
