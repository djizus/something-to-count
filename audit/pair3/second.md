# Pair 3 — second

## 1. Which one

**A, slightly.**

## 2. By criterion

- **Stakes: A.** Ch 8: "Every night I cross, I bring a day of my flood with me." Then Maylis's price: "It's a whole life with one hand." B gives the same fact as "One day of my file for every night I sit in that chair with you", which I had to decode.
- **Tension: A.** End of ch 11: "On the sixth ring the rib caught and held." I had known since ch 8 that staying awake until four was the plan. B explains it only at the vigil.
- **Progression: A.** One authority: "No other order raises it." B's prefect route ("sent down through the mayor", the letter to Pau) leads nowhere.
- **Comedy: B, narrowly.** "'The keyholder has no preference,' Larrère said through the gap, and the member who kept the minute book wrote that down." A answers with "It's soup. It doesn't need a vote." / "It isn't getting one."
- **Characters: B.** Arlette saying the words herself in the mayor's office, and ch 12: "that woman had just said it."
- **The ending: B.** Ch 15: "What did you have last night?" / "Nothing. I didn't want to sit down."
- **Clarity: A.** In B I read three pages twice: the four points plus a fifth in ch 4, "Six until nine. Fifteen hours for a lake" in ch 5, and the statutes page in ch 6. In A, none. A draws the map in ch 1 ("The Port had nowhere to put a river"), explains the three-minute rule before using it, and leaves small roles unnamed where B hands me Ducasse, Tastet, Honoré, Fernand and Kessler.

## 3. The first fifty pages

Level. The same place in both: Friday's gauge sheet in ch 3, from "It hadn't had it yet" (about p. 20 in A, p. 21 in B) to "Watch stopped at 03:11" (p. 23 in both), and locked by the last line of the motion on p. 28. A gets there half a page sooner because its ch 2 runs straight from room twelve to the envelope to the chair; B's ch 2 adds a night and the meeting on the bank.

## 4. The night of the flood

A's. Three things prepared it.

- Kévin at the grit bin: "I'd do them, Maylis. Don't keep a gate shut for me." It comes before the five o'clock "Either", so that word is hers alone. In B the folder is still a live excuse.
- "You're the water." In A she has spent a week believing the flood belongs to somebody else, so "whatever the river did behind her was the river's. She wanted it not to be her" is the same woman two minutes later. In B she has just said "I'll drive it myself", and then ducks; I believed the dip less.
- She fights first, for six rings, and loses on the rib.

B gives Lesca the better exit from the van, but A prepares the choice.

## 5. The last two chapters

B, slightly. Ch 16 is nearly the same text. Ch 15 decides it. B's rule from ch 2, "a place that has been laid is filled by morning, by her or by another", turns room twelve into a debt Lesca declined to pay, and Madame Etcheto makes her say so. A's Lesca only says "I took my river over there with me", which is true and lighter.

One caution: the place is already laid on the Wednesday in ch 8, and nobody fills it by Thursday.

## 6. What the loser does better

From B, for A to take back.

1. "'The keyholder has no preference,' Larrère said through the gap, and the member who kept the minute book wrote that down."
2. "You'll count my ponies on the common land. That won't take long. They went up a ramp, and I counted them as they went. Then you'll go to where my daughter has sat down, and you'll fetch her back."
3. "She had come to take this life from a woman who never said what she wanted, and that woman had just said it. / *That the mayor remain seated.* / In the steel room the mayor let go of the chair."
4. "'Every December you give us forty houses and a care home, a metre from my knees. What did you have last night?' / 'Nothing. I didn't want to sit down.'"
5. "'It's done my signatures,' Lesca said, low, to the right hand. 'Every one since 2015.'"

## 7. Against the comparison

Below both, though ch 13 is level with Crouch and the bingo hall of the dead is level with The Good Place.

What decides it is how late the engine is stated. Crouch puts his rule in one sentence in the first act and spends the rest of the book on it; The Good Place makes each rule a joke. Here I have seven to learn (the chair, who stays seated, the right hand, the counting, truth in the room, a day of flood per crossing, the table of the dead), and the one that matters, "The river only rises when you come", arrives at the halfway mark. Until then Maylis is driven, not driving.
