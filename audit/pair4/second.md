# Pair 4: second

Read B first, then A. Chapter 13 is word for word the same in both.

## 1. Which one

**B, slightly.**

## 2. By criterion

- **Stakes: B.** Ch. 7, on the platform: "It was raining now because Lesca was back… the one who had her mouth was the flood." Every crossing now costs a day of river. A keeps this for a line of dialogue in ch. 8.
- **Tension: B.** Bingo night. In B Maylis clamps her jaw so that Inguma takes Lesca home; when Madame Etcheto finishes the words it is "another day of the river", given by the woman it will drown. In A she keeps her teeth shut "until the hall was empty" and I did not know why.
- **Progression: B.** Maylis finds out, fights three nights, asks, then moves the hatch to the top of her own list, "before dark". A has her fight Lesca on Sunday, offer her the right arm in a note on Tuesday and say "Forget the note" on Wednesday.
- **Comedy: B.** Josiane: "She lives with a nurse, and that's to throw us off." "She wants water." / "She's got a bar full of coffee." A has the best single laugh (the corridor, below) and a line B dropped: "The association has no opinion on sand."
- **Characters: B.** Jeannot counts his points from the first page, Kévin talks in questions, Arlette's mother had "a whole set for the priest that she only used after he'd gone". A tells me Kévin "asked three questions in every sentence" and then doesn't let him.
- **The ending: A, slightly.** The last chair scene: "In here it would have to be true. I don't know."
- **Clarity: level.** One page read twice in each. A: the handover note, then "Forget the note". B: ch. 14, "A chair was pulled in to a table", with no gloss. Maïté's broken sentences in B ("In the— It's blankets") slowed me twice more.

## 3. The first fifty pages

The same place in both, because chapters 1 to 3 differ by a few lines. The hook was the last line of ch. 2, about page 15: "'Come back across,' her mouth said. Maylis hadn't said it." The point of not stopping was ch. 3, pages 22 to 27: "Watch stopped at 03:11", then the motion's last clause, "and take the other one's bed, and Coralie Mounet in it." A got there no sooner. Level.

## 4. The night of the flood

A's, by one sentence. The night itself is identical; what differs is what she carries into it. At five that afternoon, after "Either", A gives the reason she lifts the handle off: "At three, if it came, it would be the river's doing." So when she turns her back on the lamp at the dip ("She wanted it not to be her") I had already watched her do it once in daylight, when it was cheap. In B the five o'clock "Either" passes with no thought attached and the motive arrives new at the dip. B's pad, with the hatch at the top, also means Lesca's "Go to the blind first" pushes her where she was already going.

## 5. The last two chapters

B's ch. 15 is better. Lesca tells Madame Etcheto in one line ("they called twelve") what A retells over a paragraph, and "Nobody emptied hers" lands sooner. A's ch. 16 is better. The two women account to each other in the room where a lie won't come out, and Lesca's "I don't know" is proved there. B cuts that to "Forty-one" and two speeches, clean but thin for a last meeting. The final page is the same. A, slightly, on the chair scene alone.

## 6. What the loser does better

From A, for B to take back:

1. Ch. 2: "…and that finished Coralie. She put her forehead against the wall beside the door and shook… Down the corridor Bixente studied his mop."
2. Ch. 11: "If the gate went up now and Lesca never came tonight, the flood on the meadows was Maylis's alone. At three, if it came, it would be the river's doing."
3. Ch. 16: "'It folds in three,' Lesca said. 'They take it off me on Tuesday. And I've been asked which I turned the handle for. The houses, or her. In here it would have to be true. I don't know.'"
4. Ch. 8, Arlette: "Your father was the same. I asked him every day what he wanted for his dinner, and every day he said Either, and I never got used to it."
5. Ch. 14: "A chair was pulled in to a table. Somebody had sat down to play with the dead."

## 7. Against the comparison

Below, narrowly. B is level with The Good Place on invention (a room where a lie won't come out, the dead at bingo with maize) and on warmth, and its jokes are smiles, which suits it. Chapters 11 to 13 are as tight as Crouch. What decides it is the stretch from ch. 6 to ch. 10. The date does the pulling alone there, and the danger on the page is a vote, a file and a folder. A Crouch reader gets a body at risk every twenty pages; here they wait sixty.
