# Pair 5 — first

## 1. Which one

**A, clearly.** Same story, but A reads more easily on every page and its rules hold on the night of the flood.

## 2. By criterion

- **Stakes: A.** Ch. 8 puts the rule in one place: "Only the last night counts. If I'm in your village at 2:51 on Friday night, the water comes through your bank […] If I'm not, it holds." B leaves me to assemble it in ch. 11.
- **Tension: A.** Ch. 6 cuts from the council to Coralie beside the body: "The doctor's number was on the screen, with her thumb over it." B only reports the worry.
- **Progression: A.** Ch. 3 follows straight on from "Come back across". B opens "Four nights earlier" and walks the same four days again.
- **Comedy: A, slightly.** "Tell me it's Tuesday." / "It's—" / "It's Saturday." And: "the only thing that square had ever waited for her to do was move the tractor."
- **Characters: level.** A shows Maylis's love as work: "You do my road before the square, every autumn, and you've never once said so." B gives Lesca's arithmetic a face on Darrigrand's doorstep.
- **The ending: level, A by a hair.** "At the gate I thought I could do better than you" is news in A. B has already said it in ch. 13.
- **Clarity: A.** Never twice in A. Twice in B: the motion in ch. 3 ("went on in the same ink to the end of the line. The motion had one fault"), where the line is withheld and the fault turns out to be something else; and ch. 12, where "every minute Lesca stayed was the river" made me ask why the bank still broke once she had gone.

## 3. The first fifty pages

A, sooner (pages at 275 words). In A it was page 17: the last line of ch. 2 runs straight into Lesca in the bed with Coralie's fingers, and by "It hadn't had it yet" (page 23) there was no stopping. In B the same hook at page 15 is let go by "Four nights earlier". I was caught again only at page 24, when the crossing came round, and I had already seen half of it.

## 4. The night of the flood

A's. I knew the bank would go whatever she did ("whether you stay or I put you out"), so her ten minutes were real. The file had told her "Trapdoor bolted on the inside", so somebody had to knock. And "No job on that bank had ever taken her the time she gave it" told me her eight minutes were a wish before the van sank. At the dip the two are the same, and good: "She wanted it not to be her." B names her vanity at the handle. That is plainer, but it spends ch. 16's confession early, and B's looser rule made me doubt the bank.

## 5. The last two chapters

Almost the same text. A, narrowly: the times stay in figures, the steel-room confession is new, and B cuts turf "with the edge of the spade" before Jeannot fetches "a second shovel".

## 6. What B does better

1. Darrigrand's step, which A reduces to a gap in a row: "It's four when you're the one counting." … "When it's your house it's one, and they knock it down."
2. Lesca caught lying in the steel room, where A gives her a speech: "I was going to tell you it was the tide. I can't get it out."
3. Larrère's cut-off sentence about the pasture: "'The common pasture belongs.' It rang there." … "Belongs to the village, Madame le maire, and it's under a dredger."
4. The warning recalled before she takes the van, so the sinking is her doing: "Jeannot had warned her about the dip, and at dusk she had waded through it."
5. The broken sentences of the run: "No way around it." … "Up to her thighs. Colder than the rain."

## 7. Against the comparison

**Below, narrowly.** Ch. 11 to 13 are level with Crouch, and the bingo hall of the dead is level with The Good Place. What decides it is the rule load. Crouch gives one rule and runs. Here I carried six (the chair, the right hand, the counting, the truth room, the river that comes with the visitor, the last night) through ch. 5 to 8, while the clock stood still behind a kitchen farce.

## 8. Which sentences read more easily

A's. Times and dates are figures, the words are the plain ones (bank, handle, hut, trapdoor), and it stops a sentence where B joins two with ", and".

From A:

- "Tuesday 24 November 2026, 2:51 a.m. Maylis Lesca was awake in her own bed and couldn't move."
- "On Sunday night Maylis could have pushed, but she lay still. Keeping Lesca out meant having a better plan, and she didn't have one."

The same two from B:

- "It was nine minutes to three in the morning on Tuesday the twenty-fourth of November 2026. Maylis Lesca was awake in her own bed, and she couldn't move."
- "On Sunday night Maylis could have pushed, and she lay still, because keeping Lesca out meant having a better plan, and Maylis didn't have one."
