# Pair 5: second reader

Read B first, then A, which flatters A on first-read questions.

## 1. Which one

**A, clearly.** From chapter 9 on the two are the same book. A wins in chapters 1 to 8.

## 2. By criterion

- **Stakes: A.** A ch. 8 states the rule: "Only the last night counts. If I'm in your village at 2:51 on Friday night, the water comes through your bank as it came through mine, and your bank goes. If I'm not, it holds." B's "I bring a day of my flood with me" left me unsure what staying awake would buy.
- **Tension: A.** A ch. 6 cuts from the council to Coralie at the bedside: "The doctor's number was on the screen, with her thumb over it." B tells me afterwards.
- **Progression: A.** A ch. 3 opens inside the cliffhanger, with Lesca waking in the other bed. B rewinds: "Four nights earlier".
- **Comedy: A.** "'Tell me it's Tuesday.' 'It's—'" (ch. 4), and "the only thing that square had ever waited for her to do was move the tractor." B has neither.
- **Characters: A for Maylis, B for Lesca.** A: "You do my road before the square, every autumn, and you've never once said so." B gives Lesca her doubt (see 6).
- **The ending: level.** Same scenes, same last image.
- **Clarity: A.** I never read a page twice. In B I did twice: the top of ch. 3 (which woman, which village, which day), and the top of ch. 13, where the bank still breaks after Lesca has been sent home, though ch. 12 said "every minute Lesca stayed was the river". A closes that: "That bank goes tonight now, whether you stay or I put you out."

## 3. The first fifty pages

A, sooner. In A it was the first page of ch. 3, about page 16: 'Come back across' is picked up at once, from inside the woman who said it. In B it was "It hadn't had it yet", about page 19, and the crossing waits until about page 22, 2,000 words after the cliffhanger.

## 4. The night of the flood

A's. In both she drives past the handle to have "both", and in both "She wanted it not to be her" at the dip. A made me believe the first step, because she knows her sum is too kind: "No job on that bank had ever taken her the time she gave it." She goes anyway. B names her pride on the spot ("she wanted to be the one who got them"), which spends the confession of ch. 16 early. A's rule is also firm by then: the bank will go, so it is the man or the gate.

## 5. The last two chapters

Level in substance; only nouns and sentence breaks differ. B's nouns are the right ones: "wreath" for A's "flowers", "silt" for "mud", "She cut the turf along the string with the edge of the spade" for A's grass and shovel. A's breaks are easier. The best ending is A's sentences with B's nouns.

## 6. What the loser does better

1. Darrigrand on his step (B ch. 3), missing from A: "'It's four when you're the one counting,' he said. He leaned on the broom. 'When it's your house it's one, and they knock it down.'"
2. The lie that will not come out (B ch. 8), where A has Lesca recite dates: "'I was going to tell you it was the tide,' she said. 'I can't get it out.'" Keep A's rule, and let her try the lie first.
3. Lesca's doubt (B ch. 3): "It was the guess of a woman who had wanted that man alive for twelve years, and she had nothing to check it against."
4. The night on the kitchen floor (B ch. 7), which A cuts to one line: "it came down the stairs one at a time, with the sound of a sack being lowered down a step, and it found her."
5. The plain noun where A paraphrases: "A hoist stood over the bed with its sling hanging down, turning a little." Likewise "waders" (A: "the long rubber boots he hunted in", three times) and "ruts" (A: "wheel tracks").

## 7. Against the comparison

**Below, slightly.** The turn decides it. "You're the water" is a real reversal, since the rescuer is the flood, but nobody at the bingo bar hears it, and a recital of dates confirms it. In a Blake Crouch novel or in The Good Place that discovery wrecks the next chapter. Here it changes the plan to staying awake in a van.

## 8. Which sentences read more easily

A's. Times are figures, tools have everyday names, and one sentence does one job.

From A:
- "Tuesday 24 November 2026, 2:51 a.m."
- "Lesca had let his phone ring once and then turned the handle. Maylis had let it ring nine times, and the man was alive."

The same two places in B:
- "It was nine minutes to three in the morning on Tuesday the twenty-fourth of November 2026."
- "Lesca had rung once and turned the handle, and Maylis thought she could do better than that."

B also has to gloss its own words ("the pawl, the steel catch that held the winch").
