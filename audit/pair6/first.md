# Pair 6: first

Read B in full, then A.

## 1. Which one

**B, slightly.** Most pages are the same word for word. B wins the middle of the book. A wins chapter 15.

## 2. By criterion

- **Stakes: B.** Darrigrand on his swept step: "It's four when you're the one counting... When it's your house it's one, and they knock it down." Lesca's saved Port has a price before she carries her river across.
- **Tension: B.** Nobody tells the reader who brings the rain until Maylis says "You're the water" across the bar. A gives it away a scene early, at the marker post: "It was raining now because Lesca was back."
- **Progression: B.** The rule that a woman standing in the steel room can only say what's true keeps working on Lesca: "It's the ti—", then "I was sitting down", then "I'm coming to get—" and what comes out is "To stay." In A she concedes with the dates and comes anyway to "have your Port dry by 4", a motive the night itself drops.
- **Comedy: A.** The breakfast fight: "They had one nose between them, so now neither of them could breathe." B cuts it and thins the bingo.
- **Characters: B.** Coralie rules a chart on the shopping pad and judges: "I'd have had you. You answer. But what I'd have had is a river over forty-two beds." In A she only holds a wrist down.
- **The ending: A.** A plays the removal vote as a scene. B reports it in one sentence.
- **Clarity: A, slightly.** I never reread a page of A. In B I stopped once, at the van in chapter 12: "The right hand turned it with her", and a few lines later that hand covers the eyes and pulls the handbrake.

## 3. The first fifty pages

Level. It is the same place in both: chapter 3, the flood file, "That village hadn't been spared its flood. It hadn't had it yet." That is about page 24, and the two texts reach it within 70 words of each other. B's first two chapters are leaner (no "She was doing Larrère's voice"), but its Darrigrand scene sits just before the ladder and spends the saving. I met that line fresh only in B, so A had no fair chance to hook me sooner.

## 4. The night of the flood

B's. The paragraph at the dip is identical ("She wanted it not to be her"), so what decides it is what leads there.

- B restates the warning before she takes the van: "Jeannot had told her not to bring the van down... She took the van anyway." The stuck van is her doing, not bad luck.
- Lesca has said twice that she is coming for the man, and the steel room has shown what lies under that. "Go to the hut first" is hers. In A she comes for the gate, then drives past it.
- Coralie has heard the whole thing and asked for the night shift. Her "Say which, Maylis. For once" comes from someone who chose to be under that bank.

## 5. The last two chapters

A. Chapter 16 is the same in both except for three trims, and B's trims are right. Chapter 15 is where B loses. It is half A's length, the council is one sentence, and a rocket goes up from a party B has cut. A keeps the timer's last ring, Larrère's abstention, and Maïté getting the thing she wanted.

## 6. What the loser does better

Take these back from A.

1. The council (15): "She took the dial and turned it back to nothing. The bell rang. 'That's the member's time,' Marie-Jo said. She said it to Lesca this time, not to the wall."
2. Maïté (15): "'Every December I held my face still for you at that stone, for the photographer, and you bent down and kissed it. And you knew.' 'I knew.' 'I wanted somebody to say it.'"
3. Breakfast (8): "'Sixteen years, and that's the first time I've seen that hand hold on to anything,' Coralie said." It fits inside B's chart scene.
4. Bingo (7): "'A watch,' Josiane told the second row. 'A woman times a man's track to find out how long he's gone from his kitchen. It was never him. It's the wife. I said that house.'" Without it her last line, "I only had the one name wrong", is wrong by two names.
5. Lesca (10): "Lesca wanted to be looked at by the one who didn't know."

## 7. Against the comparison

Below both, though not by much.

The one thing: the premise turns over once. After "You're the water", nothing changes what the reader thinks the book is. The last third carries out, very well, what chapter 8 announced. Crouch and The Good Place each turn the premise over again late.

On the Good Place side it earns its place. It runs the trolley problem twice and makes not choosing the second fault. I smiled at the oven door, at "Either is not a person. I cannot summon Either to the station", and at "And six. Bugger off."
