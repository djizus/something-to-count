# Pair 6: second

## 1. Which one

**B, slightly.** I read A first and have allowed for that.

## 2. By criterion

- **Stakes: B.** Coralie names the price herself at the kitchen table: 'I'd have had you. You answer. But what I'd have had is a river over forty-two beds.'
- **Tension: B.** Lesca can lie sitting down ('It's the tide') and can't standing up ('It's the ti—'). Then: 'To stay. That's the one that comes out.' In A she concedes on Sunday ('You were right') and the pressure drops for two chapters.
- **Progression: B.** A tells the twist three times: the narrator in 7, the bar, then Lesca's recap of the dates in 8. B says it once at the bar and makes Coralie test it on a chart.
- **Comedy: A.** At the bingo bar: 'What's left on me to look at?' / 'You're breathing.' In the kitchen fight: 'They had one nose between them, so now neither of them could breathe.' B cut both.
- **Characters: B.** Coralie rules three columns, *What. Since when. How do you know*, and asks for the night shift herself. A is better on Maïté and Larrère in 15, but Coralie matters more.
- **The ending: A.** Chapter 15 is played as scenes; B summarises the council in one paragraph.
- **Clarity: A, narrowly.** I reread nothing in A. In B I reread chapter 8, 'So I'm asking the one who said it. That wasn't your voice', to work out who Coralie was speaking to. A also states a rule that B leaves the reader to infer: 'Once she was in, it went, and nothing done to her afterwards would stop it.'

## 3. The first fifty pages

The same line in both, in chapter 3: 'That village hadn't been spared its flood. It hadn't had it yet.' The hook is set one chapter earlier, also in both: ''Come back across,' her mouth said. Maylis hadn't said it.'

Level. B is quicker through chapters 1 and 2. A reaches the chapter 3 line about fifty words sooner, because B adds Monsieur Darrigrand sweeping his doorstep. The doorstep earns its space: 'It's four when you're the one counting'.

## 4. The night of the flood

**B.** The sentence that decides it is identical in both: 'She wanted it not to be her.' What differs is whether I believed the road to it.

- B owns the mistake that makes the dilemma: 'Jeannot had told her not to bring the van down, and at dusk she had waded through the dip herself. She took the van anyway.' In A the van just sinks, and it reads as the author's hand.
- In B both hands turn onto the track: 'Neither of them wanted any other road.' In A the right hand fights that turn, and three minutes later Maylis takes the same road herself.
- Lesca's letting go costs more in B. Standing, she can only say 'I've come to stay.' She looks at the empty chair in the hall of the dead before she sits on Inguma.

One thing against B. Coralie has checked the dates herself on Sunday, yet on Friday she says 'I'm not waking a woman of 94 for orange.' In A she has only 'the word of a woman who cuts roadsides', and her doubt fits. In B her Friday is harder to believe.

## 5. The last two chapters

**A.** Chapter 16 is the same in both. In 15, B reports the council vote in a paragraph and drops Maïté's question. Both were set up in chapter 3, and only A pays them off.

## 6. What the loser does better

Passages B should take back from A:

1. Chapter 7, the bar: ''You've had your complaint back,' he said. 'What's left on me to look at?' / The teeth came apart. / 'You're breathing,' Lesca said.'
2. Chapter 8: ''Sixteen years, and that's the first time I've seen that hand hold on to anything,' Coralie said.'
3. Chapter 7: 'She had nobody to tell and no mouth to tell it with. The one who had her mouth was the flood.'
4. Chapter 15, Maïté: ''Every December I held my face still for you at that stone, for the photographer, and you bent down and kissed it. And you knew.' / 'I knew.' / 'I wanted somebody to say it.''
5. Chapter 15, the council: 'For eleven years Lesca had watched it tick from the head of the table. It was louder from here.' With it: ''That's the member's time,' Marie-Jo said. She said it to Lesca this time, not to the wall.' And Larrère: 'I abstain.'

## 7. Against the comparison

**Below, by a short distance.**

The one thing: at both turning points the heroine does not decide. The rib lets Lesca in on the sixth ring, and the van sinks in the dip. Her one real choice, to walk on, comes after the better options are gone. Blake Crouch gives his hero a move that uses the rules to win. The Good Place lets Chidi choose at last and rewards him. Here the best rule-move, 'You'll count the fingers of my right hand', comes after the damage is done.

Two parts are level: the flood night in chapter 13 with Crouch, and the dead at bingo ('apologies received') with The Good Place.
