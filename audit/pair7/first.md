# Pair 7 — first

## 1. Which one

**A, slightly.** Most of the book is shared word for word. The verdict rests on chapters 2 to 5, 8, 10 and 12.

## 2. By criterion

- **Stakes: B.** Ch. 8, Lesca in the steel room: "If I'm in your village at 2:51 on Friday night, the water comes through your bank as it came through mine, and your bank goes. If I'm not, it holds." A never states the fail condition that flatly.
- **Tension: A.** Ch. 12: "If I get you out of me now, it may hold." The bank is still to play for during the fight in the van. In B Maylis says "The river's coming now, whatever I do with you. So this is for me," and the fight is over nothing I feared for.
- **Progression: A.** Ch. 10 keeps the back of Jeannot's neck for the evening before the crossing ("The man on the stone had never had the time"), so I know what Lesca is going for as she goes. B spends it in ch. 5.
- **Comedy: A.** Ch. 5, told from inside the body being driven: "Maylis had never smiled at Jeannot Castagnet in her life, and the whole café was watching her do it." And ch. 10: "The young man looked down his sheet for a step." B drops the first and moves the doorstep to ch. 3 without the young man.
- **Characters: A.** Lesca in ch. 12: "This was the same thing, done with a steering wheel." It is the moment she sees herself. B has no equivalent.
- **The ending: level.** One clause differs.
- **Clarity: A overall, B early.** Pages read twice. A: 3 (the last page of ch. 2, the first of ch. 3, the tied vote in ch. 6). B: 6 (the first page of ch. 3, the café in ch. 5 where I lost whose head I was in, the tied vote, the Friday rule in ch. 8, and two in ch. 12).

## 3. The first fifty pages

The same line in both, ch. 3: "That village hadn't been spared its flood. It hadn't had it yet." A reaches it at about page 22. B reaches it a page and a half later, after two paragraphs on who Larrère and Marie-Jo are and a stop at a swept doorstep. A sooner. B has the cleaner run-up in ch. 2, though (see 6).

## 4. The night of the flood

A's. Maylis's choice is the same in both, and I believed it both times: "She wanted it not to be her." The difference is the two minutes before it.

In A Maylis fights for the wheel because putting Lesca out might still save the bank. Lesca lets go because she sees that holding on is ringing once all over again: in her own motion "there was no man", and "as long as she held the wheel, nobody was going down that track at all." I knew why each hand did what it did.

In B "neither of them wanted any other road", and Maylis pulls the handbrake anyway, for herself, with a man in a cellar and ten minutes on the bank. Lesca is sitting on Inguma in one room while driving a van in another, and she lets go after four sentences that don't say why. I read it twice and still had to supply the reason. I also didn't know why she landed among the dead instead of at home. A tells me: "She had held the wheel against it for a whole ring, and this was where that put her."

## 5. The last two chapters

Level. Ch. 16 is identical and ch. 15 differs by one clause. A's ending is better earned, because of ch. 12.

## 6. What B does better

1. Ch. 2: "'That's the door,' Kévin said. 'Don't push at it. Lie still under it and you're in, and you've still got your head.'" This fixes A's one confusing page.
2. Ch. 4: "In this chair, what one of us knows, the other gets. That's how I had your rib."
3. Ch. 8: "I've been pushing it off since the rib, and all that time it was doing my job."
4. Ch. 7: "Twenty-five minutes was a trailer of leaves to the dump and back. Ten was the time it took to hook the trailer up."
5. Ch. 11: "02:49. She was awake. She laid her left hand on the steering wheel and touched each finger in turn. She counted five."

"Then she sat down on Inguma" is also the best single image in either version. It only costs too much where B puts it.

## 7. Against the comparison

Below, narrowly. Ch. 13 is as good as either, and the comedy made me smile on most pages ("Either is not a person. I cannot summon Either to the station"). What decides it is the rules. Crouch gives one rule and escalates it. The Good Place makes each rule a joke. A has five (the chair, the right hand, the truth when standing, the counting, the river that follows the visitor), handed out over four chapters, and I held them by effort until ch. 5. With B's items 1 to 3 it would be level.
