# Pair 7 — second reader

Read B in full, then A.

## 1. Which one

**A, slightly.**

## 2. By criterion

- **Stakes: B.** B states what Friday is for: "If I'm in your village at 2:51 on Friday night, the water comes through your bank as it came through mine, and your bank goes. If I'm not, it holds." A never says it that plainly.
- **Tension: A.** The van. In A the two hands fight for the wheel: "The right hand pulled it the other way, with the cracked rib behind it, and lost." B runs the same eight rings in a van and a steel room at once.
- **Progression: B.** B plants before it uses: the gravel lake and Darrigrand's step in chapter 3, the finger count in chapter 2, "I'm going to sit on it" in chapter 10. A names the lake in chapter 5 and explains it in chapter 10.
- **Comedy: A.** Chapter 5 told from the passenger seat: "Maylis had never smiled at Jeannot Castagnet in her life, and the whole café was watching her do it."
- **Characters: A.** Lesca's turn is on the page: "the motion she had written at her own kitchen table was for a seat and a bed. There was no man in it."
- **The ending: level.** Chapter 16 is the same text in both; chapter 15 differs by one phrase.
- **Clarity: A.** Pages read twice: **A two, B four.**
  - Both: the first page of chapter 3 (who is Lesca, whose bed).
  - A only: the chart in chapter 8. "Every night she crosses, mine does one more day of her flood" does not square with a river that catches up two days in an afternoon.
  - B only: the café in chapter 5, where the scene is in Lesca's head and Maylis's by turns; and two pages of chapter 12, where Lesca sits on Inguma in the steel room while driving the van, then stands in the hall of the dead with nothing to say how she got there.

## 3. The first fifty pages

**A sooner.** The same line in both: the flood file in chapter 3, "That village hadn't been spared its flood. It hadn't had it yet." A reaches it about page 22, B about page 24, because B puts the Darrigrand doorstep and Marie-Jo's sash in front of it.

## 4. The night of the flood

**A's.** Three choices are made that night and A gives a reason for each.

Maylis pulls the brake because "my bank's still standing. If I get you out of me now, it may hold." In B she says the river is coming whatever she does and "this is for me", then stops a van that is already going where she wants to go. I did not believe she would spend those rings on that with a man in a cellar.

Lesca lets go because she sees she is the one blocking the track: "As long as she held the wheel, nobody was going down that track at all." B gives her a red reflector and one sentence about the stair post, and she lets go. I took it on trust.

At the patch, A has the line B lacks: "She had put Lesca out, and it had come too late: the river was through the bank already." It makes the fight in the van cost something.

A's cost: four paragraphs of reasoning at the eighth ring. It could lose one.

## 5. The last two chapters

Level. The one difference is Lesca's answer to Madame Etcheto. A: "I was in the hall where the dead play". B: "It took me to the hall". A's is right; Inguma does not take her there in either.

## 6. What B does better

1. The Friday rule quoted above, with its first sentence: "Your river made up two days in an afternoon, so only the last night counts."
2. Maylis at the chart: "I've been pushing it off since the rib, and all that time it was doing my job." A has only "The thing that sits on me."
3. "'I'm going to sit on it,' Lesca said. 'It can't take me home while I'm sitting on its back.'" In A the secretary puts her hand over her mouth at a line that isn't funny.
4. The finger count before 2:51: "She laid her left hand on the steering wheel and touched each finger in turn. She counted five." It makes Kévin's five at 4 a.m. hers as well.
5. Coralie's thirteen hours: "she had drawn up a chart of the kind she kept for a resident. It had a pulse for every half hour since 6 ... The doctor's number was written across it and underlined twice."

## 7. Against the comparison

**Below, by a little.** The bingo hall of the dead and "apologies received" would sit in The Good Place without anyone noticing. The midpoint turn, "You're the water", is as good as a Crouch turn. What decides it: Crouch states the rule cleanly before the set piece that depends on it, and A's rule for Friday is the muddiest page in the book. With B's three sentences from the steel room, A is close to level.
