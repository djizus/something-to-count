# Pair 8: first

Read B, then A. Nine of the seventeen files are word for word the same (00, 01, 03, 05, 06, 09, 14, 15, 16). The rest differ in thirteen passages, and everything below rests on those.

## 1. Which one

B, slightly.

## 2. By criterion

- **Stakes: B.** Ch. 8, Lesca standing in the steel room: 'If I'm in your village at 2:51 on Friday night, the water comes through your bank as it came through mine, and your bank goes. If I'm not, it holds.' A has only 'Now I've got both.'
- **Tension: level.** The eight rings in the van are the same words. A's '02:49. She was awake.' is tighter; B's finger count there pays for 'You'll count the fingers of my right hand.'
- **Progression: B.** Each rule arrives before it is used: Kévin's 'That's the door. Don't push at it' (ch. 2), 'In this chair, what one of us knows, the other gets' (ch. 4), 'only the last night counts' (ch. 8).
- **Comedy: A, slightly.** 'Both mine, and I had your rib. That isn't one of the points.' I smiled. B's tidy three-point agenda lost it.
- **Characters: B, slightly.** Coralie's chart in ch. 7: 'The doctor's number was written across it and underlined twice.' A gives her two columns of ticks.
- **The ending: level.** Chapters 14 to 16 are identical. B's 'By her own count she could have had the gate up at 3' sets up 'At 3 I never tried it' better than A's 'The gate would have gone up at 3.'
- **Clarity: B.** Pages read twice: B 5, A 7. The shared five: Lesca's first waking (ch. 3); the handle slipping (ch. 5); the two-handed vote (ch. 6); the kitchen chart (ch. 8); the hands fighting over wheel and brake (ch. 12). A adds the end of bingo night, where 'Maylis felt the night go out with them' does not say she has lost, and 'The gate would have gone up at 3'. A came second and still needed more. In A's ch. 8 the second read did not settle it: 'Every night she crosses, mine does one more day of her flood' is contradicted a few lines on, when one crossing takes the river from Wednesday's level to the page for the 5th.

## 3. The first fifty pages

Level, and the same place in both: the last line of ch. 2, 'Come back across,' said by a mouth Maylis isn't driving (about page 17), locked by the file in ch. 3: 'That village hadn't been spared its flood. It hadn't had it yet.' (about page 24). Up to there the texts differ by one line, Kévin's, in B. It did not move the point.

## 4. The night of the flood

B's. Maylis's choice at the dip is the same words in both. I believed it more in B because B had given me what she was choosing between: the sum in her own units ('Twenty-five minutes was a trailer of leaves to the dump and back'), the rule that the bank goes only if Lesca is in her at 2:51, and afterwards 'By her own count'. Lesca's letting go is cleaner in B too. It ends on 'This was the same thing, done with a steering wheel', which is what she says in ch. 16: 'It was my own night I was putting right.' A adds a second motive, that Maylis can be trusted, and ch. 16 never mentions it.

## 5. The last two chapters

Level. They are identical.

## 6. What the loser does better

1. Ch. 4: 'Both mine, and I had your rib. That isn't one of the points. First point.' Keep B's rule, but say it in this voice.
2. Ch. 10: 'The secretary had her hand over her mouth.' The only sign of how 'I won't be brought back' sounds to somebody outside it.
3. Ch. 12: 'She could be trusted with the rest of the night, if somebody told her one thing.' It gives 'Go to the hut first' a reason, and it turns sour four pages later when Maylis tells Coralie 'Both'. The two sentences before it repeat 'It didn't come' and can stay cut.
4. Ch. 7: 'They walked back. Maylis did the sum at the water and again at the fence.' B's added 'Ten was the time it took to hook the trailer up' repeats ch. 6. Keep the trailer of leaves, drop the ten.
5. Ch. 8: 'The thing that sits on me.' B's addition, 'all that time it was doing my job', stopped me: pushing it off is also what kept Lesca out on Wednesday and Thursday.

## 7. Against the comparison

Below, for B as a whole. Level from ch. 11 to the end.

The one thing is the rule load. A Crouch novel gives one rule and a clock. This gives the chair, the right hand, the counting, the truth told standing up and the river that follows her, and I read five pages twice before the clock started.

The Good Place side holds up. It is a trolley problem that costs something: she pulls out a man who was dry, and the old woman drowns. The jokes made me smile: 'Either is not a person', 'apologies received' in the hall of the dead.
