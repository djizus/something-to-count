# Pair 9: first

Read A in full, then B. Nine of the seventeen files are word for word the same in both (00 to 03, 09, 11, 13, 14, 16). I read those once, in A, and B's other eight in full. Reading B second helped B on clarity.

## 1. Which one

**B, slightly.**

## 2. By criterion

- **Stakes: B.** Chapter 8, at the empty chair: 'That's where I sit if I stay with you.' In A she says 'I've seen it' and I did not know what Lesca was risking.
- **Tension: level.** The flood night is the same text. 'She was two hundred metres short of the bank when it broke.'
- **Progression: B.** Chapter 6, the council: 'She hadn't known whether he had a back lane here. He had.' Maylis brings something across and uses it. In A she only listens.
- **Comedy: level.** B adds 'Whose car, Madame le maire? For the minutes' and 'The chair votes once, Madame le maire.' A keeps 'They had one nose between them.' Each made me smile where the other did not.
- **Characters: B.** Chapter 6: ''I want that,' Maylis said. It was out before she could stop the mouth.' It is the first want she says aloud, and it is for a kitchen timer.
- **The ending: A.** Chapter 15, the removal vote played as a scene: 'It was louder from here.'
- **Clarity: B.** Pages read twice: **A 4, B 2.**
  - A: the opening of chapter 3 (who Lesca is); the vote in chapter 6 ('failed by one vote, the mayor's': whose hand?); the empty chair in chapter 8; chapter 12, 'Inguma hadn't taken her home' (why is she among the dead?).
  - B: the opening of chapter 3 (same text, counted for both); chapter 8, 'I was sitting down'. The rule said standing up *in this room*, and she had been in a kitchen. I went back to the fourth point to check.

## 3. The first fifty pages

Level. In both I could not stop from the same line, about page 25, at the flood file in chapter 3: 'That village hadn't been spared its flood. It hadn't had it yet.' The text up to there is identical.

After it, A's chapter 4 moves faster. B stops for 'There are three points, and I'll say them once', half a page of briefing. It is the one slack spot in either version's first fifty. It pays for itself in chapter 12.

## 4. The night of the flood

The choice is the same words in both: Maylis drives past the handle, the van sinks in the dip, and she turns her back on the lamp. 'She wanted it not to be her.' I believed it in both.

I believed it more in B, for two reasons, both in chapter 12. Maylis says 'If I get you out of me now, it may hold', so I knew why she fought for the brake instead of the track. And I already knew what the wheel cost Lesca, so 'Go to the hut first' read as a woman giving something up. In A I was working out the rules while she let go.

## 5. The last two chapters

**A ends better.** Chapter 16 is identical. In chapter 15 A plays Lesca's removal: the sash folded in three, the timer she used on Larrère for eleven years rung on herself, Larrère abstaining. B gives it two sentences of summary, and they read like a note left in. 'Lesca, Maylis. Surname first' is the name off the card of the dead. It needs a room around it.

## 6. What the loser does better

Passages B should take back from A:

1. Chapter 4: ''We've got a mayor. He sits in his car.' 'Not in my village. In mine he sits at my table, and I time him.'' B's three points push this out.
2. Chapter 7: ''You're breathing,' Lesca said.' And Josiane: 'Two words. I got none for a coffee, and he gets two.'
3. Chapter 8: 'Maylis pushed the heel of the hand up under the nose. They had one nose between them, so now neither of them could breathe.'
4. Chapter 15: 'For eleven years she had watched that timer tick from the head of the table. It was louder from here.'
5. Chapter 15: 'Larrère had waited eleven years for this item. He didn't ask to speak to it.' Then: ''I abstain,' Larrère said.'

## 7. Against the comparison

**Below.**

What decides it is the number of rules. By the flood night I am holding five: stay sitting to cross, the owner keeps the right hand, the counting sets the clock, standing in the steel room allows only the truth, and holding out against Inguma means the bingo hall. Crouch gives one rule and runs. The Good Place makes each new rule the joke. Here the climax in the van is a fight between two hands that I can only follow if I have kept all five.

Where it holds its own: the comedy is the smiling kind (the oven door, the six-point phone call, 'Either is not a person'), and the ending is harder than either comparison usually dares. 'I know what you picked. I was the other one.'
