# Pair 9 — second reader

Read B first, then A. Nine of the seventeen files are word-for-word the same in both (00–03, 09, 11, 13, 14, 16); I read those once. Pages are counted at 250 words.

## 1. Which one

**B, slightly.**

## 2. By criterion

- **Stakes: B.** Ch. 8, at the empty chair: "That's where I sit if I stay with you. The old woman in room twelve told you who it's for." A has only "I've seen it".
- **Tension: level.** Chapters 11 and 13 are the same text. A's chapter 12 is leaner.
- **Progression: B.** Ch. 8: "I've been pushing it off since the rib, and all that time it was doing my job." One line turns the monster into the ally. A has no such turn.
- **Comedy: level.** I smiled four times at things only A has and four times at things only B has. A's are sharper and spread over three chapters (the nose, "he gets two", "I time him"). B's are all in the council scene of ch. 6 ("It's in the inventory", "The chair votes once, Madame le maire").
- **Characters: B.** Ch. 6: "I go in at the front, Guy. In daylight, and she holds the ladder. I've never had to leave my car in the back lane with its lights off." Maylis gets one straight answer while she wears the sash, so her "Either" on the platform in ch. 11 hurts more. A sums that vote up in one line.
- **The ending: A.** Ch. 15, the removal vote played as a scene: the sash folded in three, the timer that "was louder from here", Larrère's "I abstain". B gives it one sentence.
- **Clarity: B.** Pages read twice: **B 4, A 5.** Three are the same page in both: the opening of ch. 3 (who Lesca is, whose bed), ch. 8 "only the last night counts", and the opening of ch. 14 (which Coralie, which hospital). B alone: ch. 8, "I was sitting down". A alone: the empty chair in ch. 8, and ch. 12, where Lesca lets go of the wheel and lands among the dead with no reason given. I read A already knowing the rules from B, so A's 5 is a floor.

## 3. The first fifty pages

The same point in both, on the same words: the end of chapter 3, about page 27. "That village hadn't been spared its flood. It hadn't had it yet", then the motion with Coralie's name in it.

They part in chapter 4, pages 28 to 38. B lists the rules as three points, in character. A gives the same facts as a quarrel and is funnier, and its truth rule is clear enough from "Tell me it's Tuesday". On these pages alone I would take A.

## 4. The night of the flood

B's. Maylis's choice at the handle and at the dip is the same text, and I believed it in both: "She wanted it not to be her." The difference is Lesca. In B I knew before the night what staying would cost her, and Maylis says "If I get you out of me now, it may hold". So driving past the handle is a bet with odds, and Lesca letting go of the wheel is a price paid. In A I understood why she ended in the hall only after the chapter.

B pays for this. Two sentences in ch. 12 told me what I already had: "It had come for Lesca, and not for the woman it was sitting on" and "On Wednesday night she had told Maylis whose chair that was". They slow the count of the rings.

## 5. The last two chapters

A. Chapter 16 is the same. In 15, A plays the removal as a scene, so Coralie's "Good night, Maylis" answers "Surname first". In B the name arrives before I have felt the fall.

## 6. What the loser does better

Five passages from A that B should take back:

1. Ch. 8: "Maylis pushed the heel of the hand up under the nose. They had one nose between them, so now neither of them could breathe."
2. Ch. 7: "'You're breathing,' Lesca said." and "'Two words,' Josiane told the second row. 'I got none for a coffee, and he gets two.'"
3. Ch. 4: "'We've got a mayor. He sits in his car.' 'Not in my village. In mine he sits at my table, and I time him.'"
4. Ch. 15: "She had rung it on the ends of Larrère's sentences for eleven years. Now it had rung on hers before she began one."
5. Ch. 8: "'It came out in my kitchen.' 'This isn't your kitchen.'" B's "I was sitting down" muddles the truth rule.

## 7. Against the comparison

**Below, by a little.** The flood night and the confession in the square are as good as either. What decides it is the number of rules I had to hold: the chair, who drives, the right hand, the counting, the truth rule, the river that follows her. That is six by chapter 8. A Crouch novel runs on one, and The Good Place hands them over one at a time with a joke each. B makes the six clear. It does not make them fewer.
