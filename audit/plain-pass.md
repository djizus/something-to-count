A first reading of `manuscript/00.md` to `18.md`, in order, by a reader who had not seen the book. Every sentence that made that reader stop, go back or work something out was rewritten in place. Nothing else was touched: no event, fact or joke was changed, and no chapter moved by more than three per cent.

## What had to be mended

Counted by sentence, across the whole book:

- **A pronoun with two possible owners (53).** "She", "her", "he", "his", "it" or "the man" where two women, or a woman and Coralie, or a man and a mayor, were both in reach. Worst in the scenes where the two women share one body, and in chapter 11, where Lesca's night is told as "she" to a Maylis who is also "she".
- **A meaning carried by shorthand (63).** A private word or a clipped figure standing where the plain thing should be: "the minute" used for three different things, "gave the gauge a line", "looked for the cloth of her lapel", "what she was spending, and whose it was", "it took what it had taken on Saturday", "So was both".
- **A rule of the crossing relied on without saying what happens (15).** Lines that only work if the reader has memorised who wakes in whom, or that Inguma fetches when the counting is done. Each now says the thing at that spot: "Lesca was still sitting, so she woke in the van with her", "Inguma had finished her signatures and come for her".

The rest were steps back in time with no mark on them (7), the premise in chapter 2 (6) and plans stated too tightly to follow (5).

## Three readings to check

In three places the original could be read two ways, and the rewrite had to choose. If the choice is wrong, the sentence wants the other reading said as plainly.

- Chapter 11, "Lesca had not been late." Read as: lateness was not the reason he drowned. Now "It was not that Lesca had come too late."
- Chapter 16, "I'm not asking why once." Read as: why one ring. Now "I'm not asking why you rang once." The other reading is "I'm not asking why, not even once."
- Chapter 3, Madame Etcheto's "The one it cannot fetch is found in her bed". Only "back" was added. A stranger does not understand the line here and does by chapter 15, which seems to be the intention, so the rest was left dark.

## Left alone on purpose

These made the reader slow down and were not changed, because the effect depends on the gap and the reader crosses it unaided: the opening of chapter 2, where the mayor's name arrives before the explanation; "He did not hear himself say it" in chapter 10; the buff folder, the two bowls and the suitcase in chapter 5; "Twelve. One, two." in chapter 16; the long sentence of the night watch in chapter 13.

One thing a line edit cannot settle: in chapter 6, with Lesca driving the body, "Maylis looked for Coralie's car, and found it", though the same chapter says Maylis has no say in the eyes. It reads once, so it stands, but the two do not agree.
