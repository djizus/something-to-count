# Continuity and proof of the final text

Read: `manuscript/00.md` to `17.md`, in order, in full. Line numbers are the line in the chapter file.

Nine continuity findings, two proof findings, one optional note on vocabulary. Nothing that breaks the plot. The calendar holds in both villages.

## 1. Continuity

Sorted by how much a reader would notice.

### 1. Kévin's van turns white (objects: the vans)

Chapter 14, line 171.

> Beside it Kévin had backed up his white van, opened the rear doors and put two mattresses on the floor.

This is the only place his van has a colour. Everywhere else "the white van" is Jeannot's: it is how the reader finds him at the end of the track (chapters 9, 10, 12), and in chapter 12 Maylis tells Kévin that the van in the mayor's night "wasn't yours". Earlier in this same chapter Jeannot's van has just stopped at the dip.

Correction:

> Beside it Kévin had backed up his van, opened the rear doors and put two mattresses on the floor.

### 2. The same line of the flood file is quoted two ways (objects: the file)

Chapter 3, line 169.

> *First light. CASTAGNET Jean recovered from the cellar of his duck hut. Watch stopped at 03:11.*

Chapter 6, line 143, quotes the same typed line with a sentence in the middle that chapter 3 does not have:

> *First light. CASTAGNET Jean recovered from the cellar of his duck hut. Trapdoor locked on the inside. Watch stopped at 03:11.*

Chapter 3 also says "Under that the file was typed, by the gendarmes" and gives this line straight away, as if it were the first typed line. In chapter 6 two typed lines (02:50 and 02:55) come before it.

Correction, chapter 3, line 169:

> *First light. CASTAGNET Jean recovered from the cellar of his duck hut. Trapdoor locked on the inside. Watch stopped at 03:11.*

If the locked trapdoor is being kept for chapter 6, mark the chapter 3 line as cut short instead: *First light. CASTAGNET Jean recovered from the cellar of his duck hut. […] Watch stopped at 03:11.*

### 3. A gutter overflows in the village where it has not rained (which village)

Chapter 16, line 33.

> 'That back gutter's overflowing again. Have a look at it before you go.'

This is Lesca's village, on the evening of 12 December. It has been dry there since mid-November: "Peyrebarthe had been dry for two weeks" on the 28th (chapter 3), frost on the 28th and on 2 December, "a dry roof" on the 11th (chapter 13). There is no water to overflow. Lesca also cleared the leaves out of this gutter on 28 November (chapter 3, line 133), so "packed solid" two lines later is fourteen days' worth.

Correction:

> 'That back gutter's blocked again. Have a look at it before you go.'

### 4. The river is read off a "marker post" in one village and off the pillar in the other (objects; referred to before introduced)

Chapter 7, line 75, is the first use. Nothing introduces the post.

> On the platform Lesca read the marker post.

The other three uses: chapter 9, line 157 ("So I go by the marker post, and the post says that river's behind its bank."), chapter 12, line 193 ("looked away at the marker post"), chapter 14, line 131 ("pointed the torch at the marker post").

It is the same gate and the same father's red line in both villages. In chapter 3 the line is painted "on the pillar"; in chapter 10 "She kept the torch on the pillar of the gate. Eighty over the line"; in chapter 15 "the red line on the pillar of the old gate".

Correction, four places:

> On the platform Lesca read the pillar.

> So I go by the pillar, and the pillar says that river's behind its bank.

> She looked at it, looked away at the pillar and looked at it again.

> She climbed onto the top of the bank and pointed the torch at the pillar.

### 5. The corridor is empty, then holds the last chair (numbers)

Chapter 14, line 315.

> By 4:10 the corridor was empty from the front doors to the fire door.

Four lines later: "Maylis had Madame Poey's chair by the handles. It was the last chair."

Correction:

> By 4:10 the corridor was empty from the front doors to the fire door, but for one chair.

### 6. Maylis puts in two cards and holds one (objects: the card and the envelope)

Chapter 17, line 173.

> Lesca took the envelope off the shelf and held it open. Maylis put the two cards in together.

At line 151 Maylis has the card from her raincoat and "Lesca was holding the one from the shelf".

Correction:

> Lesca took the envelope off the shelf and held it open. Each of them put her card in.

### 7. One minute for everything between the leak and the call (times)

Chapter 6, line 139.

> *02:50. Water through the bank at the repair beside the last house, across the width of a door. The keyholder gave the bank ten minutes.*

Her call to Castagnet is at 02:51 (chapters 10 and 11). Between the two she puts her hand on the bank, drives up to the gate, climbs the platform, takes out the handle, holds it for three breaths, climbs down, turns the van around and looks down the track (the opening chapter). The order of times is right; the gap is too short.

Correction:

> *02:45. Water through the bank at the repair beside the last house, across the width of a door. The keyholder gave the bank ten minutes.*

That also puts the gate up at 02:55, exactly when her ten minutes run out.

### 8. "Eighteen more turns" reads as nineteen (numbers: the nineteen turns)

Chapter 12, line 161.

> The gate hadn't moved yet. Eighteen more turns would lift it a hand. The nineteenth would need her shoulder.

One click has just been counted ("the catch dropped into its first tooth with a click"). With "more", the hand's width comes at the nineteenth click and the sticking point at the twentieth. Chapters 1, 5, 9 and 14 all have eighteen for the hand and nineteen for the stop.

Correction:

> The gate hadn't moved yet. Eighteen turns would lift it a hand. The nineteenth would need her shoulder.

### 9. Sarthou is retired in one village and serving in the other (which village) — a query

Chapter 15, line 31.

> He hadn't been in the square since he retired.

In Maylis's village the same man, in the same month, is "Sarthou, the gendarme" (chapter 5), takes a complaint (chapter 6) and stands in the road "in uniform, with his cap on" (chapter 17). Nothing says why the two differ. If it is meant, leave it. If not, cut the sentence: the one before it ("Sarthou stood at the bottom of the steps in his uniform, holding the flag upright in its shoulder strap.") stands by itself.

### Kinds with no findings

- **Dates and weekdays.** None. Every dated weekday fits 24 November as a Tuesday: Monday the 23rd, Monday 30 November, 1 December (a Tuesday), Friday 4, Sunday 6, Tuesday the 8th, Thursday 10, Friday the 11th, Saturday 12, Sunday 13 December, burial on Tuesday the 15th. The counts of days are right at each step: eighteen, seventeen and fifteen to the 12th, thirteen and ten to the 11th. Every "yesterday", "last night" and "on Tuesday" lands on the right day. Six, eleven, twelve, sixteen and eighteen years all agree with 2008, 2010, 2014 and February 2015. Eleven commemorations before this twelfth one. Nineteen days of rain from the 24th ends on the 12th, which is where 2:51 falls.
- **The river.** None. Maylis's village: a metre under on Friday the 4th after the two dry days, fifty-five under on Saturday the 5th, at the line on Tuesday the 8th, over it at 5 on the 11th, ninety over at 2:53, eighty and falling at 3:11. Lesca's 2014: seventy under on the 4th, fifty-five on the 5th, at the line on the 8th, ninety over on the night, then eighty and sixty. One thing to know: the six file entries quoted in chapter 3 carry no figures ("Under the line. Rain."), though Lesca later quotes seventy and fifty-five from that file.
- **Who knows what.** None.
- **Whose hand.** None. Checked sentence by sentence in chapters 2, 3, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9 and 13: the owner has the right hand every time, and the visitor never uses it.
- **Things that were cut.** None found beyond the file line in finding 2.

## 2. Proof

### 1. Two speeches by Coralie read as a change of speaker (quotation marks)

Chapter 11, lines 133 and 135. The first paragraph closes its quotation marks:

> By 4 it had stopped coming, and at breakfast they told me what had been done.'

The next opens a new speech with a tag, so for a clause the reader gives it to Lesca:

> 'So for twelve years I've had two answers,' Coralie said. 'Either you opened it for forty houses, and I live with the mayor.

Correction: leave the first paragraph open and drop the tag.

> By 4 it had stopped coming, and at breakfast they told me what had been done.

> 'So for twelve years I've had two answers. Either you opened it for forty houses, and I live with the mayor.

### 2. "The terrace road" (a word used in two forms)

Chapter 17, line 69.

> Maïté came along the terrace road in her good coat, carrying a light brown suitcase with two straps.

The road to the four houses has no name anywhere else. It is "the road along the edge of the high ground" (chapter 4) and the houses stand on "the high ground" (chapters 3 and 6). "Terrace" appears only here.

Correction:

> Maïté came along the road from the high ground in her good coat, carrying a light brown suitcase with two straps.

### Optional: two American words in a British text

The spelling is British and consistent throughout. Two words are American vocabulary, not spelling, and are used consistently, so they are a choice and not an error.

- "baby carriage" and "the carriage", chapter 17, lines 13 and 35. British: "pram".
- "dish towel", chapters 1, 5 and 11, six times. British: "tea towel".

### Kinds with no findings

- **Spelling.** None. No name is spelled two ways. No word appears both hyphenated and closed up. *Madame le Maire* with a capital in chapter 5 is the one Bordenave is made to correct.
- **Dates and clock times in words.** None. Screens, clock radios and the file show four digits (02:51, 18:40); prose and speech show 2:51. That split is kept everywhere.
- **Retired words.** None. "Blind" appears once, in chapter 13 ("Lesca kept the foot down, blind"), and not for the hut.
- **A word repeated by accident, a missing word, a sentence that does not parse.** None.
- **Names.** None. Arlette is "her mother" and "Arlette" in the same scenes each time; the secretary, the deputy mayor, the dealer, the priest and the young man from Pau are roles throughout.
