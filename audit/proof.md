# Continuity and proof of the final text

Read: `manuscript/00.md` to `16.md`, in order, in full. Nothing else was opened.

Calendar used. 2026: Mon 23 Nov (rib), Tue 24 Nov (rain starts, letter 41), Wed 25 (lights, room twelve), Fri 27 (card), Sat 28 (Lesca's first crossing, 06:00 to 06:20), Sun 29 (chrysanthemums), Mon 30 (Lesca's day, 6 a.m. to 9:05 p.m.), Tue 1 Dec (resignation, complaint), Wed 2 (Maylis's day as mayor, 6 a.m. to 7 p.m.), nights of Wed 2 and Thu 3 (kept out), Fri 4 (dry, a metre under), Sat 5 (rain by noon, 55 under, bingo), Sun 6 (chart), Tue 8 (river at the line, vote), Wed 9 at 2:51 (steel room), Thu 10 (bandage), Fri 11 into Sat 12 (the night), Sat 12 (ceremony), Sun 13, Mon 14, Tue 15 (burial, council). 2014: rain from 24 Nov, line reached 8 Dec, gate raised 02:55 on 12 Dec.

## 1. Continuity

Thirteen findings, sorted by how much a reader would notice. The kind is given in brackets.

**1. Chapter 5 (objects: the pad).**
"Maylis had stood over that pad every morning for sixteen years and had never once got as far as a tick."
Two days earlier, in chapter 4, on the same pad: "She ticked both." The reader has just watched her do it.
Correction: "…and had never once got as far as one tick."

**2. Chapter 5 (cut or stale).**
"'I'll be brief,' he said through the gap. 'The lights. Your father started them at the church. The church end this year, or the café?'"
This is Monday 30 November. The lights went up on Wednesday 25 November (chapter 2: "Her father had always had the Christmas lights up in the plane trees by Saint Catherine's Day. That was today."), with Kévin in the bucket, in front of Larrère's parked car. In chapter 8 one string has "gone dark". There is nothing left to start.
Correction: "'The lights. Your father took them down from the church end. The church end this time, or the café?'"

**3. Chapter 16 (numbers).**
"'My son's van came up that hill twice. He says you sent it.'"
Chapter 13 gives Kévin three trips: "'I've done one trip. Five people.'"; then "When the van had its five, Kévin shut the doors on them and drove out of the yard."; then the last load, after "'There isn't another trip,' Coralie said."
Correction: "'My son's van came up that hill three times. He says you sent it.'"

**4. Chapter 8 (the river).**
"If I'm in your village at 2:51 on Friday night, your bank goes, as mine did. If I'm not, it holds."
She is standing, and the next line insists on it: "Nothing had stopped a word of that." But her bank did not go. She raised the gate and it held (chapter 9: "No house in the Port had water in it."). The room should have stopped "as mine did".
Correction: "…your bank goes, as mine was going. If I'm not, it holds."

**5. Chapter 16 (cut).**
"'It's the eighth this year. I've kept count too.'"
Nobody else in the book counts this year's graves. "Too" answers a line that is no longer there.
Correction: "'It's the eighth this year. I keep count.'"

**6. Chapter 7 (cut or not set up).**
"When Maylis woke at 6, Lesca was still sitting in the chair. It was Lesca who got her out of bed."
The rule from chapter 4 is that with nothing to count Inguma fetches the visitor "after twenty minutes". On 30 November it is at the kitchen door within minutes and has to be given the lake. On Saturday 5 December Lesca gives it nothing on the page, yet it stays away until bingo night, and the text then says "Inguma would come for Lesca tonight" as if a day's counting had been set.
Correction: add after "…got her out of bed.": "By 6:20 Lesca had given Inguma her lake to count again."

**7. Chapter 6 (objects: the folder).**
"He opened the folder and held up the only sheet in it. The sheet was blank."
The day before, chapter 5: "And this morning goes into your file, with the time on it." and "Maylis had put three letters in that file, all of them about a handle that stuck. Now the file had a morning in it as well." The folder marked LESCA M. cannot hold one blank sheet.
Correction: "He opened the folder and held up the top sheet. The sheet was blank." (The later lines still work: "on top of the blank one", "There is no other sheet.")

**8. Chapter 1 (objects: the card and the envelope).**
"The envelope went into a drawer. They opened the door, counted one of her, paid her, gave her a sandwich and went back to their university."
The drawer comes before the door is opened, so it reads as a drawer inside the steel room. Chapter 2 has the envelope at home: "the top drawer of the chest of drawers… It had been in there since the June of the steel room."
Correction: "They opened the door, counted one of her, paid her, gave her a sandwich and went back to their university. The envelope went home with her, into a drawer."

**9. Chapter 16 (times).**
"'I had the coffee pot on in here at 5 on Saturday morning, and I counted them in,' she said."
The first loads went up the hill before 3:20 and the corridor was empty "By 4:10". At 5 there was nobody left to count in.
Correction: "'I had the coffee pot on in here at 3:30 on Saturday morning, and I counted them in,' she said."

**10. Chapter 16 (objects: the clock).**
"Three uniforms went in, then the good coat, folded, then the clock she woke up to."
The only clock the bedroom has is the clock radio, and its alarm is the one Coralie wakes to (chapter 3: "She reached across Lesca to stop the alarm."). The same night: "Maylis woke under her own lampshade. The clock said 02:54."
Correction: "Maylis woke under her own lampshade. Her phone said 02:54."

**11. Chapter 10 (which village).**
"Every morning she walked past his name, cut in capitals, with frost in the bottom of each letter."
Chapter 3: "The stone faced the square, so most days she walked past the back of it." The frost in the letters belongs to the one Saturday she went around to the front.
Correction: "Every morning she walked past the stone that carried his name in capitals."

**12. Chapter 2 (not yet introduced).**
"It lay still where the low fields should have been, from the foot of the bank to the far trees, with no ponies standing in it."
No ponies have been mentioned. They arrive in chapter 7 and are tied to her mother in chapter 8.
Correction: "…from the foot of the bank to the far trees, with none of her mother's ponies standing in it."

**13. Chapter 0 (which village: the care home).**
"At the end of the row was a care home with one light on over its stairs."
Everywhere else the care home is on one floor, with a corridor, a ramp and a step, and the light left on is the one over the back door (chapters 2, 11).
Correction: "At the end of the row was a care home with one light on over its back door."

**One query, not a finding.** Chapter 16, Sunday: "A man came in out of the rain." Chapter 8 ties the rain to Lesca, and she was sent home at 02:53 on Saturday. The text does not say whether Maylis sat in the chair on Saturday night, so this may hold. If the echo of chapter 1 is not needed, "A man came in out of the cold." closes it.

**Kinds with no findings.**

- **Dates and weekdays.** None. Every dated weekday is right for 2026. "Thirteen days away", "in ten days", "eleven days ago", "eight days ago", "every evening for ten days", "four weeks late", eleven years, twelve years, sixteen years, eighteen years and the ages (31 in 2014, 58, 94) all hold. "Nineteen days" of rain holds only if the count runs to the small hours of the 12th (24 November is day 1, 11 December is day 18).
- **Who knows what.** None.
- **Whose hand.** None. In every driven scene the owner keeps the right hand and the visitor has the left.
- **The river, levels.** In order in both villages: a metre under on 2 to 4 December, 55 under on 5 December (caught up), at the line on 8 December, 90 over at 2:53 on the 12th, 80 over and falling at 3:11. The only river finding is no. 4.
- **Numbers.** Forty houses, thirty-six and four, forty-two beds, forty-one, the eight residents, nineteen turns, nineteen bags, twenty-five minutes and ten, the kilometre (600 and 400), letters 6, 17, 41 and 42, the council votes (7, 6 and the chair; 11 and 1) all hold. The only number finding is no. 3.
- **Times inside each night.** In order, and 2:51 throughout. The only time finding is no. 9.

## 2. Proof

**Quotation marks: two.**

- Chapter 10. "By 4 it had stopped coming, and at breakfast they told me what had been done.'" The next paragraph is still Coralie: "'So for twelve years I've had two answers." With the mark closed it reads as Lesca's turn. Correction: drop the closing mark after "done.", as chapter 4 does for Lesca's three points.
- Chapter 11. "'Nobody. She was by herself.'" and then "'How many seats has your van got?' Maylis said." Two speeches by Maylis in a row; the second reads as Kévin's until the tag. Correction: "'Nobody. She was by herself. How many seats has your van got?'"

**A word used in two forms: six.**

- Chapter 6. "and a lock under the third board." Chapter 4 has "a padlock under the third board". Correction: "and a padlock under the third board."
- Chapter 16. "It stood in its box on the counter." Chapter 4 has "a crate on the counter". Correction: "It stood in its crate on the counter."
- Chapter 14. "and about the marker on the pillar of the old gate, which she had read at 8 every morning". Elsewhere it is "the marker post", and what the pillar carries is "a red line" (chapter 3). Correction: "and about the red line on the pillar of the old gate, which she had read at 8 every morning".
- Chapters 7 and 8. "on the polished floor, under long white lights" and "a polished wooden floor ran out from the steel under long white lights". The same hall is "waxed" and under "strip lights" in chapters 12 and 13. Correction: "on the waxed floor, under strip lights" and "a waxed wooden floor ran out from the steel under strip lights".
- Chapters 10 and 13. "the way she read from a patient's chart" and "the lift they used for getting a patient out of it". Chapters 7 and 8 say "resident". Correction: "a resident's chart" and "getting a resident out of it".
- Chapters 2 and 10, narration. "where the low fields should have been" and "the village field was a lake now". The narration calls this ground "the flood fields" everywhere else. Correction: "where the flood fields should have been" and "the flood fields were a lake now". (Madame Etcheto's "low fields" and Arlette's and Lesca's "village field" are speech and can stay.)

**A sentence that does not parse at first read: two.**

- Chapter 3. "'Nobody gives it that much to sleep, Maylis.'" It reads as if Inguma is given sleep. Correction: "'Nobody gives it that much just to sleep, Maylis.'"
- Chapter 14. "Lesca put the four sheets back together and left it unread." "It" has to reach back past "the four sheets" to the fourth. Correction: "Lesca put the four sheets back together and left the fourth unread."

**Spelling.** No finding. The spelling is British throughout (grey, colour, labour, neighbour, metre, pyjamas, programme, fuelled, shovelled, towards). Two words are American in a British-spelt book, if that matters: "baby carriage" (chapter 16, twice; British "pram") and "truck" (chapter 10 twice, chapter 13; British "lorry").

**Dates and clock times in words.** None. Screens, clocks and files use four figures (02:51, 18:40); the narration uses 2:51, at 5, at noon.

**Words the book no longer uses.** None. "Blind" appears once, in chapter 12 ("Lesca kept the foot down, blind."), and it is not the hut.

**A word repeated by accident, a missing word.** None.

**Names.** None. Every name is spelled one way. "Madame le Maire" with a capital appears once, in chapter 5, where the text corrects it.
