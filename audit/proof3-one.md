## 01 (2)

**1.1** Line 69. Kind 4, reads as an agreement error.
> Eighteen days was five trailers.

A length of time takes a singular verb, so this is correct. It looks like "Eggs is", which was changed in this chapter for the same reason.

Corrected: Eighteen days made five trailers.

**1.2** Line 73. Kind 7, tense.
> The roof came up to her shoulder. So had the stone.

"So had" answers a "had", and the sentence before it has "came". The reader has to supply "come up to her shoulder". The tense brief decided this one and the text still has the old sentence.

Corrected: The roof came up to her shoulder. The stone had come up to the same place.

## 02 (1)

**2.1** Line 135. Kind 5, "that box" points at nothing.
> And that box had been built to find out whether there were two of her.

Until here the room is only "a steel room the size of a garden shed" and "the steel room". The line that first called it a box was Coralie's in chapter 01 ("They shut you in a box once"), and it has been cut. The only boxes a reader has met are Kévin's cool boxes, earlier in this chapter. The café's "Still in the box, were you?" (05) and "The door of the box opened" (04) need the word to arrive here.

Corrected: And that steel box had been built to find out whether there were two of her.

## 03 (1)

**3.1** Line 215. Kind 4, "the sheet" reads as a bed sheet.
> When the light went out she got up, went down to the kitchen and unfolded the sheet on the table.

She has just got out of bed, and the sheet of paper was last named forty lines back.

Corrected: When the light went out she got up, went down to the kitchen and unfolded the sheet of paper on the table.

## 04 (2)

**4.1** Line 11. Kind 6, a word spelled two ways.
> On the last morning he told me to bring the horses round, in a voice I'd never heard out of him.

This sentence is new. Everywhere else in the book the word is "around" ("turned the pad around", "came around the church", "turned around on his stool"). The other three "round" in the book are the round reflector.

Corrected: On the last morning he told me to bring the horses around, in a voice I'd never heard out of him.

**4.2** Line 81. Kind 6, italics.
> She had written Either on the shopping pad on Tuesday, when she wanted the duck.

Words written on the pad are in italics every time: "she wrote *Either* across the bottom" (01), *You*, *Somebody else*, *soup*, *7*. The sentence used to say "that word". Spoken, the word stays plain ("to say Either").

Corrected: She had written *Either* on the shopping pad on Tuesday, when she wanted the duck.

## 05 (1)

**5.1** Line 199. Kind 5, "them" with no plural before it.
> 'Whatever I asked, I got an answer. One of them was Jeannot Castagnet, and one was what to do with forty-two people.'

"An answer" is singular. The nearest plural idea is the people she has had at her table ("I don't know who I've had at this table"), so "one of them was Jeannot Castagnet" reads for a moment as Jeannot sitting at it.

Corrected: 'Whatever I asked, I got an answer. One answer was Jeannot Castagnet, and one was what to do with forty-two people.'
