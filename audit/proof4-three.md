## Chapter 14 (1)

**14.1 (4, reads as a mistake: an old idiom; line 303).**
"By 4:10 the corridor was empty from the front doors to the fire door, but for one chair."
"But for" in the sense of "except for" is correct and old. A reader takes "but" as the start of a new clause and waits for a verb that does not come. It is the only "but for" in the book. The three words were added by an earlier correction.
Correction: "By 4:10 the corridor was empty from the front doors to the fire door, except for one chair."

## Chapter 16 (1)

**16.1 (5, an "it" beside Inguma; a corrected sentence; line 5).**
"'Inguma never came last night,' she said. 'I said my words, and I waited for it in bed until it was light. Where was it?'"
There are three "it"s in a row. The first and the third are Inguma. The second is the daylight, and it sits between them, so for a moment the sentence says that Inguma was light, of a thing that is known in this book by its weight. The sentence took this shape when "I lay there waiting" was reworded.
Correction: "'Inguma never came last night,' she said. 'I said my words, and I waited for it in bed until morning. Where was it?'"
