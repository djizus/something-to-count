## 01 (1)

**1.1** Line 59. Kind 6, a date written two ways.
> It was the 24th of November.

The sentence is correct English. It is listed because it is now the only date in the book written "the …th of". Every other day with its month is in the short form:

- narration: "On 8 December 2014" (03, line 161), "on the night of 11 December 2014" (04, line 89), "since 1 December" (17, line 17), "On Thursday 10 December" (10, line 3) and the other dated chapter openings;
- speech: "On 12 June 2014, from 2 until 5" (05, line 109), "on 1 December" (12, line 57), "on 1 January" and "From 1 January" (12, line 33), "on 24 November" (15, line 53, read aloud).

A day with no month is always "the 24th", "the 28th", "Friday the 11th", and the new sentence agrees with those.

This wording was chosen on purpose in the last change, so the choice between the two is JC's. There are two ways to make the book agree with itself.

Corrected, the smallest change (back to the form the rest of the book uses): It was 24 November.

Or the sentence stays, and the dates that are said aloud or thought with no year follow it. In these chapters that is one sentence, 05, line 109: 'For three hours,' Lesca said. 'On the 12th of June 2014, from 2 until 5. I've been on time ever since. I came at noon because you're all here at noon.' Outside these chapters it is 12, lines 33 (twice) and 57, and 17, line 17.
