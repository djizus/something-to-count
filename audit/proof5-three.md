## Chapter 12 (1)

**12.1 (5, an "it" that lost its owner; a phrase added to the sentence before; line 147).**
"'I've been told to stay by the gate, by the phone. And keep it shut.'"
The sentence used to be "I've been told to stay by the gate. And keep it shut." With "by the phone" added, the phone is the last thing named, so "keep it shut" reads for a moment as the phone. There is a second stumble in the same line: after "I've been told", the words "by the phone" can be taken for how she was told, and Larrère told her on the steps, to her face.
Correction: "'I've been told to stay by the gate and the phone. And keep the gate shut.'"

## Chapter 15 (1)

**15.1 (4, reads as a mistake: "in to" beside each other; line 11).**
"Somebody pulled a chair in to a table."
It is correct: the chair is pulled in, to a table. But "in to" in two words looks like "into" split by a slip, and "pulled a chair into a table" would be wrong, so the reader stops either way. The two other places with "in to" in the book are "gone in to work", which nobody misreads.
Correction: "Somebody pulled a chair up to a table."

## Chapter 16 (1)

**16.1 (5, an "it" with no owner, in a book where "it" is Inguma; line 105).**
"'It was in the care home before the council had its coats on,' Coralie said."
This is the first line of the scene, so "It" has nothing before it to point at. Coralie means the news of the vote. But in this book an "it" that is in a care home at night is Inguma, and this same chapter opens with "Where was it?" and "It was with me." The reader has to get to "the council" to find out which is meant.
Correction: "'The vote was in the care home before the council had its coats on,' Coralie said."
