## 06 (1)

**6.1** Line 173. Kind 5. The slightest of the three.
> If Inguma never came, all of this was hers: the desk, the stone, a square that said good morning.

The sentence before it is "'Council is at 6:30,' she said", where "she" is the secretary. The last woman named is Lesca ("beside Lesca's hand", line 169), and the desk and the stone are in fact Lesca's. Maylis was last named three paragraphs up. So "hers" has three possible owners, and the one who is meant is the furthest away. It is the same case as lines 149 and 163, which were mended for the same secretary.

Corrected: If Inguma never came, all of this was Maylis's: the desk, the stone, a square that said good morning.

## 07 (1)

**7.1** Line 83. Kind 5. A new sentence nearby took the owner away.
> 'From this handle to his trapdoor,' Lesca said. 'On foot, in this mud.'

Jeannot was last named thirty lines up, in the café (line 53). Since then the only man in the scene is Bordenave, who is standing at the bottom of his cellar steps (line 67) and is named again two paragraphs before this line, in a sentence that is new ("Bordenave would have called that weather", line 79). So "his" now reads as Bordenave's until the track and the hut arrive at line 91. This is the same word as the "his trapdoor" mended at line 105.

Corrected: 'From this handle to Castagnet's trapdoor,' Lesca said. 'On foot, in this mud.'

("Jean's trapdoor" would also do. Lesca calls him Jean to his face in 08 and Castagnet in her own thoughts in 11.)

## 09 (1)

**9.1** Line 87. Kind 5 (an "it" that can be taken for Inguma). A new sentence.
> 'This isn't your kitchen.' Lesca's fist closed. 'A week ago you asked me why it comes, and I told you I didn't know.'

This is the third "it" in five lines, and each one is a different thing. "It's the ti—" is the river. "It came out in my kitchen" is the sentence about the tide. "Why it comes" is the flood, and it points back to an exchange five chapters earlier (04, lines 95 to 105: "Why would it come now?", "Then why does it come?", "I don't know why it comes"). In this book the thing that "comes" every night is Inguma, and Inguma comes in through the door four lines later. A reader who does not remember the words of 04 will take Lesca to be saying that she does not know why Inguma comes.

Corrected: 'This isn't your kitchen.' Lesca's fist closed. 'A week ago you asked me why the river comes, and I told you I didn't know.'

## Two queries, not counted

**Who is in the chair when Inguma climbs into it.** 09, line 215: "Then she was in the chair, with Lesca beside her. Maylis stood up out of it and spoke first." Lesca is never said to stand. At line 247 Inguma "climbed into the chair and waited. It filled the seat from arm to arm." In the other three scenes in the box the chair is cleared first: 04 ("Then I'll stand", and Inguma "climbed into the empty chair"), 09, line 81 ("Lesca was already out of it, standing under the two lamps") and 13 ("Maylis got up first, and Lesca stood up after her"). If Lesca is on her feet by line 241 ("Behind Lesca, one wall of the steel room was gone"), it stands, but the text does not say so.
