## 06 (1)

**6.1** Line 69. Kind 4 (word order). A new sentence. Slight.
> She gave Inguma its work for the morning: once she was across, every metre of roadside she had cut in this village since 2008, to count before it came to take her home.

After the colon the reader expects the work and gets a clause about time. "Once she was across, every metre of roadside she had cut" then reads as the start of a new statement that is waiting for its verb, and none comes. "To count" arrives twenty words later and has to be carried back over the colon to "gave". Nothing is wrong, but it is the kind of sentence that gets read twice. Putting the verb first mends it, and says the same thing in the same order of events.

Corrected: She gave Inguma its work for the morning: to count every metre of roadside she had cut in this village since 2008, once she was across and before it came to take her home.

## One query, not counted

**Which chair Inguma climbs into.** 09, line 247: "Inguma came in over the step, climbed into the chair and waited. It filled the seat from arm to arm." Four lines up the text says "One chair was empty", meaning the place at the tables, and Lesca has just said "That's where I end up". For a moment "the chair" can be taken for that one. Line 251 ("towards the empty chair") puts it right. The text is not new and three passes have left it, so it is only noted.
