# Quirks

Read: `manuscript/00.md` to `16.md`, in order and in full, as the files stood at 20:19 on 4 October, after the changes for facts made that evening. Every quotation below was checked against those files.

The five kinds:

1. **Outside and inside at once.** A label or a held-back name for somebody the person whose head we are in knows by name.
2. **Decode.** A line whose meaning is not on the page at that moment.
3. **Comparison.** Two together, one that says less than the plain thing, one every book has used.
4. **Camera.** A place described from nowhere, or a room listed in a row.
5. **Built for effect.** A sentence a person telling this story would not write.

Each entry has one kind, the one a reader would name first. A second fault is said in the note. Speech is listed only under kind 2.

## Habits that run through the book

These are the classes. Close one and its instances go together. The instances worth changing are listed again under their chapters.

**A. The name held back, then given.** "A man … It was X." Five times: 01 (a man goes into the café), 10 (Darrigrand), 13 (Jeannot at the care home), 15 (Larrère at the garden gate), 16 (a man comes into the café). In a village of this size, seen by a woman who has worked in it for eighteen years, nobody is "a man". Start the sentence with the name.

**B. "The man" for Jeannot, once the choice is on the table.** From 09 to 13, whenever the gate is weighed against him, he turns into "a man", "the man", "the one man": 09, 10 (three times), 11 (twice), 12 (twice), 13 (four times). The reader hears a case being argued, in a book that has made him Jeannot of the six points. Coralie has the right version in 13: "The gate, or Castagnet?" Maylis thinks "Jeannot". Lesca thinks "Castagnet".

**C. Lesca's counterpart as "the other one", "that woman", "a woman with a cracked rib".** 03 and 04. The first line of 03 calls her Maylis. Use it.

**D. Larrère as "the man in the car", "the man behind the glass", "the man who paid her", "the mayor", "the member".** 01, 03, 04, 06, 15, 16. In 01 it is the postman's view of him, told inside her head.

**E. People she sees every day, with no name.** "The dealer" (05, 07, 16), "the secretary" (06, 10, 15, fifteen times), "the deputy mayor" (06, 14, 15), "the oldest member" (08). Everybody else in those rooms has a name. The book's own notes name two of them: Madame Ducasse, Monsieur Tastet. The dealer and the oldest member need one each.

**F. Everybody does everything the way she does her job.** Thirteen times:
03 "the way she used it in council"; 03 "that was how she laid everybody's wrist back"; 04 "in the voice she used for reading a dose off a chart"; 06 "the way she read a job sheet"; 07 "for as long as she would have given a pulse"; 08 "as she did for a resident's chart"; 08 "the way she counted a pulse against the clock"; 08 "the way she wrote out any job"; 10 "the way she read from a resident's chart"; 12 "in the voice she used for the prefecture"; 13 "the way she worked out a job"; 13 "the way he would have carried a sack of corn"; 16 "in the voice she called the numbers in".
Once it tells you who Coralie is. By the fifth the reader sees the pattern before the gesture. Keep 04 (the first), 12 and 16 (both are jokes), and 06. Cut the rest; the cuts are under their chapters.

**G. A feeling given a paragraph to itself.** "She was scared." (00 and 13), "She wanted to scream." (01), "She knew that smell." (01), "So it could be done." (03), "Maylis had lost." (07). The book has more than eighty one-sentence paragraphs of a few words each. Most are actions and are fine. These are the ones that state a feeling or a verdict and stand alone to be noticed.

**H. The verdict in two matched halves, closing a paragraph.** "That woman had the key. Lesca had the date." (03) "Now the file had a morning in it as well." (05) "For a week she had been afraid of the handle on that gate. Now she was more afraid of her own stairs." (05) "That made twice he had asked her about a kitchen. He hadn't asked once about a river." (06) "The mayor's vote had made the tie. Then it had broken it." (06) "She had rung it on the ends of Larrère's sentences for eleven years. Now it had rung on hers before she began one." (15). Each one stops the scene to make a remark about it.

**I. The short sentence that says the last one again.** "Lesca put it there." (07) "Her weight was on the handle." (11) "Coralie's list." (13) "And the coat." (06) "He let it." (05) "She left it like that." (10 and 11).

**J. Phrases that keep coming back.** "never once" seven times: twice in narration, and five times in speech from three different people. "one at a time" nine. "a long way off" five. "took her time" four. "with her back turned" four. "It was out before she…" three. None is wrong alone. Together they are one voice's stock, and every character has it.
