## Chapter 1 — 1

**1.1**

> 'One. A duck comes down onto standing water.'

- Kind: 1. I read Jeannot's paragraph twice.
- What I thought: he was still talking about the registered letters, so I did not know what "One" was the first of. The line about the hut being a cellar comes between the letters and the list, and the point of the list only arrives at "Three".
- What it meant: the list is a new complaint. Larrère had the gate raised to drain the water off his duck fields.
- Smallest change: let him name the subject before he counts. `'It's my water. One. A duck comes down onto standing water.'`

## Chapter 2 — 1

**2.1**

> The one it cannot take home dies in her own bed.

- Kind: 1, and 5.
- What I thought: "takes the sleeper away with it" meant the woman was carried off, body and all. So a woman who is not taken home could not be in her own bed. I read the three sentences twice and went on unsure.
- What it meant: only the sleeper goes. Her body stays in the bed with its eyes open, and it dies there if she is not brought back. I understood this in chapter 6, when Coralie watches the pulse go down.
- Smallest change: say that the body stays. `Some nights it takes the sleeper away with it and leaves her body in the bed. Before morning it takes her home. The one it cannot take home dies in that bed.`

## Chapter 6 — 1

**6.1**

> 'Since nobody's ringing, I'll say what Josiane says at her counter.'

- Kind: 1.
- What I thought: a phone. The scene just before this one ends on Coralie with her thumb over the doctor's number.
- What it meant: the bell of the kitchen timer, which Maylis is holding down.
- Smallest change: `'Since nobody's ringing that bell, I'll say what Josiane says at her counter.'`

## Chapter 9 — 3

**9.1**

> 'So I'm asking the one who said it. That wasn't your voice.'

- Kind: 3.
- What I thought: "the one who said it" and "your" were the same person, so the second sentence took back the first.
- What it meant: Coralie is speaking to Lesca and asking for Maylis.
- Smallest change: swap the two sentences. `'That wasn't your voice. So I'm asking the one who said it.'`

**9.2**

> 'Since the night of the 23rd. That was my first night on my back. It was her first night in, and it started raining.'

- Kind: 2. I went back to chapters 1 and 2 to check the date.
- What I thought: an earlier night that I had missed. Until here the start is always "Tuesday" or "the 24th" (the heading of chapter 1, "on Tuesday at 3 in the morning", "It's rained here since Tuesday", "Since the 24th"). Chapter 2 also says somebody had been under there with her "since Tuesday night", which is the second night.
- What it meant: the night chapter 1 opens on, Monday the 23rd running into 2:51 on Tuesday.
- Smallest change: name the day, as everywhere else. `'Since the night of Monday the 23rd.'` The "since Tuesday night" in chapter 2 still pulls the other way.

**9.3**

> 'I've been fighting it since the rib, and all that time it was doing my job.'

- Kind: 2. I went back to the rules in chapter 4.
- What I thought: that fighting Inguma had been a mistake from the start. But six lines above she says she kept Lesca out for two nights and the road dried, and she did that by pushing Inguma off.
- What it meant: Inguma does two things. Lying under it is the way to the chair, so pushing it off keeps Lesca out. Once Lesca is in, it is what takes her home. Maylis means only the second.
- Smallest change: `'Once she's in, it's what takes her home. I've been fighting it since the rib, and half the time it was doing my job.'`

## Chapter 11 — 1

**11.1**

> She had left hers there. She was going over to get this one out.

- Kind: 1, with some of 3 (which village's Castagnet).
- What I thought: I looked back for what "hers" stood for, and "this one" pointed at a man who is "over there".
- What it meant: she left her own Castagnet at the end of the track; she is going over to get Maylis's Castagnet out.
- Smallest change: `She had left her own Castagnet there. She was going over to get the other one out.`

## Chapter 14 — 1

**14.1**

> 'She said the left side, front to back, and they go in whichever one has room.'

- Kind: 1.
- What I thought: whichever chair, or whichever room. The two vehicles were last named a page earlier.
- What it meant: the van or the minibus.
- Smallest change: `'She said the left side, front to back, and they go in the van or the minibus, whichever has room.'`
