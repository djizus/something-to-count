4. a word I would have to look up

## 00

**0.1** Kind 1.

> When she climbed onto the platform and pointed her torch at the water, it was going past at the height of her knees, close enough to touch.

I saw her standing in the river up to her knees. Then I saw that she was dry, on boards, so the water had to be beside her or under her, and I could not tell which. The same measure comes back in chapter 12 ("It went past the platform at the height of her knees"), where she is standing on the bank beside the van, and I stopped there again. Later lines ("the river went by under the boards", "the river going by at the height of the boards") gave me the picture.

Change: measure from the boards, not from her. "…it was going past just under the boards, close enough to touch." The same in chapter 12.

## 1

**1.1** Kind 1.

> He nodded towards the flood fields, where he shot ducks from his hut, a cellar under a trapdoor in the grass.

A hut, for me, stands above the ground. I read it twice to see that the hut is the cellar. Chapter 7 shows it clearly, but that is six chapters on.

Change: "…where he shot ducks from his hut. It was a cellar dug into the field, under a trapdoor in the grass."

**1.2** Kind 1.

> On Monday the river was lower than my fields. You put the gate up, and my water ran out through it into the river.

Two pages before, the gate "was there to let the river out onto those fields". Here raising it empties the fields. I read the speech twice to see that the gate runs both ways, towards whichever side is lower, and that Jeannot wants water on his fields.

Change: one short sentence in his mouth before it. "Two. A gate runs both ways. On Monday the river was lower than my fields."

**1.3** Kind 5.

> Sixteen years, and I've had one straight answer out of you. The answer was me.

I took "the answer was me" to mean that Coralie had been the one to give the answer. Six lines later, in the festival scene, it means that Maylis once said "You".

Change: "Sixteen years, and you've told me once what you wanted. It was me." This says no more than the line already means to say.

## 2

**2.1** Kind 1.

> She said he had put it off its count, and it came at 1.

I read "put off" as "delayed", which gives the opposite sense. It means he made it lose count, so it finished early.

Change: "She said he had made it lose its count, and it came at 1."

**2.2** Kind 1.

> Kévin had told her how to get in: lie still under Inguma.

The sentence before is "There was still the barn, where the smell came from", so "get in" read as getting into the barn. It means getting into the waking dream.

Change: "Kévin had told her the way into the dream: lie still under Inguma."

**2.3** Kind 5.

> 'Come back across,' her mouth said.

I thought the other one was telling Maylis to cross back between the two places. Chapter 3 shows it was said to Coralie, and that "across" is the landing.

Change: give the mouth the whole sentence it says in chapter 3. "'Come back across. I've left you on that landing eleven years,' her mouth said." It stays strange, because no one in this house sleeps across a landing, but I would not misread who it is for.

## 3

**3.1** Kind 3.

> Under them the right wrist began to pull away. Lesca wasn't the one pulling.

For a moment I thought Coralie was pulling her fingers back. It is Maylis pulling her own wrist out from under them. The rule that the owner keeps her right hand only comes in chapter 4.

Change: "Lesca wasn't the one pulling. Maylis was."

**3.2** Kind 1.

> 'Under his name it reads that he was saved, by her.'

I read this exchange and the "Opened" one twice. I had to build the stone in my head: his name, then the verb, then her name, read downwards.

Change: let her read the three lines out. "'Castagnet, Saved, Lesca. It reads that he was saved, by her.'"

**3.3** Kind 3.

> She had the key, but only Lesca had the date.

"She" comes straight after "as he had rung Lesca", and I took it for Lesca.

Change: "Maylis had the key, but only Lesca had the date."

## 4

**4.1** Kind 1.

> If one of us stays sitting in this chair when the other wakes up, the one who stayed wakes up in the other one's body, and she drives it.

Three "one"s and two "other"s. I read it twice, and it was the next sentence, the example, that made it clear.

Change: put the example first. "I stayed sitting this morning when you woke up. So I woke up in your body, and I drove it. It works the same for either of us."

## 5

**5.1** Kind 4.

> The prefecture at Pau sends you a yellow warning.

"Prefecture", and later "prefect". I guessed "some higher office" and read on, but I would have to look up what it is and why it outranks a mayor.

Change: gloss it once, here. "The prefecture at Pau, the State's office over all these villages, sends you a yellow warning."

## 6

**6.1** Kind 4.

> At 4 that afternoon Adjudant Sarthou sat her down at the window table of the café and laid a form straight in front of him.

"Adjudant" is not an English word I know. I took it for a first name, then for a rank.

Change: drop it. Chapter 5 has already told me he is the gendarme. "At 4 that afternoon Sarthou sat her down…"

**6.2** Kind 2.

> She gave it every metre of roadside she had cut in this village since 2008.

Up to here, giving it something to count kept it away: that is how Madame Etcheto uses the words, and how Maylis tried them in chapter 2. So I thought it would not come at 2:51 and she would never reach the chair. I went back to the three points in chapter 4 and they did not settle it. It turns out, in chapter 9, that the count only sets when it takes the visitor home.

Change: three words. "For the way home she gave it every metre of roadside she had cut in this village since 2008." That tells me what the count is for without saying what she finds out in chapter 9.

**6.3** Kind 1.

> "A man can no longer." The timer rang there. He can no longer stand at his own gate without Pau watching.

I read "He" as some other man. It is Larrère finishing the sentence the timer cut.

Change: "…The timer rang there. It ends: stand at his own gate without Pau watching."

**6.4** Kind 3.

> In her own bedroom the clock radio said 18:40.

The section before is Maylis in the council room, in Lesca's village, in Lesca's body. "Her own bedroom" could be either woman's, in either village. The earlier cut used "At home it was raining" and I had no trouble.

Change: "At home the clock radio said 18:40."

## 7

**7.1** Kind 2.

> Those were the two days without rain.

"Those" points back at "Wednesday and Thursday night". But Wednesday was the day Maylis spent in the other village, and I remembered rain at home that day. I went back to chapter 6 to check: yes, "At home it was raining." The dry days are the ones after those two nights.

Change: name them. "Thursday and Friday had been the two days without rain."

## 8

**8.1** Kind 3.

> 'Thirty-four. Three, four.'

The chapter before ends with Lesca timing the walk and saying "Twenty-five". So I heard this as Lesca again, timing the walk back. Two sentences later it is Marie-Jo calling bingo.

Change: move one sentence up. Open the section with "That evening was bingo night in the village hall.", then the call.

**8.2** Kind 3.

> From the chair she couldn't hear the breath any more, so she got up.

By now "the chair" means the steel chair. For a moment I had Arlette in the steel room.

Change: "From her chair by the bed she couldn't hear the breath any more, so she got up."

## 9

**9.1** Kind 1.

> 'The thing that sits on me. I've been fighting it since the rib, and all that time it was doing my job.'

I read it twice, because in chapter 7 fighting it was exactly what kept Lesca out. Both are true: it carries Maylis to the chair, and it also takes Lesca home. The line only gives the second half.

Change: say the half that is missing. "'The thing that sits on me. It's what takes her home. I've been fighting it since the rib, and all that time it was doing my job.'"

**9.2** Kind 1.

> 'Three against three,' Larrère said. 'Then the hunters have the last vote. Castagnet. Those against.'

Six members sit at the card table and Jeannot is on his stool, so I had him down as a man watching. I went back up the scene to see if I had missed him joining. He holds a seventh vote, for the hunters.

Change: where the scene places him. "Jeannot stayed on his stool. He had the hunters' vote."

## 12

**12.1** Kind 4.

> *Flood watch, Adour, orange.*

"Adour" has not been said before. The river has only been "the river". I guessed it was the river's name.

Change: "*Flood watch, river Adour, orange.*"

**12.2** Kind 1.

> From the step Maylis could see along the old wing, up the ramp at its far end, through the open fire door and on down the main corridor, where the night shift had lined up the wheelchairs.

Five places in one sentence, and this is the layout the whole of chapter 14 stands on. I read it twice to get the order: back door, old wing, ramp up, fire door, main corridor, front.

Change: two sentences. "From the step Maylis could see the whole length of the building. The old wing came first. At its far end a ramp went up through the open fire door into the main corridor, where the night shift had lined up the wheelchairs."

**12.3** Kind 1.

> He had said he would do the six months, and she wasn't going to hand them to him.

I read the paragraph twice to find why turning the handle would cost Kévin six months. I think it is Larrère's threat: if the gate moves, the sheet with Kévin's name goes to the prosecutor. That was said forty lines earlier as "You know what's under the letter", and I had to go and join the two.

Change: name the sheet. "If that gate moved, Larrère would send the sheet with his name on it. He had said he would do the six months, and she wasn't going to hand them to him."

**12.4** Kind 1.

> Four. The steel plate was cold against her raincoat.

"The steel plate" read as part of the van. It is Inguma's back, the grey steel under the leaves from chapter 1. It has not been called a plate before.

Change: "Four. Its steel back was cold against her raincoat."
