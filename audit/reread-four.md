# Every place I went back — reader four

Read 00 to 17 in order, once, at thriller speed. Listed below is each place I had to go back. Nothing is listed that I read once and only disliked.

The five kinds:

1. read twice to understand
2. went back to an earlier page to check
3. lost, for a moment, the speaker, the head, the village or the hand
4. a word I would have to look up
5. understood only later

## 1

**1.1** — "You put the gate up, and my water ran out into it."

- Kind: 1.
- What I thought: the opening had just taught me the gate lets the river out onto the fields. Here the water goes the other way, and "it" could be the gate or the river. I read the three sentences again to see which way the water ran.
- What it meant: the river was lower than his fields, so the open gate drained his fields into the river.
- Change: "You put the gate up, and my water ran out through it into the river."

**1.2** — "'He never said he'd tell him.'"

- Kind: 1.
- What I thought: Coralie was denying it. "He did not say that." But I had just read him say it, so I went back up to the square to check, and then Maylis seemed to be correcting her.
- What it meant: she is amazed. "He didn't really say that!"
- Change: "'He said he'd tell him?'"

## 2

**2.1** — "'It has to count the leaves in the square.'" (then "'Before it comes.'" twice, none of the three with a name)

- Kind: 3.
- What I thought: the line before it is about Coralie, so I gave the first line to Coralie, and then could not place the other two.
- What it meant: Maylis says it, Madame Etcheto corrects her, Maylis repeats.
- Change: "'It has to count the leaves in the square,' Maylis said." and "'Before it comes,' said Madame Etcheto."

**2.2** — "That left the barn, where the smell came from. Kévin had told her the way in."

- Kind: 1.
- What I thought: two things at once. The card two lines above says *left*, so "That left the barn" read for a second like the lamp again. Then "the way in" I took for a way into the building, a door or a loose board, and I expected her to drive there.
- What it meant: the barn was the thing still to look at, and the way in was Kévin's way into a dream: lie still under the thing.
- Change: "What remained was the barn, where the smell came from. Kévin had told her how to get in: lie still under it."

## 3

**3.1** — "'It sits under his name. It reads that she saved him.'"

- Kind: 1.
- What I thought: with the verb between the two names I read the stone downwards as "Jean Castagnet saved Maylis Lesca", which is he saved her. Marie-Jo says the opposite, so I went back to work out where the verb sits. The same with "It reads that she opened him."
- What it meant: under his name the verb reads as something done to him, by the name below.
- Change: "'Saved who? Under his name it reads that he was saved, by her.'" and "'Opened what? Under his name it reads that he was opened.'"

## 4

**4.1** — "She had Maylis's hands too, on the steel arms of the chair, with the right one closed in a fist."

- Kind: 2.
- What I thought: a slip. The chapter before closes her left hand twice, the one that held Coralie's fingers. I went back to check which hand it had been.
- What it meant: this is another hand, closed for another reason, which the next chapter gives.
- Change: "with the right one closed in a fist and the left one open." That tells me the choice of hand is meant, and nothing more.

**4.2** — "On wet mornings Larrère held that folder over his head when he crossed the square to the town hall."

- Kind: 2.
- What I thought: in chapter 1 he spends every morning in his car, parked at the town hall door, until it opens at 2. So I went back to check whether he ever walks across the square in a morning.
- What it meant: only that the folder is his and I should know it by its rain mark.
- Change: "On wet days Larrère held that folder over his head between his car and the town hall door."

## 5

**5.1** — "The catch missed its tooth, the gate dropped on its chain and the handle came spinning back."

- Kind: 4.
- What I thought: I know "catch" as a verb and "tooth" as a tooth. I could not picture what missed what, and went back two paragraphs to "the clicks of the catch that held it".
- What it meant: the metal piece that stops the chain running back slipped off the wheel.
- Change: "The catch slipped, the gate dropped on its chain and the handle came spinning back." Chapter 10 already says it that way and I read it once there.

## 6

**6.1** — "He's done it to you, and you've come in here and done it to me."

- Kind: 2.
- What I thought: that Jeannot had watched the scene at the car that morning, as he watched from his van in chapter 1. I went back to look for him in it. He is not there.
- What it meant: he is guessing from the way she asks.
- Change: cut the first half. "'That's how Larrère asks for a thing, and you've come in here and done it to me.'"

**6.2** — "So Maylis wrote *Back at 2* on the shopping pad and stood the pad against the clock radio."

- Kind: 1.
- What I thought: she lies down at 11 and gives it eight hours, so I added and got 7 in the morning. Then I tried 2 in the night. Neither fitted.
- What it meant: 2 in the afternoon, eight hours after she wakes over there at 6. I had it only when Coralie waits for 2 at 10 in the morning.
- Change: "*Back at 2 this afternoon*", or one clause before it: "eight hours, counted from 6, when the mayor woke."

**6.3** — "I'm told I wave."

- Kind: 1.
- What I thought: nothing. Four words with no object, straight after "without Pau watching". I read the speech again from "A man can no longer."
- What it meant: Pau watches the film from the gate cameras and has told him he waves at them.
- Change: "They tell me I wave at the camera."

**6.4** — "She was five hours late."

- Kind: 3.
- What I thought: the thing in the corridor is what has just arrived, and it is the one that is late, so "She" stopped me. Inguma is "it" everywhere else.
- What it meant: either Maylis, five hours past what her pad said, or the thing. I still am not sure which.
- Change: "It was five hours late."

## 7

**7.1** — "Lesca waited for the second hand to reach the top, then walked her down off the bank and onto the track."

- Kind: 1.
- What I thought: in a book where the left hand and the right hand belong to two different women, "the second hand" was a hand. I looked for what it was reaching up to.
- What it meant: the thin hand of the watch that counts seconds.
- Change: "Lesca waited for the watch to reach the full minute, then walked her down off the bank and onto the track."

## 8

**8.1** — "'One. It's Jeannot. Two. Jean's for the stone.'"

- Kind: 1.
- What I thought: that he knew about the granite stone in the other village, the one with JEAN CASTAGNET on it. That would change everything, so I read back through the scene to see who had told him.
- What it meant: Jean is the name for his own gravestone one day.
- Change: "'Two. Jean's for my gravestone.'"

## 9

**9.1** — "'I had it at work by 5, and I told them no.'"

- Kind: 1.
- What I thought: that somebody at work had asked her for something and she had refused.
- What it meant: she told them it couldn't be true.
- Change: "'I had it at work by 5, and I didn't believe it.'"

**9.2** — "'That's where I sit if I stay with you,' Lesca said." with "'The old woman in room twelve told you who it's for.'"

- Kind: 2.
- What I thought: if she stays with Maylis she is in Maylis's body, so how does she sit at a table of the dead? The line sends me back to room twelve, seven chapters earlier, and I went.
- What it meant: the one Inguma cannot take home dies in her own bed, and that one gets the place at the table.
- Change: let Lesca say the rule here. "'The old woman in room twelve told you. The one it can't take home dies in her own bed, and they lay her a place.'"

## 12

**12.1** — "She wouldn't put him at it."

- Kind: 1.
- What I thought: "put him at it" gave me nothing the first time. With the next sentence, "she wasn't going to hand them to him", I had two lines of small words and had to read both again to find who would do what.
- What it meant: she would not ask Kévin to turn the handle, because that would cost him the six months.
- Change: "She wouldn't ask him to."

## 14

**14.1** — "She started the engine and drove back along the top of the bank, away from the gate."

- Kind: 2.
- What I thought: the chapter before stops the van past the gate, with its front wheels on the slope of the track. "Away from the gate" then sounded like further on, towards the hut. A paragraph later she is at the patch, and after that the gate lamp comes up beside her again. I went back to the end of 13 to find where the van was and which way it faced.
- What it meant: she backs off the slope, goes back past the gate towards the Port, looks at the patch, then drives past the gate a second time to reach the track.
- Change: "She started the engine, backed up onto the bank and drove back past the gate, towards the Port. Two hundred metres beyond it, where the road came up, she got out with the torch."

## 15

**15.1** — "'I knew that he was,' she said."

- Kind: 1.
- What I thought: "was" what? The sentence it answers is two paragraphs up, and between them is a paragraph about a different sentence, the one she does not read. I went back up to find the end of hers.
- What it meant: I knew he was at his hut.
- Change: "'I knew he was there,' she said."

## 16

**16.1** — "As Madame Etcheto's went by, she put her hand on the wheel and stopped it."

- Kind: 3.
- What I thought: Lesca is the subject of the sentence before, so I saw Lesca's hand stop the chair. The next line, "It never came last night", then seemed to be Lesca's.
- What it meant: Madame Etcheto stops her own chair and speaks.
- Change: "As Madame Etcheto's went by, the old woman put her hand on the wheel and stopped it."
