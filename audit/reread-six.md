# Reread audit — six

Read `manuscript/00.md` to `17.md` once, in order, at reading speed. Each entry is a place where I went back. Places I read once and merely disliked are not listed.

The five kinds:

1. read a sentence or paragraph twice to understand it
2. went back to an earlier page to check a fact, a rule, a name or a date
3. did not know for a moment who was speaking, whose head, which village, whose hand
4. a word I would have to look up
5. understood only later what a line had meant

## 1

**1.1** — kind 1

> She dug graves and lifted sacks of cement for a living, but never off her own chest.

What I thought: "off her chest" is the idiom for confessing something, so I read it as "she never spoke about herself" and the sentence stopped making sense beside the sacks.
What it meant: she lifts heavy things all day, but has never had to lift one that was lying on her.
Change: "She lifted sacks of cement for a living, but she had never had to lift one off her own chest."

**1.2** — kind 2

> You put the gate up, and my water ran out through it into the river.

What I thought: the opening pages say the gate is there "to let the river out onto those fields". So raising it should have put water on his fields. I went back to the opening to check.
What it meant: the gate is a door between river and fields, and water goes to whichever side is lower. On Monday the river was the lower side.
Change: "You put the gate up, and my water ran out through it the other way, down into the river."

**1.3** — kind 5

> Sixteen years, and I've had one straight answer out of you. The answer was me.

What I thought: that Coralie had been the one who answered, in Maylis's place.
What it meant: the one time Maylis said what she wanted, the word she said was "You". I only had it after the chairs.
Change: "Sixteen years, and you've told me what you want once. You said me."

## 2

**2.1** — kind 1 and 4

> She said he had put it off its count, and it came at 1.

What I thought: "put off" as in postponed, so the count was delayed, which should make the thing come later, not at 1.
What it meant: he made it lose its place in the count, so it came two hours early.
Change: "She said he had made it lose its count, and it came at 1 instead of 3."

**2.2** — kind 1

> It hasn't come for you, girl. It comes to that bed.

What I thought: "that bed" was the one Madame Etcheto was lying in, the only bed in the scene.
What it meant: Maylis's bed at home. The next sentence and Coralie's question settle it.
Change: "It comes to your bed."

**2.3** — kind 1

> Kévin had told her how to get in: lie still under Inguma.

What I thought: after "There was still the barn", I read "get in" as getting into the barn building, and lying still made no sense as a way through a door.
What it meant: how to get into the waking dream.
Change: "Kévin had told her how to get into the dream: lie still under Inguma."

## 3

**3.1** — kind 3

> Under them the right wrist began to pull away.

What I thought: for a moment I had lost which hand was on top of which. "Them" could have been Lesca's own fingers.
What it meant: Coralie's two fingers are on the right wrist, Lesca's left hand is over them, and Maylis is pulling the wrist out from underneath.
Change: "Under Coralie's fingers the right wrist began to pull away."

**3.2** — kind 3

> She had the key, but only Lesca had the date.

What I thought: every "she" in this chapter is Lesca, so I read "She had the key" as Lesca and then met Lesca's name in the second half.
What it meant: Maylis has the key.
Change: "Maylis had the key, but only Lesca had the date."

## 4

> Coralie took her finger off the first box and left it on the second.

What I thought: two lines earlier she puts a finger on each box and asks "The holding, or the getting away?", so I took the boxes to stand for those two now. I went back up to see what was written beside each.
What it meant: the boxes still say *You* and *Somebody else*, and she leaves her finger on *Somebody else*.
Change: "Coralie took her finger off *You* and left it on *Somebody else*."

**4.2** — kind 1

> 'With him sitting in the café? Somebody should go in and tell him.'

What I thought: tell him that a woman is carrying flowers to his wife.
What it meant: tell him he is dead, because those are grave flowers.
Change: "Somebody should go in and tell him he's dead."

## 6

**6.1** — kind 4

> At 4 that afternoon Adjudant Sarthou sat her down at the window table of the café and laid a form straight in front of him.

"Adjudant" is not in an English dictionary. I guessed a rank. He has already been introduced as "Sarthou, the gendarme".
Change: cut the word. "At 4 that afternoon Sarthou sat her down…"

**6.2** — kind 3

> In her own bedroom the clock radio said 18:40.

What I thought: the line before is in Lesca's council room, with Maylis inside Lesca, so "her own bedroom" could be either woman's. I took it for Lesca's.
What it meant: Maylis's bedroom, where Coralie is keeping the chart. Chapter 8 does the same cut with the name in it.
Change: "In Maylis's own bedroom the clock radio said 18:40."

## 7

**7.1** — kind 2

> Those were the two days without rain.

What I thought: the sentence before names Wednesday and Thursday night, so I took the two dry days to be Wednesday and Thursday. But I remembered rain on Wednesday ("At home it was raining") and went back to check.
What it meant: the days that followed those two nights, Thursday and Friday.
Change: "The two days after those nights, Thursday and Friday, were the only dry ones."

## 8

**8.1** — kind 3

> From the chair she couldn't hear the breath any more, so she got up.

## 9

**9.1** — kind 2

> 'The thing that sits on me. I've been fighting it since the rib, and all that time it was doing my job.'

What I thought: pushing it off was exactly how she kept Lesca out for two nights, so fighting it was her job. I went back to the three points in chapter 4.
What it meant: the same thing that lets Lesca in also comes back to take her home, and Maylis means the second.
Change: "'The thing that sits on me. It's what takes her home. I've been fighting it since the rib.'"

**9.2** — kind 1

> 'She was in for a day and a night. If she gets in again on Friday night, there may be no hand to come back to.'

What I thought: that the hand itself would be hurt or lost.
What it meant: the longer Lesca is in, the weaker the one part Maylis keeps, and after another night she might not get even that back.
Change: "'If she gets in again on Friday night, I may not get even the hand back.'"

**9.3** — kind 3

> At 3 that afternoon the tide brought the river up to her father's line.

What I thought: the last woman named is Coralie, at the bedroom door, so "her father" read as Coralie's.
What it meant: Maylis's father, who painted the red line.
Change: put Maylis first. "At 3 that afternoon Maylis walked along the bank to the patch. The tide had brought the river up to her father's line."

**9.4** — kind 1

> 'Three against three,' Larrère said. 'Then the hunters have the last vote. Castagnet. Those against.'

What I thought: the association is "six members" at the card table and all six had voted. I did not know who "the hunters" were or why Jeannot, on his stool, had a vote. I read the paragraph above again.
What it meant: there is a seventh vote, for the hunters, and Jeannot holds it.
Change: "'Three against three. The seventh vote is the hunters', and Castagnet has it. Those against.'"

**9.5** — kind 1

> She saw the pad lying face down and read it, standing under the lamp.

What I thought: a pad lying face down can't be read.
What it meant: the list is on the back, so face down is the side with the writing up.
Change: "She saw the writing on the back of the pad and read it, standing under the lamp."

## 12

**12.1** — kind 1

> From the step Maylis could see along the old wing, up the ramp at its far end, through the open fire door and on down the main corridor, where the night shift had lined up the wheelchairs.

Four places in one sentence. I read it twice to build the building, and I needed it right for chapter 14.
Change: three sentences. "From the step Maylis could see along the old wing. At its far end a ramp went up to a fire door, which stood open. Beyond that was the main corridor, where the night shift had lined up the wheelchairs."

**12.2** — kind 1

> It hadn't changed, as it would have in a dream.

What I thought: "as it would have" can be read as "just as in a dream", which says the opposite.
What it meant: in a dream it would have changed, and it did not.
Change: "In a dream it would have changed. It hadn't."

**12.3** — kind 1 and 2

> He had said he would do the six months, and she wasn't going to hand them to him.

What it meant: if the gate moves, Larrère sends the sheet with Kévin's name on it, whoever turns the handle. She will not make Kévin the one who sets that off.
Change: "He had said he would do the six months, and she wasn't going to have him turn the handle that sent him there."
