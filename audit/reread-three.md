# Reread audit: three

Read 00 to 17 in order, once, at thriller speed. Below is every place I went back. Nothing here is a judgement of the story.

The five kinds:

1. read a sentence or paragraph twice
2. went back to an earlier page to check a fact, rule, name or date
3. did not know who was speaking, whose head, which village, or whose hand
4. a word I would have to look up
5. understood only later what the line meant

## 01

**1.1** "You put the gate up, and my water ran out into it."

- Kind 2. I went back to the opening pages, which say the gate "was there to let the river out onto those fields".
- I thought: raising the gate puts water on the fields, so how did raising it take his water away? And "it" could be the gate or the river.
- It meant: the river was lower than the fields that day, so the open gate drained the fields into the river.
- Change: "You put the gate up, and my water ran out through it, into the river."

**1.2** "Rain fell on the card and the ink of the L ran. The one on the stone wouldn't."

- Kind 1.
- I thought "the one" was the card, or the ink.
- It meant: the L cut into the stone would not run in the rain.
- Change: "The L cut in the stone wouldn't run."

**1.3** "'He never said he'd tell him.'"

- Kind 3.
- I did not know who was speaking, and I read it as a plain statement that Larrère had not said it, which is the opposite of what the scene showed.
- It meant: Coralie, not believing it.
- Change: "'He said he'd tell him?' Coralie said."

**1.4** "'In my tray,' she said, and shut the door on him."

- Kind 1.
- I looked for who "him" was. Coralie is playing Larrère, so "him" cannot be Larrère, and there is no other man in the kitchen.
- It meant: she shut the oven door the way he shuts his window.
- Change: "'In my tray,' she said, and shut the oven door."

## 02

**2.1** "'I did a rib,' she said."

- Kind 1.
- Kévin has asked "Have you?", meaning has she pushed the thing off. I thought she was changing the subject, then that she was saying the pushing broke the rib, which is wrong: the handle did that on Monday.
- It meant, I think: yes, I pushed, and with a cracked rib. I am still not sure.
- Change: "'I pushed,' she said. 'On a cracked rib.'"

**2.2** "That left the barn, where the smell came from."

- Kind 1.
- This comes two lines after the card that says *left* and the line "That was the lamp she had seen." I read "left" as the lamp again.
- It meant: the barn was the one thing still to look into.
- Change: "There was still the barn, where the smell came from."

**2.3** "Kévin had told her the way in."

- Kind 2. I went back to Kévin in the plane tree.
- I thought he had told her how to get into the barn, the building.
- It meant: his line "The thing on your chest is the way in", the way into the waking dream.
- Change: "Kévin had told her how to get there: lie still under the thing."

## 03

**3.1** "By then it was no use to her."

- Kind 3.
- The sentence before has both women in it: "Two words were all Maylis could take before Lesca had the jaw back." I took "her" for Maylis.
- It meant: Lesca had the jaw again, too late to say anything with it.
- Change: "By then it was no use to Lesca."

## 04

**4.1** "She had Maylis's hands too, on the steel arms of the chair, with the right one closed in a fist."

- Kind 2. I went back to chapter 3, where it is Lesca's left hand that stays closed ("her left hand closed, as if those fingers were still in it").
- I thought the book had changed hands by mistake.
- It turned out, in chapter 5, that the right fist is a second, separate thing.
- Change: name both hands, so the reader sees the difference is meant. "The left lay open on the arm of the chair. The right was closed in a fist."

**4.2** "If one of us stays sitting in this chair when the other wakes up, she wakes up in the other one's body, and she drives it."

- Kind 1.
- "She" can be either woman. I read it as the one who wakes.
- It meant: the one who stays sitting.
- Change: "If one of us stays sitting in this chair when the other wakes up, the one who stayed wakes up too, in the other's body, and she drives it."

## 05

**5.1** "Lesca counted the clicks of the catch that held it." And, lower down: "The catch missed its tooth, the gate dropped on its chain and the handle came spinning back."

- Kind 4. "Catch" as a part of a machine.
- I guessed a lock.
- It meant: the piece of steel that drops into a toothed wheel and stops the chain running back.
- Change, at the first mention only: "Lesca counted the clicks of the catch, the steel tooth that stopped the chain running back."

**5.2** "Maylis had been giving that gap the same word for eighteen years, so the window was already on its way back up to meet it."

- Kind 1.
- I could not see what the window was meeting. I thought the gap.
- It meant: he expected "Either" and had already started to close the window on it.
- Change: "Maylis had answered that gap with the same word for eighteen years. He was already closing the window on it."

**5.3** "'What did you say to her?'" with, in the same speech, "And this morning the village's girl knows what I'm going to say on a Tuesday."

- Kind 5.
- I thought he was angry about something Maylis had told Madame Castagnet. Then the second sentence points the other way, to something Madame Castagnet told Maylis.
- I understood in chapter 6, when he asks "What did she tell you?": he is afraid his own words came out of that kitchen.
- Change: use the chapter 6 question here too. "'What did she tell you?'"

**5.4** "When Lesca did find something to say to him, it was a bill she had from Maylis."

- Kind 1.
- I thought Maylis had handed her a bill, on paper.
- It meant: a debt she found in Maylis's memory.
- Change: "When Lesca did find something to say to him, it was an old bill out of Maylis's memory."

**5.5** "I asked five things and I got five answers."

- Kind 2. I went back up the scene and counted.
- I found four questions at that table ("What did he do?", "So which Castagnet is it?", "What else?", "And who do I say told me?"). I could only make five by splitting one, or by going back to breakfast.
- Change: take the number out. "Everything I asked, I got an answer."
