# Round 10: Comedy

Read: `manuscript/00.md` to `18.md`, in order, in full. Nothing else.

## 1. Scores

- **Stakes: 8.** "Behind it were forty houses, and the home, with forty-two people in their beds. Out on the meadows there was one man." The second stake is nastier and just as clear: "It's a life in one hand."
- **Tension: 7.** "The dyke had six minutes, and on foot she could reach one of the two." Chapters 13 to 15 earn it. Chapters 9 to 12 let it go slack.
- **Progression: 6.** "On Tuesday the eighth of December Lesca woke in Maylis's bed, with rain on the tiles. She had the legs, the left hand and the mouth. Maylis had the right hand." This is the third time the set-up is used, and the third time finds nothing new in it.
- **Comedy: 6.5.** "She reported him. She said it had been told twice and given two lots of stars, and it got through them early and came at one." That is the top. Chapters 1 to 9 are a 7.5. Chapters 10 to 18 are a 5.5.
- **Characters: 8.** "There's a key, and a Trafic, and Guy, and then there's me, as a distance."
- **The switch between dark and comic: 8.** "And six. Bugger off." comes three paragraphs after "Under it the torch found the sole of a wader."
- **Prose: 7.** "He said it as a man turns up a card he had hoped was another." The "as a man does X" comparison is on nearly every page.
- **The ending: 7.** "Then he stepped over the string, in his waders, and got down into it." The image is right. The document that should come before it is withheld (fault 4).
- **Clarity: 6.** "The one who wakes first takes the other home in her." A reader who is working out who has the mouth is not laughing.

**Finished unpaid:** yes.
**Press it on a friend:** not the friend the bar describes. That friend would report back that it is clever and sad and stops being funny at the halfway mark, and would be right.

## 2. Against the comparisons

**Below The Good Place.** Crouch is not a comic writer. The only comedy question against him is what the jokes cost in pace, and the answer is chapters 9 and 10.

1. **Range.** The Good Place has six people who are wrong in six ways: selfish, paralysed, vain, stupid, a demon learning, a machine. This village is wrong in one way. It hides behind paper. Twelve speaking characters make the same joke (fault 1). It is a good joke and it is the book's subject, and by chapter 13 the reader can write the next line.
2. **Staying power.** The Good Place is still telling new jokes in its last episode, when it is at its saddest. This book's last new comic scene is the telephone call to Hontaube in chapter 13. After that every laugh is a callback, and most are callbacks turned to grief. The show's rule is that the joke and the ache arrive together. The book's rule, from chapter 14, is that the ache has replaced the joke.
3. **Level, and at one point above: the running gag as moral instrument.** Chidi's indecision is funny for a season before the show says it is what damned him. "Either" takes the same road and is made to cost more: a shopping pad, then "Either is not a person. I cannot summon Either to the brigade", then a hand on a winch in daylight, then "That was Either, with the river in it." The show never made a catchphrase drown anyone.

On reversals it is below. "This is the Bad Place" re-reads a season of comedy as comedy. This book's two reveals re-read only the dark half: 02:51 is the minute of a telephone call, and the kitchen clock down the line is the Castagnets'. The second would re-read the farce too, if the book let the café see it (fault 2).

## 3. The ten faults, worst first

**1. One comic engine for a whole village. (Structural, all chapters.)**
"I cannot file a forecast." "I have that as consent." "The register doesn't ask that." "Is this a request, or a notification? I've got a tray for each, and a book." "I've nothing on the sheet for this." "It has two." "I can't sign a summons to nowhere." "I've two points on that, and the mairie has both in writing." "I do not complain. I mention it." "It's in the care plan." "Through the chair."
That is Sarthou, Tastet, Ducasse, the woman at Hontaube, the man from Pau, the lieutenant, Lesca, Jeannot, the priest, Coralie and Marie-Jo, and Larrère is the twelfth. Each is funny alone. Together they are one voice in twelve coats, and the people are not funny in themselves, they are funny in the book's way.
*Do:* leave the paper joke with the four for whom it is character: Larrère, Sarthou, Jeannot, Lesca. Take it from the four who exist in the back half only to make it again: Tastet, the man from Pau, the lieutenant, and half of Ducasse. Then give room to the two people already built on another engine. Josiane is certain and wrong in sentences that never stop. Kévin is the one person in the village who wants something silly ("I'd go and look in my mum's gutter"). He has that one comic line in eighteen chapters and is then used for pathos. He is the fool the book has and does not play.

**2. The farce stops escalating at chapter 8 and its chorus is dropped without a payoff. (Structural, chapters 9 to 18.)**
"It's the wife she's after and it always was." "It's still the wife." "She had it off the wife, and the wife had it off him."
For three chapters the café has the right kitchen and the wrong caller. The reader knows from chapter 5 whose folder is on the dresser. A wrong theory in a farce exists to be proved half right at the worst moment. It never is. Lafitte's pad is last seen in chapter 10. Josiane does not speak again. When Maïté takes the bus with the suitcase the book says "She did not look at the café, and nobody came out of it."
*Do:* put Josiane in that doorway on the Sunday. The wife is leaving, the man who was calling is at the crossroads on foot with silt to the knee, and Josiane gets it right, aloud, in one unbroken sentence, to a room that stopped believing her a fortnight ago. Give Lafitte's pad its last entry in the same scene. It is the reversal that re-reads chapters 5 to 10, and it is already paid for.

**3. No new comic material after chapter 13, and the mayor's half ends with no comic scene of its own. (Structural, chapters 14 to 18.)**
"Across the square the hall was giving the home its lunch, as on each twelfth."
That sentence walks past the scene the last third needs. An hour after a mayor says into a microphone that she drowned a man, the eight people she kept dry are eating in the hall next door: Daguerre, who calls Here; Madame Dufau, who knows one year; the Lassalle sisters; Madame Etcheto. They are owed an opinion and they are the funniest people in the book. Instead chapter 16 gives the aftermath to officials with sheets, and chapter 17 removes her from office in twenty lines.
*Do:* stage the lunch. She is suspended at two; send her in at one, with the chairs to stack. Let the eight say what they think of having been the forty-two.

**4. Letter No. 42 is described, not printed. (Chapter 18.)**
"It says that at three on Saturday morning the keyholder was at the hatch of my tonne, a kilometre from her gate, pulling me out of it by the neck. It says why she thought she had to. It says the cellar was dry."
"Put it in forty-two" is planted in chapters 1, 3 and 6. The one letter the book does print, the seventeenth, is its funniest paragraph: "I enclose my bill for them. I have taken nothing off for the tow, since I ordered none." Then the letter the whole book has been filling is given as a list of "It says".
*Do:* print it. Four sides is too long; print one, numbered, in his voice, refusing to thank her. It is the last laugh and the last ache in one object, and it is the only place left to get a new joke into the ending.

**5. Lesca is funny only in someone else's body. (Chapters 2, 4, 10, 12, 16, 17.)**
"I say sit, and I'm given the code on mealtimes. I say eat, and I'm told what the council voted. I say lie on your side, and she tells me which side the rule was made for."
That is the comedy of the mayor's marriage, and it is reported to the wrong woman in chapter 8. It is never played. At her own table Lesca is grave in every scene. So one of the two leads is wrong in her own way only at council, and the reader who is asked to weigh two marriages has seen one of them be funny and the other be described.
*Do:* one early scene, chapter 2 or 4, where Coralie gives an order from the door frame and gets the code back. Twenty lines.

**6. The physical comedy of a shared body is summarised. (Chapters 9, 10.)**
"For ten minutes the bar of the loto was better run than anyone in the hall could remember."
One woman has the urn and the mouth, the other has the right hand and the prices. That is the scene, and it is given as a result. The same in chapter 10: "The association had voted before the café lit its lamps", a seven-man vote taken by telephone from a parked car in front of the people it floods. A reader who knows the film All of Me, where a second soul has the right side of a man's body, has seen this device played at full length and will notice that here it gets a spoon tipped twice and a timer dial.
*Do:* play the bar. Three orders, the hands disagreeing over the third.

**7. Coralie's gift is used once, and nobody laughs after chapter 6. (Chapters 1, 18.)**
"'I'll be brief,' she said, in the voice that came from the back of the neck. 'The—'"
The broken oven joke in chapter 18 is the best callback in the book, and it rests on a single performance seventeen chapters earlier. Between them Coralie is reduced to diagnosis. The last time anyone laughs because something is funny is "Coralie laughed, with her head back", over the soup. What Lesca crosses a world to get, and what Maylis loses, is a funny woman, and the book stops showing that she is one.
*Do:* one more turn at the oven or its equal in the middle, for Lesca. The woman who has timed that man for eleven years should be the one it is done for.

**8. Callbacks are under-planted or over-explained. (Chapters 1, 15, 18.)**
Under-planted: "Still in the box, were you?" is said once, in chapter 1, and retired in chapter 18 with "They've retired the other." The reader has forgotten there was a saying.
Over-explained: "as it had on the dashboard of a tractor under the plane trees"; "as he had once got into her bucket"; "The two of them had got the giggles, and been heard."
*Do:* use the box line twice more, at the café in chapter 6 and at the loto. Cut the three reminders. If the plant was good the reminder is not needed, and room twelve does not need it.

**9. The same sheet joke three times in the minute after the confession. (Chapter 16.)**
"He turned the sheet over to see whether it went on." "His sheet had a line for a mayor who was prevented, and none for a mayor who stood on her cross of tape with nothing more to read." "I've nothing on the sheet for this."
Here a joke weakens a dark moment. Nobody in that square is angry. Procedure stands where a reaction should be, and the confession gets smaller with each sheet.
*Do:* keep Tastet turning his page, the clapping man and the child. Cut the other two. Give the square one hot voice before the chairs are stacked.

**10. A numbered speech stops the clock. (Chapter 15.)**
"I've three things to say about it. First, I counted, as you do, by the click".
It is 03:31, the water has gone from shins to knees, and a man is waiting to be carried. Jeannot gets a hundred words uninterrupted from a woman who has just told her driver "Don't give me the minutes. Go."
*Do:* Coralie cuts him off at "Second". He delivers the third point with Castéra on his back. It is funnier and the clock keeps running.

## 4. Five things that must not be touched

1. **The care plan.** "It's in the plan now. Staff to wait. Staff not to join in." The corridor, the giggles, Bixente and his mop. It is the funniest page and it loads room twelve.
2. **Larrère's eleven minutes with no bell.** "He had been saving it since 2015, and there was less of it than he had thought."
3. **Chapter 11 as it stands.** The night told flat, then the nine rings, then "And six. Bugger off." The joke is the proof that he is alive.
4. **The Either chain.** The pad, the pawl, the brigade, Tastet's diary, the platform, "Both", "That was Either, with the river in it." Do not thin it and do not explain the one on the platform.
5. **The oven, both ends.** "There's the question of the eggs" and "The—".
