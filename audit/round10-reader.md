# Round 10: Reader

Read: `manuscript/00.md` to `18.md`, in order, in full. Nothing else opened but the brief.

## 1. Scores

- **Stakes: 8.** "Behind it were forty houses, and the home, with forty-two people in their beds. Out on the meadows there was one man." I could count them from chapter 2 and never lost the count.
- **Tension: 7.** "The dyke had six minutes, and on foot she could reach one of the two." That is chapter 15. Chapters 7 to 10 have nothing like it.
- **Progression: 6.** "The association had voted before the café lit its lamps." The day the prophecy comes true is reported, not played.
- **Comedy: 8.** "'Opened what? There's a name over it. It reads that she opened him.'"
- **Characters: 8.** "'There's a key, and a Trafic, and Guy, and then there's me, as a distance.'"
- **The switch between dark and comic: 9.** "'I'll be brief,' she said, in the voice that came from the back of the neck. 'The—'" The joke from chapter 1 cannot be finished in chapter 18, and nothing has to say why.
- **Prose: 8.** "Then the water came up the door to it and put it out."
- **The ending: 8.** "Then he stepped over the string, in his waders, and got down into it."
- **Clarity: 5.** "The one it cannot fetch is found in her bed, and plays loto with the rest." The climax stands on that sentence, and I read past it.

**Finished unpaid?** Yes. I was in at the end of chapter 2, slowed from 7 to 10, and from 11 could not have stopped.

**Press it on a friend?** On the friend who loved The Good Place, yes. On the friend who loved Dark Matter, not as it stands: I would have to tell them to read chapter 14 twice, and that is not a thing you say about a book you are pressing on someone.

## 2. Against the comparisons

**Below on ease, level on speed of premise, above on what stays.** Net: short of the bar for the reader it names.

- **Below: the rules.** The Good Place gives its rules in an orientation and repeats them every time one is about to be used. Crouch explains each mechanism three times. This book gives the three rules once (chapter 5, well, as an agenda) and the fourth, the one the ending needs, as an old woman's riddle in chapter 3. At the hinge I was working out the rules when I should have been afraid.
- **Level: premise in play.** A thing on the wardrobe on the first page, a second village by the end of chapter 2, and a dated flood coming at it. That is Crouch's pace.
- **Above: the people.** I will not remember a single person from Dark Matter except by role. I will remember Coralie, Jeannot, Larrère's window, and Monsieur Daguerre.

### What the lens asks

**What I did not understand**

- Chapter 2: that Jean and Jeannot Castagnet are one man. For a paragraph I had a father and a son.
- Chapter 3: what "plays loto with the rest" meant. I took it for colour.
- Chapter 8: "a voice called a number, whole and then in its parts" behind the steel wall. The village loto has not happened yet, so the sound meant nothing to me.
- Chapter 10: what the empty chair is, and why Maylis "looked too long" at it.
- Chapter 12: "I mean to sit on it."
- Chapter 14: that Maylis's hand on the key means she is about to open the gate on Jeannot. I read it as guarding the key from Lesca.
- Chapters 14 and 16: whether the empty chair had to be filled by somebody, and whether Lesca's refusing it is why "Twelve" is called.
- Chapter 6: why Lesca turns the winch at all that Monday morning.
- Throughout: why the host keeps the right hand, and why the mouth sometimes changes sides. I accepted both as the game; I could not have stated them.

## 3. The ten faults, worst first

**1. Chapter 14: the choice cannot be read at reading speed.**
"Maylis meant to turn the handle. Coralie was under that dyke. Maylis's right hand moved first." Then: "For as long as she did, nobody drove, since a van does not go down a track with one woman at the wheel and another at the gear lever."
The book's mirror is in those lines: the woman who struck out the handover reaches for the key, and the woman who drowned him drives for the tonne. It goes by in three sentences, and the second passage explains a stalemate I had not yet seen happen. So when Lesca lets go I did not know she was handing the night to someone whose hand was on the key, and "Go to the tonne first" read as advice, not as a bet.
Do: give the reach its own beat before "Lesca knew that reach", say in plain words what the hand is going to do, and put "If she let go, the van was Maylis's" after the reader knows what Maylis would do with it. A page more, no new event.

**2. Chapters 3, 10, 12, 14: the rule that prices the choice is a riddle.**
"The one it cannot fetch is found in her bed, and plays loto with the rest." Later: "She looked too long at the empty chair." And: "I mean to sit on it."
Sitting on Inguma means dying in your own bed. That is what Lesca is paying, and I learned it only by arriving in the hall of the dead and reasoning backwards. A cost understood afterwards is not felt.
Do: have Arlette say it flat in chapter 12, where she already tells her daughter she has turned the words inside out. One sentence: what happens to the one it cannot fetch, and where she is found. Then chapter 14 can stay as short as it is.

**3. Chapter 10: the eighth of December is a synopsis, and the Port's belief evaporates.**
"He signed it. Madame Darrigrand signed it, with the baby on her arm." Three chapters later: "the rest of forty houses with their shutters closed."
The prediction comes true to the day, the café turns, a petition is signed, a vote is lost, and it takes a page. Then on the eleventh, with the patch dark and an orange from Pau, the people who signed are asleep downstairs. I asked why, and the book had no scene to answer with.
Do: play the Tuesday in the café as a full scene. It is the day the village's joke about her stops being a joke. On the Friday show two or three houses deciding to stay, so that the closed shutters are a choice.

**4. Chapters 1, 4, 18: the experiment is loaded and never fired.**
"sealed the card in an envelope, which nobody had asked her for since." "keep it by her until someone asked." And at the end: "'Why did it rain? There, that year, and not here. And now here.' ... They left it there."
The words on the cards pay off (Maïté's verb, "you were right, that night"). The science does not. Dr Kessler never returns, nobody asks for the card, and the last chair scene points at the hole.
Do: let the two women be the ones who ask. Each asks the other for her card in chapter 18. That closes the experiment and lets the rain stay unexplained without looking forgotten.

**5. Chapter 00: the opening spends the secret.**
"When she took out her telephone the number was already in it." "It rang once."
From there on I knew she had looked down the track and rung somebody. "No one came to tell him" in chapter 2 was a lie to me the first time I heard it, and chapter 11 confirmed what I had instead of telling me something.
Do: end the opening on "and did not fit it to the winch." Why she did not is hook enough, and the van, the track and the ring stay for chapter 11.

**6. Chapter 17: the marriage is paid in one sentence.**
"That night Lesca was in the bedroom when Coralie carried her pillow back across the landing and laid it on the wall side of the bed."
Eleven years of landing, and it is the last line of a section about a vote. Tastet's summons gets more room. The chapter from Bayonne onward is a list of settlements told at file speed.
Do: keep the sentence, and give Coralie one line in the room before the chapter closes.

**7. Chapters 1 to 3: too many names too fast, and one name that is two.**
"Jeannot Castagnet had sent the mairie forty registered letters ... Jean Castagnet had sent the mairie twelve, and he had been dead for nearly twelve years."
By the end of chapter 3 I had been given some fifteen people. That I could carry. The Jean and Jeannot paragraph I could not: it is the sentence that proves the second village exists, and I spent it wondering who Jean was.
Do: add a clause there saying it is the same man, called Jean on her side since he died. "Jean's for the stone" in chapter 9 still lands.

**8. Chapter 4: the envelope twice.**
"A thing dreamed would have changed. A thing remembered would have said *right*, since she had never in her life written the other word."
I had watched Maylis open hers and read *right* in the chair one chapter earlier. Lesca's drawer and Lesca's chair tell me the same fact in reverse, at greater length, with the reasoning spelled out.
Do: cut the drawer scene to the card and its one word, and in the chair let her read *left* once and reach for it. The paragraph of deduction can go; I had already made it.

**9. Chapter 6: Lesca turns the winch for no stated reason.**
"She fitted it to the shaft, stood square and turned, counting by the click of the pawl."
The river is rising and she has told the kitchen so, yet she raises the gate. The scene exists to put her scar on Maylis's palm, and it shows.
Do: one line of purpose before she fits the handle, such as wanting to know whether it still sticks at nineteen.

**10. Chapter 8: two speeches run together, twice.**
"'...Then she tells you which you ought to have had.' 'You sat when I told you...'" And again at "'...which side the rule was made for.' 'That's the first order I've given in this house...'"
The same speaker closes and reopens on one line, as if a sentence between has been lifted out. I stopped both times to see who was talking.
Do: restore the beat or join the speech.

## 4. Five things that must not be touched

1. **"Either"**, from the eggs to the handle on its one tooth in chapter 13, to "Both", to "That was Either, with the river in it."
2. **Chapter 11 entire.** The night given whole, the reflector put out, then nine rings and Jeannot's six points.
3. **Coralie and the call log in chapter 12.** "Outgoing. Castagnet J., mobile. Not answered. Two fifty-one." And "Because you'd have told me."
4. **Monsieur Daguerre's "Here" and Kévin's five fingers.** Both set up as jokes and both collected in the water.
5. **The last page.** Letter number 42, delivered by hand, and the second shovel.
