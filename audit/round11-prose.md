# Round 11: Prose

Read: `manuscript/00.md` to `18.md`, in order, in full. 45,207 words. Nothing else opened. Counts below are from the whole manuscript.

## 1. Scores

- **Stakes: 7.** "It was forty houses, forty-two beds, a car in the yard of the home and a van at the end of a track, with ten minutes against twenty-five." (12) The stake fits in one sentence. It is then said so often ("forty houses" 19 times, "forty-two" 23) that it turns into arithmetic before the river arrives.
- **Tension: 6.** "She said them over, as the cost of a motion is read out before the vote." (14) The book's central choice is paced like a council item. Only 15 changes speed.
- **Progression: 6.** "On Monday it rained, and Lesca woke in Maylis's bed." (6) "On Saturday it rained, and Lesca woke in Maylis's bed." (9) "On Tuesday the eighth of December Lesca woke in Maylis's bed, with rain on the tiles." (10) Three rides start the same way and the third adds nothing the first two did not.
- **Comedy: 7.** "'Either is not a person. I cannot summon Either to the brigade.'" (7)
- **Characters: 7.** "'I'd have taken both. One for the commune, and one for the washing-up.'" (4)
- **Switch between dark and comic: 8.** "'Here,' he said. / 'I know you are.'" (15)
- **Prose: 7.** "Rain fell on the card and the L spread." (1) Sentences of that quality are common. They all arrive at one speed.
- **Ending: 7.** "Then he stepped over the string, in his waders, and got down into it." (18) The image is right. Two pages earlier Lesca has already told the reader what to conclude: "A woman is dead of that, and it still isn't what I did."
- **Clarity: 5.** "When the warmth beside Maylis in the chair stirred to go, Maylis had kept her forearms on the steel arms and stayed, and so the day was hers to drive." (8) This is how the book reports that the timid woman has stolen a day of the other's life.

**Finished unpaid:** yes. **Pressed on a friend:** no. Not on the reader the bar names.

## 2. Against the comparisons

**Above Crouch on the sentence. Below him on the paragraph and the chapter. Below The Good Place on range of voice. Net: below the bar.**

1. **The sentence is better made than Crouch's.** There are no intensifiers, 34 adverbs in -ly in the whole book, no semicolons, three dashes (all cut-off speech). Objects carry the feeling: the shopping pad, the kitchen timer, two fingers on a wrist. Crouch tells the reader the feeling. This book does not need to.
2. **Crouch's prose changes speed with the pulse. This narrator has one speed.** Mean narration sentence length is between 11.7 and 13.9 words in every numbered chapter. Five chapters have no sentence of 30 words or more. Crouch drops to one-line paragraphs when a man is in danger and states his premise in a plain sentence. Here the danger and the signs form for Pau are told at the same walk, and the premise is assembled by the reader from pluperfect clauses.
3. **The Good Place gives each person a different way of being wrong in speech. This book gives three people that and six people one shared trick.** Josiane, Jeannot and Coralie can be told apart with the names removed. Sarthou, Père Honoré, Tastet, the Hontaube clerk, the young man from Pau and the lieutenant all speak uncontracted officialese.

## 3. The ten faults, worst first

**1. Decisions and crossings happen off the page and are reported in the pluperfect.** (8, 9, 10, 5)
"On Tuesday night she had given Inguma the squares on every card Marie-Jo had printed for the loto. At home Coralie had a late on the Wednesday, and no alarm rang to wake Maylis first. Lesca woke first." (8)
"had" appears 718 times, "She had" 168. In 15 alone, 90. The moments that turn the book are summarised after the fact: Maylis choosing to stay in the chair (8), Lesca setting the count for her second and third rides (9, 10), Maylis choosing not to ride ("did not get up", 5). The reader works out who is in whose body and why while the scene is already running.
*Do:* write each crossing as a short live scene in the chair, in the simple past, ending on the choice to stay seated. State the rule once more each time in one plain sentence ("whoever is still sitting when the other wakes, goes home in her"). Start with the first paragraph of 8. This is a structural change in six places.

**2. One tempo.** (whole book. Worst in 14)
"Lesca let go. In the steel room she got up off Inguma's back." (14)
Sentence openings in narration are She, The, It, He or a name almost without exception. Fourteen of eighteen chapters open on a day or a clock time. The verb palette is tiny: went 221, stood 158, came 158, put 130, took 117, turned 112. Chapter 13's 85-word sentence at the wheel and 15's bare clock stamps show the narrator can move. It does so twice in 45,000 words.
*Do:* choose the six scenes of physical or moral danger (the first crossing in 4, the winch in 6, the landing in 9, the gate in 13, the van in 14, the square in 16) and let sentence length follow the body in each, as 15 does. Keep the weather as the first signal of which village we are in. Vary everything else about the openers.

**3. The narrator captions its own echoes and explains what the scene showed.**
"Maylis had seen her do it at the home, and had had it done to her." (15, at Madame Etcheto's wrist)
"It buzzed, twice, against his chest, as it had on the dashboard of a tractor under the plane trees." (15)
"She had stood in this corridor once before, with Coralie, outside the last door on the left. An old woman had said her words to something on the wardrobe [...] The two of them had got the giggles, and been heard." (15)
"She had heard it once before, down a telephone, in the dark." (17)
"He said it as a man turns up a card he had hoped was another." (15), then Kévin says it himself in 18.
"'I didn't count them,' Lesca said. She had counted everything else that night." (17)
"who had kept a gate shut on him for twelve years and had now spoken to him across it." (17)
"That minute of the night had been said aloud in that room, by somebody else, for the first time." (12)
"Maylis saw how it was done. She did the same herself, with a shorter word." (5)
"She dug the graves in Peyrebarthe, and she knew what a name on a stone was for." (1)
Also 6 ("Now she had put it on a woman who had turned nothing in her life but what she was told to"), 13 ("Lesca's plan had wanted this hour"), 14 ("He would be left a second time, and it would take both of them to do it"), 18 ("She went back to the landing of the Saturday before").
The book's method is the bare repeated object. Each caption says the reader cannot be trusted with it. The worst is at room twelve, where the best echo in the book is labelled as it lands.
*Do:* delete every one. About twenty sentences. Nothing replaces them.

**4. The confrontations between the two women repeat one beat, and silence is the default climax.** (5, 8, 10, 14)
"Lesca gave no answer." (5) "Lesca had nothing to put against it." (8) "Lesca did not answer." (10) "She shut the hand on them." (14)
Silence or a withheld answer closes an exchange eleven times. The short negative sentence used as a closer appears at least twenty-two times ("She did not knock." "She did not lie down." "He did not count the ninth." "Arlette did not ask."). Maylis asks what is in the stone in 5, 8 and 10 and gets the same wall three times.
*Do:* give each meeting a different failure. In 8 Lesca already gives the committee formula and is caught ("That's not a no"). In 10 let her answer and be wrong, or answer with the sum, so that 11 corrects her and does not merely fill a gap. Keep the withheld-action closer for five places and cut the rest.

**5. The sum and the standing facts are restated until they stop registering.**
"She gave that sum each year, in the same order, forty houses and forty-two beds against one man." (2)
"The flood of 2014 had never come to that village. It was coming now, twelve years late" (2), the third statement of one idea in four paragraphs.
The undated letter is explained three times in 8, again in 9 and again in 13. That the president and the mayor are one man is said twice in 8 by the narrator and once in dialogue. The dyke's ten minutes, the six springs and the sheet "the width of a door" are given in 00, 11 and 15 in near-identical words.
*Do:* speak the full sum three times only: at the table (2), in the café in the future tense (6), at the microphone (16). Everywhere else one number. State each standing fact once in narration and afterwards only in a mouth that has reason to say it.

**6. The narrator's position changes in the shared-body scenes.** (6, 8, 9, 14)
"Maylis heard her own voice give that in one breath. [...] The cracked rib let her have no breath, and she could do nothing with her face." (9, inside a scene told from Lesca)
"Inside her, Lesca gave the minute to the forty houses, as she did each year." (8, inside a scene told from Maylis)
"Maylis meant to turn the handle." / "Lesca meant to drive the track herself" (14)
Chapters 1 to 5 hold strictly to one head. From 6 the narrator enters both without warning. In 4 and 6 the passenger is read through the body ("Lesca could feel her in the shut fingers"), which works. In 9 and 14 the narrator simply reports both minds.
*Do:* one rule. The narrator stays with the driver and gets the passenger only through the one hand, the jaw, the pull on the wrist. Rewrite the five or six sentences that break it. The landing in 9 gains: Maylis reaching the right hand to the door needs no interior.

**7. Sentences a first reader cannot parse.**
"It was not that Lesca had come too late." (11) Nobody has suggested she had.
"'For the blessing of the dyke I am promised six thousand,' [...] 'I have prepared for nine.'" (8) Six thousand what. It is explained a chapter later.
"If Lesca let go, Inguma took her home" then, after the break, "Lesca was not in her bed." (14) Why she lands in the hall of the dead is never put in words.
"Those two minutes are your two hundred metres, and they're mine." (18)
"'Stay.' [...] 'I didn't ask what you'd keep,' Coralie said." (18) She has not yet asked anything.
"Four winters you've held that key and looked the other way" (7), against a key held since her father and letters about the winch in 2011.
*Do:* rewrite each in literal terms. In 14, one sentence from Madame Etcheto's rule in 3 ("the one it cannot fetch back") must be recalled in plain words before Lesca sits down on Inguma, because the whole chapter depends on it.

**8. Six speakers share one comic voice.** (6, 7, 8, 9, 12, 13, 16, 17, 18)
"'I do not complain. I mention it.'" (Père Honoré, 9) "'I do not record that I would have stayed.'" (Sarthou, 18) "'I have Either,' Tastet said, gently. 'It's written'" (8) "'I've got a tray for each, and a book.'" (Hontaube, 13)
The joke is the same each time: a person who can only act on what is filed. With the tags removed Sarthou and Père Honoré are one man.
*Do:* keep the filing joke for Sarthou. Give the priest his own subject (his empty church is already there, use only that). Let Tastet be timid in plain speech. Jeannot's numbered points and Lesca's "three points" also overlap. Keep the numbering for Jeannot.

**9. Stock constructions.**
The trailing "with" phrase: 174 times (", with her back turned", ", with his glass", ", with the engine running").
"with X in it": seventeen times ("with a bed in it", "with the wrong number of years in it", "with the river in it").
Comparison by "as" plus a clause is the only simile form: "as a man votes", "as a dog gets up", "as things do that are filed", "as a stage is". "as" 173, "like" 19.
"She had" plus a noun for knowing, owning, controlling and receiving: "She had the date." "She had the sum." "She had his night to the minute."
"the one chair / seat / night / hand / track / key": eighteen.
Lesca's voice marked "lower" or "low" each time she uses Maylis's mouth.
*Do:* halve each. The "with" tail first, since it comes once every 260 words and ends each sentence it is in on the same falling rhythm.

**10. Usage and consistency.**
- "phone" once (3, Kévin) against 76 of "telephone". Decide whether the young speak differently and apply it to Kévin and Bixente throughout, or change it.
- Clock times are in words in narration and in figures on screens, except "The van went off the crown onto the track at 02:57." (15)
- "*left*" is italic in Maïté's speech (17). "Saved", "Opened" (2) and "Drowned" (18) are roman. If the italic is there to tie the verb to the card, keep it and know it is the only one.
- Lesca says "I won't" and "I shall" (5) and then "I'll not give them tonight" (17), which is the village's form.
- Narrator's register slips at "got the giggles" (15) and "A mayor, it turned out, signed a great deal" (9).
- "The other aide" (13) two paragraphs after Nadine is named.
- "Four winters" (7, 18) is unexplained. See fault 7.
- Northern-English dialect ("I'll not", "sat" and "stood" as participles, "and all", "stopping") is given to Jeannot, Arlette, Maïté and Kévin and never to Coralie. If that is deliberate nothing on the page says why.
- Checked and consistent: the calendar (24 November 2026 is a Tuesday, Saint Catherine's a Wednesday, 11 December a Friday), eleven years for the landing against twelve for the flood, Jean against Jeannot, British spelling, single quotes, "round" never "around".

## 4. Five things that must not be touched

1. **The shared body written with the definite article.** "The right hand, which was Maylis's, came across in its tea towel and tipped the spoon." (6) "It crossed a t that Lesca had left bare, low, and laid the pen down." (9) This is the book's one invention at sentence level and it carries comedy and dread together.
2. **"Either", through to its last use.** "Both." (15) and "That was Either, with the river in it." (18)
3. **Monsieur Daguerre's "Here".** At the loto, at the prefect's date, in the flooded corridor. Three places, no comment, different meaning each time.
4. **The square after the confession.** "A child asked what the lady had done, and was told." (16) And Marie-Jo's chairs.
5. **Coralie's impression failing at the oven door, and the last page.** "'I'll be brief,' she said, in the voice that came from the back of the neck. 'The—'" (18) From "Whose is it?" to the end, not a word.
