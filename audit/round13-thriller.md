# Round 13 — critic's report, Thriller lens

Read: `manuscript/00.md` to `18.md`, in order, in full. Nothing else opened but the brief.

Verdict for this lens: a 7 against a bar of 8. It is below Crouch on tension and on use of its premise, level with him on chapter endings, and above him on what the central choice costs.

## 1. Scores

- **Stakes: 7.** "It's a life in one hand. You eat at my table, and I tip the spoon." (10) The private stake is as sharp as anything in Dark Matter. The public one is carried for seven chapters by a folder and six months for eels.
- **Tension: 6.** "...and when her head went forward she drove her thumbnail into the weal and kept it there." (13) Four sequences at this level: the stairs (6), the landing (9), the vigil (13), the corridor (15). Between them the book sits in meetings.
- **Progression: 6.5.** "'Four to three,' Larrère said. 'It stays shut.'" (10) The daylight plan is refused four times in the same way between 7 and 13, and each refusal returns the book to the state it was in.
- **Comedy: 8.** "'And six. Bugger off.'" (11)
- **Characters: 8.** "I don't get into a bed over somebody who can't speak. I've lifted too many of them." (9)
- **Switch between dark and comic: 8.** "He said it as a man turns up a card he had hoped was another." (15)
- **Prose: 8.** "It was not loud. It was the sound of a lorry tipping gravel, a long way to her right, and it did not stop." (15)
- **The ending: 8.** "It's four sides, and it doesn't thank you." (18) The cost is a 9. The tail that delivers it is a 6.
- **Clarity: 6.** "Whichever of us wakes first wakes at home, in her own bed. If the other is still sitting in this chair, she goes with her, and wakes inside her." (5) The rule has to be restated at the head of 6, 8, 9 and 14, and the reader still keeps a ledger of alarms and rotas in 9 and 10.

**Finished unpaid?** Yes. After the last line of 4 I would not have stopped.
**Pressed on a friend?** Yes, on one who reads slowly. Not on the Crouch reader the bar names; that reader stops in 7.

## 2. Against the comparisons

**Below Crouch as a thriller, by one point.** Three things put it there.

1. **Premise speed is level; premise use is below.** The clock is set by the last paragraph of 2 ("the flood would be there on the night of the eleventh of December, with Jeannot Castagnet out at his tonne"), which is as fast as Dark Matter puts Jason in the wrong world. Crouch then keeps opening his box: more worlds, more Jasons, each chapter a new consequence of the device. This book opens its box once. Two villages, one steel room, five crossings, and the room is mostly somewhere to hold a meeting. Dr Kessler and the two lamps never come back.
2. **Chapter endings are level.** "That the mayor remain seated, and take the other one's bed, and Coralie Mounet in it." (4) "'Are you coming up?'" (6) "Who holds my hand down then?" (10) "The telephone began to ring in her hand." (13) "'Forty-one,' Coralie said." (15) These do what a Crouch last line does. The weak ones are 9, 12, 16 and 17.
3. **Fear for the marriage is different in kind and smaller in degree.** Crouch gives fifteen years of ordinary happiness in a dinner and then removes it; the eggs and the oven door in 1 do the same in thirty lines. But Jason2 can kill, and Lesca can only be preferred. The fear is real ("I'd have this one again", 6) and it is settled at the midpoint by Coralie's own two fingers. After 9 the reader is no longer afraid Coralie will be taken.

**Above Crouch on the choice and its cost.** Dark Matter lets the family walk out; Recursion resets. Here the woman who tries to keep both loses a ninety-four-year-old in room twelve, her post, and the wife who "chose you, Maylis, over somebody who asked"; the woman who drowned a man gets the pillow back.

**Below The Good Place on reversals.** The book has four that re-read what came before: the flood "had not reached it yet" (2); 02:51 as the minute of the call (11); "I've had that for twelve years" (12); "Castagnet, house" and the kitchen clock (15, 17). None reorders the whole book, and the largest is spent on page one (fault 3).

**Where the reader is afraid, and of what.** Of Lesca going up the stairs (6: "I'd have gone up"). Of the wrong woman being let into the bed (9). Of the roof of the van (13). Of a door with a brass twelve on it (15). Nowhere in a Lesca chapter set in her own village, because nothing there can still happen.

## 3. The ten faults, worst first

**1. The catastrophe turns on a hinge that weighs one sentence. (13)**
"'Either,' Maylis said. He waited for the rest of it, and there was none."
At five on Friday the dilemma does not exist. The man is on the planks beside her, it is daylight, the handle is on the shaft, and she has known the hour of the breach since 5 and had it proved on the 8th. Against forty houses and Coralie the scene sets one line: "now it carried Kévin Labat's name and sat in the car along the crown." The reader has already been shown what that threat comes to: "He had done six months, for eels" (8), and that Kévin came out to a job and a waterproof. Her own post is gone in January with "the forty euros a month". The book knows this is her guilt and says so through Madame Darrigrand in 18, but the reader has to feel the hand come off the handle, and here the reader only shouts at her to turn it.
*Do:* keep the word and load the other pan. Use 8, which has no jeopardy of its own, to show what six months did to the other Kévin and who sent the sheet, so that the name on the paper is heavy by 13. And make Larrère act, not watch: his rule is "you touch nothing till I ring", so let the dashboard ring at the first click of the pawl.

**2. The daylight plan fails four times the same way, and the middle is played for a file. (7, 9, 10, 13)**
"It was her second paper since nine." (7) "'Then it should have gone last Monday.'" (13)
Larrère in his office, the association at the card table, the prefect's letter sent back "for my observations", Hontaube's form. Each is funny and each leaves the board as it was: gate shut, Friday night. Chapter 7 is three interviews ending in three signatures. Meanwhile Lesca says "I'm coming to stay" at the top of 7 and does nothing about it until 9, and 7 ends on the best unused danger in the book, "left her own body beside Coralie with its eyes open", which 8 never looks at. Thirteen hours of an empty body, a wife with a doctor's number under her thumb, and the chapter is about a kitchen timer.
*Do:* keep the café vote, because it turns on her own blackmail. Fold Sarthou's interview into it, cut Hontaube to six lines, and make 8 run against the body at home, so that the reader counts her working day down with her.

**3. The opening spends the book's biggest reversal. (00, against 11)**
"When she took out her telephone the number was already in it." / "It rang once."
By the committee table in 2 the reader has matched the hand on the clay and the ten minutes, and knows that "No one came to tell him" is a lie. Chapter 11's "Nobody in that village knew that she had looked down the track first" then tells the reader what the reader has known for nine chapters. What is left for 11 is the minute and the kitchen clock, which are details where there should be a floor going.
*Do:* end 00 at "She held it while she counted three breaths, and did not fit it to the winch." The hook becomes why she waited. Maïté's "That isn't what I asked" (2) and "That's not a no" (10) carry the suspicion to 11, and "It rang once" is heard there for the first time.

**4. The chapters in Lesca's village carry no danger, and one of them sits on the day of the flood. (12)**
"He paced from the steps to the stone, tore off two strips and laid a cross on the planks beside it."
The reader has just left a river over the line and a man swearing down a telephone, and gets a rehearsal with tape. Coralie's scene is the heart of the book and belongs exactly here. The first scene does not.
*Do:* open 12 at four o'clock with Arlette. Keep the eight strips and "The rest have died since, in them, of what people die of", shortened. Keep one line from the young man: "And if the mayor is prevented?"

**5. After "Forty-one" the reader is kept out of that village for two chapters, and 17 is eight endings. (16–17)**
"Madame Ducasse entered eleven, and one abstention."
16 is a second climax and earns its place. 17 then answers Madame Etcheto, visits Maïté, clears a gutter, faces Larrère, stands on the landing, ticks a box at Bayonne, loses a vote and gets the pillow. The thriller ended at "and I drowned him."
*Do:* keep the kitchen (the clock pays there), Larrère at the garden gate and the landing. Give Bayonne and the council a paragraph each. Consider 18's Port scenes before 17, so the reader sees the cost before the reward.

**6. A cliffhanger is cancelled offstage, a rule arrives when the plot needs it, and the fetch keeps perfect time. (9→10, 11, 6)**
"Her alarm would ring at six on each of the five mornings up to Friday" (end of 9), then "Nothing had sat on her, so she had not been in the chair" (first page of 10).
The threat of five mornings is withdrawn by strapping a rib, between chapters. In 11 a new rule, "what a woman had in her head came to the chair whether she sat in it or not", exists to deliver the back story, and Lesca's reason for "pushing" that night is not given. In 6 Inguma finishes its lake at the second Lesca's foot is on the stair.
*Do:* let one of the five mornings happen and cost something before she finds the trick of lying on her side. Establish in 5 that the chair holds what is in a head whether or not the head is in it.

**7. The threat of twelve chapters is given up in a paragraph, and the hall of the dead has no grip. (14)**
"She let go. In the steel room she got up off Inguma's back." / "It can go in the minutes as apologies received."
The count of rings is the right clock. But the deadlock is asserted after one stall, and Lesca reasons her way out of a plan the book has built since 4. Then the death the reader was promised ("Staying costs me my life... Nil") is declined with a formula and she walks. Nothing holds her there.
*Do:* show the deadlock twice before she concludes it: she restarts, the lever goes out again, at rings five and seven. In the hall, make the chair cost her something to leave. Her father does not look up; make her want him to.

**8. The Port believes her and sleeps under the dyke; the second pair of hands turns up and is spent without a thought. (10, 13, 15)**
"*Tuesday 8th. At the line, on her day.*" then "Lafitte's at his upstairs window. He says he's dry up there and he's stopping."
On the 8th the café has her prophecy in writing, proved to the day, and signs a petition. On the 11th every one of them is in bed behind shutters. And in 9 "It would take a second pair of hands"; in 13 Kévin stands on her platform with a torch in his teeth and she sends him to the home. That is the decision that empties the corridor, and it deserves to be one.
*Do:* one scene or half a page: who goes up to the hall on Friday and who will not, and why. One sentence at the platform where she sees that she is choosing the home over the winch.

**9. The choice the book exists for is one line, and the wrong caller is on the telephone. (15)**
"She got out, and went on."
Eight minutes earlier she was reaching for the key to drown him "because Coralie was under that dyke". At the dip she leaves Coralie under it. The plain sentence is right. What is missing is Coralie in the beat. The telephone that rings on the seat "said G. Larrère", the caller the reader least minds her ignoring.
*Do:* make it Coralie ringing back after "Both", and let her leave that on the seat.

**10. The device is shrugged off. (18, against 1 and 5)**
"It was weather, and nobody had sent it. They left it there."
Nineteen days of rain from the same date, ninety over on the same night, a breach within twenty minutes of the same minute, twelve years late: that is not weather, and a reader of Crouch will not take the word for it. Kessler's "that was the question" (1) is never asked again, and the two cards, *left* and *right*, do nothing after 4 except prove the dream is real.
*Do:* either stop raising it (cut Lesca's "I brought my weather with me" and Maylis's last question) or pay it. Paying it is better: the envelope "nobody had asked her for since" should be asked for.

## 4. Five things that must not be touched

1. The amended motion that closes 4, and the comma that makes it.
2. Two fingers on a wrist as the means of telling them apart: "That's her. In this hand." (9), and everything on that landing.
3. Nine rings against one: the call at five in the morning and Jeannot's six points (11).
4. The last page of 13: the one long sentence of the vigil, five fingers, the tin of the roof giving.
5. Chapter 15 from the fire door to the end: Kévin's "Five", the brass twelve, the line through the last name, "Forty-one". And the gauge readings that count the whole book down to it.
