# Round 14: Reader

Read: `manuscript/00.md` to `18.md`, in order, in full. Nothing else opened but the brief.

## 1. Scores

- **Stakes: 8.** "*That the mayor remain seated, and take the other one's bed, and Coralie Mounet in it.*" (4). A woman with my face is coming for my bed, and a river is coming for forty houses, both on a known date.
- **Tension: 7.** "The telephone began to ring in her hand." (13) is a nine. "Complaint of Castagnet, Jean, against persons unknown" (7) is a five. The average is the middle of the book.
- **Progression: 6.5.** "Her Monday at Bayonne and her Tuesday before the council were still to come, and the pillow was still in the box room." (18). The book has to stop and tell me where I am in time.
- **Comedy: 7.5.** "'And six. Bugger off.'" (11). I laughed aloud there, at Bixente in 3, and at "Those in favour." "There's two of us." in 5.
- **Characters: 8.5.** "'I don't cook Either,' she said. 'There's no recipe for it. Soup or duck.'" (1). I knew Coralie from that line and never lost her.
- **The switch between dark and comic: 8.** "'I'll be brief,' she said, in the voice that came from the back of the neck. 'The—'" (18). The joke from chapter 1 fails in her mouth, and that is how I learn what the night cost her.
- **Prose: 7.5.** "Then the water came up the door to it and put it out." (11). Plain and exact. Against it: "a hand" as a measure more than twenty times, and "In sixteen / eighteen / thirty years she had never" at least five times.
- **The ending: 8.** "Then he stepped over the string, in his waders, and got down into it." (18).
- **Clarity: 6.** "If the other is still sitting in this chair when that happens, the sitter goes home with the waker, and wakes inside the waker's body." (5). I followed this. I could not have repeated it, and seven more rules follow it.

**Would I have finished it unpaid?** Yes. I would have stalled in 7 and 8 and been pulled back by the landing in 9.

**Would I press it on a friend?** Yes, with the instruction to give it until the stairs in chapter 6.

## 2. Against the comparisons

**Just below, for this lens.** Chapters 11 to 16 are level with the comparisons or better. Chapters 4 to 10 are not: I worked harder in them than a Crouch novel or The Good Place ever makes me work.

- **Below Crouch on ease.** His box in Dark Matter is explained once, and after that everything frightening is a consequence of it. Here a new rule is still arriving in chapter 12. I was never afraid of a rule in advance, only informed of it at the moment it bit.
- **Level with The Good Place on one reversal, and it has only one.** "Nine minutes to three was the time on the screen of the telephone when Lesca rang him." (11) re-reads every night scene before it. "Under the time it said Castagnet, house." (15) does the same for Larrère in a smaller way. The other big fact, that she saw the van and rang once, I had from the opening and from Maïté in chapter 2, so it confirmed where it should have turned.
- **Above Crouch on people and on cost.** In chapter 15 I knew every person in the corridor by name and habit. Crouch's crowds are anonymous. He also gives the husband his wife back; this book takes Coralie from the woman who rang nine times and returns her to the one who rang once.
- **Level with Crouch on speed.** The premise is in play by the last line of 2: "that village had not had its flood yet."

**What I did not understand:** why water let onto the meadows does not reach the Port; whether Madame Etcheto takes the dead woman's chair because she drowned or because the place had to be filled; why Maylis lies still on the Thursday night (11) after refusing Lesca on the Wednesday; "one off the strimmer"; "It's for Irun"; who Père Honoré was until I worked it back.

**Where I skimmed:** the envelope and the chair in the first half of 4; the bank and the hand on the clay in 11; sheets one and two of the speech in 16; the first exchange in the steel room in 18. In each case I had already been told.

**Where I could not stop:** the end of 3 into 4; all of 6; the landing in 9; 11 through 16 in one sitting.

**What I will remember in a month:** Either gets eggs. A mayor who talks through a gap in a car window. An old woman who reports the minibus driver for giving the thing two lots of stars. A boy counting his fingers in a flood and wanting six. "Fetching a man out of a dry hole." Jeannot in the grave in his waders. I will not remember the rules.

## 3. The ten faults, worst first

**1. The rules arrive when the plot needs them. (5, 9, 10, 12, 14)**
By my count there are twelve: the hour and the bed; lying still or pushing; two in one seat; the sitter is given the other's memories, present or not; standing, nobody can lie; the waker goes home and the sitter rides; the host keeps the right hand and a word of the jaw; it fetches when the count is done; anyone can set a count; on your side it sits beside you; sit on it and it cannot fetch; the unfetched play loto with the dead. They come in over nine chapters. "Anybody could send Inguma off to count. A kind old woman had done it from the far side of a hall." (9). "'I mean to sit on it,' Lesca said." (12). And the climax halts to explain one: "And with Inguma under her she was still the one sitting in the chair, so that when Maylis woke, Lesca would wake in her." (14).
*What is wrong:* a rule I learn as it is used can surprise me but cannot frighten me beforehand. The loto scene would be twice as funny and twice as tense if I already knew that any helpful person could finish those words.
*What I would do:* put the whole set in by the end of 5, most of it in Madame Etcheto's mouth in 3, where it is already funny. Add nothing after. Then delete the re-explanations that open 6, 7, 9 and 14. This is moving sentences forward, not rebuilding.

**2. The moment the two women change sides is told to me. (13, 14, 15)**
Maylis's decision to open the gate on Jeannot is one flat sentence at the end of a plan: "The man in the cellar had been told twice." (13). In 14 the narrator has to announce what it meant: "Maylis was about to do what Lesca had done, for the same woman, to the same man." In 15 she drops it in a line: "The man was to be fetched out first, and the gate raised after."
*What is wrong:* this is the centre of the book. The woman who rang nine times is about to drown him, and the woman who drowned him stops her. I had to turn back to find where Maylis decided it. Made and unmade in two sentences, the choice at three in the morning belongs to Lesca, and Maylis follows an instruction until 02:59.
*What I would do:* give Maylis the decision in the vigil, where I cannot miss it. Coralie's message is the place: "*Etcheto back in 12. Walked it on my arm.*" Let her look down the track, then at the home, and choose him under. Then cut the narrator's sentence in 14.

**3. The main things are told more than once. (00, 2, 4, 5, 11, 16, 18)**
The night of 2014 is given five times: 00, the committee sum in 2, Lesca's account in 5, the whole night in 11, the speech in 16. 00 has "She laid her hand flat on the bank beside the water, and the clay moved under her palm." 11 has "She got out and put her hand flat on the bank beside it, and the clay moved under her palm." The envelope, the chair and the card are done in eight lines in 3 and again in forty in 4. Chapter 18 opens with Lesca telling Maylis what I read two chapters earlier: "I said it at eleven. Into a microphone, in front of the square, a metre from his widow."
*What is wrong:* each place I skimmed was a second telling. The echo that pays is Maylis's own hand on the clay in 15, and it needs one earlier version, not two.
*What I would do:* in 11 keep only what 00 did not show: the order down the telephone with the kitchen clock behind it, the reflector, the four minutes, the wader, the aftermath. Cut sheets one and two of the speech to two lines. Start 4 near "Rain was falling on the tiles". In 18 keep "Those two minutes are mine", "Don't hand me your sum to sit behind" and "Drowned", and cut the recital between them.

**4. Chapter 17 finishes Lesca's story before chapter 18 holds the women's last meeting. (17, 18)**
I watch the pillow carried back on the Tuesday, then 18 returns to the Saturday night and apologises for it: "Her Monday at Bayonne and her Tuesday before the council were still to come".
*What is wrong:* the farewell in the steel room is played after one of the two has already had her ending, so half of it is news to nobody.
*What I would do:* move the steel room to follow the midnight landing in 17, after "She left her door open, and the lamp lit." Bayonne, the council and the pillow then close 17. Chapter 18 opens at "At first light on Sunday Maylis walked down into the Port" and belongs to her. The sorting sentence goes.

**5. Chapters 7 and 8 are where I would have put it down. (7, 8)**
Seven sets three holds on Maylis in a hundred lines: an undated resignation, a blank sheet for Kévin, and a complaint with her name on it, plus her own threat to Jeannot. "'Either is not a person. I cannot summon Either to the brigade.'" is a good joke attached to a paper I lost track of, and the complaint is withdrawn before it does anything. Eight sends Maylis to the other village to look something up: "Maylis put Lesca's left forefinger on the page, under the word prefect." All that comes of it is "Pau sent me this on Wednesday, for my observations." (13).
*What is wrong:* after the newel post, two chapters of who holds which sheet on whom. The things I kept from 8 were Kévin with his cap off and "I'd keep that one in the house", and neither is why she went.
*What I would do:* in 7, keep Larrère's letter and Kévin's sheet, give them the room the square gets in 1, and cut Sarthou's form. Jeannot already has his grudge from "I forbid it." In 8, let her cross to see what opening the gate cost that village. The prefect can be found in passing.

**6. I cannot tell what Madame Etcheto's chair means. (14, 16, 17, 18)**
The voice calls "Lesca, Maylis" to an empty place. Lesca declines it. Then: "A chair was pulled in to a table. Somebody had sat down among the dead, and it was not Lesca." (16). Lesca says "I don't know yet what it cost. I heard twelve called, and a chair pulled in." (17), which reads as a trade. Then 18 says it was arithmetic: "Those two minutes are mine."
*What is wrong:* this is the price of the climax, and I was given two accounts of it.
*What I would do:* choose the two minutes, which is the harder one. Let Lesca hear twelve called without the suggestion that her place was filled.

**7. The rain is reopened in the last chapter to be dropped. (2, 5, 18)**
The whole clock rests on a flood that keeps its dates twelve years late. Chapter 5 settles that and is funny: "'We had a dry winter here in 2014,' Maylis said. 'I cut verges in December. Nobody was to blame for a dry one.'" Chapter 18 asks again and answers "It was weather, and nobody had sent it. They left it there."
*What is wrong:* I had accepted it. Being asked a second time, by the book, with no better answer, made me notice the coincidence it stands on.
*What I would do:* cut the exchange in 18.

**8. The end of chapter 9 is a puzzle, and it is solved between chapters. (9, 10)**
"Her alarm would ring at six on each of the five mornings up to Friday, and each time it would wake Maylis first. If Lesca was still sitting in the chair on any of those mornings, Lesca would come home in her." I had to work that out. By the second paragraph of 10 it is over: "when the alarm rang across the landing at six nobody had come home with her."
*What is wrong:* the threat is reasoned, not felt, and it is disarmed where I cannot see it.
*What I would do:* end 9 on "'It's twenty minutes to Bayonne,' she said. Coralie got up and took her bowl to the sink." That hurts, and it needs no working out.

**9. I could not picture the ground. (00, 1, 3)**
"On her other side the flood meadows lay a long way down" (00). "The Port was forty houses and the home, on the low ground behind the embankment" (1). The meadows and the Port are both behind the one bank, two hundred metres apart.
*What is wrong:* the entire choice is that opening the gate floods the meadows and spares the houses. Nothing tells me what keeps three hundred hectares of water out of the Port.
*What I would do:* one sentence in 00 saying what lies between them.

**10. Small things an ordinary reader is left to guess. (10, 11, 13, 16)**
"White Trafic at the end of the track" (10) before anyone has said it is a van. "It's for Irun" (13). "He's been one off the strimmer since the loto." (13). The priest appears as "One man knew, and he had been told in a confessional" (11) and has a name only when he matters: "Père Honoré looked at his shoes." (16).
*What I would do:* a word each. Name the priest in 11.

## 4. Five things that must not be touched

1. **Room twelve, chapter 3**: Bixente, the two lots of stars, "Staff to wait. Staff not to join in", the two of them shaking against the wall. Its return in 15 depends on every line of it.
2. **The newel post, chapter 6**, and its answer in 7: "'I'd have gone up.'"
3. **Jeannot's six points down the telephone, chapter 11**, and what they become: letter No. 42 and the second shovel.
4. **Coralie and the call log, chapter 12**: "'Because you'd have told me.'" and "'You needn't have.'"
5. **Chapter 15 entire**: the clock times, "Here", "Then they'll be wanting the bins out", Kévin's "Five", the pencil through the last name, "Forty-one."
