# Critic report: Characters (round 15)

Read: `manuscript/00.md` to `18.md`, in order, in full. Nothing else.

## 1. Scores

- **Stakes: 7.** "'The gate, or Castagnet? Say which, Maylis. For once.' / 'Both.'" (15). The choice has faces on both sides by the time it comes. It loses a point because the man at the end of the track is, in his own words, "in no danger" until she acts, and the book lets him say so twice.
- **Tension: 6.** "At six an alarm rang across the landing. It woke Maylis first. Lesca had stayed sitting, so Lesca came home in her." (9). That is the fourth time the same mechanism delivers the same event. Chapters 7 to 10 circle.
- **Progression: 6.** "It was the same as on Saturday." (6, second paragraph). The book says it for me.
- **Comedy: 7.** "'Either is not a person. I cannot summon Either to the station.'" (7). The village is funny in itself. The two leads are funny only when one is driving the other.
- **Characters: 7.** The ensemble is an 8 and Coralie is higher. The lead is a 6: "She wanted that." (3) is the whole of Maylis's ambition for eighteen chapters.
- **The switch between dark and comic: 8.** "'Here,' said Monsieur Daguerre, to the roof of the van." (15). Nothing in the flood is made smaller by it.
- **Prose: 6.** "Maylis was ashamed." (7), one line after an unfinished glass had already said so.
- **The ending: 7.** "Then he stepped over the string in his waders and began to dig." (18). The last image is right. The eighteen pages before it are a queue.
- **Clarity: 7.** "Her Monday in Bayonne and her Tuesday in front of the council had not happened yet, and the pillow was still in the spare room." (18). The book has to stop and reset its own clock at the start of its last chapter.

**Finished unpaid?** Yes. I would have stalled at the end of 7 and been brought back by 9.
**Pressed on a friend?** No. I would tell a friend about chapter 12 and chapter 15, which is not the same as handing them the book.

## 2. Against the comparisons

**Against Crouch: above on everyone except the lead, below on the lead.**

- Crouch's supporting people are functions. Amanda in Dark Matter exists to share a corridor. Here Jeannot, Kévin, Madame Etcheto, Sarthou and Lafitte would each be missed, and each is built from one habit that pays off under water (the numbered grievance, the five fingers, the stars, the bill, the pad).
- Coralie is better than Daniela. Daniela is fooled by the wrong husband for a month. Coralie has two fingers on a pulse and asks "Who is that?" inside ninety seconds (4), then refuses the better offer on principle: "I don't get into a bed over somebody who can't speak. I've lifted too many of them." (9). That is the same beat as Crouch's, done by a person and not a prize.
- Crouch wins where it matters most to his readers. Jason Dessen wants one thing on every page and the reader wants it with him. Here the wanting belongs to the invader. Lesca wants the bed, the rain on the roof and the woman in it, from the inside, in chapter 4. Maylis, whose book it is, wants Lesca out. The reader is given the thief's appetite and the householder's wrist.

**Against The Good Place: level in design, below in execution.**

- The design is the show's. Two people wrong in opposite ways about the same trolley: one who cannot choose, one who chose and has hidden behind the sum ever since. Each ends by using the other's line on her own Coralie. That is as neat as anything in the series.
- Chidi's indecision is loud. He wants both things too much and says so for whole scenes. Maylis's is one word. Until the platform in 13 the reader has no evidence that "Either" covers wanting and not the absence of it.
- The show's four argue the ethics in each other's faces. These two meet five times and four of the meetings are about how the chair works.

## 3. The ten faults that most hold it back

**1. Maylis has no inside to her wanting. (3, 8, 10; structural.)**
"She wanted that." (3). "'I want that,' Maylis said. / 'The timer?'" (8). "Lesca understood. At home that man held Maylis's letter and a boy's name. Maylis couldn't set a bell on him, even here." (8).
Her one day in the sash is told from the seat of the woman she took it from, and that woman also explains her failure for her. So the lead's desire is three sentences long, and "Either" stays a tic with no root. Lesca says she "gave that word up when I was thirty-one", so both of them had it before the flood, and the book never says where it came from. The source is lying in plain view and unused: seven people tell Maylis how her father did it ("Your father turned it with one hand", "You've got his key and none of his sense", "like your father never—") and she never once has a thought about him that is her own. Even at the table of the dead he is a watch beside a card.
Do: write the half-past-six council section of 8 from Maylis's seat. Let her feel the table stay standing, like it, find "prefect" on the page herself, take the dial between finger and thumb, and not turn it. Cut "Lesca understood". Then "I want the timer, and a table of people who won't sit down until I do" (10) has appetite behind it, and crossing out the note in 13 costs her something. Give her one thought about her father before the platform.

**2. After chapter 12 Lesca's reason to go is gone, and the book does not notice. (12, 14; structural.)**
"I've had it with me in the next room, and you've had the rest by yourself. You didn't have to." (12). Then: "She had come for a woman four steps away across a landing, who had said she would have her tomorrow." (14).
Her own Coralie is four steps across a landing with the door open and has just told her she has known for twelve years. Lesca's plan to die in that bed and take the other one does not flinch, and no line says why. So when she lets go in the van she gives up something the reader believes she already has at home, and the sacrifice is discounted.
Do: say the reason, because only one fits her. The other Coralie has never picked up that phone. Lesca is going to the one who does not know. One sentence, on the bed in 12 after "Nothing was sitting on it", or in the list in 14. It makes her worse and the letting go larger.

**3. The last two aftermaths are receiving lines. (16 after the wreath; 18 before the kitchen; structural.)**
"I wanted to say that to your face, with nobody writing it down." (Darrigrand, 16). "I'm saying it to your face. I'd rather you heard it from me than from the café." (Madame Darrigrand, 18).
In 16 eight people come up to Lesca one at a time and deliver a line: the young man, the lieutenant, Marie-Jo, Tastet, Arlette, Darrigrand, the young man again, Madame Etcheto. In 18 it is ten for Maylis. Each line is good. In a row the village stops being a village and becomes a queue, and no villager speaks to another. The square was alive while the sentence travelled back through it "at walking pace, row by row"; then it files past.
Do: halve both queues. In 16 keep Marie-Jo, Tastet and Madame Etcheto. In 18 keep Larrère with Sarthou, Kévin, Madame Darrigrand and Marie-Jo. Make two of them collide: let Madame Darrigrand say it while Kévin has the chain on the van.

**4. The two women talk about the mechanism, and learn each other by download. (5, 6, 7, 10.)**
"'One. When Inguma sits on two people at the same time, it puts them in one seat. That's this chair…'" (5). "In the chair Lesca had been given the other Kévin." (8). "The night before, in the chair, before she stood up, she had been given one of Lesca's afternoons." (5).
The rules are restated at the top of 6, in 9, in 10 and in 14. Everything either woman knows about the other's life arrives as a parcel. They never find anything out by asking. The proof of what is missing is the last meeting: "Your Coralie gives orders… Sit down when she tells you." / "Yours said she'd have the one who answers… Answer her." (18). That is the only time they speak as two people with the same wife.
Do: the meeting in 7 is ten lines. Put an exchange of that kind there, one question each about the other's house. Pay for it by cutting the second paragraph of 6 and the "This was her plan" paragraph of 14.

**5. The Coralie the reader loves leaves; the Coralie the reader has met four times comes back. (2, 17.)**
"That night Lesca was in the bedroom when Coralie carried her pillow back across the landing and laid it on the wall side of the bed." (17).
The book's one payment in happiness goes to the couple the reader has invested least in. Lesca's Coralie is not in chapter 2 at all. Until 12 she is a door frame and an honorific.
Do: put her in 2. One exchange across the landing on the first morning, so that the landing is a place before it is a number ("eleven years").

**6. Maylis is acquitted four times. (18.)**
"You rang nine times. You walked his track at dusk, and you ran it in the night. A woman is dead because of that, and it still isn't what I did." (18).
The guilty party delivers the verdict on the innocent one and tells the reader how to weigh them. Then Kévin tows her for nothing, Jeannot brings a letter and Jeannot picks up the shovel. Against that: one woman with a pram and a laugh in a café. The scenes had already shown the difference between the two women. This is softening.
Do: cut the speech to its last two sentences, "Don't give me your sum to hide behind. I've hidden behind my own for twelve years." Let the only defence be the letter that "doesn't thank you".

**7. The women of the village share one voice. (12, 16, 17, 18.)**
"I'm not asking you why. Not once." (Arlette, 16). "I'm not asking where you were. I've been told." (Arlette, 18). "I'll say what it is to me, and then I won't say it again." (Coralie, 18). "I wasn't giving. I was telling you whose kitchen it was." (Maïté, 17).
The men each have an instrument: the numbered list, the gap in the window, the bill, the pad, "Here". The women have a manner: terse, wronged, announcing what they will and will not say. Marie-Jo survives it because she has chairs and a timer to count. Arlette, Maïté and Madame Darrigrand in the last three chapters cannot be told apart with the names covered.
Do: strip the "I'm saying / I'm not asking / I won't say it again" frames and let each speak through her own thing. Maïté has the gutter and does it perfectly once ("That back gutter's overflowing again"). Arlette has the ponies.

**8. The narrator labels the feeling after the scene has shown it. (6, 7, 11, 15, 16.)**
"In thirty years at that counter Jeannot Castagnet had never left a glass unfinished. / Maylis was ashamed." (7). "It hurt to hear." (6). "She was ashamed of that now." (11). "She was afraid." (16).
The half-full glass did the work. The label turns a woman into a case report, and it happens to Maylis more than to anyone, which is one reason she reads as watched and not felt.
Do: keep "She was scared." in the opening and its echo in 13, where it is a rhyme. Cut the rest.

**9. The narrator steps in to explain a move, and once breaks into the wrong head. (9, 13, 18.)**
"She could not do the first thing. She did not want to look at the second. She had a word for that." (13). "It was true, word for word, and it was meant to pass for Maylis." (9). "She was thinking of the landing, the Saturday before. A woman in Maylis's body had asked her to come back across, and she had sent that woman down the stairs." (18).
The platform is Maylis's central moment and it is footnoted before she speaks. The third is worse: a chapter in Maylis's head opens Coralie's to hand the reader a recap, in the one scene where Coralie should be unreadable.
Do: cut all three. On the platform go from the boy with his cap off straight to "Either".

**10. Larrère is three props and a secret. (13, 18.)**
"'Received and accepted yesterday,' he said. 'You never raised that gate. You left it, which comes to the same thing. The key goes in my tray.'" (18).
The window, "I'll be brief" and the folder are good machinery. In Lesca's village he gets one moment as a man, silent at a garden gate with his mouth a little open (17). In Maylis's village, where he does the damage, he gets none. His order flooded the Port, the husband was dragged out alive, and he dates a letter. The Good Place gives its opponents a way of being wrong that they believe. He has one and the book does not use it at the end: by the rule he was right.
Do: in 18, in silt to the knee, let him say the rule once more as a man who still believes it, "Over the line, it stays shut", before he takes out the letter.

## 4. Five things that must not be touched

1. **Coralie at the oven door, and its failure.** "'In my tray,' she said, and shut the door on him." (1), and "'I'll be brief,' she said… 'The—'" (18). The marriage is made in the first and ended in the second.
2. **Chapter 12, the landing, all of it.** "'Why not?' / 'Because you'd have told me.'" Nothing in the book is better, and nothing in it should be moved.
3. **Two fingers on the inside of the wrist.** From "A hundred and twelve" (1) to "That's her. In this hand." (9) to the full half minute in room twelve and the line through the last name (15).
4. **Kévin's hand.** "Five, and you're awake" (3); "'Five,' he said… like a man who turns over a card and finds it isn't the one he hoped for" (15); "I wanted six, Maylis. I've never wanted anything so much." (18).
5. **Jeannot's letters.** "Put it in forty-two" (1), "And six. Bugger off." (11), "Jean's for the stone" (9), and No. 42 that "doesn't thank you", with the second shovel (18).
