# Critic report: Thriller

Read: `manuscript/00.md` to `18.md`, in order, in full. Nothing else.

## 1. Scores

- **Stakes: 8.** Three stakes run at once (the Port, the man, the bed) and the third is stated by the usurper in her own hand: "*That the mayor remain seated, and take the other one's bed, and Coralie Mounet in it.*"
- **Tension: 6.5.** The takeover threat that drives chapters 4 to 9 is switched off in a subordinate clause: "It did not sit on her, so she did not go to the chair, and nobody came home in her body when the alarm rang at six."
- **Progression: 7.** Every attempt Maylis makes turns against her, and the best one is paid three chapters later: "Nobody tells me what's under my floorboards and then tells me how to vote. Shut."
- **Comedy: 7.5.** "She said it had been told twice and given two lots of stars, and it got through them early and came at one."
- **Characters: 7.5.** Coralie carries the book: "I don't get into a bed over somebody who can't speak. I've lifted too many of them."
- **Switch between dark and comic: 7.5.** Clean in the flood ("'Here,' said Monsieur Daguerre, to the roof of the van"), slack in the hall of the dead ("The mayor of Peyrebarthe sends her apologies").
- **Prose: 6.5.** Fast and exact when timed, but it names the feeling after showing it: "Maylis was ashamed." "It hurt to hear." "She was scared."
- **The ending: 7.** The cost is right and bitter ("you were a kilometre away, fetching a man out of a dry hole"), but the hinge decision gets one line and the last three chapters are in the wrong order.
- **Clarity: 8.** I was never lost. The price is paid under tension: "It was the same as on Saturday. Lesca had stayed sitting in the chair, so she had come home inside Maylis's body."

**Finished unpaid?** Yes. From the last line of chapter 4 there was no question. Before that I wavered twice, in the square in chapter 1 and through chapter 3.

**Press it on a friend?** On the friend who reads Crouch for the marriage, yes. On the one who reads him for the machine, no.

## 2. Against the comparisons

**Below Crouch overall, level with him in one sequence, above him in one thing.**

- **Above: fear for the marriage.** Dark Matter keeps the usurper offstage. Here she is inside the body at the kitchen table, the rightful owner has one hand, and the wife says "I'd have this one again." The stairs in chapter 6 and the landing in chapter 9 are where I was most afraid, and what I was afraid of was a woman being asked a straight question at last by the wrong mouth.
- **Level: the flood.** Chapter 15 from 02:53 to "Forty-one" is as good as a Crouch set piece: a clock, a distance, a body that gives a quarter of a breath.
- **Below: the machine.** Crouch gives one rule and lets the reader run ahead of it. This book has nine (the seat, who wakes first, counting, no lying on your feet, memories leaking, the bingo table, sleeping on your side, anyone can say the words, sitting on it), and at the hinge the author changes them, not the characters.
- **Below: who drives.** A Crouch lead wants something by page thirty. Maylis's first deliberate act is the last paragraph of chapter 7; her first want said aloud is "Open" in chapter 10. Lesca's want carries the first half, which works, but when Lesca is locked out in chapter 10 nothing replaces her.
- **Below: chapter ends.** Chapters 1, 3, 4, 5, 13 and 15 end on a short turn. Chapter 9 ends on a paragraph of scheduling, 12 on a threat to telephone, 11 on a comic speech after its own revelation.
- **Against The Good Place.** The reversals re-read backwards (Coralie has known the minute for twelve years; the kitchen clock down the line; the two women swapping positions at the gate), but none is the size of that show's big one, and the opening spends the largest: by chapter 2 the reader knows she looked down the track and rang.

**Premise speed:** fast enough. The date and the man are on the table at the end of chapter 2. **Where a Crouch reader puts it down:** the square in chapter 1; chapter 3's five set-up scenes; chapter 7; the first two scenes of chapter 12; chapter 17.

## 3. The ten faults, worst first

**1. The takeover threat is turned off and on by the author. Chapters 9, 10, 13.**
Chapter 9 ends by loading five mornings: "If Lesca was still sitting in the chair on any of those mornings, Lesca would come home in her." Chapter 10 opens with all five defused offstage: "For two nights since then Maylis had slept on her side at the edge." From there to the van, Lesca can only enter by invitation, and the book's second engine is idle for three chapters. Then the defence is voided by a line of Kévin's ("The thing that sits on you doesn't need you asleep") and "something heavy landed on the roof of the van", though the rule was that it comes to that bed.
Do: stage the defence and its breaking. Let Maylis find the side-sleeping in a scene and let it fail once before Friday. Then give the van a cause from the rules. Chapter 11 already has it: the thing comes at the minute of the call. Say there that the bed is only where it has always found her, and that on the anniversary it goes to the gate.

**2. The refusal at five o'clock rests on a doubt the reader cannot share. Chapter 13.**
"The only reason to think it would break at three in the morning was the word of a woman in a steel room." But chapter 10 proved that word in public: "*Tuesday 8th. At the line, on the day she said.*" The other reason, Kévin, does not separate five o'clock from three: "If that gate moves tonight before I ring… the sheet under it goes to the prosecutor", and her own plan is to raise it at night. So the hinge of the plot buys her nothing but delay, and the text presents delay as reasoning. A Crouch reader shouts "open it" and stops believing.
Do: cut the doubt. Let her know it costs the boy the same at three as at five, know the bank will go, and still say "Either" with Larrère's car a hundred metres back. Then it is her fault in full, which is what Madame Darrigrand says in chapter 18.

**3. The decision the book is built on is given to the other woman, then done in a line. Chapters 14 and 15.**
"Maylis had woken up meaning to raise the gate. Her right hand was already on the key." Five words from the woman she crossed off the pad that morning reverse her. When "Both" dies in the mud and she must choose for herself between the man and forty-two beds with Coralie among them, it reads: "She got out and went on."
Do: make the turn hers. Let her reach the platform and hold the handle, as the opening does ("She held it for three breaths"), and put it down. At the dip, hold the beat: the lamp over the winch behind, the one light of the care home, the reflector ahead, and the line to Coralie still open on the seat so that Coralie hears which way the boots go.

**4. The last three chapters are in the wrong order. Chapters 16 to 18.**
Chapter 17 settles Lesca through to Tuesday. Chapter 18 then rewinds to the night after the flood for the two women's last meeting and has to explain itself: "Her Monday in Bayonne and her Tuesday in front of the council had not happened yet, and the pillow was still in the spare room." The reckoning arrives after its outcome is known.
Do: put the steel-room scene straight after chapter 16. Then run Lesca's settlement and Maylis's Sunday, ending where it ends now.

**5. Chapter 14 explains the plan and adds a rule while the phone is ringing.**
Between the seventh and eighth ring: "This was her plan. While she kept Inguma down, it could not get up to fetch her home." And: "Lesca was in two places at once, and she could feel both." The ring count is the best device in the chapter and it is carrying three hundred words of procedure.
Do: move the plan to chapter 12, where she tells her mother "I'm going to sit on it", and let the reader arrive at the van already knowing it.

**6. Chapter 11 replays the opening. Chapters 00 and 11.**
Opening: "It ran down the clay in a sheet as wide as a door and crossed the road in her headlights. She put her hand flat on the bank beside the water. The clay moved under her palm." Chapter 11 repeats it almost word for word and takes thirty lines to reach what is new: the reflector, the second ring not waited for, the red light going under, the kitchen clock. The opening is worth its page, but it means "Did you know he was out?" is never a question for the reader.
Do: keep the opening. Start chapter 11's account where the opening stops, at "It rang once", and give only what the reader has not seen.

**7. Recaps and glosses at speed. Chapters 6, 8, 10, 13, 15.**
At 02:53 on the night: "Larrère was in Jeannot's kitchen, on Jeannot's phone, and Jeannot was a kilometre away on the flood meadows, in his duck-hunting blind, under a hatch." The pawl is explained four times ("the steel catch that held each turn", chapters 3, 6, 11, 13); the blind three more times after chapter 1.
Do: one gloss each, at first use. Cut the opening recap paragraphs of chapters 6 and 8 to a sentence.

**8. Chapter 7 does three hinge scenes in summary.**
The folder that governs the climax is set up in twenty lines: "She signed. Eighteen years of cutting verges had come down to six lines and a gap." Her one ugly act, blackmailing Jeannot, is followed by "Maylis was ashamed."
Do: give the chapter the room chapter 6 has. The signing needs her resistance; the café needs the half glass and no verdict after it.

**9. The rain is raised as a question and shrugged. Chapters 5 and 18.**
Chapter 5 makes the dates suspicious ("I cracked the rib on Monday. It sat on me that night. The rain started on Tuesday"). Chapter 18 answers: "It was weather, and nobody had sent it. They left it there." A reader of these comparisons expects the coincidence to be paid.
Do: answer it as the minute is answered in chapter 11, or take the question out of chapter 5.

**10. Hydraulics before stakes. Chapter 1; set-up load in chapter 3.**
Page four: "Ducks only come down at night onto standing water. A hand of water is enough, the depth of a palm laid flat. On Monday the river had been low. When the river is low, a raised gate lets the water run off the meadows."
Do: trim the square to the letter, the rib and Jeannot's laugh stopping. Move one of chapter 3's five scenes (the association at the winch) later or cut it by half.

## 4. Five things that must not be touched

1. **Chapter 15, 02:53 to "Forty-one".** The bank breaking ("like a lorry tipping out a load of gravel"), the lamps going out from the bank end, room twelve, the count.
2. **The soup and the stairs in chapter 6.** "I'd have this one again", and her hand locked on the post.
3. **Coralie's call log in chapter 12.** "Outgoing. Castagnet J., mobile. Not answered. Two fifty-one."
4. **The dream test turned on waking.** The reflector that "had not changed", Kévin's "Five" in the water, and "I wanted six, Maylis."
5. **Jeannot's vote and what causes it.** The threat in chapter 7 returning as "Shut" in chapter 10, and "Jeannot's hand stayed up."
