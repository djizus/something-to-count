# Round 19 — Thriller

Read: `manuscript/00.md` to `16.md`, in order, in full. Nothing else.

## 1. Scores

- **Stakes: 7.5.** "Behind it were forty houses, and the care home, with forty-two people in their beds. Out on the meadows there was one man." Countable, repeated, and the book kills one of the forty-two.
- **Tension: 7.** "At nine minutes to three something heavy landed on the roof of the van." Chapters 11 to 13 are an 8.5. Chapters 7 to 10 are a 6.
- **Progression: 6.** "'Four to three,' Larrère said. 'It stays shut.'" The gate stays shut in chapters 6, 8, 11 and 13, in nearly the same words. The noose tightens, but the problem on 8 December is the problem of 30 November.
- **Comedy: 7.** "'Either is not a person. I cannot summon Either to the station.'"
- **Characters: 7.5.** "'And six. Bugger off.'" Jeannot is alive in every line he has. Larrère is a window and a folder.
- **Switch between dark and comic: 8.** "'Five,' he said." A boy checks in a flooded yard whether he is dreaming, and later: "'I wanted six, Maylis. I've never wanted anything so much.'"
- **Prose: 6.5.** "She was scared." That sentence appears in 00, 1, 11 and 13, with variants in 3 and 5. The fear is labelled where it should be made.
- **The ending: 7.5.** "'I know what you picked. I was the other one.'" It costs a life, a marriage and a job. It takes three chapters to say so.
- **Clarity: 6.5.** "'Two. Whichever of us wakes first wakes at home, in her own bed. If the other one is still sitting in this chair when that happens, she goes home with the one who woke.'" The mechanism needs a numbered lecture and is then explained three more times.

**Finished unpaid:** yes. From chapter 11 I could not have stopped.
**Press it on a friend:** on a friend who loves The Good Place, yes. On a Crouch reader, no. They would stall in chapter 7 and I would have to promise them chapter 11.

## 2. Against the comparisons

**Below Crouch as a thriller, with two stretches level and one above.**

- **The premise does not mutate.** Dark Matter turns one rival into a crowd of them at the two-thirds mark. Recursion keeps raising what the chair can do. Here the rules set in chapter 5 are the rules of chapter 12, and the flood announced in chapter 2 arrives in chapter 13 exactly as filed: same patch, same minute, same van, same reflector. The only thing the world does that the reader did not expect is hold sixteen minutes and not ten. Dread is constant. Surprise is absent.
- **The fear for the marriage is given up at the halfway mark.** Crouch frightens a reader with a wife who cannot tell. Coralie can tell in thirty seconds and never wavers. The fear peaks at "'I'd have this one again'" (ch. 6) and is over by "'I don't get into a bed over somebody who can't speak'" (ch. 8). After that the book has only the river, and the reader already has the river's timetable.
- **Chapter endings are level with Crouch** at 3 ("'Come back across,' her mouth said"), 4 (the motion), 11 (the phone) and 13 ("'Forty-one'"). They fall below at 9 ("She listened to all of it") and 15 (the pillow, which chapter 15 has promised twice already).
- **The cost is above Crouch.** He spares the family. This book drowns its funniest character, sends the wife to Bayonne, and gives the bed back to the woman who drowned a man. The rescued man was never in danger: "The floor was dry. It was as dry as a kitchen floor."

**Where the reader is afraid, and of what.** Chapter 4 to 6: of Lesca keeping the body and the bed. Chapter 7: of a body lying open-eyed at home past its hour. Chapter 11 to 13: of the bank, minute by minute. Nowhere else.

**Where a Crouch reader puts it down.** Chapter 7, during Larrère's eleven minutes at the council table. Chapter 10, at the roll of white tape. Chapter 15, at the form in Bayonne.

**Does the central choice land.** "'Both.'" lands, and "'That was Either, with the river in it'" lands harder. The irreversible fork, at the dip, does not: see fault 5.

## 3. The ten faults, worst first

**1. Chapter 8. The book's best fear is ended in two short scenes inside a chapter that holds seven.**
"'It did. This time I've come to stay.' / Cold came in under the kitchen door." Seventeen lines later Lesca is gone. The landing refusal, the letter to the prefect, the breakfast expulsion, the river at the line, the petition, the vote, the handover note and the hall of the dead share one chapter, and a second possession is waved through in a sentence: "At six an alarm rang across the landing, and it was Lesca who got Maylis out of bed." The threat the book built from the chapter 4 motion is beaten over breakfast with no build.
*Do:* split it. Give Saturday to Sunday its own chapter. Hold Coralie's refusal back: let her take the two steps with the pillow and not answer that night, so the reader spends a day with Lesca one doorway from the bed and Maylis reduced to a wrist. Keep the refusal line for the moment she ends it. The fear then runs into flood week.

**2. Prologue and chapter 9. The secret is shown on page one and then revealed.**
00: "She climbed back down to the van and turned it around on top of the bank, until its headlights pointed out over the meadows, along the one track that crossed them." Chapter 9: "she turned the van around on top of the bank, so that its headlights pointed down the track across the meadows." The reader has known since the prologue that she looked first and rang once. Chapter 2 confirms it with "'Did you know he was out?'" Chapter 9 then spends thirty paragraphs handing it over as news, at the point where the book should be accelerating. The fact is restated in 10, 12 (twice), 14, 15 and 16.
*Do:* cut two sentences from 00, the turn of the van and the look down the track. Keep the handle, the three breaths and "It rang once." The reader then knows she rang somebody and does not know she saw him. Chapter 9 becomes a reveal. Failing that, cut chapter 9's replay to its three new facts: the kitchen clock behind Larrère's voice, the reflector going out after four minutes, the sole of the wader.

**3. Chapters 14 to 16. Three chapters of settling up after the bank has gone.**
"She had said her sentence to a whole square. She owed it to two people face to face, in a room, and Maïté was the first." Chapter 15 announces an itinerary and keeps it: Etcheto, Maïté, the ladder, the landing, Bayonne, the council, the pillow. The statement and the vote confirm what chapter 14 did. Nothing in 15 can go wrong.
*Do:* end 14 on Etcheto's question and its answer. Keep Maïté's kitchen, Larrère silent at the garden gate, and the landing. Reduce Bayonne to the two boxes and the tick. Reduce the council to four lines. Chapter 15 goes from seven scenes to three, and 16 arrives while the flood is still in the reader's body.

**4. Chapter 11, five o'clock. The hinge of the book is a non-action against a weight the reader cannot see.**
"'Either,' Maylis said." She knows forty-two people and Coralie are under a bank that breaks at three. What stops her is a folder in a car a hundred metres away. The book admits the imbalance afterwards: "I kept that gate shut for a boy's name on a sheet of paper." In the scene the weighing gets one paragraph, and she yields to Jeannot, who has no hold on her. A thriller reader stops believing in her here. They should be grieving for her.
*Do:* put Kévin on the platform. He hauls his nets an hour later in the same chapter; move that before five. Then when she fits the handle, the boy is in Larrère's headlights with a green net in his hands, caught in the act, and turning the winch sends him to prison while he watches her do it. The counterweight becomes a person.

**5. Chapter 13, the dip. The real fork gets four lines and a false frame.**
"Coralie had a list, a minibus, Kévin's van and ten minutes' warning. Jeannot had a bolted hatch and nobody coming but Maylis. / She got out and went on." This is Lesca's exact choice with Lesca's exact numbers: winch behind, man in front, a bank with minutes left. It is the scene the whole book exists for. It goes by faster than the phone call to Hontaube. And the frame is wrong: Jeannot needs nobody to come unless she opens the gate. The choice is to turn the handle with him under it, or not.
*Do:* say it straight. She is six hundred metres from doing what Lesca did. Let Larrère's phone ring through the beat, let her take one step back toward the winch, then turn. Ten lines, no more.

**6. Chapters 8 to 13. The Port believes her and goes to bed.**
"'I wrote that eight days ago, Guy.'" Her prophecy is verified in front of the café, nine people sign for Friday, and on Friday night every signer is asleep behind shutters. "'Lafitte is at his upstairs window. He says he's dry up there and he's staying.'" Madame Darrigrand signed, and her baby leaves at 3:11 over a gendarme's head. Maylis, certain of the minute, never knocks on a door in the Port. The reader asks why, and the book has no answer on the page.
*Do:* half a scene on Friday afternoon. The Port's half-measures, seen from the bank: cars moved up to the terrace, Lafitte carrying his pad upstairs, somebody saying orange is not red. It closes the hole and it is dread for free.

**7. Chapters 8, 12, 14. Lesca's death stake has no teeth.**
"'Nil.'" and then "'The mayor of Peyrebarthe sends her apologies… she will not be taking this seat.'" Since chapter 3 the rule has been that the one who cannot be fetched is found dead. Lesca holds the fetcher down, is called to the table by name, says no, walks off and wakes in a corridor with nothing wrong. Then: "'Twelve. One, two.' A chair was pulled in." The darkest idea in the book is here, that the place laid for her was filled by someone else. The text will not say whether it is true.
*Do:* decide. If her refusal cost Etcheto the seat, let Lesca hear the chair pulled in before she is fetched, at the place with her name on the card. If it did not, give her walk out of the hall a price of its own.

**8. Chapters 4, 5, 6. The mechanism is explained four times.**
Chapter 4 shows it: "Lesca had stayed sitting, and so Lesca had gone with her." Chapter 5 lectures it in three numbered points, restates it ("'So the one who stays sitting rides the one who wakes'") and closes on it ("Lesca had stayed sitting, so Lesca came home with her"). Chapter 6 opens by explaining it again: "It was the same as on Saturday. Lesca had stayed sitting in the chair, so she had come home inside Maylis's body." The pawl is glossed as "the steel catch" in 3, 6 and 9.
*Do:* keep the meeting, it is Lesca in one scene. Cut point Two to a line, since the reader lived it a chapter earlier. Delete the second paragraph of chapter 6. Gloss the pawl once.

**9. Chapter 10. The clock is at its loudest and the book cuts to a tape measure.**
"He had a clipboard and a roll of white tape. He paced from the steps to the stone, tore off two strips and stuck them on the planks beside it, in a cross." Chapter 9 ends at five on the morning of the flood. Chapter 10 opens with a mother's visit and a rehearsal. Only the third scene, the call log, earns the delay.
*Do:* cut Arlette and the rehearsal to a page between them. Keep the ponies, "'Monsieur le maire stands here'", and the eight strips of tape for forty-two beds. Get to "'Outgoing. Castagnet J., mobile. Not answered. Two fifty-one'" twice as fast.

**10. Chapter 5. The book asks why the rain came and drops the question.**
"'Then it had been raining for two days before you did anything.'" … "'I want somebody to blame.'" The whole clock rests on a river that repeats a twelve-year-old file day for day. The book raises the why, lets a character fail to answer it, and moves on. Dr Kessler, the university and "keep it with you until someone asked" never return. A thriller reader waits for that gun to the last page.
*Do:* pay it or stop pointing at it. The cheap fix is to cut the six lines and let the replay be a given. The better one is a single late beat for the two cards, *left* and *right*, that makes them matter to the plot and not only to Maïté's verb.

## 4. Five things that must not be touched

1. **The minute stamps of chapter 13 and the quiet break.** "It wasn't loud. It sounded like a truck tipping out a load of gravel, a long way off to her right, and the sound didn't stop."
2. **"'Both.'" and its answer three chapters on.** "'I know that word. I've cooked it. That was Either, with the river in it.'"
3. **The dry cellar, and the antagonist being right.** "'He stays dry as long as you do what you're told.'"
4. **The end of chapter 11 into the eight rings of chapter 12.** A phone's ring count as the clock of a fight for a handbrake.
5. **"'Forty-one.'" and the list that did it.** "'It was your mayor's list. I pinned it up, and we did what was pinned up.'"
