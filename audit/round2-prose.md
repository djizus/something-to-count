# Critic report, round 2: prose

Read: `manuscript/01.md` to `23.md`, in order, in full. 58,162 words. Counts below were taken across all 23 chapters.

## 1. Scores

- **Stakes: 7.** "That village had not been spared December 2014. It had not had it yet." (8) The stakes are exact once stated, and they are stated at 35 per cent of the book.
- **Tension: 6.** "And what was there to take? A man would come up a ladder over there, swearing." (20) The narrator reasons at the moment the two women first face each other. Where a clock is on the page (17, 19, 21) the tension is a 9.
- **Progression: 6.** "Inguma came at 02:51, and Lesca let it put her under." (4), then "Inguma came at 02:51, and Maylis let it take her." (5). Chapters 3 to 10 run the same day-then-chair loop in the same sentences, and the river gauge is the only thing that moves.
- **Comedy: 8.** "There's nothing here that lets me un-receive it. There's no column." (11)
- **Characters: 7.** "'I don't cook Either,' she said. 'There's no recipe for it. Soup or duck.'" (1) Coralie is alive in every line. The villagers are told apart by their subjects and share one way of speaking.
- **The switch between dark and comic: 8.** "'Here,' said Monsieur Daguerre, to the roof of the van." (21)
- **Prose: 7.** "From the bar Maylis had ninety-one backs." (7) The sentence is good, and its verb does that job several hundred times.
- **The ending: 8.** "Then he stepped over the string, in his waders, and got down into it." (23)
- **Clarity: 5.** "There was no van in it. Nobody in the square that morning had said the word." (6) The word is given sixteen chapters later.

Finished unpaid: yes. Press it on a friend: yes, with the instruction to read as far as chapter 8 before judging, which no good book should need.

## 2. Against the comparisons

Below the bar, by one point, for the reader the bar names.

1. **Above Crouch in the sentence.** "Then the water came up the door to it and put it out." (17) has no adjective and no comment, and Crouch writes nothing as good. Chapter 21 beats his set pieces as prose: timed, physical, and funny inside the disaster.
2. **Below Crouch in transmission.** Crouch states his premise in a sentence a reader can repeat, and no reader of his wonders who "she" is or what "it" refers to. This book makes the reader rebuild the premise from pronouns and council vocabulary ("item", "motion", "transfer", "the other", "the word"). A Crouch reader stops in chapter 6.
3. **Level with The Good Place on the gag that turns into plot, below it on voices.** "I'll be brief" ends in "Nothing came." and "still in the box" is retired by the village in 23. Those reversals re-read what came before, as the show's do. But Eleanor, Chidi, Tahani and Jason can each be identified from one untagged line. Here only Père Honoré and Larrère can.

The word-level pattern check comes back clean: no intensifiers, no filler adverbs ("suddenly", "really", "seemed" and "very" do not occur). The tells are structural: paired negative-then-positive closers, lists and findings in threes ("three" occurs 104 times), and uniform short declaratives. The mean narrated sentence is 11 words, a third are six words or fewer, and 16 per cent begin with "She".

## 3. The ten faults, worst first

**1. The prose hides its nouns. (2, 6, 7, 12, 16, 17; structural)**
"There was no van in it. Nobody in the square that morning had said the word." (6) "The ground under the van was mud and no more than mud. Lesca wrote *mud* in the minute and went no further into it." (6) "It followed that whatever Lesca carried about until bedtime went the other way, and for a week the other one had been getting it unsorted." (6) "It had lost the ground she had liked best, and she left it on the table with the other." (16) "She had seen him at it, from the third row, with the medal not yet struck." (12)
Each of these makes the reader stop and work out what "it", "the word" and "the other" are. The withheld call at 02:51 is a secret the book is entitled to keep. These are different: facts the reader needs in order to follow the scene, kept back by habit. Do this: for each mechanism, write one plain sentence at the point where the character learns it. In 6, say what crosses ("what she thinks about by day, the other woman dreams that night") before the agenda metaphor, and say "drowned" or cut the sentence about the word. Then go through the Lesca chapters and replace every "it", "that" and "the other" whose noun is more than two sentences back.

**2. Each discovery is told two or three times, and the telling is an explanation. (5, 6, 8, 14, 16, 20; structural)**
In 5 Kévin says "That's two of you, Maylis." The narrator then says "The stone, the sash and the signature had each wanted an owner, and the card had given them one." The note then says "*There are two of us. She is the mayor.*" In 6: "She had gone down the track, then. She had knocked. That was why he had lived to write twenty-nine more letters." followed by "*She went down the track and knocked. He is alive there because of it.*" In 20, between "Inguma was in the chair." and the five words, there are about 330 words of reasoning, ending "None of that could be paid from inside another woman's coat."
The narrator's voice changes in these passages from chronicler to explainer. Do this: one carrier per discovery. Where a note states it, cut the paragraph above the note. In 20, keep the taken seat, the forearm across the ribs, "It was her night. Lesca had had hers.", the voice calling twelve, and the open hand. Cut the rest. In 14 and 16, cut the weighing to the motion and the word *Nil*.

**3. The two-village design makes the book explain everything twice. (throughout; structural)**
What the gate does is explained six times: 2 ("Shut, the gate kept the river leaning on the dyke"), 5 ("Raised then, the gate would give the river three hundred hectares"), 17 ("Raised, the gate would give the river all of that"), 19, 21 and 23. The steel room is explained in 1, three times in 4, and in 5. "Dr Kessler had called her the friend all afternoon" appears in 1 and again in 4, and "the friend" is never used again. The tonne is glossed in 1, 2 and 12. Glass eels are explained in 3 and 6. "She ran the home" is said in 2, 4 and 20. Do this: keep a register of glosses and gloss each term once in the book. The second village inherits the first village's explanations. Cut "the friend" in both places.

**4. "Had" is one word in fifty. (throughout)**
1,153 uses. Two habits make the number. The first is perception as possession: "Over the rivets she had the wall clock, which said nineteen minutes to eight, and under the clock she had Coralie." (16) "She had each of them past the edge of the leaves" (14). The second is backstory in pluperfect blocks: "In 2022 the commune had fixed a camera above each of its gates, and its mayor had sent the footage to the biodiversity police. Men had netted glass eels at those gates since before there were gates" (6). The possession verb is right when she is paralysed and her eyes are all she owns. Keep it for those scenes and use "saw", "heard" and "knew" elsewhere. Move each pluperfect block into a line of speech or an object in the room, as the Castagnet box file does in 2.

**5. The village has one voice. (throughout)**
Sarthou: "I'd a file open." (17) Maïté: "I'd a suitcase packed on the wardrobe." (22) Lafitte: "I'd the pencil in my hand" (23). Kévin, a poacher in his twenties who reads dream forums: "I'd not slept since Thursday" (23). A stranger on the telephone at Hontaube: "Then it wanted posting last Monday." (19) "'ll not" and "'d not" occur 34 times and "it wants" nine, across every speaker. Do this: keep the construction for Jeannot, Arlette, Lafitte and Madame Etcheto. Give Kévin, Coralie, Bixente and every outsider ordinary contractions. Give Sarthou the syntax of a report.

**6. People are characterised by what they do not do. (throughout)**
"Did not" occurs 89 times. "He did not say what he had been told about nets, or by whom, or what he had done with it." (17) "He did not say that she was wrong." (16) "She had not asked whether there was." (12) "She did not say that she forgave it, and Lesca had not come for that." (22) The device works when the absence is the event: Coralie not asking is the marriage. It fails when the narrator points at subtext the line before has already carried. "I baptise with water. I do not know where it has been." needs no sentence after it. Cut the pointing ones, which is about half.

**7. One simile, repeated. (throughout)**
The form is "as a [type of person] does who": about twenty instances. "He said it as a man tries a door he hopes is locked." (10) is the best. "He said it as a man turns up a card he had hoped was another." (21) is the same move. "Maylis told the hand to stop, as a person does who finds her own fingers writing in a script that leans." (15) compares the event to itself. The sister form, "for as long as it takes to count a chair" (1), "to read a number plate" (7), "to shuffle and deal" (9), occurs six times. Keep five of the first, two of the second, and cut the one in 15.

**8. Reminders are mistaken for refrains. (odd chapters)**
The refrains that work change meaning on their last use. The reminders do not change: the rib posture is restated 12 times ("with her forearm against her side"), "left-handed" 10, "in his waders" 11, "dressed for the night" 4, "a glass of white" 6. After 5, mention the rib only when it alters what she can do (the forking in 11, the run in 21). Keep the waders for 1 and for the last line of the book.

**9. The frame is formula. (3 to 17)**
Seventeen chapters open "On [weekday]", three of them "woke with [X] in her head". The descent is nine times "went in at half past four" or its twin, the return five times "wrote before she moved", the release six times a little finger. Seven chapters end on an italic note. After 8 the reader knows the ritual, so enter the chair in a clause and spend the words on what is new there. End no more than three chapters on a note.

**10. Usage and consistency.**
- Interrupted speech has no mark: "did you'" (18), "like your father never'" (21). Both read as typos. The book uses no dash and no semicolon anywhere. Allow a dash here or close the line in narration.
- "Monsieur le maire" (1, 11, 12) against "Monsieur le Maire" (2, 20).
- "crown" (16 uses) and "crest" (6) for the top of the dyke, both in consecutive paragraphs of 22.
- Dates in narration take three forms: "Tuesday 24 November" (1), "From 1 January" (5), "the twelfth of December 2014" (8). One paragraph of 8 has "on 24 November 2014" and "On the first of December".
- Written words are italic in some places (*right*, *mud*, *laughed*, *Nil*) and roman in others (Either, eggs, Received, Removed, Dressing). "Delivered" is roman and then italic in consecutive paragraphs of 22.
- "Dressing, tonight" (11) becomes "Dressing, and Tonight" (23).
- Maylis lies down at "twenty past" (5), "half past four" (7) and "twenty-five past four" (9, 13, 15, 17).
- "which is what gendarmes call their station" (7) is the one gloss in the present tense.
- The alarm goes at six, Coralie says "Fourteen minutes", and the clock reads 06:16 (15).
- "nobody" 84 times, "no one" 4.
- "The chair" is the bolted chair, the presiding office and the mayor's desk chair. The pun pays in "That the mayor remain seated" and should be kept for the Lesca chapters. In 7, "The chair had given it to Maylis" reads as a committee. "Box" has seven referents, four of them on one page of 11.

## 4. Five things that must not be touched

1. Chapter 5, the association at the winch, to "The association resolved, by seven votes, to seek an estimate."
2. Chapter 17, the night of 2014 from the telephone to "put it out", and Jeannot's numbered answer at five in the morning.
3. Chapter 21 from the ramp to "'Forty-one,' Coralie said.", with Kévin's "Five." before it and "I wanted six, Maylis." in 23.
4. The refrains that turn: the car window to "found nothing between them to wind up", "I'll be brief" to "Nothing came.", "took note", "Here", and "still in the box" retired for "had the key and gone for a walk".
5. Coralie's lines as written: the eggs in 1, the pad, and the landing in 18 from "Nine minutes to three" to "Because you'd have told me."
