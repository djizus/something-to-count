# Critic report, round 21: Thriller

Read: `manuscript/00.md` to `17.md`, in order, in full. Nothing else but the brief.

## 1. Scores

- **Stakes: 7.5.** A body, a marriage, a man and forty-two beds, all dated. "It's a whole life with one hand."
- **Tension: 7.5.** Chapters 6, 12 and 14 are at 9; chapters 1, 7 and 16 pull the average down. "*Asleep. I'm not waking a woman of 94 for orange. Keep asking me things.*"
- **Progression: 6.5.** The river advances a line a day ("Your river gauge reads what my file reads, day for day") while the story tells its first week twice and ends four crossings the same way.
- **Comedy: 8.** "'Either is not a person. I cannot summon Either to the station.'"
- **Characters: 8.** "'I don't get into a bed over somebody who can't speak. I've lifted too many of them.'"
- **The switch between dark and comic: 8.5.** In the flooded corridor: "'Here,' he said. 'I know you are.'"
- **Prose: 7.** Fast and concrete, but the body-sharing scenes stack names to dodge pronouns: "Lesca waited for the arm. Then Lesca got the left shoulder under the handle and pushed with the legs."
- **The ending: 7.5.** The aftermath costs more than the choice does. "'I know what you picked. I was the other one.'"
- **Clarity: 6.5.** The rule the plot runs on is back to front and has to be recomputed at every crossing: "Whichever of us wakes first wakes at home. If the other one is still sitting here, she goes home with her."

Would I have finished it unpaid: yes, from chapter 4 without stopping. Would I press it on a friend: yes, and I would tell them to read to the last line of chapter 4 before judging.

## 2. Against the comparisons

**Below Crouch as a thriller, by about a point. Level with him on fear for the marriage. Above him on what the ending costs.**

- **Speed of the engine: below.** Dark Matter has its man taken at gunpoint in chapter 1 and living the wrong life by chapter 3. Here the premise is stated at the end of chapter 2 ("That village had not been spared its flood. It hadn't had it yet"), which is fast, but the two women do not share a scene until chapter 5 and the possession does not run for a full day until chapter 6. Before that there are two committee meetings and an inspection of a winch.
- **Size of the opponent: below.** Crouch escalates the rival until the last page: one Jason becomes a hundred. Here the rival peaks on the landing in chapter 8 and concedes in chapter 13 after eight rings. The human obstacle in the second half is a man in a parked car with a folder. Crouch also gives a mechanism; this book asks its own question ("A winch doesn't make it rain") and answers "I want somebody to blame". That holds as a fable. A Crouch reader will notice it.
- **Fear for the marriage: level.** Lesca walking Maylis's body to the fourth stair is the equal of the other Jason in Daniela's kitchen, and Coralie detecting the swap by pulse is better than anything Daniela is given.
- **Reversal: below The Good Place.** There is no turn that re-reads the book. The secret (she saw the van) is shown in the prologue. The nearest thing is "Outgoing. Castagnet J., mobile. Not answered. Two fifty-one", which re-reads one landing, and the dry cellar, which re-reads one night.
- **Cost: above.** Dark Matter lets its man keep his wife. This one takes the wife, the job and a woman of ninety-four, and makes the rescued man say he was in no danger.

## 3. The ten faults, worst first

**1. Chapter 14. The central choice is made by a stuck van and softened by a reassurance.**
"Coralie had a list, a minibus, Kévin's van and ten minutes' warning. Jeannot had a bolted hatch and nobody coming but Maylis. She got out and went on."
This is the moment chapter 17 calls the choice ("It wasn't Either at the dip... I picked"), and it is three sentences. She does not choose Jeannot over Coralie. She tells herself Coralie is covered and goes. Room twelve, which she texted about at ten and phoned about two minutes earlier, is not in her head. So the cost arrives as a miscalculation and not as a price she agreed to pay, and "I was the other one" has to do in chapter 17 what the dip should have done. Fix: make this the longest beat in the chapter. Take the comfort away. She knows what six minutes at the winch buys (the bank end dry, twelve dry, Coralie dry) and what it does to a bolted hatch four minutes later. She looks back at the lamp over the platform. Then she goes forward knowing it. Sixty words becomes two hundred. Nothing else in the chapter changes.

**2. Chapters 3, 4 and 5. The first week is told twice, and the engine starts a chapter late.**
Chapter 3: "She read the card, looked away, and read it again. *right*". Chapter 4: "She looked away... Then she read the card again. *left*". The sentence "Come back across. I've left you on that landing eleven years" is delivered at the end of 3, in full in 4, and quoted back at the top of 5. Inguma comes down off the wardrobe in full in chapter 1, again at its close, in 2, twice in 3 and in full in 4.
Each replay is well done and each one costs forward motion at the point where a Crouch reader is deciding whether to stay. Fix by cutting, not rebuilding. Tell each event once, from the woman it costs more. Chapter 4 keeps the file, the crossing and the motion; its envelope and its first chair scene shrink to the warmth and one word on a card. Chapter 5 opens after Coralie has repeated the sentence, not on it.

**3. Chapters 15 to 17. Three chapters of aftermath, and chapter 16 tells the reader what the reader has.**
"'It was with me,' Lesca said. 'It was sitting in a chair that I wanted, with its back to me, so I sat down on top of it and held on to the arms.'" That is chapter 13 explained to a wheelchair. "Guy was here. That night. In this kitchen" was given by "the kind that hangs in a kitchen" in chapter 10 and "Castagnet, house" in chapter 14. After "Forty-one" the only open questions are what Lesca will say and whether either Coralie stays. Chapter 15 answers the first. Chapter 16 spends a kitchen, a ladder and a landing confirming things. Fix: cut the Etcheto exchange to her question and one answer. Cut the kitchen to the verb ("*Left*") and go straight to the garden gate, where Larrère's "I'll be brief" and his silence do all of it. Keep the form with two boxes. Chapter 16 should be half its length.

**4. Chapter 7. The heroine is stranded and does nothing, and the frightening half is offstage.**
"She sat still and waited for the cold." She is five hours overdue in another woman's body, with her own lying open-eyed beside Coralie, and she reads statutes and chairs a council. She never tries to get home. Meanwhile the scene with fear in it, Coralie alone for thirteen hours with a body, is three lines at the top of chapter 8: "Thirteen hours, with your eyes open, and I haven't gone in to work." Fix: give Maylis one attempt to go back at half past two (the words, the mayor's bed, anything) and let it fail. Cut Larrère's untimed speech by a third. Give Coralie's thirteen hours a real scene when Maylis lands.

**5. Chapter 13. The woman who said "I won't go" is talked out of it by the narrator.**
"If she let go, Maylis had the van. Maylis also had the winch, and nobody to stop her. All Lesca could do was tell her, once, on the way out."
The fight in the van is the showdown the book has promised since the stairs, and it runs about five hundred words, of which ring Seven is two paragraphs of reasoning. Her surrender is a calculation about a stalemate. Fix: cut the reasoning to the image that is already there ("the reflector was red, and no nearer than it had been twelve years ago") and let her hand open on that. Buy the room back with two more physical exchanges before the handbrake.

**6. Chapter 12. Four hours in a van, with the rescue she later performs available all night.**
"There was still time to go down the track and knock. But he had not come up for her in daylight... and nothing she could say to him at one would be any different until there was water at his hatch." At 03:03 she says nothing persuasive: "She grabbed the collars of both his jackets in her left hand and pulled." A thriller reader has thought of that by midnight. The honest reason is the one she gives Kévin two days later: "I waited for the river to decide, so that it wouldn't be me." Fix: put that in the van at 01:12, as hers, in place of the excuse.

**7. Chapter 13. The rule of the death changes at the moment it is used.**
Chapter 3: "If it cannot fetch her, she is found dead in her own bed in the morning." Chapter 13: "She had kept Inguma from fetching her for eight rings of a phone, and that had been long enough." Then she walks out of the hall of the dead by sending apologies, and nobody stops her. The reader cannot be afraid for Lesca in a scene whose deadline is set as it passes and whose exit is unopposed. The bill goes to room twelve, which is good, but it is paid in chapter 15, not in the hall. Fix: one line in the chapter 9 chair scene fixing how long "cannot fetch" has to last, and one moment of resistance in the hall before she gets up.

**8. Prologue, chapters 2, 5, 10 and 15. The night of 2014 is told five times.**
The prologue shows the headlights turned down the track and "The number was already in it." Chapter 10 then plays the same facts as a finding: "So Lesca had known. She had seen his van before she opened the gate." Keep the prologue. Cut the summary in chapter 5 to the date and the cellar. Cut chapter 10 to what is new: Coralie's car in the yard, the reflector, the four minutes, the sole of the wader.

**9. Chapters 7, 9 and 12. Larrère's one lever is pulled three times and is never dangerous.**
Chapter 7: "That's prison, for eels." Chapter 12: "the sheet under it goes to the prosecutor at Bayonne, with four winters of eel nets written under Kévin Labat's name." The book itself disowns the lever ("that was the reason she would give herself afterwards"), so for five chapters the obstacle is one neither she nor the reader believes. Fix: chapter 12 does it with the hand on the folder and "That's the file". Cut the restatement.

**10. Chapters 8, 10 and 15 end on a settle.**
"She listened to all of it." "Somebody has to stack them." Chapter 10 is the worst case: the decision the phone call produces (she crosses out the handover note and will do the gate herself) is held back to the first paragraph of chapter 12. Move it to the last line of 10.

## 4. Five things that must not be touched

1. The last line of chapter 4: "*That the mayor remain seated, and take the other one's bed, and Coralie Mounet in it.*" It is the best chapter ending in the book.
2. The platform at five in chapter 12, through "'Either,' Maylis said. 'Then it's shut,' Jeannot said. 'That's how the vote left it.'"
3. The three texts at ten in chapter 12, ending "Keep asking me things."
4. Kévin counting his fingers in the flooded yard ("'Five,' he said") and its answer in chapter 17 ("I wanted six, Maylis").
5. The old wing in chapter 14, from the ramp through doors nine, ten and eleven to "'Forty-one,' Coralie said."
