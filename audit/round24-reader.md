# Round 24 — Reader

Read: `manuscript/00.md` to `16.md`, in order, in full. Nothing else opened but the brief.

Verdict against the bar: short of it. A good book (7.3) that is as good as the comparisons from chapter 9 to chapter 14 and costs more to get into than either of them.

## 1. Scores

- **Stakes — 8.** "'Ten minutes against twenty-five. You turn the handle first.'" (8) I could always count what was at risk: forty houses, forty-two beds, one man, one bed with two pillows.
- **Tension — 7.5.** "In her hand, the phone began to ring." (11) Chapters 11 to 13 are a 9. Chapters 1, 2 and 6 are a 6.
- **Progression — 6.5.** "*That the mayor remain seated, and take the other one's bed, and Coralie Mounet in it.*" (3) That line is where the book starts for me, and it is on the last line of the fourth file.
- **Comedy — 7.** "'And six. Bugger off.'" (9) I laughed out loud four times: Bixente's two lots of stars, the hand signing letters on the floor, "Then it should have gone last Monday", and this.
- **Characters — 8.** "'I know that word. I've cooked it. That was Either, with the river in it.'" (16)
- **The switch between dark and comic — 8.** "'Then they'll want the bins out,' he said." (13) Said by a man in a wheelchair being pushed through a flood.
- **Prose — 7.** Easy at its best: "It sounded like a truck tipping out a load of gravel, a long way off, and the sound didn't stop." (13) Hard work at its worst: "'You didn't have to be so quick,' Larrère said to her. 'In my tray. I'll be brief. The winch.'" (1)
- **The ending — 8.** "Then he stepped over the string in his waders and began to dig." (16)
- **Clarity — 6.** "'Two. When one of us wakes, she wakes at home. If the other one has stayed in this chair, she goes home with her.'" (4) I read that three times and needed Maylis's next line to translate it.

**Finished unpaid?** Yes. I would have stopped once, in the middle of chapter 2, and the old woman in room twelve kept me.

**Press it on a friend?** Yes, with the instruction to read to the end of chapter 3 before deciding. Having to say that is the distance from the bar.

## 2. Against the comparisons

**Below, by a short distance.**

1. **Cost of entry — below Crouch.** Dark Matter needs no vocabulary and has its man taken by the end of the first chapter. This book asks me to hold a winch, a pawl, a sluice gate, a hand of water, a registered letter, an association, a keyholder and glass eels before it tells me what the gate does. The plain sentence that does ("This gate is a door in that bank. Raise it, and the river runs out over the meadows, where there's grass and nobody asleep.") arrives in chapter 5.
2. **The reversal — below The Good Place.** That show's turn is one person saying one sentence that every viewer understands at once. This book's turn, that the rescuer is the flood, is a sentence the speaker cannot finish ("'That's the—'"), in a scene that also holds "Both" and the table of the dead. I understood it. I did not feel the floor go.
3. **What stays — above Crouch, level with The Good Place.** I cannot name a secondary character from Dark Matter. I will be able to name Jeannot, Coralie and Madame Etcheto next year. And the moral shape, that asking for both is Either with a river in it, is as clean as that show's best.

**What I did not understand.** What the gate did, until chapter 5. The "red line", used in chapter 2 and explained in chapter 3. Where the Port lies in relation to the meadows (fault 7). Whether a night without Lesca slows the river or not (fault 4). Who could lawfully order the gate up (fault 6). "Paid." at the foot of the handover note. Why Marie-Jo handed back her sash in 2018; I connected it to her son's prison in chapter 14, and only because of a coat.

**Where I skimmed.** The square in chapter 1 from the postman to Jeannot's four points. The Thursday and Friday entries in chapter 2. The statutes and the first half of Larrère's speech in chapter 6. The strips of tape in chapter 10.

**Where I could not stop.** The end of 2 into 3. All of 5. All of 9. From Jeannot's van pulling up on the bank in 11 to "Forty-one" at the end of 13, without looking up.

**What I will remember in a month.** A man swearing at her in six points because he is alive. "Twelve. One, two." Coralie talking into the oven, and the time she couldn't. The car window going all the way down. Kévin counting five fingers in the water. Jeannot's waders in the grave.

## 3. The ten faults that most hold it back

**1. The first two chapters load the reader before they pay him. (1, 2)**
"'One. A duck comes down at night onto standing water, and onto nothing else. Two. A hand of water does it.'" I was asked to follow a quarrel about a gate before I knew what the gate was for. By the end of chapter 2 I was carrying: nineteen turns, letter No. 41, the hand of water, the eels, the key, the envelope, the stone, the sash, the words, the bingo of the dead and the finger count. Chapter 2 has six sections, and three of them are diary entries ("On Thursday Maylis took the envelope out of the top drawer", "On Friday Maylis woke with grey water in her head").
*Do:* Put the chapter 5 sentence about the gate into the square in chapter 1. Cut the association meeting on the bank from chapter 2: "'The keyholder has no preference'" repeats what the shopping pad and the car window already showed, and the nineteen turns are already known. Fold Thursday and Friday into one night. The other woman's voice should come out of Maylis's mouth a third sooner than it does.

**2. The woman who came to take the body leaves in a paragraph. (12)**
"She had come to take this life from a woman who never said what she wanted, and that woman had just said it." Then an italic line, then "In the steel room the mayor let go of the chair." The thing I was afraid of since chapter 3, a stranger in your bed with your wife, ends because of one sentence, after eight rings of slapstick over a gear lever. I was told she decided. I did not see it cost her. And I cannot tell what her five words did: at the handle in chapter 13 Maylis does not hear them ("She wanted the man and the gate, both, and she wanted to be the one who got them"), yet in chapter 16 Lesca says "I sent you down that track" and Maylis says "I drove past the handle to be the one who did."
*Do:* On ring eight, give Lesca the thing chapter 10 gave her: a Coralie four steps away who has known for twelve years and left her door open. Let that be what she lets go for. Then let Maylis hear "Go to the blind first", in her own voice pitched low, during the three breaths at the handle, so the five words tip something I can see.

**3. Chapter 8 ends on her name at the table of the dead, and she never thinks of it again. (8, 9, 11)**
"LESCA, MAYLIS." Chapter 9 opens "'Hand,' Coralie said." Nowhere in 9 or 11 does Maylis remember that a place is laid in her name and that a laid place is "filled by morning, by her or by another." In 12 she "looked once at the lit hall, where one chair stood empty." I turned the page on the best chapter ending in the book and it was not picked up.
*Do:* Put the card in her head at the dip. If she knows a chair is waiting for somebody, "both" stops being hope and becomes a bet she knows one person loses. That is a worse and better choice.

**4. The river's rule contradicts itself. (7, 8, 11)**
Chapter 7: "Since then the river had made up everything it had missed, and it was back on the file, to the centimetre." Chapter 8: "'One day of my file for every night I sit in that chair with you.'" Chapter 11: "If it didn't get her into that chair before four, nobody crossed tonight, and the bank held." The first says a missed night costs the river nothing. The second says each night buys it a day. The third stakes the whole night on the rule, with the river already going "past the platform at the height of her knees." I did not know what staying awake could save.
*Do:* Choose one, and have Maylis say it once in her own plain words in chapter 8. In chapter 11, one sentence naming what has still to come across: the last line of the file.

**5. The room where only the truth can be said is never pointed at the book's question. (4, 10, 15, 16)**
"'Standing in this room, a woman can only say what's true.'" Lesca is asked three times which she turned the handle for, and answers the same three times, the last in that room: "'In here it would have to be true. I don't know.'" At home in chapter 10 she tried both answers aloud and "That sounded no more true, and no less." I waited for her to try them in the room, where a false one would stop in her mouth. She never does.
*Do:* In chapter 16 let her try "for forty houses" and "for Coralie" in the steel room, and let me hear which comes out whole, or that both do, or neither. Any of the three is new. A third "I don't know" is not.

**6. Six sittings, and the legal route leads nowhere. (2, 3, 6, 8, 15)**
"Maylis ran the left forefinger down it and stopped under *on the order of the prefect*." Then the other finger, then "the line above that one", then "written". I skimmed. The letter to the prefect in chapter 7 comes back in 11 as an envelope that "said nothing about a gate." And chapter 3 promised "a mayor, a gendarme and the director of a care home, during office hours"; Monday delivers a car window and a café. The director is asked for at ten past six and never heard of again.
*Do:* Cut the statutes to two lines. Keep chapter 6's council for Larrère's speech with no bell and the question the hand cuts off. Give the prefect's letter an answer that arrives too late, or take the letter out.

**7. The climax is arithmetic on a map I was never given. (13, 16)**
"The winch was six hundred metres behind her, and the red reflector was four hundred metres in front. The bank had six minutes left." I could do the sum. I could not see the ground. The Port and the meadows are both on the land side of one bank, and I never learned why water let onto one does not reach the other. The patch is placed "two hundred metres along" only in chapter 8.
*Do:* One walk along the bank in chapter 1, naming in order the care home, the patch, the gate, the track and the blind, and what stands between the meadows and the houses. A drawn map before the opening would do more than any sentence.

**8. Chapter 15 explains chapters 12 and 13 to me. (15)**
"'The place was laid because I held on over there, for eight rings of a phone. Who filled it was settled out on a track, by the other one.'" A mayor tells a woman of ninety-four that there are two of her and that the other one drowned, and the woman takes it without a blink. I needed the explanation, and that is the fault.
*Do:* Fix fault 3, then cut this scene down to her question, "Twelve. One, two", "That's my door" and "Nobody emptied hers."

**9. The names. (throughout)**
Lesca, Larrère, Labat, Lafitte, Lassalle. Darrigrand, Daguerre, Dufau, Ducasse. Castagnet, Castéra. In chapter 8, "'I wrote that eight days ago, Guy,'" is Lafitte speaking to Larrère about Lesca. I stopped there, and again in the café in chapter 5, to work out which L I had. The card dealer is "the dealer" for fifteen chapters and Fernand for one line.
*Do:* Rename Lafitte and Lassalle, and two of the Ds.

**10. Lines that depend on something done offstage. (3, 5, 7, 8, 13)**
"'Six until nine,' Lesca said to the right hand. 'Fifteen hours for a lake.'" "'It's done my signatures.'" "*Paid.*" The sash returned "in a plastic bag" in 2018. Maylis's card, which comes out of her pocket in the flood and is buttoned back in at the grave without my knowing what it is there to do. Each cost me a re-read, and two I never solved.
*Do:* One clause each. For the lake: she gave it the gravel loads. For the plastic bag: it was the week her son went to prison.

## 4. Five things that must not be touched

1. **Chapter 9 entire.** The night of 2014 told flat, then nine rings, then "'Who's dead?' said Jeannot."
2. **The chain of twelve.** "'That's my door'" at the bingo (7), "'Twelve. One, two.'" in the dark (14), and "'Forty-one,' Coralie said" (13).
3. **The oven door, three times.** "'In my tray,' she said, and shut the door on him" (1), "'It stays shut'" (8), and "'I'll be brief,' she said… 'The—'" (16).
4. **"'Either,' Maylis said"** on the platform in daylight, with the man standing behind her (11), and what Coralie makes of it in 16.
5. **Kévin's hand.** "'Five,' he said" in his own headlights (13), and "'I wanted six, Maylis. I've never wanted anything so much'" (16).
