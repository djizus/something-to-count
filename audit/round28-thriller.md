# Round 28: Thriller

Read: `manuscript/00.md` to `16.md`, in order, in full. Nothing else.

## 1. Scores

- **Stakes: 7.** "Ten minutes against twenty-five. You turn the handle first." The sum is clear and the reader can do it. But the book has two stakes, the bank and the bed, and at the climax they stop touching each other (fault 1).
- **Tension: 6.** The best of it is as tight as the comparison: "'Are you coming up?'" (5). But the fear for the marriage is over by Sunday breakfast in 8, and the watch in 11 passes in "The screen said 23:34. Then the screen said 01:12."
- **Progression: 6.5.** The main contest is lost twice in the same words, to an injury and not to a choice: "On the first breath the rib caught and held" (7), "On the sixth ring the rib caught and held" (11).
- **Comedy: 8.** "Either is not a person. I cannot summon Either to the station."
- **Characters: 8.5.** "I don't get into a bed over somebody who can't speak. I've lifted too many of them."
- **The switch between dark and comic: 8.** "The mayor of Peyrebarthe sends her apologies. She has a ceremony at 11, and she will not be taking this seat."
- **Prose: 7.5.** "The gate was a post on top of that line, no bigger than a nail." The tic that shows: "Maylis hadn't told it to." / "She hadn't told them to." / "Maylis hadn't said it."
- **The ending: 7.** "Then he stepped over the string in his rubber boots and began to dig." The right last image, and it costs both women the same woman. It has no last turn.
- **Clarity: 6.** "Then there was no steering wheel, and no rain." At the moment of most danger I did not know what the danger was (fault 5).

Would I have finished it unpaid? Yes. Would I press it on a friend? Yes, on the friend who loved The Good Place. Not on the friend who wants Crouch.

## 2. Against the comparisons

**Below Crouch as a thriller. Level with him, or above, on the choice.**

- **Below: the usurper never wins.** In Dark Matter the other Jason has the house and the wife for most of the book, and that is what the reader is afraid of. This book has the same asset and it is its best: the stairs in 5, the landing in 7. Then Coralie puts Lesca out with her own hands one scene later. Lesca's whole gain, by her own count, is "one bowl of soup at your table." After chapter 8 nobody is afraid for that bed again.
- **Below: the heroine waits.** A Crouch chapter ends because his man did something and it went wrong. Here the heroine's nature is to not act, which is the subject, but the nine hours on the bank in 11 are not staged as a refusal. They are skipped.
- **Level or above: the mechanism.** The dip, the nineteenth turn, the reflector, 2:51, the word Either: each is planted in comedy and comes back as the thing that kills. Crouch does not plant this carefully. And the choice is harder than his. "She wanted it not to be her" is the trolley problem answered by a person, and The Good Place would have been glad of it.

A Crouch reader will also see a steel box and a chair. The book survives that, because its box is used once and its people "counted one of her, paid her, gave her a sandwich."

## 3. The ten faults, worst first

**1. Chapter 12. The two plots come apart at the climax.**
"The river's coming now, whatever I do with you. So this is for me."
Since 7 the book has run on one rule: "If I'm in your village at 2:51 on Friday night … your bank goes. If I'm not, it holds." That contest is lost between two chapters, to a rib, and written off in this line of dialogue. The fight in the van that follows, the best physical scene in the book, then decides nothing about the water. Cut the line. Let Maylis fight for eight rings believing that getting Lesca out might still save the bank, so that "You're the water" is a weapon. Let her find out it was too late at 02:55, with her hand on the clay. The discovery costs one sentence in 13 and makes the eight rings a race.

**2. Chapters 7 and 8. The threat to the marriage is raised at midnight and removed at breakfast.**
"I'd have the one who answers. I'd have you tomorrow." Then, next scene: "'I do this all week,' she said."
Coralie preferring the other one is the most frightening idea in the book. It lasts one page break. Lesca's motion in 3 was to take "the other one's bed, and Coralie Mounet in it", and she never gets one evening of it. Give her days. Not the bed; Coralie's line on the landing must stand. But let Sunday to Tuesday belong to the one who answers, at that table, with Maylis in the right hand watching Coralie be happier. Move the chart, and the wrist pinned to the table, to after Tuesday the 8th, when Larrère says his line at the café door and the forecast is proved. Then Coralie gives up the woman she would rather have, knowing it. As it stands she gives up a stranger.

**3. Chapter 11. Nine hours in a van, with the answer available.**
"There was still time to go down the track and knock. She didn't go."
At 03:03 "she grabbed the collars of both his jackets in her left hand and pulled." She could have done that at 18:00, at 23:00, at 01:12. The 5 p.m. scene makes her not-acting a scene. This, the second Either, is eight words. Stage it. Take her down off the bank at 01:12, as far as the first water, and bring her back, for a reason the reader watches her choose.

**4. Chapter 12. Lesca gives up in six lines.**
"'I'm letting go,' Lesca said, with the mouth."
In 8 she says "I won't be sent home", and she sits on the thing itself to prove it. Her reason for stopping is two sentences about a hand and a reflector. The reader has to work out that holding on is ringing once again. Make her do the sum on the page: the dashboard clock, the van dead on the slope, the reflector no nearer. She can have the body or the man. Let her try for both first and lose a ring by it.

**5. Chapters 8 and 12. The rules move when the plot needs them to.**
"Your river made up two days in an afternoon, so only the last night counts."
The two dry days are the proof. This sentence then cancels what they proved, to keep the deadline. And when Lesca lets go she lands in the hall of the dead, not at home, and walks out of it by sending apologies. I did not know if she could die, what decided it, or how long she had. State one more rule in the chair in 8, where the others are stated: what happens to a visitor who has held it down. Then the walk up the aisle has a clock.

**6. Chapters 6 and 11. Larrère holds a folder, and the heroine's only weapon misfires.**
"'I saw your car on 1 December.' … 'And who says so?'"
She plays the back lane and nothing moves. His last act is "the smallest thing that had happened to her since Friday", in the book's own words. And his real reason for "shut" on the night, a husband kept at his hut, is heard only as a kitchen clock: "He stays dry as long as you do what you're told." Let the lever buy one thing, or cut the attempt. Best: it buys the daylight opening, on his terms, and then at 5 p.m. she has no order to stand behind when she says Either.

**7. Chapter 10, first half. A rehearsal on the morning of the flood.**
"He read it to her off his sheet, from the anthem to the photograph."
Chapter 9 ends at 5:10 a.m. on the day. The book then gives 900 words to a visitors' book and a roll of tape. Keep Coralie laying eight strips and "The others have died since then, in those beds." Cut the point of order on the kiss, the medal and the camera row. Arlette needs half her lines.

**8. Chapter 3, first half. This is where a Crouch reader stops.**
"'Opened what? There's a name over it. It reads that she opened him.'"
Funny, and in the way. Between "So it could be done" and "Tuesday had been the 24th" stand a committee, a verb and a doorstep. Come into the committee at Larrère's three minutes. Move Monsieur Darrigrand and his step to the space cut from 10.

**9. Chapter 6. The council gets four pages and the bed gets nine lines.**
"The motion fails. The mayor speaks after the prefect."
The stake of the long scene is the running order of a ceremony in the other village. The stake of the short one is "the doctor's number was on the screen, with her thumb over it." Cut back to that bed two more times, with the chart getting worse, and take it out of Larrère's speech.

**10. Chapter 9. The secret arrives as a summary, and "rang once" is worn out before the square.**
"After that a prefect pinned a medal on her coat and read out a speech that didn't say whose order she had disobeyed."
The reader has known since "That's not a no." The night should stop at the sole of the boot. And the phrase is used in 00, 9, 10, twice in 12, in 13 and in 14. Drop Maylis's "You rang once" at the top of 12; the open palm does it.

## 4. Five things that must not be touched

1. **The platform at 5 p.m. (11).** "'Either,' Maylis said. / 'Then it's shut,' Jeannot said." The comic word, in daylight, with the man standing on the bank.
2. **The dip.** From letter seventeen to 02:59, and "She wanted it not to be her."
3. **The cross on Bordenave's pad.** She keeps Lesca out for two nights, so the forecast fails, so Jeannot votes shut: "If she can be out on Friday the 4th, she can be out on Friday the 11th." Her one success is what sinks her.
4. **Coralie and the phone (10).** "Outgoing. Castagnet J., mobile. Not answered. 02:51." And the landing in 7.
5. **Room twelve to the hall (13).** Kévin counting five in his own headlights, the hatbox on the wardrobe, "'Forty-one,' Coralie said."
