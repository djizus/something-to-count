# Critic's report: Comedy

Read: `manuscript/00.md` to `16.md`, in order, in full. Nothing else.

## 1. Scores

- **Stakes: 7.** One man against forty-two beds, and a marriage on each side. "If she turned that handle, a man drowned and her hand had done it. If she went on, whatever the river did behind her was the river's doing." (13)
- **Tension: 7.** Chapters 11 to 13 hold. Chapters 7 and 8 leak. "On the sixth ring the rib caught and held." (11)
- **Progression: 6.** The same request is made three times in the steel room: "Stay in your own bed." (4), "Then stay in your own bed on Friday" (8), "So I'm asking. Don't come on Friday." (8).
- **Comedy: 7.** "'Either is not a person. I cannot summon Either to the station.'" (6) I smiled through 1, 2, 4, 5, 6, the second half of 7, the car-window meeting in 8, the first half of 10 and the phone call in 11. I stopped in the places listed in section 3.
- **Characters: 7.** The village is an ensemble a reader would miss. "'One. It's Jeannot. Two. Jean's for the stone.'" (7) Maylis is the straight man in her own book for two thirds of it.
- **The switch between dark and comic: 8.** "'I'll be brief,' she said, in the voice that came from the back of the throat. 'The—'" (16) The joke that will not come out is the grief.
- **Prose: 7.** Fluid and plain. Two tics a reader hears: "the way" forty-three times, "at a time" eighteen times ("counted the change into Maylis's left hand one coin at a time", 16).
- **The ending: 8.** "He went and got it. Then he stepped over the string in his rubber boots and began to dig." (16) Chapter 16 earns the 8. Chapter 15 pulls it down from higher.
- **Clarity: 6.** "Maylis felt the night go out with them." (7) I did not know, on the page, whether she had just won or lost.

**Would I have finished it unpaid?** Yes. From chapter 5 I was reading for the people.

**Would I press it on a friend?** On a friend who loved The Good Place for its people, yes. On a friend who reads Crouch for speed, no: chapters 7 and 8 would lose them.

## 2. Against the comparisons

**Crouch: above.** He has no comic register. This book has a village that is funny in every scene it enters, and it keeps that through a flood.

**The Good Place: below on comedy, level on the moral problem, level on the switch.**

1. **Level: the flaw is the running gag, and the running gag is the hinge.** The series makes Chidi's indecision a joke for a season and then the thing that damned him. This book does it with one word. *Either* is written on a shopping pad, said to a demon, found unsayable in a room where only the truth comes out, said with her weight on the handle, and at the last named for what it is: "That was Either, with the river in it." (16) That is as good as the series at that thing.
2. **Below: range.** The series runs four kinds of funny at once, one per person. This book has one, deadpan procedure, and nearly everyone plays it (fault 1).
3. **Below: the reversal.** "You're the water" re-reads the rescuer as the flood, and it lands, because the reader has the dry boards and Bordenave's cross first. The second reversal is better and is not lit: the old woman's kindness on bingo night keeps the flood in the village, and she is the one it drowns (fault 5).

## 3. The ten faults that most hold it back

**1. One comic instrument. Whole book; it shows from chapter 8.**
"I shall lay it myself, and the village will receive a bill for my labour." (8) "That item has been carried eleven times." (10) "Then write that I never voted for her to be in here." (10)
Larrère, Lesca, Sarthou, Jeannot, Bordenave, Marie-Jo, the secretary, the woman at Hontaube, the young man from Pau, Arlette and Coralie with her boxes all make the same joke: a form applied to something that is not a form. It is the book's subject, so it belongs. But by chapter 10 I could hear the next one coming, and a joke heard coming gets a nod, not a smile. The people who are funny some other way are the ones I remember: Josiane, who is wrong by inference; Madame Etcheto, who gives a demon its work like a member of staff; Daguerre and his "Here"; Kévin, who asks his questions in pairs.
*Do:* cut the weakest procedure jokes so the shape stays rare enough to surprise. Sarthou's bill for sand (8) and Arlette's visitors' book (10) go first. Add nothing. Josiane and Kévin are already in every set piece; they get the air.

**2. Coralie's disappointed exit, seven times in six chapters. Chapters 4 to 9.**
"Coralie picked up the bowl of coffee and poured it down the sink." (8) "'I asked where you'd be,' Coralie said. She went up to the spare room." (9)
The beat is: she asks, Maylis gives the wrong answer, Coralie does something to an object and leaves. The envelope (4), the keys (5), the pillow (7), the shut door (7), the coffee (8), the pencil (8), the spare room (9). A running gag has to climb; this one repeats. It also turns the funniest person in the book into an examiner. From chapter 7 I stopped looking forward to her scenes, which is the opposite of what chapter 16 needs me to feel.
*Do:* keep the three that change something (the envelope, the pillow, "me, as a distance") and the bag in 16. Let the coffee scene end on "Friday." Let chapter 9's first scene end on her hand holding the rib while Maylis laughs. Keep the lines ("It's twenty minutes to Bayonne" pays in 16); cut the gestures.

**3. Chapter 15 is a list of payoffs, and the best one is reported.**
"On Tuesday the council gave Lesca three minutes to speak against her own removal, and she turned the timer back to nothing herself."
The kitchen timer is the best comic object in Lesca's village. It is set up in 3 and played in 6 by the wrong woman. Its owner's turn with it gets one sentence. Around that sentence the chapter ticks off the bath, the verb, "I'll be brief", the timer and the name in seventy-seven lines, and I could hear the ticking. Two of them are superb (Larrère saying nothing; "Nobody emptied hers"). They are weakened by the company.
*Do:* play the council scene. Marie-Jo sets the timer in front of the mayor and, for the first time since 2018, says it to her face. Twenty lines. It gives the chapter one scene that is not an epilogue.

**4. The meeting joke is dropped after its first outing. Chapters 4, 8, 16.**
"'There's nobody in the chair.'" (4) "She didn't call anything to order." (16)
The first steel-room scene is the funniest supernatural scene in the book because Lesca runs it as a meeting: items, points, a vote of two, an empty chair. The two visits in chapter 8 have no agenda and no chair, and both repeat chapter 4's request. So the last line quoted above pays one setup made twelve chapters earlier.
*Do:* merge chapter 8's two steel-room visits into one and run it as a meeting. The tide is an item she cannot move, because the word will not come out standing. That removes a repeated scene and gives the gag its middle step.

**5. Bingo night does not state its bet, and does not light its best irony. Chapter 7.**
"Inguma would come for Lesca tonight, and Lesca wasn't going to give it anything to count. All Maylis had to do was keep the mouth shut until it came." Then: "'That's till morning,' Madame Etcheto told Coralie, and settled her hands on the duck."
A farce runs on the audience knowing exactly what each person wants. The first quotation is the whole bet, and on first reading its second clause sounds like Lesca's intention, not Maylis's plan. I worked out why the jaw was clamped a page later. Then the ending: out of kindness, the old woman finishes the wrong woman's prayer, the flood stays another night, and she is the one it kills. It is the darkest joke in the book and it gets "Maylis felt the night go out with them."
*Do:* say the bet plainly before the first number is called. After the tablecloths lie down, one plain sentence: Maylis had lost, and who had done it for her.

**6. The hinge of the book is half a beat short. Chapter 11.**
"The river went by under the boards. / Her weight was on the handle. / 'Either,' Maylis said."
This is the right hinge. But three chapters earlier Larrère asked her the same question in the same form, "Open, or shut?", and she said "Open" twice, in public, and stopped only when he held Kévin's name against the glass. Here his car is a hundred metres off and six paragraphs back, and the word comes out in answer to Jeannot. I believed it a second late. The moment where the joke becomes the catastrophe cannot afford that second.
*Do:* put in her eye, in that instant, what the word is protecting her from. One sentence. Do not explain the word.

**7. The rule of the mouth changes from scene to scene. Chapters 4, 5, 7, 8.**
"The owner keeps her right hand." (4) "She got to the mouth first." (5) "Maylis had used herself up at the bar." (7) "Maylis let her have the mouth." (8)
The right hand is a clean rule and every joke built on it works: the spoon, the tap, the brake, the hand that signs letters on the floor. The mouth is whatever the scene needs. Maylis takes it at will in 5, holds it for two hours in 7, then cannot say a word on the landing.
*Do:* add it to Lesca's points in chapter 4: the mouth goes to whoever gets there first, and the owner pays for it in breath. The rib already keeps that account.

**8. The first ninety-five lines of chapter 7 have one smile in them.**
"She pushed until 4 again, flat on the kitchen floor with her forearm across her side."
A woman sleeps on her kitchen floor to dodge a demon that was said to come "to that bed", and it comes down the stairs for her anyway. That is a comic situation and it is told as a report. So is the walk to the hut with the watch. After the council scene in 6, this is where I stopped smiling for longest outside the flood.
*Do:* give the kitchen floor one line in her job-sheet voice, the one that says the file is "thinner than the manual for the village mower". Shorten the timed walk; the numbers are what the reader needs from it.

**9. Three callbacks at the worst moment of the evacuation. Chapter 13.**
"'I put that handle in number six,' he said. / 'I wrote to them too.' / 'I know you did. They took note.'"
Hourcade is waiting to be carried and room twelve has just failed to open. "The gate would have gone up at 3" is the knife in this exchange. The three lines after it are bookkeeping, and for those lines I could see the threads being tied.
*Do:* cut the three lines here. The letters and who was written to are already in number forty-two in chapter 16, where there is time for them.

**10. Arlette at the tap. Chapter 8.**
"I'd have liked to hear the second one. I'd have missed the first, looking for my purse."
The joke is that Maylis said "open" twice and her mother cannot believe it. It takes six sentences and a purse to arrive, and I had to read it again to find it.
*Do:* two sentences. "Twice" is the joke.

## 4. Five things that must not be touched

1. **Coralie at the oven door, all three times.** "'In my tray,' she said, and shut the door on him." (1), the soup consulted (8), and the one she cannot finish (16). The third only works because the first two are funny.
2. **Jeannot's six points at 5 in the morning,** and the sentence after them: "A man had just sworn at her in six points, and he was alive." (9) The best single use of comedy in the book.
3. **The council in chapter 6.** The timer pinned under one hand, "Eleven years I've been timed like an egg", both hands going up and "The chair votes once, Madame le maire", then Inguma coming along under the table past a row of shoes. Also "She had worked the roadsides out at the speed of the village tractor. Inguma was doing them on foot."
4. **Josiane and Bordenave's pad.** The village is wrong for five chapters ("It's adultery whichever she's after, and it's in that house") and right about the house: "I only had the one name wrong." And the cross on the pad is what makes Jeannot vote against his own life. The misreading drives the plot. Leave every step of it.
5. **Madame Etcheto as a comic figure.** "Staff to wait. Staff not to join in." The duck on her knees. Counting the sandbags: "Nineteen. That's not many." Her death costs because she was funny first. The same goes for Daguerre's "Here", through to the prefect, and for the small comedy around the confession in 14: the deputy who "turned the sheet over to see whether there was more on the back", the man beyond the last loudspeaker who claps.

- **02.** The care plan for a demon. The last line is the hook the premise needed. **9**

- **04.** The meeting with nobody in the chair, then the chrysanthemums. Two comic scenes of different kinds, and both build. **8**
- **05.** The funniest chapter: the window going all the way down, the small m, Josiane's theory. Then the stairs, and I was not smiling at all. Clean. **9**
- **06.** The opening is plot; "Either is not a person" saves it. The mayor's day is the book at full strength. **8**
- **07.** Slow for a third. Bingo is good and would be better if I had known the bet. The landing is the best scene between the two women and the wrong one. **7**
- **08.** Seven sections and it feels like it. The meeting through the car window is the best thing in it. Two steel-room visits where one would do. **7**
- **09.** "Duck." Then the night told straight, then six points. Short, and every part works. **9**
- **10.** The young man from Pau and his tape, then "The others have died since then", then no jokes for the rest of the chapter. Right each time. **8**
- **11.** Hontaube's form is the best procedure joke in the book. "Either" at the handle is the right hinge, half a beat short. Long, and I did not notice. **9**
- **12.** Hand against hand at the wheel. "You can put that in the minutes as apologies received." She refuses death in the only idiom she has. **9**
- **13.** "Here." "I know you are." "Five." The comedy is down to single words and each one hurts. One exchange too many at Jeannot's arrival. **9**
- **14.** The confession, with the clap and the turned sheet to keep it human. "Somebody has to stack them." **8**
- **15.** A list. Two great beats in poor company, and one scene missing. **6**
- **16.** Everything the book set up comes back changed: the oven, the box joke at the café door, forty-two. No next chapter to want; it closes at **8**.
