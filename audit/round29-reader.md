## 2. Against the comparisons

**Below, by a little.** Level on people and on comedy, above on what the ending costs, below on how clearly the machine is explained and on how the first third holds.

- **Crouch explains his machine in one sentence and repeats it until nobody can lose it.** Here I had to carry eight rules, each said once: stay sitting and you drive; the owner keeps the right hand; counting delays the fetch; standing in the steel room you can only say true things; pushing keeps you out of the chair; the visitor brings her river; hold on against it and you go to the hall of the dead; it does not need you asleep. By chapter 11 I could not have told you why 4 a.m. was safe.
- **The Good Place makes sure you are ahead of the fools before a reversal lands.** The reversal here is that the woman who came to save a man is the flood. It lands as "You're the water", and I was level with the bingo hall, not ahead of it. I understood it a chapter later, from a chart.
- **Above both on cost.** Dark Matter ends with the family intact. Here a woman of ninety-four drowns in the one room we were told to watch, the job goes, Coralie takes the pillow, and the man who was saved says "It doesn't thank you." That is better.

What I did not understand: why Larrère ordered the gate raised on the Monday, with the river low; how a river has tides; why "It takes a small m" is a joke; what the lake was until chapter 10.
Where I skimmed: Sarthou's complaint form (6); the rehearsal with the young man from Pau (10); the levels on the marker post from 7 onward.
Where I was lost: the last page of 2; who wanted Inguma to come and who did not during the bingo (7); the three changes of village inside 6.
Where I could not stop: the stairs (5); the landing (7); Coralie and the phone (10); 11 from the platform to the end of 13.
What I will remember in a month: the car window going all the way down; the oven door; six points at 5 in the morning; five fingers; "drowned him" arriving late on the bank road; two fingers on a wrist.

## 3. The ten faults, worst first

1. **The book's reversal happens off the page (7).** "Maylis read it with her. Yesterday morning that same post had said a metre. The boards had been dry then." Then, forty lines later, "You're the water." She works out that Lesca brings the flood, and the text does not let me work it out with her. So the hall's mistake ("She wants water") is not funny on first read, because I am as puzzled as they are. Give her the thought on the platform in plain words: she kept Lesca out for two nights and the river stood still for two days. Then every line of the bingo is a joke I am in on, and the reversal lands as one.

2. **Too many rules, each said once, and Inguma has two jobs (4, 7, 8, 11).** "Second point. Inguma takes the visitor home when it has finished counting, and anybody who knows the words can give it something to count." It also brings Maylis to the chair at 2:51, which is the opposite job. Then: "I'll sit up in the van, awake, until 4, when it gives up." I did not know why 4, or what staying awake would do. At the top of 7, where she sets out to keep herself out of the chair, state the machine in three lines the way she writes a job on the pad: what brings her to the chair, what keeps the visitor, what sends the visitor home. Say it again in 11 before the night watch.

3. **On Friday nobody asks the obvious man (11).** "She needed a second pair of hands… She had tried the café first." Sarthou is "the gendarme at the Port. He was also Jeannot's cousin", and his doorstep is wet. She rings another village before she walks to his door. I spent chapter 11 saying "ask Sarthou". One exchange closes it: he refuses, in his own grammar, because it is orange and the complainant is on his own land. That also loads his line in 16, "I do not record that I would have stayed."

4. **The first crossing is given fifteen lines (2).** "Then she was in the chair in the steel room. The arms of the chair were warm, though she hadn't been sitting there long enough to warm them." The chapter spends pages on eels and lucid dreams, skips Thursday, and then does the event the book is built on in half a page. This is where I was first lost. Give the steel room the space the corridor of room twelve gets, and cut the jump from Wednesday to Friday.

5. **The flood day opens with a rehearsal (10).** After "And six. Bugger off" I wanted Maylis's Friday. I got a visitors' book, a tape plan, and backstory that is new this late: "Lesca had signed the gravel company's contract at this desk, and the village field was a lake now." Keep Coralie laying the strips and "Eight." Move the rest earlier or cut it. The second half of the chapter, the phone, is the best scene in the book and it is sitting behind this.

6. **Chapter 6 is three chapters (6).** A blackmail, a complaint, a second blackmail, a crossing, a minute of silence, a file, a wait on the floor, soup, a council. The village changes three times with no signal. "'Complaint of Castagnet, Jean, against persons unknown,' Sarthou said" is where I skimmed. Break the chapter at the crossing. Cut the complaint to the one thing it is for: Jeannot's "That's how Larrère asks for a thing."

7. **Chapter 15 is a summary (15).** "On Tuesday the council gave Lesca three minutes to speak against her own removal, and she turned the timer back to nothing herself." The timer has been set up since chapter 3 and its last ring is reported in a sentence. Play it as a scene of one page, or fold 15 into the end of 14. And 16 says goodbye to ten people in turn; the priest and the first Larrère exchange could go.

8. **I cannot tell which numbers matter.** "It was thirty-six houses and the care home against four." "Its tractor pulled your van out of the track, in January 2017." Forty houses, forty-two beds, forty-one letters, forty-one alive, nineteen days, nineteen turns, nineteen bags, seventy under, fifty-five under, ninety over, eleven years, twelve, sixteen, eighteen. Some rhyme on purpose and I liked finding them. But by chapter 7 I had stopped reading figures. Keep 2:51, ten minutes, twenty-five minutes, nineteen, forty-two. Round or drop the rest.

9. **The hall of the dead lets Lesca walk (12).** "'The mayor of Peyrebarthe sends her apologies,' she said, in the voice she used for the prefecture." Funny, and hers. But chapters 2 and 8 promised that the one who will not go home dies, and she leaves by declining. The price is paid, by room twelve, but I only learn that at the top of 14 and only hear it said in 15. For the length of the flood I thought the rule had been waived. Let her hear "Twelve. One, two" and the chair pulled in as she walks away, at the end of 12. Then I go into 13 already afraid of that door.

10. **Things a reader from outside is not given.** "On Monday afternoon the president of the association had rung and told her to raise it." Why? It cracks the rib, drains the hut and starts the book, and nobody ever says. "'It takes a small m,' Lesca said" means nothing to me. A river that rises "on the falling tide" needs one clause saying the sea comes this far up.
