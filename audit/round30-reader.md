## 2. Against the comparisons

**Below, by a little.** Three things put it there.

1. **Crouch never makes me stop to work out a rule.** His premise fits one sentence and its rules are said once. Here the rules come in seven instalments over nine chapters: shared knowledge, staying seated, the right hand, Inguma takes the visitor home, standing means truth (all 4), pushing keeps her out (7), only the last night counts (8), holding on against Inguma puts you in the hall of the dead (12). I was still learning how it worked while the bank was going.
2. **The Good Place lands its reversal in one line and everything before it re-reads in a second.** This book has that reversal: the woman who came to warn the village is what she is warning it about. I got it in three pieces over two chapters, and the largest piece was a recital at a kitchen table.
3. **Above both in what it leaves behind.** Crouch's endings escape. This one stays and pays: a man alive who says he was in no danger, an old woman dead in her bed, and a grave dug by the two of them. Maylis is Chidi, and where the series tells me his indecision hurt people, this book puts the handle under her weight and lets her say "Either" (11). That is better than the comparison.

**What I did not understand**

- Why the two villages had different winters in 2014. One woman saw the left lamp and one the right, and six months later one village had nineteen days of rain and the other had "a dry winter" (4). I waited for a reason and never got one.
- Why Inguma left Lesca alone all of Saturday 5 December (7). Nobody is shown giving it anything to count that morning.
- Whether Madame Etcheto took Lesca's chair in the hall of the dead. "'Twelve. One, two.' Somebody pulled a chair in to a table." (14)
- "'Lesca, Maylis. Surname first.'" (15) I did not know what she was claiming by it.
- The card in the chest pocket. It says *left*, it comes out with the phone in the flood (13), Jeannot's letter is buttoned in beside it (16), and Maïté's verb is "*Left*" (15). I saw it was meant and could not say what it meant.
- "There was a padlock under the third board." (4) It is mentioned twice and never opened.
- What the merger with Hontaube was for, beyond one phone call.

**Where I was bored, lost or skimming**

- Chapter 8, the chart: I was being told what I had just watched.
- Chapter 10, Arlette and the rehearsal. Chapter 11, the call to Hontaube. Both good, both in the way.
- Any stretch where two wills share a face: the bar in 7, the breakfast table in 8.

**Where I could not stop**

## 3. The ten faults that most hold it back

**1. The turn comes in three pieces and never lands (7 and 8).**
"Yesterday morning that same post had said a metre. The boards had been dry then. She had nobody to say it to and no mouth to say it with." Then, in a bingo hall where everyone mishears it: "'You're the water.'" Then, next morning: "'I kept her out twice. On Wednesday night and on Thursday night I pushed that thing off my chest until 4, and she didn't get in. For those two days the river stood still and the road dried.'"
The first is too cryptic for me to get it. The second is a riddle played for a laugh. The third is a recap. This is the book's best idea and I never had the moment of getting it.
*Do:* let me get it with Maylis at the marker post, in one plain sentence of her own, with Lesca reading the same post through her eyes. End a chapter there. Then "You're the water" is a blow and not a puzzle, and the chart shrinks to Coralie testing a thing I already know.

**2. The middle runs one loop three times (5, 7, 8).**
Lesca drives the body, Coralie nearly chooses her, the wrist pulls away, Inguma fetches her: on the stairs (5), on the landing (7), at the breakfast table (8). Between them come two steel-room meetings four pages apart (8) that open the same way: "'It's the ti—'" and "'I w—'".
*Do:* one meeting in chapter 8, not two. Join the landing and the breakfast table into one scene, so that Coralie hears Bayonne's answer, tests the chart and puts Lesca out in a single night. That removes a loop and brings the hall of the dead forward.

**3. One rule is broken on the page (7).**
"This morning nobody had, and it came for me after twenty minutes." (4) Yet on Saturday Lesca drives from 6 until the bingo with no words said. Then: "Inguma would come for Lesca tonight, and Lesca wasn't going to give it anything to count."
Chapter 5 showed me the words at the kitchen door. Chapter 7 skips them, and I stopped believing the clock on her visits.
*Do:* show her give it a day's counting at 6 on Saturday, through Maylis's mouth, with Maylis failing to stop her. Two sentences.

**4. Sitting and standing each carry two rules (4, 8).**
Staying seated means you drive the other body. Standing means you can only say what is true. Then, about a lie told in a kitchen: "'I was sitting down.'" (8) I had to go back to chapter 4 to see that this was the second rule and not the first.
*Do:* tie the truth rule to the room and not to posture. Lesca can lie in the kitchen because it is a kitchen.

**5. Chapter 6 carries nine scenes and three kinds of leverage.**
"'With no date on it, it isn't a letter. It's a sheet of paper that I keep, and you're the one who puts the date on it.'" On top of that there is a blank sheet for Kévin's name, a file that "Hontaube opens", and Sarthou's complaint with "one name" in it. By chapter 11 I could not have said which paper threatened what. The good part of the chapter, Maylis liking a square that says good morning, sits behind all of it.
*Do:* one lever, Kévin's name on a sheet. Drop the Hontaube file. Cut Larrère's speech by half; it needs "timed like an egg" and the question the bell cuts off.

**6. The night is held back by two chapters of approach (10, 11).**
Chapter 9 ends with the plan changed. Then come Arlette in the office, the young man with the tape, Darrigrand's step, and a woman at Hontaube saying "'Then it should have gone last Monday.'" It is about five hundred lines before 2:51.
*Do:* open 10 on Coralie in the doorway with the speech. Move the rehearsal earlier, to a chapter where Lesca's village is being set up. Halve the Hontaube call.

**7. Too many sentences where a body part is the subject (5, 7, 8).**
"Lesca pulled at the jaw and Maylis held it, so all Josiane got was a noise through the nose." (7) "Maylis's right hand went over the mouth, and the left came up to drag it off." (8) The stated rule is that the owner keeps her right hand. In practice the mouth, the jaw, the eyes, the legs and the chest all change hands. It works in 12, where it is one hand on a wheel against one hand on a brake. In the middle I was sorting limbs.
*Do:* two contested things only, the right hand and the mouth. Say once that the mouth goes to whoever gets there first. Elsewhere Lesca simply does it.

**8. The hall of the dead blurs who caused the death (12, 14).**
Lesca refuses the empty chair and walks out. Then: "'Twelve. One, two.' Somebody pulled a chair in to a table." I could read that as a place that had to be filled. If so, the choice at the dip, "She wanted it not to be her", is partly taken off Maylis, and that page is the best in the book.
*Do:* lay a new place for room twelve. Leave the card with Lesca's name where it is, unclaimed.

**9. Forty-one means two things in the last chapter (13, 16).**
"'Forty-one,' Coralie said" closes chapter 13. In 16: "'Forty-one,' Maylis said", and sixty lines later, "'I paid to register forty-one of them.'" Add forty houses, forty-two beds and letter forty-two. The count of the living is the hardest number in the book and it shares its digits with a running joke. The years blur the same way: eleven, twelve, sixteen, eighteen.
*Do:* change the number of letters so that forty-one belongs to the care home alone.

**10. Lesca's fall is two sentences, and only chapter 1 has a date at its head (15, throughout).**
"On Tuesday the council gave Lesca three minutes to speak against her own removal, and she turned the timer back to nothing herself." After chapter 14 that reads as a note. Separately: 3 and 4 open on the same morning in two villages, 10 and 11 on the same Friday, and I worked that out from the weather each time.
*Do:* give the removal one short scene, or cut it to the single line where she gives her name, and make that line plain. Put a day and date at the head of every chapter.
