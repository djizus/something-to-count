# Critic's report: Thriller

Read: `manuscript/00.md` to `16.md`, in order, in full. Nothing else.

## 1. Scores

- **Stakes: 7.5.** "'I'd have this one again.'" (5) The wife prefers the woman driving the body. From that line the book has something to lose that is not a flood.
- **Tension: 6.5.** "'On Friday night I'm coming across with you, and I won't be sent home,' Lesca said." (8) The antagonist gives her date, her hour and her intention, then does exactly that. Nothing she does after chapter 7 is a surprise.
- **Progression: 7.** "'If she can be out on Friday the 4th, she can be out on Friday the 11th.'" (7) Her one success at keeping Lesca out is what loses her the vote. That is progression. Chapter 6 and the first half of 10 are not.
- **Comedy: 7.5.** "'Either is not a person. I cannot summon Either to the station.'" (6)
- **Characters: 8.** "'I don't get into a bed over somebody who can't speak. I've lifted too many of them.'" (7)
- **The switch between dark and comic: 8.** "'Five,' he said." (13) The dream-counting joke from chapter 2, paid in a flooded yard with a wheelchair half up on a van.
- **Prose: 7.5.** "It wasn't loud. It sounded like a truck tipping out a load of gravel a long way off, except that the sound didn't stop." (13)
- **The ending: 8.** "Then he stepped over the string in his rubber boots and began to dig." (16) It costs a death, a job and a marriage, and gives nothing back but a second shovel.
- **Clarity: 6.** "She had held the wheel against it for a whole ring, and this was where that put her." (12) A rule stated at the moment it is applied, at the climax.

**Would I have finished it unpaid?** Yes. From the end of 10 I could not have stopped.

**Would I press it on a friend?** Yes, telling them it starts at the end of chapter 2 and that chapter 6 passes. On a friend who reads only for what Crouch does: no.

## 2. Against the comparisons

**Below Crouch as a thriller. Above him on the two things the brief names: the marriage and the cost.**

- **Below: the antagonist's pressure.** In *Dark Matter* the other Jason holds the house until the last forty pages, and then there are a hundred of him. Lesca's last act in Maylis's village before the climax is Sunday breakfast on 6 December, where Coralie pins her wrist to the table. That is 46% of the way through. She next acts at 70%, and she is gone two minutes later. For a quarter of the book the double only talks.
- **Below: surprise.** A Crouch turn is one the reader could not have called. Here every turn is announced. The prologue shows the headlights and the phone. The kitchen clock behind Larrère's voice (9) tells us where he will ring from four chapters before "Castagnet, house". Kévin tells us staying awake will not work. Lesca cannot lie standing up, so we always know what she means to do. The book runs on dread, which it does well, and never on shock.
- **Above: the fear for the marriage.** Daniela sleeps beside the impostor for weeks and has to be told. Coralie finds the second woman in a pulse on first contact ("Who is that?"), and then makes her more dangerous by wanting her. Crouch never lets the wife be the one who decides. This book does, three times.
- **Above: what the ending costs.** Jason drives off with his wife and son. Here the last number is "Forty-one", the wife leaves with a pillow, and the man who was saved says in writing that he was in no danger.
- **Level: speed of premise.** The double speaks through her mouth at 10% of the length, the clock is set at 16%. That is Crouch's pace.

## 3. The ten faults that most hold it back

**1. The double is out of the book before the choice it exists for. (12–13)**
"Lesca had gone out of her the way warmth goes out of a chair, and those words were what she had left behind."
The fight between the two women ends at 2:53 on top of the bank. The choice between gate and man starts at 2:55 and is made at 2:59, six hundred metres away, by one woman alone. The book's two engines peak in separate scenes. Lesca also loses nothing by force: she thinks it over for one ring and resigns ("Then she saw what she was holding"). And between the two halves the reader is taken to a bingo hall for five hundred words while a bank has ten minutes.
*Do:* keep Lesca in the body down the track. She says "I know where the dip is. Let me drive." Let her drive it, with the right hand on the wheel against her, and let the van go into the dip with both of them in it. Then the words, the count of five fingers and her letting go happen with the lamp behind and the reflector in front, and "Go to the hut first" is said at the one place where it is an instruction. Maylis still takes her three steps back and turns away alone. At the very least, move the hall of the dead to the head of 14, so that 2:51 to 4:16 runs unbroken and "Twelve. One, two." comes after the reader knows who it is.

**2. Lesca takes the last week off. (8–10)**
"'I've tried for three nights to keep you out, and I can't.'"
Maylis cannot keep her out on the 6th, 7th or 8th, and by the rules Lesca could stay sitting on any of those mornings. She does not, and nobody says why. The week before the deadline is the week an antagonist should be worst. Instead it holds three conversations in the box, a remembered night and a rehearsal.
*Do:* give her one of those days and let her do harm with it that cannot be undone. The Tuesday vote is the place. Maylis says "Open" twice and stops at Kévin's name on the glass. Lesca is the woman who filmed the gates and sent that boy to prison. Put her in the mouth for the third "Open".

**3. Lesca's plan for Friday never looks at the care home. (12)**
"This time the man came first."
She told Coralie herself that the bank breaks thirty metres from the back door (5). She has said "There's no both." Driving to the hut first puts the river on the woman she has crossed to have, and on forty-two beds. In chapter 8 she notes that Coralie is at the care home only as one fewer person to stop her. Her two minutes of reckoning in the van weigh the bed and the man and never the building. Chapter 10 asks which she opened the gate for, the houses or Coralie. Chapter 12 puts the same sum in front of her and she does not see it.
*Do:* one exchange. Let it be the thing that opens her hand.

**4. The price of resisting Inguma is quoted three times and then waived. (2, 8, 12)**
"'You can put that in the minutes as apologies received.'"
"The one it cannot take home dies in her own bed." A place is laid. Her name is on the card. Then she holds on, lands in the hall, declines the seat and walks out. Etcheto taking the chair is the right payment, but Lesca's own exit costs her a sentence of procedure. The same chapter adds a rule as it uses it (holding on for one ring puts you in the hall). So did chapter 8 ("only the last night counts") and chapter 4 (the truth, standing). A reader cannot fear a penalty whose terms arrive with the verdict.
*Do:* state in chapter 8, in the box, what holding on does and what it costs. Then in 12 make her leave the hall because the chair is filled, not because she excused herself.

**5. Chapter 6 spends itself on the wrong village's vote.**
"'Seven and seven. In a tie the chair's vote decides. The motion fails. The mayor speaks after the prefect.'"
The undated resignation is the leash on Maylis for the rest of the book and gets twelve lines. The file she crossed for gives her one fact the reader lacked (the trapdoor locked from inside). The real danger, a body lying open-eyed at home with Coralie's thumb over the doctor's number, gets eighty words. Twelve hundred go on who speaks first at a ceremony. This is where a Crouch reader puts the book down.
*Do:* keep the laugh in the minute, the unfinished "did you—" and the back lane. Cut the vote. Give the words to Larrère's office and to the bed at 18:40.

**6. Her defence fails the same way four times, and the fifth is announced. (7, 8, 11)**
"'The thing that sits on you doesn't need you asleep,' Kévin said. 'I was wide awake, both times.'"
"The rib caught and held" ends her resistance on the 4th, the 6th, the 9th and the 11th. Then, six hours before the night watch, Kévin tells her and us that sitting up awake will not work. 23:34, 01:12, the thumbnail in the cut and five fingers counted are good, and they are spent on an outcome already given.
*Do:* cut Kévin's two sentences. Chapter 2 planted it. Let the reader hope until the roof bends.

**7. The night is told twice, and its secret is out on the first page. (00, 9)**
"Before she fitted the handle to the gate, she turned the van around on top of the bank so that its headlights pointed down the track across the flood fields."
The prologue shows the van turned, the look down the track, the number already in the phone. "Did you know he was out?" (3) is therefore answered before it is asked, and chapter 9 walks the same bank again in summary.
*Do:* keep the prologue as it is; "It rang once" sells the book. Start chapter 9's memory at the reflector and cut everything before it. Cut the medal-and-election paragraph after the boot; chapter 3 has it.

**8. Four votes. (3, 4, 6, 8)**
"'Four to three,' Larrère said. 'It stays shut.'"
The verb on the stone, the meeting in the box, the council, the association. The only one the plot turns on is the fourth, and by then a show of hands is the book's habit, not an event.
*Do:* in 3, refuse Larrère's verb without a count (keep the timer cutting off Maïté). In 6, see fault 5. Then the hand Jeannot keeps up until the car has left the square is the only one in the book.

**9. The man who gave the order pays offstage. (13, 15, 16)**
"Larrère turned around in the mud, holding the folder up out of it, and went back to his car."
In both villages he rings from the husband's kitchen. In one he is faced by Maïté at a garden gate while the protagonist is up a ladder. In the other he accepts Maylis's resignation and walks away, and his exposure is four pages in an envelope we do not see opened.
*Do:* let Maylis say "Castagnet, house" to him in the mud, in front of Sarthou and Madame Darrigrand. One line. She has the screen.

**10. On the day of the flood the book goes to the dry village to lay tape. (10)**
"'A point of order,' Lesca said. 'You have the flowers as one item and the kiss as the next. They're one item.'"
Eleven hundred words of Arlette's coat, the visitors' book, the point of order and Darrigrand's step, between the dawn phone call and Friday in the rain.
*Do:* open the chapter on Coralie pressing strips of tape ("Eight"). Keep Arlette's "Sleep on your side, Maylis" and cut the rest of her scene. Move the swept step to chapter 3 or lose it.

## 4. Five things that must not be touched

1. **The platform at 5 p.m. (11).** "'Either,' Maylis said." / "'Then it's shut,' Jeannot said." The man is above ground, the handle is on, it is daylight, and her own word shuts the gate. Everything after is consequence.
2. **The cross on Bordenave's pad (7, 8).** The prophecy audited by a café score pad, and her two nights of pushing turned into the evidence against her.
3. **Coralie's fingers on the wrist (3, 7).** "'That's her,' she said. 'In this hand.'" The detection, and the refusal that follows it.
4. **Chapter 13 from the dip to "Forty-one".** The clock times, the three steps back towards the lamp, "She wanted it not to be her", the key thrown and handed back, Coralie reading the next name when asked if the old woman answered.
5. **The call log (10).** "Outgoing. Castagnet J., mobile. Not answered. 02:51." The one turn I did not see coming, and it re-reads eleven years of landing.
