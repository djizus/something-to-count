# Critic report: Reader

Read: `manuscript/00.md` to `16.md`, in order, in full. Nothing else.

**Verdict against the bar: 7.5. Just under it.** Chapters 9 to 14 are at or over the bar. Chapters 6 to 8 and the clarity of the rules keep the whole book below it.

## 1. Scores

- **Stakes: 8.** "'The gate, or Castagnet? Say which, Maylis. For once.'"
- **Tension: 7.5.** 9 or 10 across chapters 11 to 13, 6 across chapters 6 to 8. "On the sixth ring the rib caught and held."
- **Progression: 7.** The ask does not move for four chapters. Chapter 4: "That floodgate is opened on Friday afternoon. In daylight, while the tide is going out, by order". Chapter 8: "On Friday at 2, on the falling tide, with nobody on the fields, by your order."
- **Comedy: 8.** "'Either is not a person. I cannot summon Either to the station.'"
- **Characters: 8.5.** "'I know that word. I've cooked it. That was Either, with the river in it.'"
- **The switch between dark and comic: 9.** "'I'll be brief,' she said, in the voice that came from the back of the throat. 'The—'"
- **Prose: 7.** It never made me re-read a sentence for sense, but one gesture is used until it shows: "Maylis hadn't told it to." / "Maylis hadn't said it." / "She hadn't told them to."
- **The ending: 8.** "Then he stepped over the string in his rubber boots and began to dig."
- **Clarity: 6.** "When the warmth beside her got up to go, she stayed sitting. In the other village the mayor woke up, and Maylis went with her."

**Would I have finished it unpaid?** Yes, but I would have put it down once, at the start of chapter 6, and picked it up the next day. From chapter 9 I read to chapter 13 without stopping.

**Would I press it on a friend?** On one who reads slowly, yes. Not on the friend who reads Crouch on a plane: that friend gives up in chapter 6.

## 2. Against the comparisons

**Below Crouch on ease, above him on people, level with The Good Place on making the problem funny, below it on the reversal.**

- **Below.** A Crouch reader is never behind the rule. He has one device and says what it does, then multiplies the consequences. Here I counted eight rules (the box, the chair, staying seated, the right hand, the counting, the truth rule in the steel room, the river that comes with her, the bingo of the dead) and was half a step behind from chapter 2 to chapter 8.
- **Below.** The Good Place says its reversal out loud, to the whole room, and the room understands. This book's reversal is "You're the water", and the room thinks she is thirsty. So did I, for a page.
- **Above.** Crouch's choice is between worlds. This one is between a handle and a man, and a single word makes it, in daylight, with the man standing there. Nothing in Dark Matter is as good as "Either" on the platform. And I would miss Jeannot, Kévin, Monsieur Daguerre and Madame Etcheto; I cannot name a minor character in Crouch.

### What the lens asks

**What I did not understand**

- Why the same creature carries Maylis to the chair and also throws the visitor out.
- Who owns the mouth. In chapter 7 Maylis holds the jaw shut all evening by will. In chapter 8 she needs her hand over it.
- What counts as Lesca being "in". She is sent home on Sunday morning and does not drive again until Friday, yet the river climbs all week.
- What the nets under Jeannot's floor are, and why a duck hunter locks his trapdoor from inside.
- Why the flood fields, once full, do not reach the Port.
- What the card in her chest pocket is for after chapter 2.

**Where I was lost or skimming**

- Lost: the first third of chapter 6.
- Skimming: the timed walk in chapter 7 and the council vote in chapter 15.
- Ahead of the book and waiting: the fourth account of 6 a.m. on Saturday, in chapter 4.

**Where I could not stop:** the end of chapter 5, then chapters 9 to 13.

**What I will remember in a month**

- Larrère's window going all the way down.
- Coralie doing Larrère to the oven door.
- "And six. Bugger off."
- "Either" with her weight on the handle.
- "Here." "I know you are."
- Kévin counting five fingers in the flood, and "I wanted six".

I will not remember how the crossing worked.

## 3. The ten faults, worst first

**1. The turn of the book is whispered. Chapters 7 and 8. Structural.**

> "'You're the water.'"

This is the moment that should re-read the first seven chapters: the rain started the night she first came, and "I don't know why it comes" was the only answer the steel room would let out. The proof is two dry days, given in about 120 words in chapter 7, where the attention is on Bordenave's cross and Jeannot's joke. On the platform the thought is withheld: "She had nobody to say it to and no mouth to say it with." The three words then go to a hall that mishears them. I was sure only at Coralie's chart the next morning, where it arrives as a tidy explanation and not as a blow.

*What to do:* give the two nights of pushing and the two dry days the weight of a scene. Let Maylis reach the thought in plain words on the platform on Saturday, so that I go into the bingo knowing what she is trying to say with her teeth shut. The hall's mishearing is funnier and worse when the reader is ahead of the hall. This is also what the middle lacks: it is the point where Friday's plan changes from "open it by order" to "keep her out", and at present I did not feel the plan change.

**2. Chapter 6 holds three chapters of business. Structural.**

> "Eighteen years of cutting roadsides had come down to six lines and a gap."

In the first three pages she signs away her job, is named in a gendarme's complaint, blackmails Jeannot and sees Larrère's car in the lane. That is four pieces of leverage (the undated letter, the blank sheet for Kévin, Sarthou's form, the nets), and I could not have said who held what over whom. Then the chapter crosses to the other village for the best idea in the middle of the book and has no room left for it. The signing got 150 words and I felt nothing.

*What to do:* split at "So she would do what Lesca had done to her, and stay sitting." Give the village day its own chapter. Cut Sarthou's complaint or slow it down: it exists only so that she threatens Jeannot, so that he votes Shut two chapters later, and I only saw that chain looking back.

**3. The rules are each given once, in passing, and never in plain words. Chapters 2 to 8.**

> "It was her first night in, and it started raining."

Each rule is clear on the day it is given and is then relied on as if I had kept it. By chapter 6 I was holding seven and unsure of the three listed above.

*What to do:* Lesca chairs meetings and lists items. In the chapter 4 meeting, let her give the rules as items, in her voice, in five lines. Then settle the mouth and settle "in", one sentence each.

**4. Lesca's Friday plan puts the river on the woman she is crossing for, and nobody says so. Chapters 8, 10, 12.**

> "'Your pad says Coralie is at the care home that night, so the only one left to stop me is you, with one hand.'"

By chapter 8 she knows that her being there breaks the bank, and that Coralie is thirty metres from the patch. She goes anyway, for that Coralie's bed, and in the van "the man came first". I wanted to shout at the page. A book may have a woman lie to herself. It may not leave the reader as the only one who notices. Chapter 12 has her see that there was "no man" in her motion; it never has her see that there was no Port in it either.

*What to do:* one line from Maylis in the Wednesday steel room ("She'll be under that bank"), and no answer comes out of Lesca.

**5. Chapter 15 closes accounts in order.**

> "'Lesca, Maylis,' Lesca said. 'Surname first.'"

The verb, the timer, the name: three props, three scenes, each paid on schedule. After chapter 14 I knew what each would do before it did it. It is the only chapter where I felt the hand arranging things.

*What to do:* keep Madame Etcheto ("Nobody emptied hers") and Maïté at the gate. Move them to the end of 14. Cut the council to the bell she rings on herself and the name. Let "Good night, Maylis" end 14.

**6. I never saw the ground. Chapter 1.**

> "First came the care home, then the patch she repaired every spring with four wheelbarrows of clay, then the floodgate."

The climax is a sum in metres, and I did it on trust. I could not picture where the fields lie against the houses, or what keeps a full basin out of the Port.

*What to do:* one look from the top of the bank in the opening, river on one hand, and on the other, in order, everything she will have to run between.

**7. Jeannot's nets. Chapters 4 and 6.**

> "Afterwards his nets were found under the floor of the hut, green and folded wet. There was a padlock under the third board."

The vote in chapter 8 turns on this, and so does the locked trapdoor. I did not know whether he poaches eels, hides them for Kévin, or what the padlock locks.

*What to do:* one clause saying what the nets are for.

**8. Saturday, 6 a.m., is told four times.**

> "'You held her hand.' 'I held yours. She put two fingers on it, and I took them.'"

The end of chapter 2, the start of 3 and the start of 4 each give a new head. The fourth, in the steel room, gives nothing new except the rule about staying seated.

*What to do:* cut the recap to that rule.

**9. The card. Chapters 2, 13, 16.**

> "She took the letter and buttoned it into her chest pocket, with her card."

It says *left*. Maïté's verb is *Left*. Sarthou says "she left it". I could not tell whether I was meant to hear that or had invented it.

*What to do:* pay it in the last steel-room scene, or take the card out of chapters 13 and 16.

**10. Kévin's mother is identified by surname only until the last chapter. Chapters 3 and 6.**

> "His mother had been Lesca's deputy mayor until a night in 2018, when she handed her sash back across the council table in a plastic bag."

Why Marie-Jo speaks "to the wall" is one of the best quiet jokes in the book. It depends on matching Labat to Labat across three chapters.

*What to do:* name her in that sentence.

## 4. Five things that must not be touched

1. Chapter 11, the platform: "'Either,' Maylis said. 'Then it's shut,' Jeannot said."
2. Chapter 9: the phone that rings nine times, and Jeannot's six points.
3. Coralie's Larrère at the oven door, all three times, and above all the third, which she cannot finish.
4. Chapter 13 from "Here." / "I know you are." through Kévin's five fingers to "Forty-one."
5. Chapter 10: "Outgoing. Castagnet J., mobile. Not answered. 02:51." and "Because you'd have told me."
