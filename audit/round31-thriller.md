# Round 31 — Thriller

Read `manuscript/00.md` to `16.md`, in order, in full. Nothing else opened.

Overall, through this lens: **7**. The bar is 8.

## 1. Scores

- **Stakes: 7.** Countable and in place by chapter 3: "Behind it were forty houses, and the care home, with forty-two people in their beds. Out on the fields there was one man". One point lost because all that holds the gate shut is "a sheet of paper that I keep". Another because Lesca's one brush with death is settled by "apologies received".
- **Tension: 7.** Chapters 2 to 5 and 11 to 13 run at 9. Chapters 6, 7 and 9 run at 5: "Nothing came at 2:05. Nothing came at 2:30."
- **Progression: 6.** Between the stairs in 5 and the doorstep in 11, six scenes end with Coralie withdrawing. Chapter 7 ends on the sentence its first scene ended on: "Maylis heard the pillow go down on the single bed."
- **Comedy: 7.** The running joke is the trigger of the plot, which is the best structural idea in the book: "'Either,' Maylis said. 'Then it's shut,' Jeannot said." It costs pace at the bingo, six days from the flood.
- **Characters: 8.** "I don't get into a bed over somebody who can't speak. I've lifted too many of them."
- **The switch: 7.** Clean in the corridor: "'Nineteen,' Madame Etcheto said. 'That's not many.' This time nobody called out." Wrong in the hall of the dead, where a joke pays off the book's best threat.
- **Prose: 7.** Fast, and exact about objects: "Then the water rose up the door and covered it, and the red light went out." Marked down for sentences that tell me what the scene has just shown: "Maylis knew what that meant by now."
- **The ending: 8.** "'I know what you picked. I was the other one.'" It costs more than Dark Matter's does. One point off for the shape of the last three chapters.
- **Clarity: 6.** What Lesca risks by refusing to go home is said once, in chapter 2, inside a joke: "The one it cannot take home dies in her own bed." It is collected ten chapters later with "She knew what that cost."

**Finished unpaid?** Yes. I would have stalled once, in chapter 6. From the last line of 8 I did not stop.

**Press it on a friend?** Yes, for chapters 10 and 13, and I would tell them to get as far as the stairs in 5.

## 2. Against the comparisons

**Below Crouch**, by one point.

- **Fear for the marriage peaks in 5 and is retired in 7.** In Dark Matter the other Jason is in the house with Daniela and she does not know. That ignorance runs for most of the book and is why nobody stops reading. Here Coralie takes a pulse on the landing and names them both: "'That's her,' she said. 'In this hand.'" It is the best thing she does, and at the halfway mark it removes the thing I was most afraid of. Nothing of that kind replaces it until the river.
- **The heroine's plan for the night has no chance.** Crouch's man tries something new each chapter and the reader half believes each try. Maylis's plan is to stay awake, and she has been told twice that this does not work: "The thing that sits on you doesn't need you asleep." So I waited for it to fail.
- **Level on speed, clock and hooks.** The other self speaks at about 4,200 words and the date is set by 6,000, which is Crouch's pace. The dated file lines do the work of his ampoules. The endings of 00, 02, 03, 05, 08, 11 and 13 are his standard.
- **Above on cost.** Crouch lets Jason keep his family. Here nobody keeps anything, and the choice is wrong, not merely hard: "By her own count she could have had the gate up at 3."

**Against The Good Place: level once, below once.** Coralie's "'2:51,' she said. 'I've known that for twelve years.'" re-reads every scene on that landing, and it is that show's kind of reversal. "You're the water" is the same size of reversal and gets half the effect, because the hall hears it as thirst and chapter 8 then explains it over a chart.

## 3. The ten faults, worst first

**1. Chapter 11. The night only happens because she does at 3:03 what she could have done at 5:30, and the book does not make me believe she couldn't.**
"Her weight was on the handle. 'Either,' Maylis said." Then, at the trapdoor: "She knocked two more times. The chimney pipe went on smoking. She walked back". Nine hours later: "She grabbed the collars of both his jackets in her left hand and pulled."
At 5 the man is on the bank beside her, the café pad has proved the prediction for the 8th, and Kévin has just taken away Larrère's lever ("Don't keep a gate shut for me"). Nothing on the page holds that handle but her. The book means this, and Madame Darrigrand says so in 16, but I need to believe it at 5 p.m., not be told on Sunday.
What to do: keep the word and put her hope on the platform before it. She thinks that if Lesca is kept out at 2:51 there is no flood, so an open gate would be a flood made by her own hand, for nothing. That is the motive of 3 a.m. ("She wanted it not to be her"), so the two choices would rhyme. Then give the hope a chance. Take Kévin's second warning out of 11. Use what chapter 1 planted: Coralie straps the rib and says "Now you can." A strapped rib can push. Have Maylis ask for the strapping on the back step at 4. Then the six rings in the van are a fight I think she might win.

**2. Chapters 12 and 14. The death the book threatens is waived with a joke.**
"'You can put that in the minutes as apologies received.'" Then she opens her fingers and walks.
The card with her name at the end of 8 is the best cliff in the book. It is cashed in 400 words, at no price she feels, and I never believed she would stay at that table. The price comes two chapters later in one line: "Twelve. One, two. Somebody pulled a chair in to a table."
What to do: split the hall across chapter 13. End 12 with her seated, the grain of corn held over the square. Play the whole flood with the reader believing she is dead at that table. Open 14 with the grain going back in the dish, the apologies, and then twelve called behind her. The joke survives and the debt is paid in order.

**3. Chapter 6. The heroine's only day in the other world is the slackest chapter, and its cliff leads nowhere.**
"the council of Peyrebarthe watched its mayor not lift one." Chapter 7 opens at home, and Lesca's village never mentions it.
She reads the file before noon and the chapter has 1,500 words still to run. The danger of the day is named in one clause ("It came with Coralie finding her dead") and shown once, in the cut to 18:40, which is the only tense moment in the chapter.
What to do: move the file to the end, after the council, so that staying late is her choice and not Inguma's slowness. Cut to the body in the bed three times. Let one person at that table see the mayor freeze, and have it cost Lesca something in 10.

**4. Chapters 5 to 11. One beat, six times.**
Stairs (5), turned to the wall (6), pillow (7), landing and pillow (7), coffee down the sink (8), "She went up to the spare room" (9). Each scene has a new line and the same exit. The result is that chapter 7 ends where it began.
What to do: end 7 at the bingo, on "'That's till morning,' Madame Etcheto told Coralie". Lesca has the body for a whole night with Coralie upstairs, and that is a cliff. Open 8 on the landing. End the bandage scene in 9 on "I asked where you'd be" and drop the exit.

**5. Chapters 14 to 16. About fifteen pay-off scenes in 6,100 words, and the calendar steps backwards.**
"The village council met on Tuesday at 6" (15), then "At first light on Sunday Maylis walked down into the Port" (16).
After "Forty-one" the engine is off, and the book calls the roll twice, once per village: Larrère, Maïté, Marie-Jo, Coralie. Chapter 15 is 1,171 words of ticks.
What to do: fold 15 into 14, so the confession chapter ends on "'Lesca, Maylis,' Lesca said. 'Surname first.'" and not on chairs. Open 16 with the date written out. Cut Marie-Jo's single chair in 16, which repeats her two hundred in 14.

**6. Chapters 2 and 12. Lesca's price is said once, as folklore, 30,000 words before it is needed.**
"She knew what that cost. She had seen the card with her name on it."
I had to work back to room twelve to know what holding the steering wheel would do to her.
What to do: give Arlette the rule in 10, in her own mother's words, when she says "you're using them to leave yours". One line.

**7. Chapter 9. Nothing is frightening between the stairs in 5 and the van roof in 11.**
"All day she had been waiting for that cold to come back to the kitchen door." From chapter 5 Inguma is a bailiff both women use, and at the bingo it lifts tablecloths.
The Thursday night has the best image of the middle and spends it as a way into a flashback: "Its arms were cold. Lesca wasn't in it yet". She is held until 4:57, an hour past the time it has always given up, and nobody remarks on it.
What to do: let that night frighten before the flashback starts. An empty cold chair, and a thing that does not get down at 4.

**8. Chapter 9. The 2014 night is told seven times, and the replay walks the prologue again.**
"Before she fitted the handle to the gate, she turned the van around on top of the bank so that its headlights pointed down the track". I had that from the first page.
What to do: start the replay at the reflector. The new facts are the van she recognises, the second ring she does not wait for, and four minutes watching the red light go under. Those are enough.

**9. Chapters 13 and 16. The choice is explained while it is being made, then twice more.**
"If she turned that handle, a man drowned and her hand had done it. If she went on, whatever the river did behind her was the river's doing. She wanted it not to be her." Then in 16: "I waited for the river to decide, so that it wouldn't be me."
What to do: cut the two "If" sentences. "She wanted it not to be her" carries it. Keep the line to Kévin and cut nothing from the last steel room, where the motive changes ("to be the one who did") and is new.

**10. Chapter 1. The first place a Crouch reader stops.**
"'I've got a registered letter for the mayor,' the postman said into the gap. 'Who is also the president of the landowners' association, it says here.'"
After the prologue and the thing on the wardrobe, the square runs 900 words on a postman, a window and a handle before anything threatens again. Every piece of it is used later, so none can go.
What to do: keep the premise alive inside it. She is raking beside the parking space where the stone stood in her picture. Give her one look at that space, and at the man's name she could not read, while Jeannot is talking.

## 4. Five things that must not be touched

1. **"Either" on the platform**, and the speed of Jeannot's answer. Fault 1 asks for what comes before it, not for a different word.
2. **The call log in 10.** "Outgoing. Castagnet J., mobile. Not answered. 02:51." and "Because you'd have told me."
3. **Chapter 13 from the dip to "Forty-one".** Her own van across the track, the bank holding sixteen minutes, "Don't tell me what he was", Kévin's "Five", the line through the last name.
4. **The hand on the stair post in 5, and its answer on the landing in 7.** The stairs are the most frightening page in the book.
5. **Bordenave's cross.** "If she can be out on Friday the 4th, she can be out on Friday the 11th." The proof that Lesca can be kept out is what sends Jeannot to his hut. That is the tightest piece of plotting here.
