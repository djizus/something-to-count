# Round 32 — Characters

Read: `manuscript/00.md` to `16.md`, in order, in full. Nothing else.

## 1. Scores

- **Stakes: 8.** "The trapdoor was twenty-five minutes away on foot. The bank had ten." (9) Every number in that sum is a person the reader has met.
- **Tension: 8.** "Coralie took the third step with the wrist still in her fingers." (7) The book's tension is in people, not in water, and that is where it is strongest.
- **Progression: 6.5.** "Your river made up two days in an afternoon, so only the last night counts." (8) One line cancels the two nights of pushing in 7. Between 6 and 9 the marriage and the plan both stand still.
- **Comedy: 8.** "Either is not a person. I cannot summon Either to the station." (6)
- **Characters: 7.5.** "And then there's me, as a distance." (8) The best person in the book is the one asking the question, not either of the two it is about.
- **The switch between dark and comic: 8.5.** "'I'll be brief,' she said, in the voice that came from the back of the throat. 'The—'" (16) The joke that dies in her mouth does more than the flood.
- **Prose: 7.** "She wanted to scream." (1) The narrator labels a feeling the scene had already delivered. It does this at most of the peaks.
- **The ending: 8.** "It's four pages, and it doesn't thank you." (16)
- **Clarity: 6.** "If one of us stays sitting in this chair when the other wakes up, she wakes up in the other one's body, and she drives it." (4) Four rules as a list, two more added in 8, and I still stopped in 12 to work out why Lesca wakes among the dead and then in Bayonne.

Would I have finished it unpaid? Yes. Would I press it on a friend? Yes, and I would say "get to the stairs in chapter 5".

## 2. Against the comparisons

**Above Crouch, below The Good Place. Against the bar as a whole: just under.**

- **Above Crouch on the wife.** Daniela in *Dark Matter* is chosen between. Coralie does the choosing, by pulse: "That's her. In this hand." She has a want neither Maylis can supply (Bayonne, Ithurbide, "a floor and a night of my own to run"). Crouch makes a reader afraid for a marriage by showing it warm and taking it away. This book does it by letting the rival be better at the marriage than the owner: "I'd have this one again." That is the harder trick and it works.
- **Level with The Good Place on people each wrong in their own way.** Larrère cannot sign. Jeannot cannot be told. Coralie will not ask the one question that matters ("Because you'd have told me"). Maylis cannot choose. Lesca cannot not. Kévin wants six fingers. None of these is a quirk; each one costs somebody on the 11th.
- **Below The Good Place on the two leads as company.** Chidi's indecision is a torrent; Maylis's is one word. In the series the four leads are funnier than anyone around them. Here the neighbours are funnier than the leads, and after chapter 6 Maylis enjoys nothing. A reader smiles at Jeannot, Larrère, Etcheto and Coralie and waits for the two women the book is named for.

The lens questions, answered:

- **Distinct?** Yes. Lesca talks in items and motions and the narrator never has to say which one is speaking. Maylis turns every feeling into a measurement ("It wasn't much. She took more than that to the dump in a morning").
- **Wanted-for?** Lesca, yes, from her first page. Maylis, only in 1, 6, 11 and 16.
- **Softened or excused?** Maylis is excused by mechanics at 3 a.m. (fault 1). Lesca is easier to like than her record allows (fault 4) and is forgiven too fast in 15 (fault 7).
- **Described from outside as a woman?** Neither. Nobody in the book has a face or a figure. The price is fault 9.
- **Who would be missed?** Madame Etcheto, Jeannot, Kévin, Larrère, Sarthou, Arlette, Marie-Jo. Not Josiane, Bordenave, the dealer, Bixente or Nadine.
- **Which relationship carries it, and does it pay?** Maylis and Coralie. It pays in the last ten minutes (the strapped rib, eleven stairs, "the car door shutting on a pillow") but it idles for five chapters first (fault 2). The Lesca and Coralie scene in 10 pays more than anything on Maylis's side.
- **Does the reader care who lives?** Yes for Jeannot and for Etcheto. For Lesca at the table of the dead, yes. The death the book chooses is the right one.

## 3. The ten faults, worst first

**1. The book's moral weight is at 5 p.m. and its page weight is at 3 a.m. (11, 13, 16)**

> 'Either,' Maylis said.
> 'Then it's shut,' Jeannot said. 'That's how the vote left it.'
> He was off the platform and down the steps before she could take it back.
> She lifted the handle off.

This is the moment the whole book turns on. Madame Darrigrand says so in 16 ("At 5 on Friday it was light"), and Maylis says so ("I waited for the river to decide, so that it wouldn't be me"). On the page it is a reflex in one word, and then the handle comes off with nothing between the two sentences. She said "Open" twice in public on Tuesday. She could not say "Either" in the steel room. An hour earlier she asked Coralie back. The relapse may be true but the reader is not inside it, and Jeannot is still on the bank for another half page while she does nothing.

Because 5 p.m. is thin, the reader's judgement lands on the dip at 3 a.m., where she has every excuse the plot can supply: a sunk van, a rib, a handle that takes "two arms and a knee". She refuses the excuse ("At 3 I never tried it"), but the letter that offers it has the last word.

Do: rebuild the platform scene so that it is the longest beat in the chapter. Give her the three things that stop her mouth, in order: the man whose hut she would fill asking her to his face, in the grammar of Coralie's boxes; Larrère's car a hundred metres off; and her father, who "stopped four turns short, to leave a man his water", which is Either done with a handle. Then let her stand with the handle fitted after Jeannot has gone down the steps, with him still in reach, and take it off knowing what she is doing. Put Madame Darrigrand on the road below, watching, so that her line in 16 is a witness's.

**2. The marriage plays one beat four times and waits. (4, 8, 8, 9)**

> Then she tore the page off the pad, folded it and put it in her coat pocket. (4)
> Coralie picked up the bowl of coffee and poured it down the sink. (8)
> She left the pencil lying across the pad. (8)
> 'I asked where you'd be,' Coralie said. She went up to the spare room. (9)

Coralie asks, Maylis answers with a measurement, Coralie leaves with an object. By the third the reader is ahead of the scene and Coralie has become the bearer of the theme. Nothing changes between them from the landing in 7 to the doorstep in 11.

Do: keep the landing and "me, as a distance". Cut the Bayonne exchange in 8; chapter 16 does it once and better. End the bandage scene in 9 on "Duck" and the hand on the rib, with no exit line. That is the one time she answers, and it gives Friday something to take away.

**3. Only one of the two women covets. (6, then nothing)**

> If it never came, all of this was hers. The desk, the stone, a square that said good morning. That morning, standing in front of the stone, she might have said yes to it. But it came with Kévin's cap and one pillow.

Chapter 6 is where Maylis comes alive: she slows down to be greeted, she keeps thirteen people standing, "I want that" is out before she can stop it. Then the temptation is closed in a paragraph and never returns. For the back half Lesca wants Maylis's life and Maylis wants only to be left alone. A book about two versions of one person needs both to be tempted.

Do: in the steel room in 8, standing, where only true things come out, have Lesca offer the trade. Let Maylis try to say she does not want the desk and the square, and stick. Her "Both" would then come from a woman who has just learned she wants more than she knew.

**4. Lesca's hardness is reported; her loneliness is shown. (3, 6, 10)**

> His mother had been Lesca's deputy mayor until a night in 2018, when she handed her sash back across the council table in a plastic bag.

A jailed boy in the village coat, a mother's field turned into a lake, four houses knocked down, a deputy lost. Each is a sentence, and most reach us through Maylis. What the page shows in Lesca's own chapters is a woman mending a widow's roof and eating soup standing up. Only Darrigrand's step ("It's four when you're the one counting") shows the sum hurting someone to her face. She is therefore the easier woman to like, which tilts the pair.

Do: in 3, from inside Lesca, show one sum made and won against a person in the room. Kévin taking his cap off to her is the obvious one: let her see it and let it stand. Tell the night of the plastic bag in four lines.

**5. Coralie asks the book's question down the phone in a flood. (13)**

> 'Then which do you want?' Behind Coralie somebody was calling a name. 'The gate, or Castagnet? Say which, Maylis. For once.'

Water is at her back door and she has forty-two people to move. "For once" is the author.

Do: give her a practical need for the same answer. "Is the gate going up or not? I need to know which end to start." Then "Both" fails her as information, and "Start at the bank end" is Maylis sending her to room twelve without knowing it.

**6. The father is three men's stick and nobody's memory. (1, 8, 9, 11)**

> 'You've got his key and none of his sense.' (9)
> He marked a number. He didn't look up, and she took the hand away. (8)

Larrère uses him twice, Jeannot twice. He is the source of both women: the keyholder who never rang and never asked. The image at the table of the dead is right. But Maylis never once remembers him herself, so "Either" has a cause the book points at and will not touch.

Do: one memory of her own, three lines, at the handle. It serves fault 1.

**7. Chapter 15 forgives Lesca in an afternoon.**

> 'It would have been right for both lines.'
> 'Yes.'

Within a hundred lines of the confession Marie-Jo says her name, Maïté holds her ladder, Larrère is shown to be the worse party and Coralie says "Good night, Maylis". Each is good. Together they are a soft landing, and Lesca's line about the verb is her being neat about her own crime.

Do: cut her line; Maïté's "with yours under it" has said it. Let Maïté hold the door open and Lesca go up the ladder unasked.

**8. Maylis has a mother across the yard and never crosses it. (8)**

> 'My ponies are on that grass, Maylis.'

Arlette is Lesca's conscience in three scenes and has the old words. On Maylis's side she gets nine lines at a tap. It makes Maylis's isolation look arranged.

Do: one refusal. Maylis stops at the yard door on the night of the 9th and does not knock.

**9. A book fought over a bed, and nobody wants anything in it but a pillow. (1, 3, 5)**

> The eyes went up the stairs to Coralie and stayed on her, the way they had stayed on Jeannot at noon.

Touch between the women is always clinical: two fingers on a wrist, a towel, a bandage. That is the book's best idea about Coralie. But sixteen years in one bed pass without a sentence of appetite from inside either woman, and Lesca's look at Coralie shares a simile with her look at a drowned man. When she says in 16 what she went up the stairs for, it is stronger than anything the stairs showed.

Do: one sentence from inside Maylis at 10 p.m. in chapter 1, and one from inside Lesca on the landing in 3. No description of Coralie is needed.

**10. The Port's verdict comes from a woman with two earlier lines. (16)**

> 'At 3 Sarthou carried her out over his head, and you were a kilometre away, fetching a man out of a dry hole.'

It lands. It would land harder from somebody the reader knows.

Do: the fix is in fault 1. Put her on the road at 5 p.m.

## 4. Five things that must not be touched

1. **The landing, chapter 7.** "I don't get into a bed over somebody who can't speak. I've lifted too many of them." Coralie tells the two apart by a pulse and refuses the one she prefers.
2. **"Because you'd have told me." Chapter 10, whole.** Twelve years of a landing explained by a call log. Nothing in it should be added to.
3. **Madame Etcheto, from room twelve to "Nobody emptied hers".** Including the half minute Coralie holds her wrist, and the hatbox on the wardrobe.
4. **Jeannot's lists.** From "One. Those are my boots" through "And six. Bugger off" to a letter that "doesn't thank you". He is the same man with a shovel as with a stamp.
5. **"She wanted it not to be her."** Do not make her nobler at the dip. Fault 1 asks for more at 5 p.m., not less at 3.
