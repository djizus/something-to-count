## 2. Against the comparisons

**Below Crouch on getting in, level or above from chapter 9.**

- Crouch's premise fits in one line and is running by the end of his first chapter. Here the double speaks on the last line of chapter 2, what she wants is the last line of chapter 3, and how it works is chapter 4. The opening page of the flood night is what carried me across those three chapters.
- Crouch gives his machine one rule and never adds to it. This book adds to its rules in nine places (fault 1).
- Chapter endings are as good as his. Fourteen of sixteen made me turn the page. The fear for the marriage is better than his, because Coralie can say no and does.

**Level with The Good Place on making the problem funny, below it on the reversal.**

- The moral problem is funny because of who is wrong and how: one woman who cannot pick soup or duck, one who cannot stop chairing. "That was Either, with the river in it" is the whole book in six words.
- "You're the water" is a real reversal and I did not see it until the dry Friday. But it re-reads two weeks of rain. It does not re-read the people.
- The ending costs more than either comparison charges. That is the book's advantage and it should keep it.

### What I did not understand

- **What the steel box did.** A university builds it, it makes two of her, Inguma smells of it and has its rivets. The university never comes back. I do not know whether the thing on the wardrobe is the box, or a spirit, or both.
- **Why the river travels with Lesca.** I accepted it. I was never told.
- **Why Larrère ordered the gate up on the Monday.** The river was low. That order cracks the rib, and the rib starts everything.
- **"Friday."** (8) Lesca gets one word out round Maylis's fingers. A promise to come back? A warning? I do not know.
- **The padlock under the third board** (4, 6). The nets I understood. Not the lock.
- **Who was winning at the bar** on bingo night, until the narrator told me.
- **What sitting in the chair in the hall of the dead does.** I was told it was final. Then it was not.

### Where I was bored, lost or skimming

- Ch 3, the file. Lost, then slow: a new woman, a new village, known faces in other lives, and a deduction about dates.
- Ch 6, the first page and a half. Lost: three scenes of leverage in forty lines.
- Ch 7, the middle of the bingo. Skimmed the numbers.
- Ch 8, the association's vote. Skimmed.
- Ch 10, Arlette and the rehearsal. Impatient: Friday had been promised and I was in the wrong village.

### Where I could not stop

Ch 5 from the window to the stairs. Ch 7 from midnight on the landing. Ch 9 from the phone at 5. Ch 11 from the platform to the end of ch 13, in one sitting.

### What I will remember in a month

"Either." "Then it's shut." The window that goes all the way down. Coralie doing the mayor through the oven door, and the third time, when she cannot. Chrysanthemums carried past the café to the wrong house. "And six. Bugger off." The red reflector going out. A hand held on a pulse to find out who is in the body. Kévin counting five fingers in the flood and wanting six. "Forty-one." A confession going back through a crowd at walking pace. The second shovel.

I will not remember the box.

## 3. The ten faults, worst first

**1. The rules arrive in nine instalments, and the narrator has to referee the two biggest scenes.** (2, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 11, 12)
The instalments: Madame Etcheto's five in ch 2; "three points" and then "a fourth point, and I didn't have it on my list" in ch 4; a count can be sized to a day in ch 5; it counts "on foot" in ch 6; unfinished words do not count, and anyone can finish them, in ch 7; the river comes with her, and "the river catches up the day I'm back", in ch 8; "The thing that sits on you doesn't need you asleep" in ch 11; a ring lays a place in ch 12.
Then, at the bar: "Lesca hadn't finished the words, so Inguma had nothing to count and would take her home. For one breath, Maylis had won." And a page on: "Inguma had gone to count the corn, so nothing would take Lesca home tonight. Maylis had lost."
What is wrong: a reader who needs the score read out is not afraid. In ch 12 the commentary is also false to what follows it, since Lesca sits in the chair and walks away.
What I would do: fix the rules at four, said once in ch 4 (stay sitting and you drive; the owner keeps the right hand; the words send it away to count, and it takes the visitor home when it has finished; standing in that room you cannot lie). Keep Coralie's three-column chart in ch 8 as the one restatement. It is the clearest page in the book. Cut the ch 12 paragraph whole. Madame Etcheto's line in ch 2 and the card in ch 8 already tell me what the hall is. Cut both "had won" and "had lost". Ch 7 has already planted the terms: "All Maylis had to do was keep the mouth shut until it came."

**2. Chapter 10 puts the brake on at the moment the book is fastest.** (10)
Ch 9 ends on "*before dark*". Ch 10 opens with Arlette in a doorway and then: "'A point of order,' Lesca said. 'You have the flowers as one item and the kiss as the next.'"
What is wrong: I wanted Friday night and got a mother's visit and an order of ceremony. The kiss is now set up three times (6, 9, 10). The second half of the chapter, Coralie and the phone, is among the best scenes in the book and I reached it annoyed.
What I would do: open ch 10 on the tape strips ("Eight of them were in their beds that night") and go from there to the landing. Cut the point of order about the kiss and the medal. Ch 14 explains both by itself. Halve Arlette, keeping the lake and "I want you at my table at 7", or move her to before ch 9.

**3. The box is the book's opening promise and it is never paid.** (1, 2, 13, 16)
"They opened the door, counted one of her, paid her, gave her a sandwich and went back to their university." They never come back. Maylis puts the card in her chest pocket in ch 2. It "came out with" the phone in ch 13. In ch 16 the letter goes in "with her card". The card never does anything.
What is wrong: a reader who came for two versions of one person is given an experiment in chapter 1 and a village spirit by chapter 8, with no word on how the two are one thing. An object carried to the last page is a promise.
What I would do: one paragraph in the last meeting in the steel room (16). The lamps are not mentioned there. Let the room end on its own terms, with one lamp, or none, and let the card be put down somewhere.

**4. Five votes.** (3, 4, 6, 8, 15)
In ch 8 the same vote is told three times: live at the card table, then by Arlette ("Three and three and Castagnet"), then by Coralie through the oven door ("Three against three. It stays shut").
What is wrong: the vote is the book's comic engine, and by ch 8 I could hear it turning over. It also makes ch 15's vote, the one that matters, the fifth of its kind.
What I would do: keep ch 3, ch 6 and ch 15. In ch 8 keep the sheet against the glass with "*Labat, Kévin*" on it and Jeannot's hand, and let Arlette or the oven carry the count. Not both, and not after a full live tally.

**5. Chapter 3 asks the most of me when I know the least.** (3)
"Over there the rain had begun on the same date as in her file, twelve years later. If the dates went on matching, she could read every line of the file as this year's, over there."
What is wrong: six scenes in the head of a woman I met a page ago. The idea is explained twice in a row, and this is the weaker time.
What I would do: let the five file entries do it. I saw the dates match before she told me. Go from "Today was the 28th" straight to "That village hadn't been spared its flood. It hadn't had it yet." Cut the paragraph between.

**6. The order that starts the story has no reason.** (1)
"On Monday afternoon the president of the association had rung and told her to raise it."
What is wrong: this breaks the rib, puts her on her back and lets Lesca in. Jeannot's complaint in ch 6 turns on whose order it was. Nobody says what it was for.
What I would do: one line. Jeannot's "Three" in ch 1 is unsaid and waiting for it.

**7. Chapter 6 opens with three pieces of leverage in forty lines.** (6)
"With no date on it, it isn't a letter. It's a sheet of paper that I keep, and you're the one who puts the date on it."
What is wrong: the undated resignation, the blank sheet for Kévin, Sarthou's complaint and Maylis's threat about the nets all land before I have sat down. These papers are what is in that car on the bank in ch 11. I had to go back.
What I would do: give the Larrère scene another ten lines, so that I see the blank sheet before I am told what it is for. Say what the padlock under the third board locks, or drop it.

**8. Names that look alike.** (8, 10, 13, 16)
Darrigrand (the swept step, the baby) and Daguerre ("Here"). Lesca, Larrère, Labat, Lassalle.
What is wrong: in ch 13, in the water, I stopped to work out whose baby it was.
What I would do: rename Daguerre or Darrigrand, and one of the L's.

**9. The one cut to Coralie's bedside.** (6)
"In the other village it was raining. The clock radio said 18:40."
What is wrong: for five pages "the other village" has meant Lesca's. Here it means home. It is also the only time the book leaves its two heads.
What I would do: name the place in the first three words, or let Maylis see the chart in ch 7 and cut this.

**10. Too many figures compete with the four that matter.** (throughout)
"Eleven years I've been timed like an egg." "Twelve winters the mayor has been up at that house." "Sixteen years, and I've had one straight answer out of you." "Eighteen years of cutting roadsides." Also four winters, six years, thirty years, 2008, 2015, 2017, 2018, 2019.
What is wrong: I needed 2:51, ten minutes, twenty-five minutes and four minutes. I had them, but I had stopped trusting myself with numbers by ch 6. Madame Darrigrand's "At 3 Sarthou carried her out" is also out by eleven minutes against the page.
What I would do: keep twelve and sixteen. Cut every year-date and count that no later scene uses.

## 4. Five things that must not be touched

1. **"Either", all the way down**: the shopping pad, Sarthou's form, the platform ("'Then it's shut,' Jeannot said"), and Coralie's "That was Either, with the river in it."
2. **Two fingers on the wrist.** The pulse counts, and the landing in ch 7 where Coralie tells the two women apart by holding one hand.
3. **Jeannot by numbers**: "One." in ch 1, the six points at 5 in the morning, letter 42 and the second shovel.
4. **Chapter 13 as it stands**, clock times included: the dip, "She wanted it not to be her", Daguerre's "Here", Kévin's five fingers, "Forty-one."
5. **The square in ch 14**: the sentence going back row by row, the young man asking for the case, "Somebody has to stack them."
