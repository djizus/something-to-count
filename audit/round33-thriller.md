# Round 33 — Thriller lens

Read: `manuscript/00.md` to `16.md`, in order, in full. Nothing else.

## 1. Scores

- **Stakes: 7.5.** "The trapdoor was twenty-five minutes away on foot. The bank had ten." The sum is measurable and the reader can do it. What drags it: the human antagonist's hold on her is "That's prison, for eels."
- **Tension: 7.** "Maylis grabbed the post at the bottom of the stairs. The dish towel fell off, which left her bare palm on the wood, cut and all." Chapters 5 and 11 to 13 are at the bar. Chapters 6, 10 and 15 are not.
- **Progression: 6.5.** "Your mayor's thirty centimetres out. If she can be out on Friday the 4th, she can be out on Friday the 11th." Her one success discredits her warning, which is how a plot should tighten. Against it: the same night is told three times (00, 9, 13) and two chapters on the clock are spent in the other village's committee work.
- **Comedy: 7.** "Either is not a person. I cannot summon Either to the station."
- **Characters: 8.** "I don't get into a bed over somebody who can't speak. I've lifted too many of them."
- **The switch between dark and comic: 7.** Clean: "'Here,' said Monsieur Daguerre" under the prefect's date. Not clean: "You can put that in the minutes as apologies received", spoken in the hall of the dead at 02:53 with the bank going.
- **Prose: 7.** Plain and fast. It explains at the one moment it must not: "This was the same thing, done with a steering wheel."
- **The ending: 7.** "'Forty-one,' Coralie said." is a 9. Three chapters follow it.
- **Clarity: 6.** "She had held the wheel against it for a whole ring, and that ring had laid a place for her here. But she had let go before she was sitting in it." A rule stated in the paragraph that needs it.

**Finished unpaid?** Yes. From the end of 10 I did not stop.
**Press it on a friend?** Yes, but not as a thriller. I would say a village comedy with a flood in it, and I would warn them about chapter 6.

## 2. Against the comparisons

**Below Crouch, by one point. Level with him in chapters 5 and 11 to 13.**

1. **Crouch enlarges his premise in the last act; this one is full size by chapter 3 and never grows.** Dark Matter ends with a street full of Jasons. Recursion ends with the chair unmaking the world. Here it is two women and two villages from "Call her Lesca" to the last page. The box of June 2014 is never opened again, and the card that says *left* rides in a chest pocket to the cemetery ("buttoned it into her chest pocket, with her card").
2. **Crouch's wife cannot tell the two men apart, and that is the fear. Coralie can, in twenty minutes.** "She bent down and looked into one eye, then into the other. [...] 'Who is that?'" From there the reader is never afraid she will be fooled, only that she will prefer the other one ("I'd have this one again"), and chapter 8 closes that too. The heroine herself is never in danger of more than a rib.
3. **The right-hand rule is better than anything Crouch does with a double.** The owner inside her own body, with one hand, holding the stair post while the other walks her up to her own bed. Above the comparison for those four pages.
4. **The choice costs more than Dark Matter's.** Crouch gives Jason his family back. This book takes the marriage ("the car door shutting on a pillow"). Level with Recursion on cost.
5. **The Good Place plays its reversal as a scene, to the face of the person responsible.** This book's reversal, that the rescuer is the flood, is three words a bingo hall hears as a request for a drink. It re-reads chapters 3 to 7 correctly. The jolt is spent on a joke.

## 3. The ten faults, worst first

**1. The decision that sinks the Port is made at 5 p.m. in chapter 11, in four lines, and the book stages 3 a.m. as the choice.**
"'Either,' Maylis said. 'Then it's shut,' Jeannot said. 'That's how the vote left it.' He was off the platform and down the steps before she could take it back."
At that moment she has everything Lesca asked for in chapter 4: daylight, the handle fitted, the one man who uses the fields standing behind her, nine signatures, an orange warning, and Kévin's release an hour old. "Before she could take it back" is not true to the scene: he then sits at his van window with the engine running and gives a speech, and she has already locked the box. A thriller reader stops believing here, or stops respecting her, and neither is the reaction the book wants until Madame Darrigrand says it in 16.
*Do:* keep her weight on the handle. Let him go down to his van, say his piece through the window, and drive off the bank onto the track with the handle still on and the chain still tight, so that he is the one calling it. Let her watch the reflector to the end of the track with her hands on the handle. Lift it off when the torch goes underground. The hole becomes the longest held breath in the book, and the dip at 02:59 becomes the second time she does it.

**2. The book never shuts the obvious door: on the one night that matters, nobody gives Inguma something to count.**
Chapter 3: "She gave it that, blade by blade, and it was at it till daylight." Chapter 10: "She said them so she could stay in her own bed till morning." Chapter 2: "You've been lying where it sits since Monday, and you've given it nothing to count." By the book's own rule a long count keeps it off the bed all night. Maylis tries her side, the kitchen floor and staying awake in a van. She never tries the words again after the "Either" joke in chapter 2. Worse, in chapter 6 she gives it eight hours of roadside at 11 and "Inguma came down at its usual minute and sat on her", which contradicts the grandmother. And the rule is worded "If I'm in your village at 2:51", which invites a second question: why not drive the body to Bayonne?
*Do:* make the third of her three nights in chapter 7 the night she says the words, properly, with a count, and show why it comes anyway. One stated rule, placed in Lesca's "three points" in chapter 4: what the count delays and what it does not. Then make chapter 6 obey it. Change "in your village" to "in you".

**3. The hall of the dead cuts into the countdown, and Lesca leaves it on a rule made up as she arrives.**
Chapter 12 leaves the van at 02:53 for a page and a half of bingo. "One more ring and she would have sat in it for good. As it was, Inguma would have to take her home from here." The book planted her death twice (room twelve's "dies in her own bed", the card in chapter 8) and here the narrator refunds it in three sentences. She then declines her seat with a committee joke.
*Do:* end chapter 12 on "Then there was no steering wheel, and no rain." Go straight to 13. Open 14 with the hall: it already begins there ("Behind her, in the hall of the dead, the voice went on calling her name"), and "Twelve. One, two. Somebody pulled a chair in to a table" then lands as the answer to her refusal instead of an echo of it. Cut the three sentences of ring arithmetic. Let her not know why she was let go until the number is called.

**4. On the morning of the flood the book goes to a rehearsal.**
Chapter 9 ends at 5:10 with Maylis rewriting her plan. Chapter 10 opens in the other village: "He read it to her off his sheet, from the anthem to the photograph." Arlette, the cross of tape, Darrigrand's step, eight strips. This is where a Crouch reader puts the book down. Chapter 6 has the same fault at half the cost: thirteen hours of risk for a file page that adds one fact ("Trapdoor locked on the inside").
*Do:* in 10, keep Arlette to half a page, keep Coralie's tape and "The others have died since then, in those beds", cut the kiss, the medal and the young man's questions, and get to the landing. In 6, cut Larrère's speech by half and let the file give her something she can use on the night and did not have: who rang, and from where.

**5. The rival stops being frightening at the end of chapter 8.**
"I'd have had you. You answer. But what I'd have had is a river over forty-two beds." With that line Coralie is out of Lesca's reach, and a third of the book remains. Lesca's threat in the same chapter, "the only one left to stop me is you, with one hand", is a threat to take something nobody is offering her. And chapter 10, from inside her head, never looks at what she now knows: she told the truth room "If I'm not, it holds", then tells her own Coralie "I'm going to get him out." The book marks the lie once ("In her own office Lesca could tell herself anything") and moves on.
*Do:* hold Coralie's verdict back. In 8 she pins the hand and asks her question; she does not say whom she would have. Going into Friday the reader should not know. In 10, make Lesca try the sentence "If I stay home he lives" out loud in her own bedroom, where nothing stops a lie, and have her not say it.

**6. Chapter 15 is a chapter of curtain calls between the reader and the scene they are waiting for.**
After "Forty-one" the reader wants what Coralie says to Maylis. They get the confession (which earns its place), then Maïté's verb, a council vote and "Good night, Maylis". "The motion is carried, Madame le—" is the only beat in 15 with a pulse.
*Do:* fold 15 into 14. Keep Madame Etcheto at the minibus ("Nobody emptied hers"), keep the bell rung on her own time and "Surname first". Put Maïté's gate scene after the ceremony the same evening at half length. In 16, the bus, Josiane's capper and the café silence can go to four lines; the reader has the charge from Madame Darrigrand already.

**7. Larrère's hold is withdrawn before it is used.**
"I'd do them, Maylis. I want you to know I would. Don't keep a gate shut for me." Kévin says this at the sand bin, an hour before the platform. So at 5 p.m. the blackmail the book spent chapters 6 and 8 building (the blank sheet, "*Labat, Kévin*" against the glass) weighs nothing, and the villain's only remaining job is a phone call.
*Do:* move Kévin's release to the platform at dusk, after the handle has come off. At 5 p.m. she should still believe the handle costs him six months. Her line in 16 ("You told me on Friday not to keep that gate shut for you. I didn't.") still stands, and now it is about the night.

**8. The secret of twelve years arrives as a download, and it is the third telling of the night.**
"Lesca wasn't in it yet, but the night of the flood came to Maylis anyway." The chair hands over whatever the plot needs (the ladder afternoon in 4, Kévin's prison in 6, the whole night in 9). What Lesca has hidden from a prefect, a council and a widow costs Maylis nothing to get. The prologue already gave the bank, the handle, the turn of the van and the single ring; 13 gives them again.
*Do:* make her take it. She sits first, on purpose, in a cold chair, to get what Lesca will not say standing. One sentence. Then cut 9's account to what 00 withheld: the reflector, whose van, the boot. Drop the paragraph of medal, councillors and stone; the reader has all of it.

**9. The reversal is muffled.**
"'You're the water.' [...] 'She wants water,' one of the Lassalle sisters said." The deduction is good and fair ("Yesterday morning that same post had said a metre. The boards had been dry then."). But the sentence that turns the book is thrown to the hall for a laugh, and the plain statement comes next morning as a chart.
*Do:* let the hall mishear it, but give Lesca one beat first. She is driving the face. Let the left hand stop pouring before anyone speaks. The reader should see it hit her before Josiane gets to it.

**10. The box is furniture.**
"They opened the door, counted one of her, paid her, gave her a sandwich." The premise promises a village that sat in that chair ("Still in the box, were you?"), and Kévin says "I've had that twice, wide awake." Nothing comes of either. A reader of Crouch waits for the premise to widen and it does not.
*Do:* pay Kévin. One line in 16 is enough: what he saw the two times it sat on him, set beside the other Kévin's six months. Or cut "twice" from chapter 2 and stop promising it.

## 4. Five things that must not be touched

1. **The stairs, chapter 5.** The hand on the post, Inguma coming down past Coralie's ankle, "Are you coming up?"
2. **The cross on the pad, chapter 7.** She keeps Lesca out for two nights, the river stands still, and her prophecy is marked wrong in front of the man it was meant to save.
3. **The call log, chapter 10.** "Outgoing. Castagnet J., mobile. Not answered. 02:51." and "Because you'd have told me."
4. **Chapter 13 from the glass doors to the last line.** Coralie counting the chairs before she looks at Maylis. "Don't tell me what he was." Kévin's five fingers at 4 a.m. and his "I wanted six" in 16. The full half minute on the wrist. "Forty-one."
5. **The confession, chapter 14.** "Then I opened the gate, and I drowned him", the sentence travelling back through the square at walking pace, and "Somebody has to stack them."
