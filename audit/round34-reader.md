## 3. The ten faults, worst first

**1. Chapters 5 to 11: the same refusal four times, and six shows of hands.**
"'That gate moves when I say so.'" (6) "'Four to three,' Larrère said. 'It stays shut.'" (8) "'The association owns it, and its president says it stays shut.'" (11)
The book counts hands in 3, 4, 6, 8, 8 again at the oven, and 15. By the vote in 8 I was reading the result and skipping the count. Worse, chapter 7 changes the problem: the gate no longer matters if Lesca stays out. The next public scene is another vote on the gate.
*Do:* let Larrère refuse once in public, at the card table in 8, where Jeannot's hand decides it. Cut his refusal in 5 to the window going down and the threat to her file. Cut the scene in 11 to the order ("touch nothing until I ring") and the back lane. Cut one of the two votes in 3. This is cutting, not rebuilding.

**2. Chapter 11: the handle comes off and I am not told why.**
"'Either,' Maylis said." Then: "She lifted the handle off. The catch let go of its one tooth, and the chain went loose and sank back into the water."
This is the hinge of the book. It is daylight, the handle is on, and Jeannot is above ground, walking to his van. In 8 she said "Open" twice with the folder against the glass. In 9 she moved the trapdoor to the top of her list. Kévin has just told her not to keep the gate shut for him. Then one word, and she locks the box, with nothing from inside her. Madame Darrigrand's charge in 16 depends on my believing this minute. I believed the word. I did not believe the hand.
*Do:* give two or three sentences of what she is afraid of with her weight on the handle: the car a hundred metres off, a legal vote, a man's hut, and nothing to show for it but a dream. Then the handle comes off.

**3. Chapter 6 is three chapters.**
"His mother, Marie-Jo, had been Lesca's deputy mayor until a night in 2018, when she handed her sash back across the council table in a plastic bag."
Resignation, complaint, nets, back lane, the crossing, the stone, Kévin's cap, the minute's silence, the file, the wait, the soup, the council, and a cut to the other bedroom. This is where I was most lost. The paragraph quoted hands me two people's histories in the other village in five lines.
*Do:* break the chapter at the crossing. Cut the bedside insert ("In the other village it was raining"): chapter 7 opens on the same chart and does the job. Give Marie-Jo's sash its plastic bag in the council scene, where she is talking to the wall.

**4. The night of 2014 is told six times.**
"Before she fitted the handle to the gate, she turned the van around on top of the bank so that its headlights pointed down the track across the flood fields." (9)
The prologue has this, almost word for word. Then the file in 6, the whole night in 9, the phone in 10, "You rang once" in 12, and the microphone in 14. And in 9 it arrives by itself: "the night of the flood came to Maylis anyway". She does nothing to get it.
*Do:* keep in 9 only what the prologue held back: Coralie's car in the yard, the reflector, the four minutes it goes on shining, the boot. Cut the paragraph that begins "After that a prefect pinned a medal", which I have had since 3. Have Maylis sit down in the cold chair on purpose to take it.

**5. Chapter 10 rehearses a scene I am about to watch.**
"He read it to her off his sheet, from the anthem to the photograph."
Chapter 9 ends on "before dark". I want Friday. I get a walk through the ceremony that chapter 14 will show me.
*Do:* keep Darrigrand's step and Coralie's eight strips of tape. Cut the point of order about the kiss and the medal. Go to the landing sooner.

**6. Chapter 12: the hall of the dead has no rule I can follow.**
"Inguma hadn't taken her home. She had held the wheel against it for a whole ring, and this was where that put her."
She had already let go. She arrives anyway, sits, stands, sends her apologies and leaves. I laughed at "apologies received". I did not know what it cost her, or whether the seat that "Twelve" then fills was filled because she left it. Chapter 15 says the river did it. Chapter 14 let me think the chair did.
*Do:* one sentence in the hall saying what holding on has done to her, and one that separates her empty chair from the chair pulled in at "Twelve".

**7. The turn is worked out in silence.**
"She had nobody to say it to and no mouth to say it with." (7)
Maylis sees it at the marker post. I am given two readings of the post and left to do the sum, a page before the funniest scene in the book.
*Do:* let her finish the thought at the post in one plain sentence. The bar scene is then the first time it is said aloud, and the joke is safe.

**8. Chapter 3: the deduction by file.**
"*25 November 2014. Under the line. Rain.*"
Five lines that differ by a date, then "Tuesday had been the 24th", then a sentence about what she had assumed that morning, which I had not been told she assumed.
*Do:* two file lines, not five. Cut "Ever since she stood at the stone that morning, she had taken the other village for the lucky one." "That village hadn't had its flood yet" carries it alone.

**9. Chapter 1: the water before I care about it.**
"'On Monday the river was low. You put the gate up, and my water ran out through it into the river. Last night I sat over mud.'"
On page 4 I do not know what a duck hunter wants with a hand of water or which way a gate lets it run. The prologue gave me the bank. This asks for the tide as well.
*Do:* cut Jeannot's points one and two to a single line about ducks needing standing water. Keep point three, which is about Larrère.

**10. Maylis, Maïté, Marie-Jo, Madame Etcheto, Madame le maire.**
"Marie-Jo Labat turned a white kitchen timer to three" and, eight lines on, "Maïté Castagnet, Jean's widow, held her handbag".
Two of them are introduced in one scene, at one table, and I swapped them once.
*Do:* change one of the two first names.
