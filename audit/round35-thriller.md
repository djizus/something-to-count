# Round 35 — Thriller

Read: `manuscript/00.md` to `16.md`, in order, in full. Nothing else.

## 1. Scores

- **Stakes: 7.** "Forty houses, forty-two beds, a car in the yard of the care home and a van at the end of a track." (10) Countable and personal. Held at 7 because the only human threat in the book tops out at "That's prison, for eels" (6), and the supernatural one is walked away from (12).
- **Tension: 7.** At its best: "Lesca took the body up one stair. Maylis's arm pulled it back down." (5) At its worst the hardest fight in the book is a summary: "She pushed until the church clock struck 4, when Inguma got down and left the way it had come." (7)
- **Progression: 8.** "Her mayor got Friday the 4th wrong. That's on the pad, with a cross by it. So I go by the marker post" (8). Maylis's one win is what loses her the vote. Every chapter changes the question.
- **Comedy: 8.** "Either is not a person. I cannot summon Either to the station." (6)
- **Characters: 8.** "I don't get into a bed over somebody who can't speak. I've lifted too many of them." (7)
- **Switch between dark and comic: 8.** "'Nineteen,' Madame Etcheto said. 'That's not many.' This time nobody called out." (11)
- **Prose: 7.** "The street lamps of the Port went out one after another, starting at the bank end." (13) Against it, lines that explain what the scene has just shown: "This was the same thing, done with a steering wheel." (12)
- **The ending: 8.** "I know what you picked. I was the other one." (16)
- **Clarity: 6.** The rule the last third runs on is announced, not found: "One night matters. If I'm in your village at 2:51 on Friday night, your bank goes, as mine was going. If I'm not, it holds." (8) By then the reader is carrying nine rules, and the minutes of the last night do not agree with the minutes of the trial walk.

**Finished unpaid?** Yes. From 5 onward I did not consider stopping.
**Press it on a friend?** On one who reads Crouch for the idea, yes. On one who reads him for speed, no: that reader is at risk in 1, 4 and 10.

## 2. Against the comparisons

**Below Crouch on sustained jeopardy, level on hooks and speed, above on what the ending costs.** Net: just under the bar.

- **Level: speed of premise and chapter endings.** The thing is on the wardrobe in line 5, the double speaks at the end of 2, the other village and the date arrive in 3. Crouch has Jason taken in chapter 1 and across by chapter 3; this keeps pace. Eleven of the seventeen endings are true hooks, and "Are you coming up?" (5) is better than most of his.
- **Below: the reader is safe too often.** A Crouch chapter never leaves the viewpoint character out of danger. Here whole scenes risk nothing but embarrassment (the chrysanthemums in 4, the stamps in 8, the tape and the kiss in 10). Crouch also keeps the usurper in the house to the last act. This book retires that fear at the top of 8 ("what I'd have had is a river over forty-two beds") and lives on the flood from there. And his box has one set of rules learned by trial; these are handed over at a meeting and added to by decree.
- **Above: the marriage and the price.** Crouch's wife is the prize. Here she prefers the double and says so ("I'd have this one again"), then refuses her on principle, then leaves the original. And where Dark Matter and Recursion both escape their endings, this one makes the heroine's best act "fetching a man out of a dry hole" and takes the wife for it. The Good Place did the trolley problem as a joke; this book does it with a real handle and finds the true motive: "She wanted it not to be her."

## 3. The ten faults, worst first

**1. The book's real choice is played in twelve lines and decided by a tic (11).**
"'Either,' Maylis said. 'Then it's shut,' Jeannot said. 'That's how the vote left it.' He was off the platform and down the steps before she could take it back."
At 5 p.m. it is daylight, the handle is on, Jeannot is above ground beside her. Chapter 16 names this as the guilty moment ("You had the key on your belt then"). But nothing on the page stops her hand except the word, and by then Kévin has already released her ("Don't keep a gate shut for me"), so the reader thinks: turn it. If that beat is not believed, the whole night is avoidable.
*Do:* make it the centre of the chapter. Run the handle to eighteen, the gate a hand up and water moving towards his hut. Put the reasons not to turn in play at that moment: Larrère's folder a hundred metres off, the nineteenth turn with one arm, the chance the bank holds if she keeps Lesca out. Move Kévin's release to after dusk, when it is too late to use. Let "Either" come last, after other words have failed. Cut "before she could take it back", which excuses her.

**2. The endgame rule arrives by assertion and is cancelled in a clause (8, 13).**
"If I'm not, it holds." Nothing lets Lesca know that; the no-lie rule proves she believes it, not that it is so. Then chapter 12's whole victory is undone in passing: "She had put Lesca out, and it had come too late: the river was through the bank already."
*Do:* let "it holds" stick in her mouth. She cannot know it, so she cannot say it. Maylis then goes into Friday on a maybe, which also gives fault 1 its honest reason: opening the gate at 5 puts a river on three hundred hectares "for a flood that hasn't come", Larrère's own line. In 13, cut the explaining sentence; the clay moving under her palm says it.

**3. The minutes of the last night contradict the trial walk (7, 13).**
In 7: "Fifteen" at the dip, "Twenty-five" at the trapdoor, and "Ten was the time it took to hook the trailer up." The reader is made to do that sum twice. In 13, from the same dip: "The bank had six minutes left. She could be at the handle inside those six minutes." She then covers 400 metres of flooded track in four minutes on "a third of each breath", and 400 back in three on a quarter. The "no both" the book stands on is 25 minutes on Saturday and about 10 on Friday.
*Do:* fix one end. Either lower the trial ("Nine" and "Fifteen" against ten still makes no both), or say on Saturday that this was a walk and what a run would cost that rib. Same chapter, same kind of gap: she says "Start at the bank end" and room twelve is the one left. One line from Nadine (nine to eleven were in their chairs, twelve was in her bed) closes it.

**4. Maylis's only win before the last night is summarised (7).**
"On Thursday night she took a blanket down to the kitchen … She pushed until 4 again." Two nights, about 140 words. This is the passive heroine's first act of will, and what it buys is the midpoint turn. The turn then has to be worked out from a marker post ("Yesterday Bordenave had found a metre there, and his cellar had been dry").
*Do:* play one of the two nights whole, breath by breath to the stroke of 4. Let her see the dry boards as the thing she paid for.

**5. Defying Inguma, promised as fatal, costs Lesca nothing (12).**
"'The mayor of Peyrebarthe sends her apologies,' … She opened them with the other hand. Then she stood up straight and walked." The card with her name, "Nil", and "The one it cannot take home dies in her own bed" all say the seat is death. She sits in it, makes a joke of minutes, and leaves. The line is funny and it lets the air out of the one supernatural stake.
*Do:* make leaving a debt she hears incurred. Bring "Twelve. One, two. Somebody pulled a chair in to a table" from the top of 14 to the end of 12, behind her as she walks, and have her not turn round. The reader then goes into 13 knowing a seat has been filled and by which door.

**6. The clock that makes 6 the tensest chapter is not applied to Lesca (5, 6, 8).**
Maylis, thirteen hours away: "It had been 64 at 6" down to 34 and a doctor's number. Lesca drives from 6:10 a.m. to 9:05 p.m. in 5 with no cost shown, then stays "Since 6 yesterday" in 8 with a cold hand reported afterwards.
*Do:* one rule for both. Show the left hand failing during her days ("had to tell the left hand twice" is the seed, plant it in 5 and 7). Then "office hours" has a limit, every visit is a countdown, and "Nil" is a decision the reader has watched coming.

**7. The human antagonist cannot frighten, and his one lever is spent early (6, 8, 11).**
"That's prison, for eels." Six months, for a man who volunteers for them at 11 in the morning. After that the folder against the glass and the hand laid flat on it are threats the book has already let out.
*Do:* with fault 1, hold Kévin's release back. Until it comes, what is under the letter should be worth obeying.

**8. Friday stalls in the other village (10).**
"'A point of order,' Lesca said. 'You have the flowers as one item and the kiss as the next. They're one item.'" About a thousand words of commemoration logistics on the day of the flood, before Coralie says "2:51".
*Do:* keep Darrigrand's step and the eight strips ("The others have died since then, in those beds, of the things people die of"). Cut Arlette to four lines and the sheet-reading to nothing. Get to the landing.

**9. The first put-down point is page three (1).**
"The fields were made to take the river through that gate when it ran too high and to give it back on the next tide. The gate belonged to the landowners' association, but Maylis kept the key." Between the thing on the wardrobe and the kitchen sit seventy lines of gate, association, registered post and duck water.
*Do:* keep the postman at the window; it founds the comedy. Cut the hydrology to two sentences and let Jeannot's three points carry the fields.

**10. One device decides four contests, and one formula ends three sections.**
"On the first breath the rib caught and held" (7), "the rib gave Maylis one breath" (8), "she had no push to give it at all" (8), "On the sixth ring the rib caught and held" (11). And: "Maylis hadn't told it to." "Maylis hadn't said it." (2) "She hadn't told them to." (4)
*Do:* let her lose a different way each time. Once by choice is already there (Sunday in 4); once by sleep; keep the rib for the last night only.

## 4. Five things that must not be touched

1. **One ring against nine.** "It rang once." (00) and "The phone rang nine times. 'Who's dead?' said Jeannot." (9)
2. **The stair post (5),** to "Are you coming up?"
3. **The chain from the cross to the vote.** She keeps Lesca out, the river stops, the prophecy fails, Jeannot votes "Shut." (7, 8)
4. **The dip (13).** Three steps back towards the lamp, then "She wanted it not to be her."
5. **Room twelve to the hall (13).** The pulse taken for the full half minute, "There was a hatbox on it. Nothing else.", "Forty-one."
