# Critic report: Characters

Read: `manuscript/00.md` to `16.md`, in order, in full. Nothing else.

## 1. Scores

- **Stakes: 8.** "'The gate, or Castagnet? Say which, Maylis. For once.'" (13). The public stake and the private one are the same sentence.
- **Tension: 7.5.** "Each time she came back in, she looked down the corridor before she looked for a chair." (13). Chapters 11 to 13 are as tight as Crouch. Chapters 6 to 8 let go of the rope three times for a committee.
- **Progression: 7.** "'So that's a fourth point, and I didn't have it on my list.'" (4). The rules arrive in instalments, and a new one is still arriving in chapter 8. Each instalment costs a scene that the people are not moving in.
- **Comedy: 8.** "'Either is not a person. I cannot summon Either to the station.'" (6).
- **Characters: 7.5.** "'I wanted six, Maylis. I've never wanted anything so much.'" (16). The ensemble is an 8.5, Coralie an 8.5, Lesca an 8, Maylis a 6.5 at the one moment she has to be a 9.
- **The switch between dark and comic: 8.5.** "'It's Saturday.' 'Then they'll want the bins out,' he said." (13). The comic tics keep running through the flood and the flood is worse for it.
- **Prose: 7.5.** "She heard the passage, then the front door, then the car door shutting on a pillow." (16).
- **The ending: 8.** "'It's four pages, and it doesn't thank you.'" (16). Maylis's ending costs her everything it should. Lesca's costs her less than she owes (fault 2).
- **Clarity: 7.** "Lesca hadn't finished the words, so Inguma had nothing to count and would take her home." (7). At the bingo I had to stop and work out who wanted the thing to come for whom.

Would I have finished it unpaid? Yes. Would I press it on a friend? On a friend who loves The Good Place, yes. On a Crouch reader I would say "wait for chapter 9".

## 2. Against the comparisons

**Above Crouch on characters.**

- The double is a person. Crouch's second Jason is a thief with a motive. Lesca is a thief with a motive, a motion written in her own hand and a room she cannot lie in: "'To stay,' she said. 'That's the one that comes out.'" (8).
- The wife refuses to be the prize. Daniela is what the Jasons fight over. Coralie stops two steps across the landing and says "'I don't get into a bed over somebody who can't speak. I've lifted too many of them.'" (7). Crouch has no scene like it.
- The village. Crouch's supporting cast carries plot. Here Jeannot, Madame Etcheto, Sarthou, Kévin, Daguerre and Darrigrand each exist for a line nobody else could say.

**Below The Good Place, by one person and one relationship.**

- The ensemble is level. Everyone is wrong in their own way: Jeannot is right in forty-one letters and wrong about the river, Sarthou bills the village for his own sandbags, Josiane has "only the one name wrong".
- Maylis is Chidi without the talk. Chidi's indecision is agony the viewer is inside. Maylis's is a word, and at her hinge (fault 1) the word stands where she should.
- The Good Place is carried by people making each other better. The two Maylises only wrestle and part. The book has prepared the one thing each could give the other and neither gives it (fault 3).

## 3. The ten faults that most hold it back

**1. Chapter 11. The real choice is made by the catchword, with nobody behind it.**
"'Which is it going to be? Shut, or open?' / The river went by under the boards. / 'Either,' Maylis said." Then: "He was off the platform and down the steps before she could take it back."
This is the moment the village judges her on: "'At 5 on Friday it was light, and Castagnet was up on the bank at your van window... You had the key on your belt then.'" (16). It is daylight, the handle is fitted, Jeannot is above ground. It is the only point where both was possible. The narration is further outside her here than anywhere in the book, and "before she could take it back" turns it into a reflex, which excuses her. Chapter 5 has already called it "the only word she had". If it is a reflex she is not responsible, and chapter 16 says she is.
Do: rebuild the beat as a decision. Cut "before she could take it back". Give her the seconds after the word in which Jeannot is still on the bank and the handle is still on, and show what she is looking at when she lifts it off: Larrère's car a hundred metres away, the folder, Kévin's name. Let her choose the shut gate with her eyes open. Then "You had the key" lands on a person.

**2. Chapters 10 and 15. Lesca pays at home for the smaller act, and her Coralie gives her name back without knowing the larger one.**
"She had told her it was for Castagnet." (10). The motion said: "*and take the other one's bed, and Coralie Mounet in it.*" (3). The ending: "'Good night, Maylis,' she said, and went into the spare room." (15).
The square hears about 2014, which the book has shown to be defensible. What Lesca did in 2026 is not: she took a river over forty-two beds to get into another woman's bed, and she knew it by chapter 8. That is confessed in six lines to Madame Etcheto and never to the woman it was done against. Her Coralie forgives on half the account. That is softening.
Do: the motion is a sheet of town hall paper on Lesca's kitchen table, and it is never seen again. This Coralie already reads what is left on that table ("You left this on the kitchen table, and I've read it"). Let her find it before the last scene of 15. Then let her decide what to call the woman four steps away. If it is still "Maylis", it is worth twice as much.

**3. Chapters 6 and 16. The two selves never exchange the one thing each learned.**
In Lesca's kitchen Maylis hears: "'Do you know what I'd give for a woman at this table who sat down when she was told? I'd carry my pillow back across that landing tonight.'" Lesca's hand goes for Coralie's, and "Maylis put the left hand over it and held it there. / 'It's good soup,' Maylis said." Maylis stops the other woman's reconciliation with her own silence. Nobody ever mentions it again. Lesca, for her part, learns that Maylis's Coralie wants an answer, and never says so.
The last meeting has room for it and spends it on a tally: "'Is yours in the house?' ... 'Across the landing. Four steps. Yours?' / 'She took the pillow.'"
This is the premise's relationship, and it pays only as a truce. Do: in the last chair scene, Maylis owns what her hand did over the soup and hands Lesca the sentence she heard. Lesca has nothing to hand back that is not too late. That asymmetry is the ending, and it is one exchange long.

**4. Chapters 1 to 16. Coralie states the diagnosis six times in the same terms.**
"'Sixteen years, and I've had one straight answer out of you'" (1). "'You've never once asked me for anything.'" (3). "'I've waited a long time to be asked for one thing in this house.'" (4). "'I've had sixteen and a shopping pad.'" (5). "'I've had sixteen years of her saying nothing'" (7). "'I've asked you one question for sixteen years, and drawn you boxes for it'" (16).
By chapter 4 the reader has it. After that Coralie is the book's spokeswoman on Maylis, and Maylis is described where she should be seen. The pad already does this work without a word.
Do: keep the first and the last. In between let the boxes speak, and let Coralie talk about what she wants. Her Bayonne speech in 7 is where she comes most alive.

**5. Chapters 7, 13 and 16. The book threatens Maylis's life and does not collect.**
"'Push on it and it goes through into the lung.'" (7). "Something gave in her side. She heard it go." (13). Then she runs six hundred metres, wades to her hips, pushes a dozen wheelchairs, and on Sunday the woman who made the lung speech says "'Then you'll dig it. With that side. I'll strap it first.'" and leaves.
It lowers Maylis's cost and it cracks Coralie, who would not walk out on that side.
Do: collect or downgrade. The cheap way to collect: pink in the sink again on Sunday, hidden again, and Coralie's hand that "stayed on it a little longer than a bandage needs" becomes her knowing.

**6. Chapters 8 and 9. The father is the source of Either and the book does not notice.**
"'For thirty years he stopped four turns short, to leave a man his water, and he never rang me once.'" (9). That is the original both: a gate raised and not raised, so nobody is refused. It comes from Jeannot mid-rant and Maylis hears nothing in it. Everyone quotes her father at her, and in the hall of the dead "He marked a number. He didn't look up, and she took the hand away." (8). He is never alive on the page for one line.
Do: one beat in chapter 9, after the call, where she counts the four turns and knows whose habit she has. No flashback needed.

**7. Chapters 4 and 16. In Maylis's village Maïté is a set of clues.**
"'That's winter things,' she said. 'In the— It's blankets.'" (4). "She had the fare ready in her glove, in coins, but it wasn't enough." (16).
Two bowls, a folder under the bread, a strapped suitcase, a bus. I can solve her and I cannot say what she wants: to leave Jeannot, to leave Larrère, or to leave the street. In Lesca's village she is a person in one word ("*Left*"). Here the broken sentences stand in for her.
Do: let her finish one sentence in chapter 4, about the suitcase or about him.

**8. Chapters 10, 13 and 14. Three women refuse to know in the same sentence.**
Lesca's Coralie: "'I've never wanted to hear which one it was. Don't tell me now.'" (10). Arlette: "'I'm not asking where'" (10) and "'And I'm not asking you why... I don't want to be told.'" (14). Maylis's Coralie: "'Don't tell me what he was.'" (13).
In chapter 10 it is the whole scene and it is the best in the book. Lent to the mother twice, it thins. Arlette's own voice is the opposite: she over-tells ("the phone's in the passage, on the tiles").
Do: give Arlette a refusal in her own manner in 14, long and beside the point.

**9. Chapters 8 and 16. Maylis's mother lives across the yard and vanishes.**
"Her mother lived on the other side of the yard and kept her ponies on the flood fields." (8). After the flood Maylis loses her job and her partner in one Sunday and the yard is empty. Lesca's Arlette gets three scenes. This one gets a joke about stamps.
Do: one line of her in 16. A light on across the yard that Maylis does not cross to would be enough, and would be in character for both.

**10. Chapters 9 and 13. Nadine holds the list at the climax and is nobody.**
"'Ask Nadine. I only lift them.'" (13). She runs the corridor for the worst twenty minutes of the book with Coralie's list round her neck and has no trait. Bixente at least has the mop and the Basque.
Do: one line of her in chapter 9, where Coralie has only Bixente to talk to.

## 4. Five things that must not be touched

1. **Chapter 10, the landing.** "'Why not?' / 'Because you'd have told me.'" The whole scene, including that she never touches her.
2. **The oven door, three times.** Chapters 1 and 8 whole, and chapter 16 breaking on "'The—'". It is the marriage, and it is the cleanest switch from comic to dark in the book.
3. **Kévin's hand.** "'Five,' he said." in the yard and "'I wanted six'" on the van step.
4. **Jeannot's six points down the phone and letter forty-two.** "'And six. Bugger off.'" "'I was in no danger, and I'll say so to anyone.'" He is never softened into gratitude.
5. **"She wanted it not to be her."** (13), with "'I waited for the river to decide, so that it wouldn't be me.'" (16), and the fact that the walk saves nobody. This is Maylis unexcused. Fault 1 asks for the same honesty four chapters earlier.

Answers the brief asks for, not covered above:

- **Distinct?** Yes, down to how each one thinks. Lesca decides in motions ("the only form she had for it was a motion"). Maylis measures in trailers ("Twenty-five minutes was a trailer of leaves to the dump and back").
- **Described from outside as a woman and not as a person?** Neither, anywhere. They are described by work, hands and what they will not say.
- **Who would be missed?** Jeannot, Madame Etcheto, Kévin, Sarthou, Daguerre, Darrigrand with his step. Not Nadine, Bixente, the dealer or the deputy mayor.
- **Which relationship carries it?** Maylis and Coralie, and it pays, in loss.
- **Does the reader care who lives?** Yes. I was afraid for Coralie behind the fire door, and room twelve hurt.
