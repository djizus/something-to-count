# Round 36: Reader

Read: `manuscript/00.md` to `16.md`, in order, in full. Nothing else opened but the brief.

## 1. Scores

- **Stakes: 8.** "'I'd have this one again.'" By chapter 5 I was afraid of a bowl of soup: that Coralie would prefer the visitor, and be right to.
- **Tension: 7.** "Then the screen said 01:12. She hadn't seen it change." The night the whole book points at goes by in a paragraph, and chapters 6 and 8 wait for a date I was given in chapter 3.
- **Progression: 7.** "That village hadn't had its flood yet." A turn like that comes in every chapter up to 5 and from 9 on. In 6 and 8 the situation at the end is the situation at the start with one more sheet of paper in it.
- **Comedy: 8.** "'Either is not a person. I cannot summon Either to the station.'" I smiled from the postman in chapter 1 to the switchboard at Hontaube in chapter 11.
- **Characters: 8.** "'I wanted six, Maylis. I've never wanted anything so much.'"
- **The switch between dark and comic: 9.** "'Here,' he said. 'I know you are.'" That is said in a corridor filling with river, and it made the corridor worse.
- **Prose: 7.** "She watched her sentence travel back through the square and down the road, row by row, at walking pace." It reads fast. The cost is the grammar of the shared body ("the mouth", "the eyes", "Lesca walked her down"), which I had to translate for most of chapter 5.
- **The ending: 8.** "then the car door shutting on a pillow."
- **Clarity: 6.** "'One night matters. If I'm in your village at 2:51 on Friday night, your bank goes, as mine was going. If I'm not, it holds.'" I read that three times and could not have explained it to a friend.

**Finished unpaid?** Yes. I slowed in 6 and 8 and did not stop. From the last page of 11 to the medal in 14 I did not get up.

**Press it on a friend?** Yes, with a warning: stay with it until the old woman in room twelve.

## 2. Against the comparisons

**Just short of the bar.** Level with both comparisons on people and on the switch from funny to terrible. Below both on ease. Faults 1 to 4 below are what stand between this draft and "as good".

- **Below Crouch on ease.** Dark Matter runs on one rule: the box opens on the world your state of mind picks. A Crouch reader never reads a paragraph twice. Here I counted nine rules: lie still and you are in the chair; stay sitting and you wake in her body; the owner keeps the right hand; Inguma fetches the visitor when it has finished counting; nobody standing in the room can lie; the chair passes memories; the body left behind runs down; the visitor brings her river; the one it can't take home joins the dead at bingo. Each is fun when it arrives. Together they are a load, and the two the ending depends on (the river and the dead) are the two I held least well.
- **Below The Good Place on the reversal.** "This is the Bad Place" is one line nobody can miss, and it re-reads a whole season. The equal line here is "'You're the water.'" When it was said I did not know what it meant.
- **Level with Crouch on chapter ends.** "'Come back across,' her mouth said. Maylis hadn't said it." "'Are you coming up?'" "'Forty-one,' Coralie said." Each of those turned the page for me.
- **Above Crouch on people.** His secondary characters serve the plot and leave with it. I will miss Madame Etcheto. Level with The Good Place on everyone being wrong in their own way: Larrère hides in procedure, Jeannot in numbered points, Josiane has the right house and the wrong name, Maylis has Either, Lesca has motions.
- **Level with The Good Place on the moral problem, and it costs more.** It is the trolley problem run twice: Lesca pulled the lever and killed one, Maylis would not pull it and killed one. The second death is the person I liked best, and the man who was saved was "a man in a dry hole".

**What I did not understand**

- The Friday rule (fault 2).
- What Lesca did by refusing her seat among the dead (fault 5).
- Why Larrère ordered the gate up on the Monday (fault 6).
- Why Lesca cannot say "Either" to Larrère's window (fault 8).
- What the card that says *left* was for (fault 9).
- "'It takes a small m.'" I don't know why a small m is right or why it is funny.

**Where I was bored, lost or skimming**

- Skimmed: the first two pages of 6 (the dictated letter, the complaint, the poster about nets); Arlette at the tap in 8.
- Lost: one page at the bar in 7. I did not know why Maylis needed the mouth shut, so I did not know that Madame Etcheto finishing the words was a disaster.
- Bored: nowhere for longer than a page.

**Where I could not stop**

The last page of 2. All of 5. The phone call in 9. Chapter 11 from "'Either,' Maylis said" to the end, then 12, 13, and 14 as far as the medal.

**What I will remember in a month**

The oven door. "Staff to wait. Staff not to join in." The dead playing bingo with a card laid for her. Larrère's window going all the way down. Jeannot's six points at 5 in the morning. Kévin counting five fingers in his own headlights. "Here." The sentence about drowning him going back through the square. Jeannot stepping over the string with the second shovel.

**What I will have forgotten:** the rules, the association, and who complained about whom.

## 3. The ten faults that most hold it back

**1. The turn is found in silence, said as a riddle and explained by a chart (chapters 7 and 8).**
"Maylis read it with her. Yesterday Bordenave had found a metre there, and his cellar had been dry. / She had nobody to say it to and no mouth to say it with."
This is where the book turns over: the woman who came to stop the flood is the flood. I read straight past it. At the bar I heard "You're the water" exactly as the hall heard it. I got it the next morning, from three columns headed "*What. Since when. How do you know.*"
What I would do: let Maylis finish the thought on the platform, in two plain sentences, so that I walk into the bingo knowing. Then the hall's mistake is funny because I am ahead of it, Madame Etcheto's kindness is a disaster I can see coming, and breakfast is Coralie testing a thing I already believe.

**2. The Friday rule is foggy, and the climax blurs it further (chapters 8, 12, 13).**
"'Keeping me out for a night or two changes nothing, because the river catches up the day I'm back.'" Then: "She had put Lesca out, and it had come too late: the river was through the bank already."
The last five chapters stand on this rule. It is the only reason Maylis sits in a van for nine hours and does not open the gate at dusk. If I have not taken it in, she looks stupid from 5 until 2:51. It arrives as a statement with no reason behind it. Then Lesca is in for one minute, Maylis throws her out, and I can't tell whether that did anything. The bank held 16 minutes where the file gave it 10, and nobody says whether those 6 were hers.
What I would do: say the rule once, in Maylis's words, where she finds it (see fault 1). In 13, one line that settles what throwing Lesca out bought.

**3. Chapters 6 and 8 run on paper and votes.**
"'Three and three and Castagnet. She's got you at her counter saying open, twice.'"
That is Arlette telling me a scene I had finished two minutes before. Chapter 6 opens with an undated resignation, a blank sheet, a complaint form and a poster. Chapter 8 has a petition, a vote, a report of the vote and a job list. The association's vote was the fourth vote in the book. By then I could not have said which sheet in Larrère's folder threatened whom.
What I would do: cut Arlette at the tap. In 6, reach the crossing by the second page, because the day as mayor is what the chapter is for. Keep of the complaint only what later chapters use: Sarthou's line about Either and Jeannot's "'That's how Larrère asks for a thing.'"

**4. The vigil is spent in a paragraph and defused before it starts (chapter 11).**
"'The thing that sits on you doesn't need you asleep,' Kévin said."
Staying awake until 4 is the simplest thing in the book to be afraid about. I was told at dusk that it would not work, and then given the night in 150 words.
What I would do: cut Kévin's reminder. Chapter 2 already made it fair. Then Inguma landing on the roof, with her wide awake and five fingers counted, is a shock. And give me one page in the van between 11 and 2:49, with the handle fitted behind her and the reflector at the end of the track.

**5. What Lesca's refusal cost is hidden in a bingo call (chapters 12, 14, 15).**
"'Twelve. One, two.' / Somebody pulled a chair in to a table."
Chapter 2 gave me a rule that frightened me: "The one it cannot take home dies in her own bed." Lesca breaks it by sending her apologies and wakes up in a hospital corridor. Either she got out free, which is cheap, or Madame Etcheto sat down in her chair, which is the darkest thing in the book. I think it is the second. I am not sure.
What I would do: make it sure. The voice has been calling "Lesca, Maylis" since chapter 12. After the chair is pulled in, it stops.

**6. The first order in the book has no reason (chapters 1 and 6).**
"'He had that gate raised to empty my water. That's his answer to letter forty.'"
The cracked rib, the nights on her back and everything after them come from Larrère telling her to raise the gate on a low river. The only reason offered is spite, and spite cuts against a man who needs Jeannot down at his hut every night of the season. Sarthou asks whose order it was and never why.
What I would do: one line. The corn growers wanted Sunday's water off their fields.

**7. Coralie's lecture on the rib is a gun being loaded (chapter 7).**
"'He said his mouth tasted of coins. He coughed, and it came out pink on the sheet.'"
She is walking out with her pillow, angry, and stops in the doorway to describe a death, symptom by symptom. Each symptom then turns up on schedule.
What I would do: two details, given as an order ("If your mouth tastes of coins, you wake me"). The sink does the rest.

**8. Lesca can't say "Either" in the square (chapter 5).**
"'Ei—' Lesca said. 'You have three minutes.'"
Two nights earlier I learned that "standing up in this room, a woman can't say a thing she knows isn't true", shown by a word that breaks. Here a word breaks in the open air. I spent the café scene waiting to learn that the rule had got loose in the village. It hadn't.
What I would do: don't break the word. She opens the mouth for it and chooses not to.

**9. The card does nothing (chapters 2, 13, 16).**
"When she took the phone out of her chest pocket to see the time, the card from the steel room came out with it. She pushed the card back in."
I am shown it three times.
What I would do: give it a job in the last scene in the steel room, or drop the two later mentions.

**10. The aftermath steps backwards (chapters 15 and 16).**
"At first light on Sunday Maylis walked down into the Port."
Chapter 15 ends on Tuesday night. The book is strict about its calendar everywhere else, and this is the one place I turned back to check.
What I would do: put the date at the head of 16, as chapter 1 does.

## 4. Five things that must not be touched

1. **"I'll be brief."** The window, the oven door, the soup that "will be consulted", his silence at the garden gate, and Coralie bent at the oven in 16, unable to finish it.
2. **Madame Etcheto, whole.** The care plan, the counting, "That's my door", the duck on her knees, and that she is the one who dies. The hatbox on the wardrobe.
3. **Chapter 13 from the dip to "Forty-one".** The chairs, "Then they'll want the bins out", Kévin's five fingers, the line through the last name.
4. **The sentence at the microphone and what the square does with it.** The deputy turning the sheet over to see if there is more on the back. "A child asked what the lady had done. Somebody told the child."
5. **Jeannot.** The numbered points, "Jean's for the stone", nine rings against one, letter 42 that "doesn't thank you", the second shovel.
