# Round 37: Reader

Read 00 to 17 in order, in full, with nothing else open.

## 1. Scores

- **Stakes: 8.** "The trapdoor was twenty-five minutes away, on foot, in the dark, with the dip under water. The bank had ten." (10) I knew what could be lost and who would lose it.
- **Tension: 7.** "On the sixth ring the breath wouldn't come." (12) Chapters 10 to 14 are a 9. Chapters 6 to 9 are a 6, because I was doing bookkeeping.
- **Progression: 7.** "That village hadn't had its flood yet." (3) Three real turns (3, 7, 11). Between them the same night, chair and morning come round too often.
- **Comedy: 8.** "Eggs is what Either gets in this house." (1) I smiled from the registered letter to the stairs, and again at the bingo.
- **Characters: 8.** "I've held hands for a living since I was nineteen, so I know when a hand is holding on, and I know when it wants to be let go." (4)
- **The switch between dark and comic: 8.** "'Then they'll want the bins out,' he said." (14) Said in a flooded corridor, and it made the corridor worse.
- **Prose: 8.** "She watched her sentence travel back through the square and down the road, row by row, at walking pace." (15)
- **The ending: 8.** "'I know what you picked. I was the other one.'" (17)
- **Clarity: 6.** "'The count tells it when to take the visitor home,' Lesca said. 'It doesn't keep it off.'" (9) A rule corrected one chapter before the last day.

Would I have finished it unpaid? Yes. Would I press it on a friend? Yes, and I would tell them that 6 to 9 are work.

## 2. Against the comparisons

Overall: just under the bar. About 7.5. Chapters 1 to 5 and 10 to 15 are level with the comparisons. Chapters 6 to 9 are below them.

- **Below, on ease.** Dark Matter has one rule and Recursion has one rule, and a reader can carry either into any scene. I counted eleven here: knowledge shared in the chair, staying seated, the owner's right hand, the count, no lying on your feet, the rain, the one night that matters, the count that does not keep it off, the empty body that cools, the hall of the dead, the push that costs the rib. Chapter 4 delivers four of them as a comic meeting and I had them at once. The other seven come one at a time, some just before the scene that needs them.
- **Level, on chapter ends.** 2, 3, 5, 10, 12 and 13 end the way a Crouch chapter ends. "'Come back across,' her mouth said. Maylis hadn't said it."
- **Level in design, below in delivery, on the reversal.** "You're the water" re-reads the first six chapters the way The Good Place's turn re-reads a season: the rain did start the night she arrived. But the turn is first given as a sum (fault 1).
- **Above, on what stays.** I cannot name a villager from Wayward Pines. In a month I will still have Jeannot's numbered points, Kévin's hand and Coralie's pad.

**What I did not understand**

- Why Lesca brings the water, and exactly which of her visits count.
- What Larrère is guilty of. He rang from the Castagnet kitchen. So?
- Whether Madame Etcheto died because Lesca refused the chair in the hall. I think so. I am not sure.
- What stands on the fields in December that three corn growers vote to protect. Lesca calls it "three hundred hectares of grass".
- "It takes a small m." (5) I do not know why.

**Where I skimmed**

- The jaw at the bar (8) and the hand over the mouth in the kitchen (9).
- The association's vote at the card table (9).
- The rehearsal with the tape, up to the count of eight (11).

**Where I could not stop**

- From the file in 3 to the motion at its end.
- The stairs (5).
- From Lesca's night in 10 through to "Forty-one" in 14, apart from the first half of 11.

**What I will remember in a month**

- Kévin counting five and wanting six.
- The window going all the way down.
- "I drowned him" arriving late from the second loudspeaker.
- The mayor sending her apologies to the dead.
- Jeannot's letter 42 and the second shovel.

## 3. The ten faults, worst first

**1. The book's biggest turn is delivered as arithmetic, and the rule behind it will not hold still.** (7, 9)
"The rain came when Lesca came. When she was kept out, it stopped. A flood was coming on the 11th because Lesca had brought it over with her."
This is the moment that changes what the book is about, and it happens in Maylis's head after "Then she did the sum." I had not noticed two dry days, because the two nights of pushing are told through the rib and the sink and nobody looks at the sky. So I could not get there before her; I was told. Two chapters later the rule is changed: "Keeping me out for a night or two changes nothing, because the river catches up the day I'm back." And it wobbles earlier: on Wednesday Lesca is in her own village with Maylis driving her, yet "At home it was raining." (6)
*Do:* put the dry weather on the page on Thursday and Friday (a dry road under her boots, Josiane's chairs out, Jeannot cursing his mud) so the reader is half a step ahead of the sum. State the rule once, in its final form, and make it about the chair, not about "coming over".

**2. Whose hand is it?** (5, 6, 8, 9, 13)
"Lesca let go of the bowl and grabbed that wrist with the left hand to drag it off. When the wrist wouldn't come, she went for the fingers. She got the little finger up, then the one next to it." (9)
Five chapters ask me to track left hand, right hand, jaw, eyes and legs between two owners, and in 6 the ownership is reversed. The stair post (5), the timer (6) and the handbrake (13) are worth it. The spoon, the coffee tap, the jaw at the bar and the hand over the mouth are the same fight again, and each one stopped me to work out who held what. "At 8 Lesca walked her down through the Port" (5) also leaves "she" unclear for a paragraph.
*Do:* keep the three fights that decide something. Cut the bar and kitchen fights to one move each.

**3. Lesca's price is set, then waived, and the bill goes to someone else too quietly.** (9, 13, 15, 16)
"Inguma hadn't taken her home. She had held the wheel against it for a whole ring, and this was where that put her." (13)
Chapter 9 shows the chair with her name and has her write "Nil". In 13 she lets go of the wheel, lands there anyway, declines the seat and walks out, and Inguma takes her home to a hospital that finds nothing. Then, in the dark: "Somebody pulled a chair in to a table." (15) If that is Madame Etcheto taking the place Lesca refused, it is the darkest thing in the book, and I only suspected it a chapter later. In 13 I was pleased for Lesca. I think I was meant to be, and then to pay for it. The payment did not reach me.
*Do:* have Madame Etcheto say in 2 that a place laid at that table is always filled. Then her walk-out in 13 carries dread, and the chair in 15 needs no explaining in 16.

**4. The hinge of the whole book takes five lines.** (12)
"'Either,' Maylis said. / 'Then it's shut,' Jeannot said. 'That's how the vote left it.'"
At 5 the handle is on, it is daylight, and the man is standing on the bank. This is the one moment when nobody dies. I believed the word, because the book trained it for eleven chapters. But I was shouting at her, and the page gives me only "The river went by under the boards." Madame Darrigrand makes my objection in 17. In 12 I needed to feel what "Open" would cost Maylis in that second: Larrère's car a hundred metres off, the folder, Kévin's six months, being the one who did it.
*Do:* give the platform the three breaths the opening gives the handle.

**5. The middle repeats its shapes.** (6 to 9)
"'Those in favour of the petition.'" (9)
A vote is called six times in the book. There are five meetings in the chair. A word sticks in the throat six times ("It's—", "no—", "Ei—", "ti—", "I w—", "to get—"). Inguma comes down at 2:51 and is pushed or not pushed on eight separate nights. Chapter 9 has seven sections and I skimmed two of them. Each device is good the first time and at its payoff ("To stay", "It came out whole").
*Do:* cut one chair meeting (fold the first one in 9 into the second). Let the association vote run half its length. Tell the three nights of pushing in 7 as one.

**6. Larrère's phone call proves nothing I can name.** (4, 6, 10, 14, 16)
"You stood in my kitchen that night and rang her from my phone, with his van gone down to the fields, and you never told the woman you'd sent to the gate." (16)
The trail is laid with care: the folder on the cupboard, the two bowls, the back lane, the kitchen clock, "Castagnet, house". But the order he gave was to keep the gate shut, which keeps the husband dry, and Lesca saw the van herself. So what did he do? I think he risked forty houses so that he would not be the man who drowned his lover's husband. That would make him the third person in the book who "wanted it not to be her". Nobody says it, and the thread ends with a man saying nothing at a gate.
*Do:* give Maïté the one sentence that names it.

**7. The rib is loaded and never fired.** (7, 14, 17)
"Push on it and it goes through into the lung." (7) Then: "Something gave in her side. She heard it go." (14) Then: "Then you'll dig it. With that side. I'll strap it first." (17)
I was made afraid of the coins and the pink. After the trapdoor she runs, wades, pushes six wheelchairs and two days later cuts turf. No doctor. Lesca wakes on a stretcher in Bayonne having done nothing; Maylis, with the injury, never gets there.
*Do:* let it cost her a night in the same hospital, or take the dead man out of Coralie's warning.

**8. The first half of 11 stands between me and the night.** (11)
"He looked at his sheet. He left the kiss on it."
Chapter 10 ends with the plan changed and me leaning forward. Then come Arlette in the doorway and a rehearsal with tape. The count from eight to forty-two is very good. The rest is where I would have put the book down for the evening. The bedroom scene that follows is the best scene in Lesca's village.
*Do:* cut the rehearsal to the tape, Darrigrand's step and the count. Get to the landing sooner.

**9. Chapter 16 is thin.** (16)
"She took the dial and turned it back to nothing. The bell rang."
After the square, the council vote tells me what I already know will happen, neatly. The chapter's two things worth having are "*Left*" and "Good night, Maylis". Meanwhile I have been kept from Maylis and Coralie for two chapters since "Forty-one".
*Do:* fold the gutter and the landing into the end of 15. Give the council four lines.

**10. Small stumbles.**
"'Little Lesca,' he said. 'You've gone off your head.'" (14)
The narrator has called the mayor "Lesca" since 3, and Jeannot calls Maylis "little Lesca" and, in the last line, "Lesca". One chapter after Lesca was driving, I stopped. The last line is worth it. The one in 14 is not. Also "It takes a small m" (5), and corn against grass on the fields.

## 4. Five things that must not be touched

1. Kévin at 4 a.m.: "'Five,' he said." And in 17: "I wanted six, Maylis. I've never wanted anything so much."
2. The shopping pad, from the three ticked boxes in 1 to "That was Either, with the river in it."
3. Coralie on the bed in 11: "2:51 … I've known that for twelve years." And "Because you'd have told me."
4. The village hall in 14: "The last chair was empty. / 'Forty-one,' Coralie said." With Monsieur Daguerre's "Here" before it.
5. Jeannot whole: the six points down the phone ending "Bugger off", letter 42 that "doesn't thank you", and the second shovel.
