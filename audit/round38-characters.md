# Critic report: Characters (round 38)

Read: `manuscript/00.md` to `17.md`, in order, in full. Nothing else.

## 1. Scores

- **Stakes: 8.** "Under that lamp was the bank. Under the bank was Coralie, who had said 7, and be in the house." The choice is between two people the reader likes, and a third pays for it.
- **Tension: 7.** "Each time she came back in, she looked down the corridor before she looked for a chair." From 10 to 14 it does not let go. In 6, 7 and 9 the people wait while the rules are worked out.
- **Progression: 6.5.** Chapter 4: "Stay in your own bed." Chapter 9: "Then stay in your own bed on Friday." The argument between the two women stands in the same place for five chapters.
- **Comedy: 7.5.** "I don't cook Either. There's no recipe for it. Soup or duck." The comedy comes from the people, and it thins whenever the scene belongs to the mechanism.
- **Characters: 8.** "I don't get into a bed over somebody who can't speak. I've lifted too many of them." Coralie is a 9, the village 8.5, Lesca 8, Maylis 7.
- **The switch between dark and comic: 8.5.** "'I'll be brief,' she said, in the voice that came from the back of the throat. 'The—'" The same oven door, a third time, and it will not come.
- **Prose: 7.5.** "It wasn't much. She took more than that to the dump in a morning." Held down by the narrator summing up what a scene has just shown (faults 5 and 10).
- **The ending: 8.** "Then you'll dig it. With that side. I'll strap it first." Maylis's ending costs her everything it should. Lesca's does not (fault 1).
- **Clarity: 6.** "So that's a fourth point, and I didn't have it on my list." The reader is still being handed rules in chapter 9 ("The count tells it when to take the visitor home. It doesn't keep it off"), and I read the sum in chapter 7 twice.

**Finished unpaid?** Yes. **Pressed on a friend?** Yes, with a warning that 6 and 7 are the price of 8 to 14.

## 2. Against the comparisons

**Above Crouch. Below The Good Place. Level with the bar taken as a pair.**

- Above Crouch on the wife. In Dark Matter the wrong Jason has Daniela for a month and she has to be told. Coralie knows by a wrist: "That's her. In this hand." Then she refuses the one she prefers. She has a want that predates the plot (the floor at Bayonne, Ithurbide's torch and list) and she keeps it. Crouch has no one like her.
- Above Crouch on the usurper. Jason2 is a silhouette. Lesca is the better company of the two women for a third of the book, and her self-deception is caught by her own mouth: "This time I'm coming to get—" then "To stay."
- Below The Good Place on the central pair. Its people change each other by coming to like each other. The two Maylises bargain and wrestle; neither is ever glad of the other. So "Don't come again." / "I won't." costs nothing, because nothing was there to lose (fault 2).
- Below The Good Place on the accounts. That show bills every act. Here Lesca's worst act is unbilled in her own village (fault 1).
- Level on "each wrong in her own way". One cannot choose; the other chose and has edited the account for twelve years ("No one came to tell him"). Chidi's flaw, with a body count.

Who would be missed: Jeannot, Madame Etcheto, Kévin, Sarthou, Maïté, both Arlettes. Not missed: Nadine, Bixente, the dealer, the deputy mayor. Does the reader care who lives? Yes. The book makes the reader want both, gives one, and takes the one with the best lines.

## 3. The ten faults that most hold it back

**1. Lesca is billed for the wrong death, and her ending is won on half a confession. (3, 11, 15, 16)**
"*That the mayor remain seated, and take the other one's bed, and Coralie Mounet in it.*" Against that, chapter 11, to her own Coralie: "For a man who hasn't drowned yet... I'm going to get him out." In her office "Lesca could tell herself anything", and she tells Coralie the same thing. The square then hears 2014. The council removes her for 2014. The act that cannot be defended, three weeks of coming back after "It comes with me", gets this: "'It was you.' 'Yes.' Madame Etcheto waited for more. There wasn't any." Then she gets "Good night, Maylis" and a lamp left on. She ends warmer than Maylis. No sentence softens her; the structure does.
*Do:* in the last scene of 16, before the good night, Lesca gives Coralie the motion sheet, or says its last clause. Coralie reads that she was to be taken in another woman's body. Then "Good night, Maylis" either still comes, and is worth ten times what it is now, or the door shuts. Either is better than what stands.

**2. The two women are never people to each other. (4, 9, 13, 17)**
The steel room strips Lesca twice ("It's the ti—", "To stay") and Maylis once, over Either. Maylis's own crossing mirrors Lesca's: "all of this was hers: the desk, the stone, a square that said good morning... she might have said yes to it"; "'I want that,' Maylis said"; "For sixteen years Maylis had sat down and said nothing, in the wrong kitchen." None of it is ever said to Lesca, in the one room where it would have to be true. So the pair stay invader and invaded until the last page, and their only moment of fellow feeling is the best exchange they have: "'Is yours in the house?' 'Across the landing. Four steps. Yours?' 'She took the pillow.'" They share thirty-one years, a father and a night behind a tent, and speak of none of it.
*Do:* in 9, before "Both", Lesca asks what Maylis wanted on Wednesday. Standing up, Maylis has to answer: the timer, the square, the soup she sat down for. It replaces the recap of the count rule in the same scene. After that they are level, and the parting in 17 has something to part.

**3. Jeannot learns what he is off the page. (5, 8, 17)**
"'What?' Jeannot said. 'Have I got something on me?'" "'What's left on me to look at?'" The reader has waited since the chrysanthemums for somebody to tell him he is a name on a stone. In 17 it has happened between chapters: "It says why she thought she had to." The village's best talker is denied his reaction to the book's premise.
*Do:* in the cemetery, before the envelope. She tells him, or he says back what she told him. He answers in points. One page.

**4. The Either that decides the book is said from outside her. (12)**
"'Which is it going to be? Shut, or open?' The river went by under the boards. 'Either,' Maylis said." An hour earlier she asked Coralie back. A minute earlier she was leaning on the handle. Kévin has released her from the blackmail. The page gives nothing of why the old word comes, and then she takes the handle off herself. Madame Darrigrand names this as the true hinge in 17, so it has to be believed, and it reads as the plot needing the gate shut.
*Do:* no paragraph of motive. One beat using what the book already built: in the steel room the word stuck; here it comes out easily, to the face of a man who has just said no to her. She has never once acted against a person's stated wish. Let the reader see her see that.

**5. Lesca's turn is argued by the narrator. (13)**
"She had told Maylis there was no both. She had wanted both herself, the bed and Castagnet. But the motion she had written at her own kitchen table was for a seat and a bed. There was no man in it." Then: "This was the same thing, done with a steering wheel." The stronger-willed woman changes her mind inside one ring of a phone, and the reader is given the reasoning and the parallel, in order, like minutes.
*Do:* keep "the reflector was red, and no nearer than it had been twelve years ago" and "There was no man in it." Cut the rest of that paragraph and the steering-wheel sentence.

**6. Lesca's Coralie is one sentence until chapter 11. (3, 6, 11)**
"Madame le maire will sit down," three times, in 3, 6 and 11. The bed with one pillow is what Lesca drowns a village to escape, and the marriage in it is a light under a door and a bowl of soup eaten standing. The bedroom scene in 11 is the best thing in Lesca's half and it arrives for a woman the reader has met for two hundred words.
*Do:* in the last scene of 3, give her one line that is not the formula: something she does for Lesca that Lesca does not notice, as Maylis clears a road.

**7. Larrère in Maylis's village is one note and walks away. (6, 9, 12, 17)**
"'There is no other sheet,' he said." In Lesca's village he is a man: "Eleven years I've been timed like an egg", and a gate Maïté will not open. In Maylis's he blackmails with a boy's name, takes a vote from a car, and leaves holding a dry folder. His one human thing, "You'll stay away from that house", is played only as a misunderstanding. When Maïté takes the bus, "nobody came out of" the café, and he is not there.
*Do:* put him where he can see the bus go. One look. It pays him in this village as the garden gate pays him in the other.

**8. Maylis has no mother after chapter 9. (9, 17)**
"My ponies are on that grass, Maylis." That is all of her. Lesca's Arlette has four scenes and some of the best lines in the book ("a whole set for the priest that she only used after he'd gone"). Maylis's lives across the yard and is absent on the night and on the Sunday the village turns. It reads as forgotten.
*Do:* four lines in 17, at the yard tap. What she says about the key will tell us where Either came from.

**9. The reader cannot see her or tell her age. (1, 4)**
"Little Lesca." "The village's girl." "It hasn't come for you, girl." She reads as twenty-five until "I gave that word up when I was thirty-one", which needs a sum. The face the two women share is never described; Lesca's first appearance gives "She sat much straighter than Maylis did." Neither is described as a woman from outside, which is right, but neither is described at all.
*Do:* her age in a clause in 1. In 4, one thing on the shared face that each wears differently.

**10. The narrator says Maylis's wants for her. (1, 4, 9, 12)**
"What she wanted was for Coralie to stay awake and keep her fingers on that wrist. But Coralie had to be up before 6, so Maylis didn't ask." The same wanted, but, so appears three times in chapter 1 (wrist, duck, glass), and later as "What scared her was Monday" and "that frightened her more than asking did". Coralie's wants are never narrated, and she is the most alive person in the book for it.
*Do:* keep the duck, because the pad shows it. Cut the label wherever an object already carries it.

## 4. Five things that must not be touched

1. **The landing, chapter 8.** The wrist, "That's her. In this hand", Bayonne told to the wrong woman, the pillow taken back.
2. **The oven door, three times.** Chapters 1, 9 and 17, and the third one unfinished.
3. **"Because you'd have told me."** All of the bedroom in 11, to "2:51. I've known that for twelve years."
4. **Jeannot's points.** From "One. Those are my boots" to six on the phone at 5 a.m. to letter 42: "It's four pages, and it doesn't thank you."
5. **"That's my door."** Through "Twelve. One, two. Somebody pulled a chair in to a table" to "Forty-one." And beside it Kévin's hand: "I wanted six, Maylis."
