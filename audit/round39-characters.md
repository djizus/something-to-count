# Round 39 — critic, lens: Characters

Read: `manuscript/00.md` to `17.md`, in order, in full. Nothing else.

## 1. Scores

- **Stakes — 7.** "The trapdoor was twenty-five minutes away, on foot, in the dark, with the dip under water. The bank had ten." The sum is exact. The bill, when it comes, is one woman of ninety-four who had refused twice to be moved.
- **Tension — 7.5.** "'Either,' Maylis said. / 'Then it's shut,' Jeannot said." Chapters 12 to 14 hold like Crouch. Chapters 7 and 9 do not.
- **Progression — 6.** "The count only decides when it comes back to take the visitor home." (ch. 9) Nine chapters in, the two women are still being told how the thing works.
- **Comedy — 8.** "Either is not a person. I cannot summon Either to the station."
- **Characters — 7.** "That's her. In this hand. I've had sixteen years of her saying nothing, and I know it when I've got hold of it." Coralie is a 9. The pair she stands between is a 6.
- **The switch between dark and comic — 8.5.** "'Here,' he said. / 'I know you are.'" The bingo gag carried into the flooded corridor without a seam.
- **Prose — 7.5.** "It wasn't much. She took more than that to the dump in a morning."
- **The ending — 6.5.** "The mayor of Peyrebarthe sends her apologies." One of the two women walks out of her own death on a joke, and the last meeting between them is twenty lines.
- **Clarity — 6.** "It had taken her home, but not to her bed." By chapter 15 the rules have been amended so often that I took this on trust.

**Finished unpaid:** yes. **Press it on a friend:** on the friend who loved The Good Place, yes. On the Crouch reader, no: they would stall in chapter 9.

## 2. Against the comparisons

**Above Crouch. Below The Good Place. Net: just under the bar.**

- **Above Crouch on the partner.** In Dark Matter the wife takes the usurper home and notices only that he is more attentive. Here the usurper gets one supper ("I'd have this one again") and is caught on a landing by a pulse. Coralie refuses a bed "over somebody who can't speak", has a want that is not either woman (Ithurbide's floor in Bayonne), and leaves with it. No Crouch spouse does that much.
- **Above Crouch on the usurper.** His is a villain with no inside. Lesca has a motion in her own handwriting, "and Coralie Mounet in it", and a room that will not let her say "I won't" or "to get him out". "To stay" is the only one that comes out.
- **Below The Good Place on the pair.** That show makes its people change each other by argument, face to face, for hours. Maylis and Lesca meet four times, and three of the meetings are agenda items about a ghost's timetable. Maylis is Chidi: "Either" is the stomach ache. But Chidi is told what he is by Eleanor to his face. Maylis is told by Coralie, superbly, and never by the one person who gave the word up at thirty-one.
- **Level with The Good Place on "each wrong in their own way".** Maylis will not choose. Lesca chose and typed over it. One Coralie asks with boxes, the other kept a landing so as not to ask. Jeannot votes against whoever is right. Larrère talks through a gap.

Direct answers to the lens:

- **Distinct?** Yes, by ear alone. One says "Either". The other says "All those in favour" to a room of two.
- **Wanted-for?** Lesca from chapter 3. Maylis not until "I want that", the timer, in chapter 6, and then "Come back across the landing" in chapter 12. That is late, and it is the point.
- **Softened or excused?** Maylis is not: "The arm is the excuse you've written for me. It isn't mine." Lesca is, in the last three chapters (fault 3).
- **Described from outside as a woman?** Not by the narrator, who never tells me what either looks like. By Josiane, four times (fault 10).
- **Who would be missed?** Jeannot, Madame Etcheto, Kévin, Arlette, Monsieur Daguerre. Not Bordenave, the dealer, Nadine or Bixente.
- **Which relationship carries it?** Maylis and Coralie. It pays in full: "I know what you picked. I was the other one."
- **Do I care who lives?** I was afraid for Coralie for four pages of chapter 14 and minded Etcheto. I was never afraid for Lesca.

## 3. The ten faults, worst first

**1. The book builds a room where a standing woman cannot lie, and never asks it the book's question. (ch. 10, 11, 13, 17)**
"Maylis had been given the whole night, but nothing in it told her which of the two Lesca had turned the handle for." Then Lesca tests the two answers in her own bedroom, where she can say anything: "That sounded no more true, and no less." In the steel room Maylis says "You rang once" and, at the end, "Forty-one". She never says: forty houses, or her? This is the scene the premise owes: one version of a woman asking the other the only thing she cannot find out alone. Likewise "I gave that word up when I was thirty-one" is the best line about the two of them, and nobody picks it up.
*Do:* in chapter 17, Maylis asks. Lesca says both sentences standing, and both come out whole, because she does not know. That is the bedroom scene with the proof attached. Pay for the space by cutting the rule recital in chapter 9 ("The words keep it off the old woman in room twelve. They won't keep it off you…").

**2. The catastrophe needs Maylis alone at 3, and at dusk she sends away the arms she spent the morning begging for. (ch. 12)**
At 11: "She needed a second pair of hands." At dusk Kévin is on the platform, having said "I'd do them, Maylis. I want you to know I would. Don't keep a gate shut for me." Then: "Kévin could turn the handle while she went back down for Jeannot. She wouldn't ask him to." Three sentences, a noble reason, and the both she tells Coralie she wants is driven off the bank with a pump running. It is the same woman who wrote *Either* because duck cost Coralie an hour at the stove. The book does not make the link, and nobody ever charges her with it. Marie-Jo thanks her for the van instead.
*Do:* make it a scene. Kévin offers to stay at the handle. She refuses. Let him, or Coralie in 17, name it: she would not ask. As written, her worst decision reads as her kindest.

**3. Lesca's death is waived, and she ends the book better off than she began. (ch. 9, 13, 15, 16)**
The price is announced: "The one it cannot take home dies in her own bed." She accepts it: "I've worked out what I'd be giving up, and I've written it down. Nil." She holds the wheel, lands in the hall, and leaves by sending apologies. She wakes on a stretcher and "signed it lying down". By Tuesday she has lost a sash and a medal and gained a first name from Coralie, a gutter from Maïté and her mother's silence. She brought a river onto another village to get into another woman's bed. Nobody in her own world can hold her to that, and the one in the other world says a number.
*Do:* keep the apologies line, it is her whole voice. Make the exit cost something she uses. The text already has it: "The fingers had locked on the wood. She opened them with the other hand." Let that hand not come back. A mayor who signs things wakes in Bayonne and cannot sign the form. And move Maïté's gutter off the evening of the confession.

**4. The village's verdict on Maylis is given by a walk-on. Everyone the reader loves comforts her. (ch. 17)**
"'You had the key,' Madame Darrigrand said." She has had three lines before this. Then Kévin tows the van for nothing, Marie-Jo stands her a chair ("Just the one. Sit down on it"), Jeannot writes letter 42 and digs. Etcheto's daughter is a clause: "Her daughter is having her buried here on Tuesday." Coralie takes a share herself: "That part is mine."
*Do:* give Darrigrand's speech to someone I would miss. Bordenave kept the pad, bailed his cellar and stayed at his upstairs window. Or put the daughter at the cemetery gate in place of the priest. Do not change who dies.

**5. Lesca arrives as a file. (ch. 3, 6)**
"The other Maylis Lesca was the one whose village had its flood in 2014 and made her mayor. Call her Lesca." The narrator steps forward once in the book, to hand over a name tag and a summary of what the next three pages show. Her hardness then comes the same way: "Lesca had put cameras on the gates and sent the film to the gendarmes, and Kévin had done six months in prison, for eels." The sash "in a plastic bag" is mentioned four times and never seen. On the page she is brisk and funny. I am told her deputy has not spoken to her since 2018, and I never watch her earn it. The one live instance is the best: "It was thirty-six houses and the care home against four."
*Do:* keep "Call her Lesca". Cut the résumé around it. Give her one more live sum like Darrigrand's, with Kévin or Marie-Jo, in chapter 3.

**6. Maylis has no mother. (ch. 3 against ch. 1–14)**
"Lesca crossed the yard to her mother's kitchen." Arlette is among the best people in the book: "I've voted for Larrère against her three times." In the other Peyrebarthe the same yard exists and she is never named, alive or dead. The father gets a tall L, a red line, a shovel and a seat at the bingo. Over there her field is still a field, with ponies on it, under the water Maylis is deciding about.
*Do:* place her. One line if she is dead. If she is alive, her ponies are on those three hundred hectares and that is a scene.

**7. Lesca's turn in the van is argued by the narrator. (ch. 13)**
"Twelve years ago she had let his phone ring once and then done what she had come to do. Holding this wheel was the same thing." The second lead's one act of renunciation takes three paragraphs of reasoning inside a single ring of a phone, with a weight on her chest.
*Do:* cut from "She had told Maylis there was no both" to "the same thing". Keep the reflector, "no nearer than it had been twelve years ago", and "I'm letting go".

**8. A woman who never says, says it four times in one chapter. (ch. 17)**
To Kévin: "I waited for the river to decide, so that it wouldn't be me." To Coralie: "I had to pick one. I picked." To Lesca: "I drove past the handle to be the one who did." To Jeannot: "At 3 I never tried the handle." Three different motives, and chapter 14 had already given the first: "She wanted it not to be her."
*Do:* cut the Kévin one. Keep Jeannot's, which refuses an excuse, and Lesca's, which is new and is said in the room that vouches for it.

**9. Coralie is never frightened. (ch. 5)**
At 6 on Saturday a stranger used her partner's mouth and she got out of the bed "holding her fingers in her other hand". By Monday breakfast: "When she looked up she was close to laughing." By supper: "I'd have this one again." The preference is the engine of the book and I believe it. I do not believe sixteen years with someone and no fear of what is sitting in her chair.
*Do:* one beat at the soup before the laugh. She serves from the far side of the table, or keeps the door behind her.

**10. Josiane's line, the third time. (ch. 5, 6, 8, 17)**
"A woman takes flowers to the wife because she's after the husband or because she's after the wife." Good in the café. Good again when Larrère borrows it in council. At bingo: "She's just told him which he is." By then it is the only thing Josiane is, and the two leads have been read from outside as women who must be after somebody for a third scene with no one answering. The Lassalle sisters have already done the misreading better: "She wants water." / "She's got a bar full of coffee."
*Do:* cut Josiane's bread-and-water speech in chapter 8. Keep "I only had the one name wrong."

## 4. Five things that must not be touched

1. **The shopping pad**, from "Eggs is what Either gets in this house" through "And then there's me, as a distance" to "I'll ask it once more, with no box."
2. **The landing, chapter 8.** The pulse taken mid-step, "I don't get into a bed over somebody who can't speak. I've lifted too many of them", and the unanswered question about Castagnet.
3. **The other Coralie and the phone, chapter 11.** "Because you'd have told me." "The phone doesn't say how long it rang. It says not answered."
4. **"Either" on the platform, and Jeannot never grateful.** "Then it's shut." "It's four pages, and it doesn't thank you. I was in no danger, and I'll say so to anyone."
5. **Kévin's hand.** "Five" in the headlights with a wheelchair half up, and "I wanted six, Maylis. I've never wanted anything so much."
