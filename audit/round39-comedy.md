# Round 39 — Comedy

Read: `manuscript/00.md` to `17.md`, in order, in full. Nothing else.

## 1. Scores

- **Stakes: 8.** "'The gate, or Castagnet? Say which, Maylis. For once.'" (14). Forty houses, forty-two beds, one man and a marriage are all on the same question.
- **Tension: 7.** "It was an hour and nine minutes short of 4." (12). Chapters 12 to 14 hold. Chapters 7 and 9 do not.
- **Progression: 6.** "Keeping me out for a night or two changes nothing, because the river catches up the day I'm back." (9). The book says here that the two nights of chapter 7 bought nothing.
- **Comedy: 7.** "'Eleven years I've been timed like an egg,' he said." (6). Six set pieces of that quality, and a third of the book between 7 and 11 where the smile goes.
- **Characters: 8.** "'I don't get into a bed over somebody who can't speak. I've lifted too many of them.'" (8).
- **The switch between dark and comic: 8.** "'The mayor of Peyrebarthe sends her apologies,' she said, in the voice she used for the prefecture." (13). She says it to the dead, who have laid her place.
- **Prose: 7.** "It had gone the colour of the sky, and very wide." (12). Plain and exact. Marked down for the stuck-sentence device, used six times.
- **The ending: 8.** "'I wanted six, Maylis. I've never wanted anything so much.'" (17).
- **Clarity: 6.** "The count only decides when it comes back to take the visitor home." (9). A rule the reader needed in chapter 2, stated in chapter 9.

Would I have finished it unpaid: yes. Would I press it on a friend: yes, with a warning to push through chapter 7.

## 2. Against the comparisons

**Below The Good Place on comedy, by about one point. Level with it on the switch.** Crouch is not funny, so he is not the measure for this lens.

What puts it level:

- **The callbacks turn dark.** "Twelve. That's my door" is a bingo joke in 8 and a death in 15. "Still in the box, were you?" becomes "Had the key and went for a walk, did you?" in 17. "I'll be brief" ends with a man who says it and then cannot speak. This is what The Good Place does with its running jokes, done as well.
- **Everyone is wrong in their own way.** Larrère hides behind procedure, Jeannot behind contrariness, Maylis behind "Either". Lesca hides behind a true sentence: "Every word of it was true, and it was typed." Coralie never asks. Josiane has the right house and the wrong name.

What puts it below:

- **The problem itself is never funny, only its handling.** The Good Place put its people on a real trolley and made the dilemma the joke. Here the comedy is in the committee around the dilemma: the verb for the stone, the timer, the medal taken back "until things are clearer". That is good, and it is the book's own. But at the gate the only comic handle on the choice is "Either" then "Both", and the book leans on that one word nine times.
- **The lead stops being comic company.** Eleanor is funny in every scene she is in. Maylis is funny in chapter 1 and silent from 7 to 11.
- **The big reversal arrives as arithmetic.** See fault 1.

## 3. The ten faults, worst first

**1. The book's reversal is delivered in its least funny chapter, a chapter before its joke (7, 8).**
"The rain came when Lesca came. She had nobody to say it to and no mouth to say it with."
The rescuer is the flood. That is this book's "this is the Bad Place". It arrives as a paragraph of deduction on a wet bank. One chapter later it gets the delivery it deserves: "'You're the water.'" / "'She wants water,' one of the Lassalle sisters said." / "'She's got a bar full of coffee,' said her sister." By then the reader already knows, so the bingo line surprises only Lesca.
Do: cut the deduction from 7, and the last paragraph that repeats it. Keep the facts in plain view: the cross on the pad, the jam jars, the rain back by noon. Let the reader get it at the bar with the hall mishearing it. Coralie's chart in 9 ("*What. Since when. How do you know.*") already explains it and needs no change. This is a cut, not a rebuild. The risk is a cold reader lost between the bar and the chart, about 600 words; test it on one.

**2. Maylis loses her voice for a third of the book (7 to 11).**
Chapter 1: "'Don't. He'd only ring me to dig the hole.'" Chapter 6: "'I go in at the front, Guy,' Maylis said. 'In daylight, and she holds the ladder.'" Then chapter 8, entire: "'You're the water.'"
The premise takes her mouth, and when she has it back she uses it for explanations, measurements and instructions. The driest voice in the book is hers and it is gone when the book is darkest. The narration can still carry it, and once does: "It was a night's overtime, by order of the president. He would argue about every hour of it." (12).
Do: no new scenes. Put that counting-the-cost voice back into chapter 7, which has none of it, and give her one reply of the chapter 1 kind in each of the scenes where she already speaks: the petition, the association vote, Kévin at the sand bin.

**3. Coralie asks, Maylis answers with a measurement, Coralie leaves the room: three times running at the same pitch (9, 9, 10).**
"'It's twenty minutes to Bayonne,' she said." Coralie pours the coffee down the sink.
"'There's a key, and a handle, and Larrère, and a van. And then there's me, as a distance.'" Coralie leaves the pencil on the pad.
"'The van will be at the gate from dusk.'" / "'I asked where you'd be,' Coralie said. She went up to the spare room."
The second is the best line and the first is needed for chapter 17. The third is the same beat again, and it undoes the best small joke in the book, which comes just before it: Maylis answers "'Duck'" at once when the question is asked through a bandage.
Do: cut the third. End the bandage scene on Coralie's hand holding the rib.

**4. The rules are told straight in the steel room, and Lesca stops chairing (9).**
"'The words keep it off the old woman in room twelve. They won't keep it off you,' Lesca said. 'It sits on you at 2:51 whatever you say to it. The count only decides when it comes back to take the visitor home. 2:51 is my minute.'"
Chapter 4 made the rules funny by making them an agenda: "'Then it's carried, or it's a tie, and in a tie the chair decides.'" / "'There's nobody in the chair.'" Chapter 9 gives seven sentences of rule with no form at all. It also corrects what chapter 2 let the reader believe. And "She didn't call anything to order" in 17 pays off less than it should, because she had already stopped in 9.
Do: cut the lecture to two sentences and give them as an item. She had three points and "a fourth point, and I didn't have it on my list". This is the fifth.

**5. The stuck sentence is used six times in two scenes (4, 9).**
"'It's—' Maylis stood with her mouth open on it." Then "It comes because no—", "'It's the ti—'", "'I w—'", "I'm coming to get—".
By the fourth the dash gives it away. Only two matter: "To stay" in 9 and "'I won't.' It came out whole" in 17.
Do: keep the Tuesday test in 4 as the demonstration and "to get— … To stay" in 9 as the use. Cut "It's the ti—" and "I w—".

**6. The best council scene is cut in half by the pulse chart (6).**
"They put their pens down to watch." Then: "At home, in Maylis's bedroom, the clock radio said 18:40."
Larrère without the timer is the comic high point of the first half. The cut comes as he gets going. It is the third cut to Coralie in the chapter, and chapter 7 reports the same numbers anyway.
Do: delete the 18:40 cut. Move "She had made a joke of it then" to chapter 7.

**7. The meeting chaired from a car stops before it builds (9).**
"'We can't shut the door,' the oldest member said. 'The president's on the other side of it.'"
This is the best comic premise in the second half and it runs thirty-five lines. Six members sit there and one speaks. The scene's laugh is handed to Coralie's oven door afterwards.
Do: two more exchanges of procedure through the gap before the sheet with Kévin's name goes up. This is the one fault here that needs lines added.

**8. Coralie's speech about the rib is a plant, and reads like one (7).**
"He said his mouth tasted of coins. He coughed, and it came out pink on the sheet."
Six sentences of a stranger's death, said by a woman walking out with her pillow, so that the coins and the pink can return forty lines later. It is the only place where Coralie talks for the author.
Do: cut it to the instruction and one consequence: "Push on it and it goes through into the lung. I've seen one do it." The coins and the pink can be Maylis's own discovery.

**9. The night of the flood is told in full twice (00, 10).**
00: "felt the clay move under her palm", "It rang once." 10: "It rang once and nobody answered."
The opening already shows the bank, the platform, the turned van and the single ring. That is good for the comedy: the reader hears "no one came to tell him" in 3 as a true sentence used to lie. But chapter 10 then retells 600 words the reader has, with the book's voice switched off.
Do: cut chapter 10's version to what the opening did not show: whose reflector, Coralie's car in the yard, the four minutes until the red light goes out, the boot.

**10. The laugh in the minute of silence is reported, not shared (6).**
"All she could find was Jeannot and his letter seventeen, the one that billed the village for two wheel tracks its own tractor had left when it pulled him out."
The situation is right. The trigger is a clause of explanation, so the reader watches her laugh and does not join in.
Do: let her hear him. One line of Jeannot's, in his numbered points, is enough to set it off.

## 4. Five things that must not be touched

1. **"I'll be brief."** The window gap, Coralie at the oven door in 1 and 9, Larrère at the garden gate in 16 ("He said nothing."), and Coralie's unfinished one in 17: "'I'll be brief,' she said, in the voice that came from the back of the throat. 'The—'"
2. **Bingo night and its return.** All of chapter 8's hall: the jaw, the coffee over Josiane's shoes, Madame Etcheto finishing the words and so undoing Maylis. Then the same hall for the dead, "apologies received", and "Twelve. One, two."
3. **Kévin's fingers.** The joke in 2, the count at 4 a.m. in the flood, and "I wanted six".
4. **Jeannot's numbered points and letter forty-two**, through "And six. Bugger off." to the letter that "doesn't thank you" and the second shovel.
5. **Coralie's shopping pad.** "'I don't cook Either,' she said. 'There's no recipe for it.'" through to "I know that word. I've cooked it. That was Either, with the river in it."
