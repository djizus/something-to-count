# Critic report: Thriller

Read: `manuscript/00.md` to `17.md`, in order, in full. Nothing else.

## 1. Scores

- **Stakes: 7.** "I'd have this one again." (5) The marriage is in real danger and the wife prefers the intruder. But the other village risks nothing: "I've worked out what I'd be giving up, and I've written it down. Nil." (9)
- **Tension: 6.** "She wanted one day as the woman who signed things." (6) Ten days before the flood the heroine takes a day off the countdown, and the book goes with her.
- **Progression: 6.5.** "Her mayor got Friday the 4th wrong. That's on the pad, with a cross by it." (9) The village line is tightly caused: her two nights of resistance cost her the vote. The three chapters set in Lesca's village mark time.
- **Comedy: 7.5.** "Either is not a person. I cannot summon Either to the station." (6)
- **Characters: 8.** "I don't cook Either," she said. "There's no recipe for it. Soup or duck." (1)
- **The switch between dark and comic: 8.** "'Here,' he said. / 'I know you are.'" (14) A running joke carried into the flood without a bump.
- **Prose: 7.** "Then the water rose up the door and covered it, and the red light went out." (10) Plain and exact at its best. It drops when the narrator argues a turn the scene has shown.
- **The ending: 8.** "I know what you picked. I was the other one." (17)
- **Clarity: 6.** "The count only decides when it comes back to take the visitor home." (9) That is the eighth rule, in chapter 9 of 17, and it changes what the previous eight chapters were doing.

**Finished unpaid?** Yes. After chapter 5 I would not have stopped. I would have skimmed the council room in 6 and the roll of tape in 11.

**Press it on a friend?** Not on the friend who gave me Dark Matter. I would lose them in chapter 6.

## 2. Against the comparisons

**Below Crouch, by about a point, for propulsion. Above him for consequence.**

What puts it below:

1. **Rules.** The box in Dark Matter has one rule, set once, and every later chapter exploits it. The chair in Recursion is the same. Inguma has a dozen by chapter 9: knowledge passes in the chair; whoever stays seated wakes over there; the owner keeps the right hand; the count sets the return; nobody can lie standing; the rain follows Lesca; pushing keeps you out; the words don't keep it off Maylis; one night matters; the one it can't take home dies; the empty body runs down; the owner's hand weakens. Each is added when the plot needs an exit closed. A Crouch reader stops trusting the game.
2. **The middle.** Crouch's man always has a physical next move. Between chapters 6 and 11 Maylis's move is, several times, to lie still or not lie still. The jeopardy in that stretch is a pulse chart, a rib and a committee vote.
3. **The intruder is disarmed at the midpoint.** Jason2 has the wife and she doesn't know. Here Coralie knows by chapter 8 and refuses: "I don't get into a bed over somebody who can't speak." That is better drama than Crouch's, and it ends the threat nine chapters early. After it Lesca says "to stay" and has no method.

What puts it level or above:

1. **The hook and the first five chapter ends** are level with Crouch: "It rang once."; "Maylis hadn't said it."; the motion amended to "and Coralie Mounet in it"; "She hadn't told them to."; "Are you coming up?"
2. **The reversals are level with The Good Place.** "You're the water" re-reads every wet day since chapter 1. "Fetching a man out of a dry hole" re-reads the rescue.
3. **The cost is above Crouch.** His heroes are right in the end. This one is wrong, stays wrong, is told so on a doorstep by a woman holding a baby, and loses her wife for it.

**Where I was afraid, and of what:** the fourth stair (5); the pulse going from 64 to 34 (6, 7); the coins and the pink in the sink (7); the landing at midnight (8); "Etcheto back in 12" (12); the lamp over the handle going by the window (14); the dark end of the corridor (14). I was never afraid for Jeannot and never afraid of Larrère.

**Where a Crouch reader puts it down:** the council room in 6; the rehearsal in 11.

## 3. The ten faults, worst first

**1. Chapter 9 (and 4, 7): the rules are still being amended at the midpoint, by assertion, and Friday has no win condition the reader can hold.**
"One night matters. If I'm in your village at 2:51 on Friday night, your bank goes, as mine was going. If I'm not, it holds."
Lesca cannot know this. It is announced because the climax needs it. Then the plan: "I'll sit up in the van, awake, until 4, when it gives up." But the book has already shown that being awake is no defence (she was awake under it in chapter 1 and pushed awake for two nights in 7). Only pushing works, and she has said she can't push. So the vigil cannot succeed by the book's own rules, neither woman notices, and at "02:49. She was awake" the reader does not know what is being attempted. The peak of the countdown is blurred.
*Do:* fix every rule by the end of 7 and add none after. Move the failed experiment with the words from 9 into 7. Let Maylis draw "one night matters" from evidence the reader has seen, or cut it and let her simply fear it. Give Friday one line: push it off until 4, or she is in. Then the rib is the clock, and every ring in the van is legible.

**2. Chapter 12: the real choice of the book is made at 5 p.m. in sixty words, by a verbal tic.**
"'Either,' Maylis said. / 'Then it's shut,' Jeannot said. 'That's how the vote left it.'"
Jeannot is above ground, it is daylight, the handle is fitted. Everything after follows from this, and chapter 17 says so: "At 5 on Friday it was light, and Castagnet was up on the bank at your van window." The scene gives it one click of the catch. "Either" has always answered "what do you want"; here Jeannot asks what she will do, with her weight already on the handle, and the word does the plot's work. Worse, Kévin has just cancelled the only outside force: "Don't keep a gate shut for me." Nothing holds her hand but herself, and the scene doesn't show it holding.
*Do:* make this the longest scene in the chapter. Let her turn. Three teeth, four, the chain coming up, the river starting to pull at the gate. Jeannot's hand on the handle. Larrère's car a hundred metres off with her letter on the seat. Let her feel that she could, and stop. Then the word. The 3 a.m. choice becomes the second time she is asked, which is what the ending already claims.

**3. Chapter 6: the heroine crosses for no errand.**
"She wanted one day as the woman who signed things."
With ten days left she goes over to enjoy being greeted. Nothing she does there bears on Friday. The intercut pulse chart is the only tension, and it is Coralie's. The council scene is funny and long, and the thing it yields ("When you stood on that platform, did you—") is cut off by a bell.
*Do:* send her across to get what Lesca refused her in 4 ("That's an item for another night"): what happened on the platform. Let her go for the file, the typed line from the gendarmes, and Larrère's unfinished question. Then she comes home with half the secret and a pulse of 34, and chapter 10 no longer has to hand her the night for nothing.

**4. Chapter 13: the hall of the dead sits on top of the climax, and Lesca's death is waived with a joke.**
"'The mayor of Peyrebarthe sends her apologies,' she said, in the voice she used for the prefecture. 'She has a ceremony at 11, and she will not be taking this seat.'"
Two faults in one place. The bank has ten minutes, Lesca has just said "Go to the hut first", and the book stops for five hundred words of bingo. And the rule planted in 2 ("The one it cannot take home dies in her own bed"), shown as a laid place in 9, is escaped by standing up. She wakes on a stretcher: "Bayonne has had you for three hours and found nothing." Maylis's crossing cost a pulse of 34. Lesca's costs a signature.
*Do:* end 13 at "Then there was no steering wheel, and no rain." Open 15 with the hall. And don't let her rise by will: her fingers stay locked on that chair until "Twelve. One, two" is called and somebody else pulls a chair in. Then her life is paid for on the page by room twelve, which is what she tells Madame Etcheto in 16 anyway.

**5. Chapter 14: she stands at the gendarme's gate with the bank going and doesn't wake him.**
"It crossed the road, went over Sarthou's sandbags and on through his hedge."
Sarthou owns the siren. The care home is waiting for it: "On red Sarthou turns his siren, and I move them." Forty houses are asleep. She is five metres from his door at 2:55 and drives away. He turns the siren at 3:11, after the break. A thriller reader shouts at the page here, and the book never answers.
*Do:* either she leans on the horn and beats on his shutter as she goes, and he is in the street by 2:57; or she doesn't, and Madame Darrigrand adds it to the charge in 17. The second costs more and is the better one.

**6. Chapters 1 and 4: the best hook is spent in three chapters and its clock is dropped.**
"She opened her pocket knife and cut eighteen marks down the rake, starting level with her shoulder."
The rake is never seen again. In 4: "It isn't a grave. It's for a night." Her own name and date on a stone was the reason to turn pages; it is explained away and replaced by the job of getting a signature for a man who doesn't want saving. From 4 to 7 Maylis herself is in danger of embarrassment and the sack.
*Do:* bring the rake back, one mark cut off each morning, to open chapters. And when Lesca explains the stone, let Maylis notice that it doesn't say which Maylis Lesca; the card in 9 then lands on a fear that was kept alive.

**7. Chapters 00, 10, 11, 13, 15: the secret is told five times.**
"It rang once and nobody answered. She didn't wait for the second ring." (10)
The reader has had this since the prologue. Chapter 10 retells it in six hundred words of summary, delivered free by the chair ("the night of the flood came to Maylis anyway"). By the confession in 15 the sentence carries no news; only the square's reaction does.
*Do:* keep the prologue. Cut 10's retelling to the three things the prologue lacks: the reflector, Coralie's car in the yard, the four minutes of red light. Play them as scene, not report.

**8. Chapter 11: the day of the flood opens with a rehearsal.**
"He read it to her off his sheet, from the anthem to the photograph."
Chapter 10 ends on Friday morning with the plan changed. The next thousand words are a roll of tape in the other village. All of it is set-up for 15 and none of it is in danger.
*Do:* move the rehearsal back into chapter 3 or 6, where there is room. Keep in 11 only the mother and the phone.

**9. Chapters 4, 6, 10, 12, 14, 16: Larrère's secret is planted five times and then explained.**
"You stood in my kitchen that night and rang her from my phone, with his van gone down to the fields, and you never told the woman you'd sent to the gate." (16)
The folder on Maïté's cupboard, the back lane, the kitchen clock on the line, the back lane again, "Castagnet, house" on the screen. The reader has it by 12. Maïté's speech then states it in full, and the one man who might have been dangerous says nothing and walks off.
*Do:* cut two of the plants (the second back lane, and the clock in 14). In 16 let Maïté say only "From my phone, Guy."

**10. Chapters 7, 13, 17: the turns are argued instead of seen.**
"Twelve years ago she had let his phone ring once and then done what she had come to do. Holding this wheel was the same thing." (13)
Lesca's reversal, the hinge of her whole story, is a hundred and fifty words of reasoning inside one ring of a phone. The same in 7, where "The rain came when Lesca came" follows a paragraph laid out as a proof. And in 17: "At the gate I thought I could do better than you. I drove past the handle to be the one who did." The line before it is right: "Maylis had meant to say forty-one and nothing else."
*Do:* in 13, keep the reflector "no nearer than it had been twelve years ago" and the hand opening; cut the argument. In 17, let her say forty-one and nothing else.

## 4. Five things that must not be touched

1. **The shopping pad, from first box to last.** "I don't cook Either" through "That was Either, with the river in it." It is the book's spine and its only clock that never stops.
2. **Kévin's fingers.** "I wanted six, Maylis. I've never wanted anything so much."
3. **The old wing.** "Nine was open." "Ten was open." "Eleven was open." "Twelve was shut." And the last chair in the hall: "Forty-one."
4. **Coralie and the phone in chapter 11.** "2:51. I've known that for twelve years." "Because you'd have told me."
5. **The square after the sentence.** "The loudspeaker on the bank road said 'drowned him' after the square had finished hearing it." And Marie-Jo: "Somebody has to stack them."
