# Round 41: Reader

Read: `manuscript/00.md` to `17.md`, in order, in full. Nothing else.

## 1. Scores

- **Stakes: 7.5.** "Behind it were forty houses, and the care home, with forty-two people in their beds. Out on the fields there was one man, at his duck hut, and no one came to tell him." The sum is on the table by chapter 3 and never blurs. It loses marks because the threat to Maylis's own body is raised and never collected (fault 3).
- **Tension: 7.** "On the sixth ring the breath wouldn't come." Chapters 12 to 14 are as tense as anything in Crouch. Before chapter 6 I was curious and amused but never afraid.
- **Progression: 7.** "Then she was in the chair." That sentence, or its twin, opens a scene in chapters 2, 4, 9 (twice), 10 and 17. Every chapter changes something, but chapters 6 to 9 change it in the same three rooms.
- **Comedy: 7.5.** "And six. Bugger off." I smiled from the oven door in chapter 1 to the Hontaube phone call in chapter 12. I stopped in chapter 7 and in chapter 16.
- **Characters: 8.** "There's a key, and a handle, and Larrère, and a van. And then there's me, as a distance." Coralie is the person I read for. Maylis is the hardest of the cast to get near, because the narration stays outside her until the dip in chapter 14.
- **The switch between dark and comic: 8.** "The mayor of Peyrebarthe sends her apologies. She has a ceremony at 11, and she will not be taking this seat." The joke is the character, so it does not let the dark off.
- **Prose: 7.5.** "The L cut in the stone wouldn't run." I never reread a sentence for its grammar. I did notice that nearly every scene closes the same way, on an object or a flat fact.
- **The ending: 7.5.** "I know what you picked. I was the other one." It costs enough. It is also fifteen short closures in two chapters, and one of them is muddled (fault 9).
- **Clarity: 6.** "The bank was a dark line across the end of the fields, with the roofs of the Port behind it." I could not draw this place, and the climax is made of its distances.

**Would I have finished it unpaid?** Yes. I was at risk once, in the square in chapter 1. After the last line of chapter 2 I was not.

**Would I press it on a friend?** On the friend who loved The Good Place, yes, now. On the friend who reads Crouch on a plane, no: they would stop in chapter 1 or chapter 7.

## 2. Against the comparisons

**Overall, for this lens: just below the bar. 7.5 against an 8.**

- **Below Crouch on rules a reader can play.** Crouch gives the box one rule, lets you learn it by watching it go wrong, then lets you use it before the hero does. Here I counted ten rules by chapter 9, most of them read out by Lesca as agenda items, and the one the night depends on is announced, not shown (fault 1).
- **Below Crouch on the first twenty pages.** Dark Matter has its man taken at gunpoint before you have settled. This book opens with a thing on a wardrobe, which is as good, then spends five pages on a registered letter and a drainage quarrel.
- **Level with The Good Place on the moral problem, and above it on cost.** The show did the trolley problem with a real trolley and then reset. This book builds the lever for fourteen chapters, puts one woman on each side of it, lets neither be right, and keeps the body. "You're the water" re-reads the rain from page one the way the first season's last scene re-reads the season.
- **Above Crouch on the marriage.** His wives are wanted. Coralie wants something herself: "I posted it today, and I posted it for me."

**What I did not understand**

- Why opening the gate puts the river on the fields and not on the Port, when both lie on the land side of the same bank.
- Whether Lesca counted as "in the village at 2:51" on the night. Maylis pushes through that minute and Lesca wakes in the van at 2:52.
- Whether Madame Etcheto sat down in the chair Lesca refused, or simply died the same night.
- Why Lesca's flood fields are now a gravel lake, and what that has to do with the flood.
- What Larrère wanted on the night: his members' corn, Jeannot dry, or Jeannot out of the house.
- Whether Maylis has a punctured lung on the last page.

**Where I skimmed.** Jeannot's account of how the gate drained his field (1). The day-by-day sum that proves the rain comes with Lesca (7). The river heights: seventy under, fifty-five under, ninety over, eighty over. The run-through of the ceremony programme (11). Larrère's third telling of the resignation threat (12).

**Where I could not stop.** The last line of chapter 2 into chapter 3. The stair post (5). Bingo night (8). Chapters 10 to 15 in one sitting.

**What I will remember in a month.** Kévin counting his fingers in the headlights and getting five. The thing in leaves and rivets that sits with its back to you. Two fingers on a wrist. "She wants water." The dead at bingo. Jeannot's numbered points. "Drowned him" arriving late from the second loudspeaker. "Forty-one."

**What I will have forgotten.** The landowners' association, the merger with Hontaube, how the eel blackmail fits together, and every date except the 11th.

## 3. The ten faults that most hold it back

**1. The rule the night hangs on is told, not shown, and it blurs at the minute it counts. (Chapters 9, 12, 13, 14.)**
"One night matters. If I'm in your village at 2:51 on Friday night, your bank goes, as mine was going. If I'm not, it holds." Lesca says this and the book takes it as law. I never see it work. Then on the night Maylis fights through 2:51, Lesca arrives at 2:52, leaves at 2:53, and the narrator rules: "She had got Lesca out of her, but too late: the river was through the bank already." Four chapters of pushing, staying awake and the fight in the van are settled in a clause, by a law I could not check. The same looseness lets Lesca resist Inguma, land among the dead, decline her seat and go home.
*Do:* show the rule once before the night. Give Maylis one earlier night with Lesca kept out, standing at the patch at 2:51, watching the line of water stop. Then on the 11th let her see the sheet start in the lamp or the mirror the second Lesca wakes in the van. Replace "too late" with what she sees. Say in one line in chapter 16 whether chair twelve was a trade.

**2. The ground is never drawn. (Chapters 00, 7, 12, 14.)**
"From up there the river was on one side of her and the fields were on the other, far below." The forty houses are on that side too, two hundred metres along. Nothing ever says what keeps the fields' water out of the Port. Chapter 7 then puts "the roofs of the Port behind" the bank, seen from the fields, which is the wrong side. In chapter 14 I followed 600 metres, 400 metres and "a kilometre and two hundred" as sums. I did not see them.
*Do:* walk it once, early, in order: Port, last house, patch, road up, gate, track, dip, hut, and the rise or wall between Port and fields. The Monday the handle threw her against the rail is the place. Fix the sentence in chapter 7.

**3. The lung is loaded and never fired. (Chapters 7, 14, 17.)**
Coralie: "He was dead before the doctor had his coat off." Maylis then pushes three nights, coughs pink, hauls a heavy man out of a hole ("Something gave in her side. She heard it go."), runs, wades to her ribs, and on Monday digs a grave. The carer who gave the warning straps her and leaves. I spent chapter 17 waiting for her to fall down.
*Do:* collect it or shrink it. Either the rib costs her something on the night that the reader sees (she cannot run the last two hundred metres; she goes up the hill on a mattress), or Coralie listens to her back in chapter 17 and says what it is. If neither, cut the dead man down to one sentence.

**4. Chapters 6 to 9 run the same three scenes in rotation.**
A night under Inguma, a meeting in the steel room, Larrère at his window. The window opens a few centimetres in chapters 1, 5, 6, 9 (twice) and 12. "I'll be brief" is said eight times. By chapter 9 I knew the shape of a scene from its first line. Chapter 7 is the thinnest in the book: two nights of pushing, a calendar sum and a walk.
*Do:* fold chapter 7 into its neighbours and keep only the walk with the watch. Cut the six-line steel-room scene in chapter 9 that exists to state a rule. Cut the window scene in chapter 12, which repeats the chapter 6 threat. Keep the window going all the way down (5) and "I'll be brief" followed by nothing (16), which are the two that pay.

**5. Chapter 1 gives homework before it has earned it.**
"That gate lets water through both ways. On Monday the river was lower than my fields. You put the gate up, and my water ran out through it, down into the river. Last night I sat over mud." This is page six. I had a thing on a wardrobe and a stone with a date, and I was being taught drainage, registered post and an association's presidency. Chapter 2 then opens with three plants in one scene: lights, eels, fingers.
*Do:* keep the postman, because that is Larrère in one move. Cut Jeannot to "you let my water out" and his count of forty-one letters. Hold the association until chapter 5, when it matters who can order the gate.

**6. The "Either" that the village convicts her for is three lines long. (Chapter 12.)**
"'Either,' Maylis said. 'Then it's shut,' Jeannot said." She has the handle on and her weight on it, in daylight, with Jeannot above ground. Chapter 17 hangs her for this moment. But the word has been a joke in six chapters by then, so I heard the joke. The narrator gives me "The river went by under the boards" and nothing of her.
*Do:* go inside her for five lines here, as the book does at the dip. Let her reach for "open" and find what stops it: his vote, his two weeks' water, the car a hundred metres off, his face asking. Then the word.

**7. What Lesca's decisiveness cost her village arrives in subordinate clauses. (Chapters 3, 6, 11.)**
"Lesca had signed the gravel company's contract at this desk. The company had taken her mother's field with the rest, and the flood fields were a lake now." Also four houses knocked down, cameras on the gates, Kévin's six months, a deputy's sash in a plastic bag. Together they are the case against the woman who chooses. Scattered like this I did not add them up until I went looking.
*Do:* give Lesca one walk past the lake in chapter 3, with the reason she signed. Let Darrigrand's step come sooner.

**8. Too many names and numbers that look alike.**
Maylis, Maïté and Marie-Jo meet at one table in chapter 3: "Maïté Castagnet, Jean's widow, held her handbag in front of her with both hands and looked at the radiator." Add Madame Etcheto, Madame Darrigrand, Jean, Jeannot, Josiane, and Lesca, Larrère, Labat, Lassalle, each in two villages. The durations do the same: sixteen years with Coralie, twelve since the box, eleven across the landing, eighteen in the job. I kept re-sorting them.
*Do:* rename Marie-Jo and one of the L families. Cut eighteen. Let the landing date from "the February after the flood" wherever the number is not the point.

**9. The sixteen-year question is asked again after she has answered it. (Chapter 17.)**
"'Stay.' 'That's what you want,' Coralie said." Six lines later: "I'll ask it once more, with no box. What do you want, Maylis?" She has just said. The grave is a fine answer, but it reads as a second go at a question already used up. Around it, chapters 16 and 17 tick off Etcheto, Maïté, Larrère, the council, Larrère again, Darrigrand, Kévin, the bus, the café, Marie-Jo, Coralie, the steel room, the priest and Jeannot, each with a neat last line.
*Do:* ask the question once. Put the grave first, as the small thing she can say, and let "Stay" come out late, at the bag by the door. Fold chapter 16's council vote into a paragraph and give the room to Lesca and her Coralie.

**10. Larrère's reason on the night is unclear, so his exposure lands soft. (Chapters 12, 14, 16.)**
"He's in his hut, yes. He stays dry as long as you do what you're told." I had the folder on the cupboard, the two bowls, the car in the lane and the kitchen clock on the line. I still could not say whether he kept the gate shut for corn, for Jeannot's life, or for his evening. Maïté's charge in chapter 16 is the best speech she has, and I was working out what it proved while she said it.
*Do:* one line from him on the phone at 2:53 that shows which.

## 4. Five things that must not be touched

1. **Kévin's fingers.** The count in the bucket (2), "Five" in the headlights (14), "I wanted six, Maylis. I've never wanted anything so much" (17).
2. **Two fingers on the wrist.** From the first page, to "That's her. In this hand" on the landing (8), to the full half minute in room twelve (14).
3. **The phone log scene in chapter 11.** "Because you'd have told me."
4. **Bingo, both halls.** "She wants water." Madame Etcheto finishing the words and doing the harm. The dead with their corn, and apologies received.
5. **Jeannot's numbered points, through to letter 42 and the second shovel.**
