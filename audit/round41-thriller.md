# Round 41: Thriller

Read: `manuscript/00.md` to `17.md`, in order, in full. Nothing else.

## 1. Scores

- **Stakes: 7.** "The trapdoor was twenty-five minutes away, on foot, in the dark, with the dip under water. The bank had ten." (10) Concrete, measured, plural. Marked down because the human antagonist's hold is a job and a poaching charge, and the death rule is waived when it comes due.
- **Tension: 7.** "The last line on the chart said 36." (6) Chapters 6 and 12 to 14 are at the bar. Chapters 7 to 10 are not.
- **Progression: 6.** "'Either,' Maylis said." (2, 5, 6, 9, 12) The clock moves every chapter, the situation changes category only twice between 4 and 11.
- **Comedy: 7.** "'Either is not a person. I cannot summon Either to the station.'" (6)
- **Characters: 8.** "'I don't get into a bed over somebody who can't speak. I've lifted too many of them.'" (8)
- **The switch between dark and comic: 8.** "'Here,' he said. / 'I know you are.'" (14)
- **Prose: 7.** "Twelve years ago she had let his phone ring once and then done what she had come to do. Holding this wheel was the same thing." (13) Fluent and plain, but it argues its biggest turn.
- **The ending: 8.** "'I know what you picked. I was the other one.'" (17)
- **Clarity: 6.** "One night matters. If I'm in your village at 2:51 on Friday night, your bank goes, as mine was going. If I'm not, it holds." (9) The rule the climax stands on is announced, and the climax does not let the reader check it.

**Finished unpaid?** Yes. Hooked by the last line of 2; nearly lost at 4 and again at the vote in 9; could not stop from 11.

**Press it on a friend?** On one who loved The Good Place, yes. On one who reads Crouch for speed, not yet: they stall between 7 and 10 and they throw it at the platform in 12.

## 2. Against the comparisons

**Below Crouch on fear and escalation, level on premise speed, above on the choice.** Overall 7: below the bar as a thriller until chapter 11, at or over it afterwards.

- **Premise speed: level.** The other woman speaks through Maylis's mouth about 4,400 words in ("'Come back across. I've left you on that landing eleven years,' her mouth said"), and by the end of 3 she has written down what she wants: "and take the other one's bed, and Coralie Mounet in it." Dark Matter is no faster.
- **Fear of the double: below.** Dark Matter frightens because the other Jason lives in the house for a month and Daniela cannot tell. Here Coralie tells in under a minute ("'Who is that?'", 3), again on the landing ("'Which of you is that?'", 8), and has it on a ruled chart by 9. The book moves the fear to a better place, "'I'd have this one again'" (5), but the double never wins a round, so the middle has unease and no dread.
- **Escalation: below.** Crouch changes the size of the problem each act. Chapters 4 to 9 run one configuration five times: chair, Lesca drives, café, stairs or landing, Inguma. The one true reversal, "The rain came when Lesca came" (7), is the equal of a Good Place reversal: it re-reads the whole warning. It arrives 40 per cent in, and then the double goes off-stage until the night.
- **The choice: above.** Dark Matter hands its choice to Daniela. This book plays the lever twice, with opposite answers, charges one life each way, and excuses neither: "She wanted it not to be her." The Good Place makes the trolley a joke about philosophy; this makes it a gate with a name on the key.

## 3. The ten faults that most hold it back

**1. Chapter 12, the platform at 5. The book's hinge turns on one word and nothing is felt to turn it.**
"'Either,' Maylis said. / 'Then it's shut,' Jeannot said. 'That's how the vote left it.'" Then: "She lifted the handle off."
Jeannot is above ground, beside her, in daylight, with the handle fitted. Those are the exact conditions the book has asked for since 4. Kévin released her an hour earlier ("'Don't keep a gate shut for me'"). Nothing on the page holds her hand: no thought of the letter, the corn, her father's four turns, the car a hundred metres off. She then undoes the handle herself in a sentence. This is where a Crouch reader stops believing the night had to happen. The 2 p.m. falling tide, the hour she herself named in 9, passes without a line.
Do: make it a contest. Take the chain to the third or fourth tooth with him shouting. Put the weight on the platform with her, in his mouth or in her eyes. Let the word come out against her, as "Ei—" did in 5, and let her hear it. Then show the window closing: once his van is on the track the gate cannot move, and she knows it. One line at 2 p.m., with a shovel in her hand, saying the hour has gone.

**2. Chapters 9 and 14. The rule that decides the climax is declared, already disproved, and never confirmed.**
"One night matters. If I'm in your village at 2:51 on Friday night, your bank goes." Nobody learns this; Lesca says it. Maylis's plan follows: "'I'll sit up in the van, awake, until 4, when it gives up.'" Two pages earlier in the same chapter: "She lay on her back and stayed awake. At 2:51 it came down off the wardrobe." So the plan the reader is asked to hope for has just failed in front of them. Then in 14: "She had got Lesca out of her, but too late: the river was through the bank already." The reader cannot tell whether the fight for the handbrake changed anything.
Do: let the 2:51 rule be found, from the file's "02:50" against the two dry days, by Maylis, with the pad. Give the van plan one reason to work that the wardrobe night did not have, or replace it. And when Lesca leaves the body at 2:53, show the rule working: the rain stops on the windscreen. The bank then goes at 3:11 on water already in it, which is crueller and clear.

**3. Chapters 4 to 9. The middle repeats, and the double is absent for the three days before the night.**
Stairs (5), spare room (7), landing (8). Josiane's theory in 5, 6, 8. The oven in 1 and 9. After the kitchen table on Sunday 6th, Lesca does not drive Maylis again until 2:52 on Saturday; the body-snatch, the book's Crouch engine, is replaced by "'The association will be consulted. At 4. Here will do.'" The countdown device from 1, "cut eighteen marks down the rake", is never seen again.
Do: cut one of the three stair scenes (7's can be three lines inside 8). Give Lesca one incursion between Tuesday and Friday that takes something that cannot be handed back, so the reader sees "I may not get even the hand back" happen and does not only hear it. Bring the rake back once, with few marks left, or cut it from 1.

**4. Chapters 7 and 14. The lung is planted as fatal and costs nothing.**
Coralie: "He was dead before the doctor had his coat off." Maylis has the coins and the pink by Thursday. At the trapdoor: "Something gave in her side. She heard it go." She then runs 400 metres in three minutes, wades the Port, pushes wheelchairs until 4:10, walks the village on Sunday and cuts a grave on Monday.
Do: pay it or shrink it. Paying it is better: she goes down in the hall after "Forty-one", and Coralie, counting heads, has to choose whether to leave the row. That also puts Maylis on a stretcher the same morning as Lesca. Otherwise cut the dead man from Coralie's warning and keep the coins.

**5. Chapter 13. The hall of the dead lets Lesca leave for a joke.**
"'The mayor of Peyrebarthe sends her apologies… You can put that in the minutes as apologies received.'" Since 2 the rule has been that the one Inguma cannot take home dies. She holds the wheel against it, sits in the chair, and walks out. The line is funny and it voids eleven chapters of threat. The woman who brought a river and tried to take a body and a wife is charged an office and a medal.
Do: keep the line, make leaving cost. The seat has to be filled or paid for, and she has to see it: "Twelve. One, two. Somebody pulled a chair in" (15) is that price, heard from the dark with no cause attached. Move it into 13, in her sight, as the reason the voice stops calling her.

**6. Chapters 6 and 12. Larrère cannot frighten anybody.**
"'That's prison, for eels.'" His hold is an undated letter and a blank sheet, set against forty houses, and Kévin removes it before dusk on the night. Why he wants the gate shut is never clear enough to fear: the corn, or the kitchen he is ringing from. "The screen said *Castagnet, house*" is the best reveal in the book and it gets one line in the middle of a handbrake.
Do: decide what he is protecting and let the reader see it before 16. Give the screen a paragraph: she now knows where he is standing, and he is telling her the husband "stays dry as long as you do what you're told".

**7. Chapter 4. The rules meeting briefs the reader on what chapter 3 dramatised.**
"'The second item is how this works. There are three points, and I'll say them once.'" Shared knowledge, staying seated, the owner's right hand, Inguma's count: all four were shown in 3. Only the fourth point, the truth rule, is discovered on the page, and it is the only part of the scene with tension.
Do: keep the chair joke, cut the three points to a line of Maylis already knowing them, and spend the room on the truth rule and the date.

**8. The 2014 night is told six times, and the prologue spends its secret.**
00 shows the headlights down the track and "It rang once." After that the file (3), Lesca's account (4), the file again (6), the full replay (10), the call log (11) and the speech (15) uncover by degrees what the reader has held since word 365. Chapter 3 is inside Lesca's head and thinks "no one came to tell him" as if it were all she knew.
Do: keep the prologue to the last word. Cut 10's replay to what is new: the reflector, Coralie's car in the yard, the four minutes watching the red light go under. In 3, let Lesca feel the sentence as a thing she says.

**9. Chapter 13. The antagonist's turn is reasoned at the peak of the action.**
"She had told Maylis there was no both. She herself had wanted both, the bed and Castagnet. But the motion she had written at her own kitchen table was for a seat and a bed. There was no man in it." Three paragraphs of argument between ring eight and "I'm letting go". The image before them already does it: "the reflector was red, and no nearer than it had been twelve years ago."
Do: stop on the reflector, go to "'I'm letting go.'"

**10. Chapters 7, 8, 10. Three chapter ends that close where they should pull.**
10: "She crossed out the line about the trapdoor and wrote it again at the top, above the key, with *before dark* after it." 7 ends on a paragraph that explains its own reversal ("But he would only need saving if Lesca was here on Friday night"). 8 ends on a pillow going down for the second time in two chapters.
Do: end 10 on "'And six. Bugger off.'" End 7 on the trapdoor sounding hollow and "'Twenty-five.'" End 8 on Coralie's unanswered "'What is he to you?'"

## 4. Five things that must not be touched

1. **Chapter 6, the cut between the council table and the bed:** Larrère untimed for the first time in eleven years while the chart goes 64, 58, 47, 36 and Coralie's thumb stays over the doctor's number.
2. **Chapter 11, the call log:** "'2:51,' she said. 'I've known that for twelve years.'" and "'Because you'd have told me.'"
3. **The rings:** "It rang once" (00), nine rings at 5 a.m. (10), the count of eight through the fight for the handbrake (12 and 13), and what is on the screen when she answers.
4. **Chapter 14 from the dip to "Forty-one":** the handle left at eye level, "'Don't tell me what he was'", Kévin counting five in his own headlights, the hatbox on the wardrobe, the empty chair in the hall with the coffee pot on.
5. **Chapter 17, Coralie going:** "'Don't give me the measurement. I could. I'm not going to.'" and the car door shutting on a pillow. It is the cost, and it is the right size.
