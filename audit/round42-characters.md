# Round 42: Characters

Read: `manuscript/00.md` to `17.md`, in order, in full. Nothing else.

## 1. Scores

- **Stakes: 7.** "The trapdoor was twenty-five minutes away, on foot, in the dark, with the dip under water. The bank had ten." The sum is clear and has a face on each side. But the second village's flood rests on a rule the book proves with two dry days and then admits is thin: "It was only two days. Bordenave would have called that weather."
- **Tension: 7.** "She had to be awake at 2:51." Chapters 12 to 14 hold. Chapters 7 to 9 do not: Inguma arrives the same way five times.
- **Progression: 6.** "'Either,' Maylis said." She says it, or fails to, eight times (chapters 1, 2, 4, 5 twice, 6, 9, 12). Only the one on the platform changes anything. Until chapter 12 her line repeats where it should climb.
- **Comedy: 7.5.** "I don't cook Either. There's no recipe for it. Soup or duck."
- **Characters: 7.5.** "She has the kitchen door open before I've switched the engine off. Nobody else in this village has ever opened one before I knocked." When the villain gets that line the ensemble is at 8.5. Lesca is 8, Coralie 8. Maylis is 6.5 until chapter 12, and she is the lead.
- **The switch between dark and comic: 8.** "'Here,' he said. 'I know you are.'" Monsieur Daguerre's running joke is paid in a flooded corridor and neither the joke nor the flood gets smaller.
- **Prose: 7.** "She heard her on every stair coming down, all eleven, slower than she had gone up." Against it: every feeling in the book is turned into a count or an object, by every character, in the same way.
- **The ending: 7.5.** "Then he stepped over the string in his rubber boots and began to dig." Maylis pays in full. Lesca gets "Good night, Maylis" on the page where she is removed, and has done worse.
- **Clarity: 6.** "Lesca's wrist went out from under the left hand. The right hand took the dial." I had to stop and work out whose hand was whose, more than once. I also do not know why Lesca is let out of the hall of the dead.

**Finished unpaid:** yes. **Pressed on a friend:** yes, on one who likes a village comedy. Not on a Crouch reader, who would stall in chapter 7.

## 2. Against the comparisons

**Above Crouch on the people around the leads.** Crouch has nobody you would miss outside the marriage. This book has six: Jeannot, Madame Etcheto, Larrère, Kévin, Maïté, Marie-Jo. Each has a way of speaking that is theirs alone (Jeannot numbers his points, Sarthou never contracts a verb, Maïté cannot finish a sentence, Marie-Jo talks to the wall).

**Level with The Good Place on "each wrong in their own way".** Maylis will not choose. Lesca chose and has told half of it for twelve years. Larrère will not sign. Jeannot will not be told. One Coralie never asked; the other would not wake a woman of ninety-four. Nobody is right, and nobody is a fool. The reversals re-read what came before: "You're the water" re-reads the rain, and "2:51. I've known that for twelve years" re-reads the landing.

**Below Dark Matter on the double.** In Dark Matter the other self wants your marriage and the reader is afraid for it from the first chapter to the last. Here that fear is set at the end of chapter 3 ("and take the other one's bed, and Coralie Mounet in it") and is over by chapter 8, when Coralie herself sends Lesca back down the stairs. After that the two versions have nothing to fight over except a gate. And the envy runs one way only: Lesca wants Maylis's life for the whole book, Maylis wants Lesca's for one afternoon.

## 3. The ten faults, worst first

**1. Maylis crosses for a day and never meets the other Coralie (ch. 6).**
"But all of it came with Kévin's cap and one pillow."
That sentence is a summary of the scene the chapter needed. Lesca gets seven scenes with Maylis's Coralie. Maylis gets none with Lesca's. She sees the two Kévins, the two Maïtés and the two Larrères, and learns what the desk costs from a cap and a count of pillows. The one person who would show her the price is offstage. So her temptation is never tested against the thing she loves, and it dies the same afternoon. *Fix:* cut the ten minutes on the office floor and send her home at noon. Coralie calls her Madame le maire, takes her wrist between finger and thumb, and goes back across the landing. Maylis wants to say the sentence Lesca said in her bed, and it is not hers to say. Then let the wanting of the desk survive into chapters 7 to 9, so the envy runs both ways.

**2. Maylis is cargo for the middle of the book (ch. 5, 7, 8).**
"All Maylis had to do was keep the mouth shut until it came, so that Lesca couldn't give it anything else to count."
In the three big comic scenes (the car window, the café, the bingo) the lead's whole part is to resist. Everything she does between chapters 4 and 12 is a refusal, except chapter 6, where she blackmails Jeannot, crosses, and lands the back-lane line on Larrère. Chapter 6 shows how alive she is when she is after something. *Fix:* give her one aim of her own in each hijacked scene, pursued with the right hand while Lesca talks. In the café she is there to get the complaint withdrawn. At the bingo she has come to get Madame Etcheto to teach her the words for calling Inguma back. Then the jaw is a fight between two purposes and not a woman holding her teeth shut.

**3. The two women never have it out (ch. 4, 9, 10, 13, 17).**
"Lesca wasn't in it yet, but the night of the flood came to Maylis anyway."
The centre of Lesca's life reaches Maylis as a download, with nobody in the room. Their meetings are procedure jokes and logistics. Their last scene is twenty lines. This is the book's title relationship and it is the thinnest one in it. *Fix:* in chapter 10 make Lesca tell it, in the steel room, where a sentence that is a dodge sticks. Let her try "No one came to tell him" and have it stop in her mouth. The book's best device is then used on its central lie, in front of the only person who is her. "You rang once" in chapter 13 keeps its force.

**4. Lesca's worst act is excused by the plot (ch. 13, 15, 16).**
"'The mayor of Peyrebarthe sends her apologies,' she said, in the voice she used for the prefecture."
By chapter 9 she knows she brings the river, and she comes on Friday anyway, "to stay". That kills Madame Etcheto and floods forty houses. Chapter 2 laid down the rule: "The one it cannot take home dies in that bed." When it comes due she leaves the hall on a quip, another woman takes a seat, and the reckoning is "It was you." "Yes." Then two chapters punish her for 2014, and her own Coralie never hears about Friday. *Fix:* she does not talk her way out. She hears twelve called, hears the chair pulled in, and understands that is why she is being let go. On the stage she says both sums. Or in the bedroom she tells Coralie, who then says "Good night, Maylis" knowing it. The line would be worth twice as much.

**5. Maylis never says her reason (ch. 14, 17).**
"She wanted it not to be her."
It is the truest sentence in the book and only the reader gets it. To Kévin she says "That wasn't why." To Jeannot, "At 3 I never tried the handle." To Coralie, "I had to pick one. I picked." Lesca says her sentence to a full square. Maylis says hers to nobody, so Coralie leaves on less than the reader knows, and the question asked for sixteen years gets a fine answer (the grave) in place of the true one. *Fix:* she says it in the kitchen, before "Stay." Coralie can still go.

**6. What it costs Lesca to be the one who signs comes too late (ch. 11).**
"She had counted her ponies up the ramp of that truck."
The lake, the ponies, the four houses and Darrigrand's swept step are what make Lesca a chooser with a bill, and they arrive two chapters before she leaves the book. In chapter 3 she reads as a wronged mayor who mends a widow's roof. *Fix:* Arlette is already at the stove in chapter 3. Put the ponies there. Put Darrigrand and his broom in the square in chapter 3, where she walks past the stone.

**7. "Either" has no root, and the root is in plain view (ch. 9, 13).**
"He marked a number. He didn't look up, and she took the hand away."
Every man in the village beats her with her father ("Your father turned it with one hand", "He'd have cut his hand off first", "Your father slept on that platform"). She is "little Lesca" and "the village's girl" at forty-three. The father will not look up for either daughter. And neither woman thinks one thing about him in seventeen chapters. Her mother does not exist in Maylis's village at all. *Fix:* six lines of the day the key and the coat were handed over, and what she was not asked. One line on where her mother is.

**8. Coralie's own want arrives as a speech (ch. 8).**
"I held my first hand on that floor, at twenty, under a night nurse called Ithurbide."
For seven chapters she is the woman who asks what Maylis wants. Then she states a biography on a landing. The showing (the list on a cord, the tape pressed flat with a thumb) comes four chapters after the telling. *Fix:* the opening of chapter 10 is in her point of view and has room. Better, give the chapter 2 corridor one beat where she runs the night and likes it.

**9. Everybody dodges the same way (throughout).**
"Maïté ... looked at the radiator." "The deputy mayor looked at the water jug." "The priest looked at his shoes." "Marie-Jo kept her eyes on the wall." "Lesca looked at the top of the wardrobe."
When six people evade by looking at an object, it is the narrator's habit and not theirs. It is a running joke for Larrère (the church) and Marie-Jo (the wall). Keep those two. Give the others their own dodge, as Maïté already has in her broken sentences.

**10. The body-part grammar shuts Maylis out of her own chapters (ch. 5, 8).**
"The legs stopped inside the door. The eyes had gone to the back of his neck, and Lesca left them there."
"The mouth", "the jaw", "the left hand", "the legs": it is consistent, and it reads like an operating manual at the moments a reader most needs to know what Maylis feels. *Fix:* cut half of it. Where it stays, follow it with her reaction in her own terms, as the book does once and well: "It was red from the weather and it needed a barber."

## 4. Five things that must not be touched

1. **The phone-log scene, chapter 11.** "'Why not?' 'Because you'd have told me.'" And "I wanted you in the house, and I thought I could have you without it, if I kept a landing between us."
2. **Jeannot, whole.** The six points down the phone ending "And six. Bugger off." "You've got your shed. Leave me my hole." "I was in no danger, and I'll say so to anyone." The second shovel.
3. **The two refusals of a way out in chapter 14.** "She wanted it not to be her." And Kévin counting five in his headlights, with "I wanted six, Maylis. I've never wanted anything so much" in chapter 17.
4. **Madame Etcheto and the number twelve.** "Staff to wait. Staff not to join in." "That's my door." "Twelve. One, two." The hatbox on the wardrobe.
5. **The shopping pad, from first box to last.** "Eggs are what Either gets in this house." "And then there's me, as a distance." "I know that word. I've cooked it. That was Either, with the river in it." The car door shutting on a pillow.
