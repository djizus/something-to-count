# Round 7: Characters

Read: `manuscript/01.md` to `17.md`, in order, in full. Nothing else.

## 1. Scores

- **Stakes: 7.** "Behind it were forty houses, and the home, with forty-two people in their beds. Out on the meadows there was one man." The village's stakes are exact. What each woman can lose to the other is never fixed (fault 2).
- **Tension: 6.** "02:49. She was awake. She laid her left hand on the wheel and touched off the fingers, and made five." Chapters 12 to 14 run at 9. Chapters 1 to 8 run at 5, on a calendar and no fear.
- **Progression: 6.** "She had a date now, and a drowned man, and a plan that wanted an order. She knew nobody whose job it was to give one." That sentence closes a scene in chapter 5 and is still the position in chapter 12.
- **Comedy: 7.** "'Either is not a person. I cannot summon Either to the brigade.'"
- **Characters: 7.** "'I know what you're called,' Arlette said. 'I'm the last that uses it.'" The second rank is at 8. The two leads together are at 6.
- **The switch between dark and comic: 8.** "'Here,' he said. / 'I know you are.'" Said in a flooded corridor, and neither half damages the other.
- **Prose: 7.** "On Friday Lesca meant to be sitting in the steel chair when Maylis woke, so as to wake inside her at the gate, drive the track to the tonne herself, and keep the bed afterwards." Chapters 11, 12 and 13 each open by announcing what a woman wants, in the narrator's voice, before a scene can show it.
- **The ending: 7.** "Then he stepped over the string, in his waders, and got down into it." The last image is right. The two women's own ending is thirty lines in chapter 15 and is not one.
- **Clarity: 6.** "Maylis had been awake when Inguma pinned her, so the body was still hers. Lesca, coming in from the chair, got one arm." A rule issued at the moment the plot needs it.

**Finished unpaid:** yes. If I had stopped, it would have been in chapter 2, where Lesca is a title, a timer and a stone before she is anybody.
**Press it on a friend:** yes, telling them it starts in chapter 4. On the reader the bar names, no: that reader is gone by the winch meeting in chapter 3.

## 2. Against the comparisons

**Above Crouch on the people. Below Crouch on the double as a threat. Below The Good Place.**

- **Above Crouch: the wife.** Daniela in Dark Matter is the thing stolen, and she sleeps with the wrong Jason without knowing. Coralie finds the intruder with two fingers on a wrist, is offered the better version, says "I'd have you tomorrow", and sends her downstairs: "I don't get into a bed over somebody who can't speak. I've lifted too many of them." Crouch has no page like it.
- **Below Crouch: the other self.** Jason2 takes the house, the wife and the son, and the reader is afraid of him for three hundred pages. Lesca gets twenty minutes, a soup and a landing. After chapter 9 her claim on the bed is dead, and the book goes on as if it were live.
- **Below The Good Place: everyone is wrong the same way.** Its four are wrong in four ways, out loud, and change each other by talking. Here Maylis ("Either"), Coralie ("Because you'd have told me"), Maïté ("Nor did you"), Larrère ("Nothing came") and Lesca ("No one came to tell him") are all wrong by withholding. It is one fault across five people. And the two leads, who are the premise, get five short scenes together.
- **Level with The Good Place: one reversal.** The call log in chapter 10 re-reads Inguma's hour, the landing, eleven Decembers in the second row and the sentence "No one came to tell him."

The lens questions, answered:

- **Distinct?** Yes, by ear. Maylis prices ("That's one. It's free."). Lesca moves motions ("Those in favour."). I never needed the tag.
- **Wanted-for?** Lesca from chapter 4, in writing. Maylis from chapter 8 ("I want that"), earned by a postman who walks past her to a man in a car.
- **Softened or excused?** Both, in the last three chapters. Lesca by who receives her confession (fault 3). Maylis by the narrator and by a ditch (fault 1).
- **Described from outside as a woman?** Neither lead: no face, no age, no body but a rib and a palm, and work in every scene. The exception is Maïté in Maylis's village (fault 9).
- **Who would be missed?** Jeannot, Madame Etcheto, Kévin, Arlette, Maïté in black. Not Tastet, Père Honoré, Nadine, Bixente or Marie-Jo (fault 10).
- **Which relationship carries it, and does it pay?** Maylis and Coralie, with Lesca and her Coralie as its shadow. It pays in chapter 9 and chapter 10. It underpays in chapter 17 (fault 6).
- **Does the reader care who lives?** About Jeannot, by chapter 6. About Madame Etcheto, from "She reported him." Her death is the best page in the book.

## 3. The ten faults, worst first

**1. The moment Maylis is guilty passes in three sentences, and the moment she is punished for is decided by a ditch. (Ch. 12, 14, 15)**
"Maylis did not get out and fit the handle, since nobody had told her to. She knew that for the word she wrote on the shopping pad. It was Either."
At five on Friday it is daylight, Jeannot is above ground at her window, the key is on her belt. This is the plan Lesca gave her, and it is the charge Madame Darrigrand brings in chapter 17. The book spends three sentences on it and has the narrator supply the diagnosis. Then at three the choice is made for her by mud, and afterwards she is let off twice: "She had given it ten minutes. It had held sixteen," and "I'd have been back at the handle in time, but my own van was sat across his track."
*Do:* write the five o'clock scene at length. She gets out, fits the handle, has it on the shaft, and takes it off again with Jeannot watching and the folder on a passenger seat in her head. Strike the diagnosis; the reader will make it. At the dip, give "She could not be the one who did not come" a page. In chapter 15 cut "I'd have been back at the handle in time." She must not be told she nearly managed both.

**2. Neither woman is ever a danger to the other, because the terms of the trade are never stated. (Ch. 5, 8, 11, 13)**
"Inguma fetches. It comes for whichever of us has gone visiting and takes her home, always." Against: "'Have Friday,' Maylis said. 'I'll have yours.'" And: "Before she took that life."
Is the trade for a night or for good? Rule three says a night. Chapter 11 says a life. Arlette and Lesca's Coralie behave as if she may not come back. I could not fear a theft whose size I did not know. This is where the book loses to Dark Matter.
*Do:* add a fourth point in chapter 5, in Lesca's numbered voice: what it takes to stay. Say in chapter 8 that the trade is permanent, and that each is giving away a Coralie. Then Lesca sitting on Inguma in chapter 13 is the act the whole book has pointed at, and her letting go costs a life, not an arm.

**3. The confession costs offices, not people, and Larrère's kitchen takes the weight. (Ch. 15, 16)**
"Nobody came up the steps. Nobody shouted." Then, seven hours after "I drowned him": "'I've wanted somebody to say it.'" And: "'That back gutter's coming over again,' she said. 'Look at it before you go.'"
Lesca loses a medal, keys and a sash. Her mother declines to hear it, her Coralie sets a date, and the widow hands her a ladder. The one person entitled to hate her turns out to have had a suitcase packed and a lover in the kitchen, and aims the anger at him: "He rang you from here. Guy." The reader already had that from the ticking clock in chapter 14. Repeating it here moves the blame at the exact moment Lesca has taken it.
*Do:* let Maïté be the wronged widow for one full scene before she is anything else. She hears "I don't know" and shuts the door. The gutter and Guy at the gate come another night, or Lesca does the gutter unasked and unthanked. One person in the square should say something to her face.

**4. The two women have no ending with each other. (Ch. 15)**
"Neither of them looked at the chair Lesca had taken, and neither sat. The door stayed shut, and Inguma did not come back."
Their last exchange is about the sum. Neither says what she took from the other, though the last two chapters show it: Lesca goes to a door and asks, Maylis asks for a job. Neither mentions Coralie, whom they fought over for nine chapters. The premise is two versions of one person, and it closes on a tally and a door.
*Do:* double the scene. In a room where neither can lie, each says one thing about the other's Coralie and one about herself. Their last words should be to each other.

**5. The marriage is shown at ease once, and Maylis owns nothing that is not her father's. (Ch. 1, then throughout)**
"Sixteen years, and I've had one straight answer out of you, and that was me."
That straight answer is the one time Maylis wanted something, and the book never shows it. After the eggs in chapter 1, every scene between them is Coralie asking and Maylis failing. Meanwhile each detail that makes Maylis is inherited: "the tall L her father had put on every delivery note," the key "where her father had carried it," the shovel "where her father had kept it." The village sees her as her father's girl. So does the narrator.
*Do:* give half a page of 2010, the question Coralie asked and the word Maylis used. Give Maylis one thing she is good at or fond of that no Lesca before her had. One more evening of ease before chapter 5.

**6. Coralie leaves without saying what only she can say. (Ch. 17)**
"That was Either, with the river in it."
Lesca's Coralie has lived twelve years with "Or you opened it because I was in that building." Maylis's Coralie was in that building while Maylis walked the other way. That is the mirror the two villages were built to make, and she files it under indecision.
*Do:* one line. She was under the bank with forty-two, and Maylis went to a man who had told her to bugger off. She need not know about the other Coralie. The reader does.

**7. Maylis judges the one ring and is never handed her own unanswered telephone. (Ch. 9, 13, 15)**
"'You rang once,' she said." And earlier, from Marie-Jo: "I'd rung you at four and at five." And Maylis: "'I was thinking.'"
Lesca cut a call off after one ring. Maylis let two ring out on a mower in 2020 and lost a village its mayor. Lesca was in that body at the bar and heard it. In chapter 15 Maylis gets the last word, "I let it go to nine," and Lesca "set nothing" against it.
*Do:* let Lesca set it. One sentence, in the room where it cannot be a lie.

**8. Larrère is a man in one village and a folder in the other. (Ch. 8, 17)**
Lesca's village: "He had been saving it since 2015, and there was less of it than he had thought." Maylis's village, the morning after his order drowned a woman: "'Received and accepted yesterday,' he said. 'That gate never moved. You did, which comes to the same. The key goes in my tray.'"
What he wants, which is Maïté, is reported by her and never seen in him. In chapter 17 he walks through silt to date a letter.
*Do:* in chapter 17 put him where he can see the Bayonne bus leave. Let the letter be dated by a man who has just lost the only thing he wanted.

**9. Maïté in Maylis's village is a housecoat, two bowls, a suitcase and a bus. (Ch. 5, 17)**
"She gave the suitcase up to the driver. She had the fare ready in her glove, in coins."
In Lesca's village she is one of the best people in the book. In Maylis's she is known by which man is in her kitchen, and she leaves without a line. Why that morning? I had to guess.
*Do:* give her one sentence at the bus stop, to Maylis, who brought her chrysanthemums for a living husband.

**10. Marie-Jo's quarrel is never told, and four villagers are names. (Ch. 2, 9, 15)**
"Marie-Jo had been her first deputy, until the night she handed her sash back across the council table in a carrier bag."
Over what? The lake, the four houses? She is in both villages, she is Kévin's mother, she is the mayor one of them never got, and I cannot say what she is like. Tastet is a hat. Père Honoré is a seal on a secret. Nadine and Bixente are a rota.
*Do:* name the vote that filled the carrier bag and give Marie-Jo the case against Lesca's sums in her own mouth, once. Fold Nadine into Bixente.

## 4. Five things that must not be touched

1. **The oven.** Coralie doing Larrère into it in chapter 1, and failing to in chapter 17: "'I'll be brief,' she said... 'The—'".
2. **The landing, chapter 9.** "'Then she can say so in the morning.'"
3. **The call log, chapter 10.** "Outgoing. Castagnet J., mobile. Not answered. Two fifty-one." And "Because you'd have told me."
4. **Room twelve and the hall, chapter 14.** Two fingers on that wrist, "Forty-one," and Kévin's hand: "He said it as a man turns up a card he had hoped was another." With its answer in chapter 17: "I wanted six, Maylis."
5. **Jeannot.** The blackmail in chapter 7 ("He had left the little off. She heard it go."), the ninth ring in chapter 11 ("And six. Bugger off."), and letter forty-two.
