**Finished unpaid?** Yes, from the last line of chapter 4. Before that I was reading on the creature and on Coralie, and at the winch meeting in 3 I checked how many pages were left.

**Press it on a friend?** On one who reads slowly and likes village comedy, yes, with a warning about the first three chapters. On a Crouch reader, no.

## 2. Against the comparisons

**Below, as a read. Level from chapter 10 to the end.**

1. **Crouch states a rule once and never changes it.** In *Dark Matter* the box has one rule and the climax is that rule multiplied. Here the chair has three numbered rules (5), a negotiation I could not parse (8), and three new facts at the climax (13): a woman pinned awake keeps her body, the visitor gets one arm, and a hall of the dead has a chair with her name on it. I felt the book reaching for what it needed.
2. **Crouch makes me afraid for the marriage because the impostor can keep it.** Here the visitor is fetched home by nightfall and the owner is watching from her right hand. The landing scene (9) is as good as anything in *Dark Matter* for what it does to the three of them, but I was never afraid it would be permanent.
3. **The reversals re-read the book, as The Good Place does.** "The cellar was dry" (14) turns the rescue inside out. "Castagnet, house" (14) turns a clock ticking down a telephone (11) and a man who "did not altogether want it said" (10) into a second guilty party. Maïté's "left" (16) re-reads the stone. That is level with the comparison. The central problem is the trolley problem, which the series also played for laughs; this book's answer (the one who will not choose kills a third person, and the one man was never in danger) is its own.

**What I did not understand:** how either woman makes sure she is the one "left sitting"; what "keep the bed afterwards" could mean when Inguma always fetches; why Lesca's name is called in the hall of the dead; why the flood arrives twelve years later to the day (the book raises it in 5 and drops it on a joke about the winch); and, for two chapters, whether the other village was in 2014 or 2026.

**Where I skimmed:** the Darrigrand step and the priest in 4; the road signs in 6 and 7; Sarthou at the microphone and the 2020 election list in 9; the tie-break procedure in 10; the telephone call to Hontaube in 12.

**Where I could not stop:** the first crossing (4); Coralie's "Two fifty-one" (10); all of 11, 12 and 14; the sentence at the microphone (15).

**What I will remember in a month:** a thing the size of a ewe that has to be given something to count. "Eggs is what Either gets in this house." A winch that stops at nineteen under seven men. "And six. Bugger off." A dry cellar. Kévin counting his fingers in the flood and wanting six. Monsieur Daguerre calling "Here". "Forty-one."

**What I will not:** the chair's rules, Hontaube, the signs, the gravel lease, Tastet, the four demolished houses, which Sarthou lives where.

## 3. Ten faults, worst first

**1. The rules of the chair cannot be held, and the climax adds to them.** (5, 8, 13)
"Two. Whoever stays sitting goes with the one who wakes." Then: "Maylis had been awake when Inguma pinned her, so the body was still hers. Lesca, coming in from the chair, got one arm".
The whole Friday depends on who is sitting when, and I could not work out how either woman controls it. In 8 both seem to trade nights and then both claim the chair. In 13 the fight for one arm rests on a rule I was given in the sentence that needed it.
*Do:* cut the rule to one sentence a reader can say back ("the one who wakes first carries the other"), and have Maylis say it back in her own pricing terms in 5. Before the Friday, give one plain paragraph: what each means to do, and what happens to the gate in each case. Show "pinned awake, she keeps the body and the visitor gets a hand" on a small scale earlier, so that 13 spends it.

**2. The week the countdown was built for is missing.** (between 9 and 12)
"He had been told the week before, through his own car window, that he would say exactly that." (12)
In 6 Lesca tells a café the river will reach the line on Tuesday week and tells Larrère what he will say. Tuesday the 8th comes, the river reaches the line, he says it word for word, and I get it in the pluperfect. Maylis's village vanishes from the 6th to the 10th. That is the scene where a village finds that the woman who thinks she is the mayor was right, and does nothing.
*Do:* write Tuesday the 8th in Maylis's village. The yellow from Pau, Larrère's sentence, Lafitte's pad getting an entry it cannot laugh at, Sarthou laying his bags. It is the missing middle step of the thriller and a comic scene the book has already paid for.

**3. Nobody can keep the other's life, so the taking has no teeth.** (4, 5, 13)
"*That the mayor remain seated, and take the other one's bed, and Coralie Mounet in it.*" against "Inguma fetches. It comes for whichever of us has gone visiting and takes her home, always."
Lesca's longest stay is a day of counting. In 13 she means to "keep the bed afterwards" and sets Inguma nothing to count at all. I never feared Maylis would lose Coralie for good.
*Do:* give Lesca a count that could be endless (the book has "the sand on the coast, from Bayonne to the border"), have her set it on the Friday, and have Maylis understand it means the right hand for life. Arlette's ponies then undo something, where now they undo nothing I knew of.

**4. Maylis deserts her gate in an aside and comes back in another.** (8, 12)
"There was also a woman who would be at the home that night, thirty metres from the patch, and Maylis left her out of the sum." And the return: "she no longer meant to wake in anybody's bed but her own. Before she left the house she had put her pencil through the handover".
For eight days she intends to leave the Port and Coralie to the river so as to be mayor for a morning. That is the worst thing she does, and it and its undoing are both told in passing. I did not notice the first and did not feel the second.
*Do:* make "Then I'll sit" say what it costs, in her words, with Coralie named. Play the pencil going through the handover as a scene at the head of 12.

**5. The opening hook misleads, and the premise is said plainly nowhere in the first three chapters.** (1 to 3)
"That village had not been spared December 2014. It had not had it yet." (2)
I read that as a village still in 2014. "We had a dry winter here in 2014" does not arrive until 5. The two women do not meet until 9,000 words in, a fifth of the book.
*Do:* end 2 by saying it straight: it is 2026 there too, and the rain that began on her 24 November twelve years ago began there this morning. Bring the Thursday envelope in 3 on stage instead of "She had slit her own envelope on Thursday".

**6. I learned what Lesca risked only after she had risked it.** (10, 13)
"'Lesca, Maylis,' the voice called from the far end, as it gave the numbers".
When she lets go of the arm she gives up wants: rain, a bed, a man who owes her diesel. Then she is in the hall of the dead with a chair laid for her, and I did not know that was the price. A sacrifice I can only cost afterwards does not land as one.
*Do:* in 10, where the hall first shows, say in one line whom the empty chair takes. Then "Lesca let go" is a woman stepping off something.

**7. The book has to be read with a calendar.** (3/4, 6/9, 9/10)
"On Saturday it rained, and Lesca woke in Maylis's bed and drove Maylis's body, with Maylis awake in the right hand, for as long as Inguma took to count" (9), which is the opening of 6 again.
Chapters overlap, run back, and tell one night twice. I was tracking weekday, date, village, and the matching line of a 2014 file. Chapter 9 ends on a Saturday and 10 opens on "Wednesday" with no word for the four days. The second possession day repeats the first: a list in a café, a warning nobody takes.
*Do:* head each chapter with the date and the village. Give the second visit one new thing the first could not do.

**8. Side business sits where I wanted the story.** (4, 9, 12)
"'Is this a request, or a notification?' the woman said. 'I've got a tray for each, and a book.'" (12)
It is funny, and it is 450 words at the head of the chapter where the river is over the line. Chapter 9 carries a withdrawal of a letter, a job application, a walk to the tonne, a loto with five turns, and the landing. The road signs come up three times and do nothing.
*Do:* cut the signs and the 2020 list. Halve the Hontaube call. In 4, move the Darrigrand step and the priest so they do not stand between the first crossing and the motion.

**9. I could not tell what Maylis was buying when she threatened Jeannot.** (7)
"'The complaint at the brigade. Take it back. And the one in the mayor's tray.'"
The cost is exact ("He had left the little off. She heard it go."). The gain is not stated. I worked out later that it is why he bolts the hatch on her on the Friday.
*Do:* one line of her reckoning before she walks into the café: what the complaint does to her with Larrère's letter in the folder.

**10. Small things that put me out of the book.**
"she watched her sentence go back through six thousand people" (15), in a village with one outdoor employee and two hundred chairs. "The Port", which I took for a harbour until 6. Prefect, brigade, commune, list, "earlies", "the code", never glossed. Two men called Castagnet, Jean there and Jeannot here, before I knew there was a here and a there.
*Do:* cut the six thousand to what the square holds. Give the Port one sentence of ground in 1. Gloss each office once, in passing, as "It's where a village keeps its mayor" does.

## 4. Five things that must not be touched

1. **Chapter 14, entire.** The clock times, the van in the dip, the dry cellar, the key thrown back along the crown, Daguerre's "Here", Kévin's five fingers, room twelve, "Forty-one."
2. **The shopping pad.** From "Eggs is what Either gets in this house" to "That was Either, with the river in it", and "What do you want, Maylis?" answered at last with a grave.
3. **Jeannot Castagnet.** The numbered letters, "Put it in forty-two", the six points down the telephone at five in the morning, No. 42 brought by hand, and the second shovel.
4. **Coralie on the landing in 10, and the night in 11.** "Outgoing. Castagnet J., mobile. Not answered. Two fifty-one." It explains the creature, the hour and the marriage in four words, and 11 then shows it without a line of comment.
5. **The stone with no verb, through to its answer.** "It reads that she opened him" (2), "I knew that he was… and I drowned him" with the loudspeaker a moment late (15), Maïté's "left" (16), and Larrère at the garden gate saying "I'll be brief" and finding nothing.
