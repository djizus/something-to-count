# Round 8: Characters

Read: `manuscript/01.md` to `17.md`, in order, in full. Nothing else opened but the brief.

## 1. Scores

- **Stakes: 6.** "If she carried that one, she would stay in Maylis's body for good, and her own would die in its bed." (10) The sentence counts Lesca's body and leaves out the woman who would spend her life in one hand. The village's stakes are exact. The taking of a life is never made frightening.
- **Tension: 7.** "She was two hundred metres short of the bank when it went." (14) Chapters 11 to 14 are at 9. Chapters 1 to 5 rest on a thing that only sits.
- **Progression: 6.** "First business is how this works, so that it's said once and we both have it the same." (5) The two women first speak in chapter 5 of 17. By that point in Dark Matter the double is in the house.
- **Comedy: 7.** "Either is not a person. I cannot summon Either to the brigade." (7) Funny and built, in one key: everybody is funny through paper.
- **Characters: 7.** "I don't get into a bed over somebody who can't speak. I've lifted too many of them." (9) Coralie 9, Lesca 8, the village 8, Maylis 6 until chapter 7, the two women as a relationship 5.
- **The switch between dark and comic: 8.** "'Five,' he said. He said it as a man turns up a card he had hoped was another." (14)
- **Prose: 7.** "The tall L came out of the wrong hand like a fence post set by a boy." (7) Against it: Maylis "priced" something seven times, and Larrère has "his mouth a little open" three times.
- **The ending: 7.** "Then he stepped over the string, in his waders, and got down into it." (17) Maylis pays in full. Lesca pays with an office and is handed the pillow in one sentence.
- **Clarity: 5.** "The one who wakes first takes the other home in her." (3) The rule is given three times and I still worked it out on paper. Chapters 8, 9 and 11 hold two plans for the Friday night that cannot both be Maylis's.

**Finished unpaid:** yes. I would have stopped at chapter 3 without the brief, and could not have stopped after chapter 7.
**Pressed on a friend:** no. I would read a friend chapter 14 aloud and not hand over the book.

## 2. Against the comparisons

**Below the bar, by one relationship.**

- **Above Crouch on the spouse.** Daniela in Dark Matter is what Jason wants. Coralie wants something that is neither woman: "I posted it this afternoon, and I posted it for me." She also sets the book's moral line, on a landing, in two sentences. No Crouch wife does that.
- **Below Crouch on the double.** Jason2 is a thief and the reader is afraid of him in the marriage bed. Lesca intends the same theft and writes it as a motion: "That the mayor remain seated, and take the other one's bed, and Coralie Mounet in it." Nobody in the book calls it theft, least of all the woman it is done to. The reader is never afraid for that bed.
- **Below The Good Place on how people are wrong.** Eleanor, Chidi, Tahani and Jason fail in four ways. Peyrebarthe fails in one: Lesca's minutes, Jeannot's registered letters, Sarthou's file, Ducasse's column, Tastet's diary, Lafitte's score pad, the trays at Hontaube. Each is good. Together they are one joke with seven owners. The Good Place also changes its people by putting them in a room together. Maylis and Lesca change in separate rooms and never tell each other.
- **Level with The Good Place on the reversal that re-reads.** 02:51 turning out to be the minute of a call, and Madame Etcheto alive in the front row the morning after she drowned, both send the reader back through the book.

## 3. The ten faults, worst first

**1. The two women never have their scene. (8, 10, 13, 15)**
"'For a day,' Lesca said. 'I'm not coming over for a day. I'm coming to stay.'" The chapter ends before Maylis answers. Two nights later she has learned that Lesca would have got into bed with Coralie in her body, and she "gave that the time she would have given a reading on the gauge", then asks for the mayor's chair. Lesca has used her mouth, opened her palm, lost her her job and tried to take her partner. Maylis is never angry. In chapter 13 Lesca lets go because "a van does not go down a track with one woman at the wheel and another at the gear lever", which is a reason about Jeannot. Maylis is not in the reckoning. Their last exchange is about the sum, and they part without a word about each other. This is the relationship the premise sells and it is the weakest in the book.
*Do:* give chapter 10's steel room to it. Maylis says what a life in one hand would be, in her own short words, and means it. Lesca has no minute to answer from. Then in the van Lesca lets go of a person and not only a gear lever. In chapter 15 give them one line each about the other before the door shuts.

**2. Maylis's plan for the Friday is not hers. (8, 9, 11)**
"I'll be in my bed with the telephone off. I'll sleep till you get up for your stone, and then I ride you." This woman bought grease when the letters failed and has carried the key since her father. She would not leave forty houses and Coralie under a bank with nobody at the gate, for one day in a sash. The book knows it and spends one clause: "Maylis left her out of the sum." Then chapter 9 has her write a handover "in case Lesca rode her", which is a different plan, and chapter 11 goes back to the first. The plot needs her to abandon the gate so that chapter 11 can turn her round.
*Do:* keep the handover and cut the telephone-off plan. Maylis trades the night to the woman who has done it before and takes the day of the stone in exchange. That is Either turned into a bargain, and it is in character. "That was the woman she had written a handover for" then lands with nothing in its way.

**3. Larrère takes weight that belongs to Lesca. (14, 16, 17)**
"Under the time it said Castagnet, house." The kitchen clock is a good reveal and it changes the subject. From there both villages have a worse person than either woman to look at: an adulterer who dates the letter and is summoned to the brigade. What he did to Jean is also unclear. His order was shut, and shut is what kept a man in a tonne dry: "He's dry as long as you do what you're told." A cold reader cannot say what "You said it made no odds where he was" convicts him of.
*Do:* have Maïté say in plain words what he did (knew, rang from her kitchen, sent nobody, let Lesca carry it twelve years). Have Lesca refuse the relief as she refuses the houses: his call does not touch her one ring. Cut his punishment in 17 to the car in the silt.

**4. Nobody uncompromised accuses Lesca. (15, 16)**
"Nobody came up the steps. Nobody shouted." The widow had a lover that night. The partner has known for twelve years. The priest was told. The mother says "I don't want telling." Each person Lesca faces after the microphone already knew or has something of their own to hide. Maylis gets Madame Darrigrand at a pram full of silt: "You had the key." Lesca gets no such sentence, the Monday statement at Bayonne is skipped, and three days later the pillow comes back.
*Do:* Darrigrand is in chapter 4 with his step and is the one man with clean hands and a grievance. Put him in the square. Give him one sentence to her face. Play the Monday at Bayonne, even at half a page.

**5. For six chapters Maylis is a gap that other people describe. (1 to 6)**
She says "Either" eight times by the end of chapter 8 and writes it once. Everything known about her wants comes from Coralie: "Sixteen years, and I've had one straight answer out of you, and that was me." The narration keeps the same distance from her as the village does, so the word reads as vacancy. She becomes a person in chapter 7, at "She would have done it again that afternoon, to anybody."
*Do:* show one preference swallowed before chapter 4. On the dyke in chapter 3 she is asked pawl or new winch. She has written three letters and in chapter 7 she orders "One pawl". Let her have the word ready, see six men turn, and say "Either". Then every later "Either" is a suppression and the reader is on her side from chapter 3.

**6. Coralie's leaving follows the book's symmetry more than her own reasons. (17)**
"'Stay.' It was out before Maylis had weighed it. 'I didn't ask what you'd keep,' Coralie said." For sixteen chapters the question would have been satisfied by "A holiday. A dog." Here Maylis wants one thing aloud, unweighed, for the first time, and it is ruled out of order. Three reasons are stacked on one page: room twelve, "Either, with the river in it", and Bayonne. One pillow goes home in chapter 16 and one leaves in 17, and the design shows.
*Do:* pick the reason. Room twelve is the true one. Let "Stay" land as the answer she waited sixteen years for, let Coralie hear it, and let her go anyway and say why it is too late.

**7. Nobody believes Lesca will die, and she walks past the man she drowned. (10, 13)**
"A visitor Inguma could not fetch would be called to it." The loto of the dead is explained by the narrator in three sentences, and three chapters later Lesca is out of it in a page, on a joke, saved by ponies set up that afternoon. And: "Further along sat Jean Castagnet, in two jackets, with a card in each hand. She stopped at neither of them." The whole book has walked her towards that man. She has smiled at the living one in a café. She passes the drowned one in a clause.
*Do:* stop her at his place. He need not look up. Let her knees go as far as the chair, and let what she takes to the microphone come from that table.

**8. The book's two debts to Coralie are paid in summary. (16)**
"Then Lesca told her that she had twice asked the other Coralie back across a landing". The confession that matters most in the marriage is reported. So is the result: "That night Coralie carried her pillow back across the landing and laid it on the wall side of the bed." Lesca's whole motion is carried in one line after a council vote.
*Do:* write the landing confession as speech. Keep the last line, but put Lesca in the room when the pillow arrives.

**9. Lesca's Coralie has no life of her own. (2, 4, 10, 16)**
Maylis's Coralie has the Bayonne floor. Lesca's has a bunch of keys and a title to say. She wants only Lesca's truth, so she has nowhere to go when she gets it. Eleven years on a landing and a home to run should have made a different woman, and the two speak and gesture alike: the wrist, the open door, "Lie on your side." This also softens Lesca's ending, since the woman who takes her back has nothing to leave for.
*Do:* give her one thing she wanted across those eleven years that was not Lesca, and let her mention it on the landing in 16.

**10. Marks and loose ends in the village. (throughout)**
"priced" seven times for Maylis, plus "weighed a bale" and "a reading on the gauge". Larrère's mouth "a little open" in 8, 10 and 16. Jeannot and Lesca both number their points. Marie-Jo "handed her sash back across the council table in a carrier bag", cited twice and never explained, so her "Maylis" over the chairs in 15 pays a debt the reader was not shown. Kévin's "six months" likewise. In ridden scenes the narration slides between heads: chapter 8 is Maylis's and reads "Lesca wanted one thing of the morning".
*Do:* halve the pricings. Give Larrère the open mouth once, at the garden gate. Say in one line what the carrier bag was about. Hold one head per scene when two are in a body.

## 4. Five things that must not be touched

1. **Chapter 10, the call log.** "Outgoing. Castagnet J., mobile. Not answered. Two fifty-one." And "Because you'd have told me."
2. **Chapter 11, nine rings.** "'Who's dead?' said Jeannot Castagnet" through "And six. Bugger off." It makes him alive the morning of the night he might die.
3. **Chapter 14, the corridor to the hall.** "'Here,' he said. 'I know you are.'", Kévin's five fingers, Coralie's two fingers on a dead wrist, and "Forty-one."
4. **Chapter 9, the landing.** "'Whose is that?' 'Hers,' Lesca said. 'Then she can say so in the morning.'"
5. **Chapter 15, from the sentence to the chairs.** "Then I opened the gate, and I drowned him." "Madame Castagnet will lay the wreath." "Somebody has to stack them."
