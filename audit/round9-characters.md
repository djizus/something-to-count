# Round 9 — Characters

Read: `manuscript/00.md` to `19.md`, in order, in full. Nothing else.

## 1. Scores

- **Stakes: 7.** "*That the mayor remain seated, and take the other one's bed, and Coralie Mounet in it.*" (4). The personal stake is on the table by chapter 4 and it is a good one. The stake that should frighten, a life lived in one hand, arrives in 11 as a description of something that never happens.
- **Tension: 6.** "On the night of Friday the eleventh it will be ninety over, and rising." (6). That sentence, near enough word for word, is said at least eight times between 2 and 10. The reader holds the date and the figure for nine chapters and waits for the calendar.
- **Progression: 6.** "'Then it should have gone last Monday.'" (14). The daylight plan is refused by procedure at the mairie counter (7), on the dyke (11) and down a telephone (14). It is one beat played three times.
- **Comedy: 7.** "'Either is not a person. I cannot summon Either to the brigade.'" (7).
- **Characters: 7.** "'Because you'd have told me.'" (13). Lesca, both Coralies, Jeannot, Madame Etcheto and Kévin are people. Maylis is a word until chapter 12.
- **The switch between dark and comic: 8.** "'I'll be brief,' she said, in the voice that came from the back of the neck. 'The—'" (19).
- **Prose: 8.** "Then the water came up the door to it and put it out." (12). It is one manner, held without a slip. What that manner costs the people is under faults 1 and 7.
- **The ending: 8.** "Then he stepped over the string, in his waders, and got down into it." (19). Maylis's ending is paid in full. Lesca's is cheaper than it should be (fault 4).
- **Clarity: 6.** "*Nil.*" (11). The mayor's one private reckoning in the middle of the book is written in bookkeeping and cannot be read.

**Finished unpaid?** Yes. From 12 without stopping; 7, 9 and 11 on trust.
**Pressed on a friend?** On one friend, with an instruction to get as far as 6. Not on the reader the bar names.

**Against the bar: 7 where the bar is 8.**

## 2. Against the comparisons

**Above Crouch on the wife and the village. Below Crouch on the double. Below The Good Place on the two leads.**

1. **The wife is a person, which Crouch's is not.** Daniela is a place Jason is trying to get back to. Coralie has a rota, a care plan that says "Staff not to join in", and an application she "posted for me". She can tell which woman is in the body by two fingers on a wrist, and she prefers the wrong one out loud: "I'd have this one again." That is a better rival-self problem than Dark Matter poses. The book then declines to run it (fault 2).
2. **The double is never dangerous.** By page sixty of Dark Matter the other Jason is in the house and in the bed, and the reader is afraid. Lesca announces "I'm coming to stay" at the end of 8, tries the stairs in 10, is sent down them by Coralie in the same scene, and is told no in 11. The two women meet five times and conduct each meeting as a sitting. They never come to like, hate or fear each other.
3. **Maylis is Chidi seen only from outside.** The Good Place makes a man whose indecision hurts people funny and lets us inside the stomach ache. "Either" is funny from across the table and nowhere else. The reader is given the answer she swallows once in fourteen chapters ("She had her answer ready, and it was the pawl"). The reversal, on the other hand, is level with the comparison in kind: Coralie has had the call log for twelve years, and every scene on that landing re-reads.

**The brief's questions, answered.**

- *Distinct?* In speech, completely. In want, no: Lesca's is stated in chapter 4 and has a bed in it; Maylis's is asserted in chapter 8 and has a sash in it that she has never been seen to want.
- *Softened or excused?* Maylis is not excused; the village's "gone for a walk" sees to that. Lesca is charged in full for 2014 and let off for 2026 (fault 4).
- *Described from outside as a woman?* Neither. Neither is described at all beyond a palm and a rib. The cost is that desire has no body either: what Lesca crosses a world for is rendered as the position of a pillow.
- *Who would be missed?* Madame Etcheto, Jeannot, Kévin, Sarthou, Monsieur Daguerre. Not missed: Tastet, Lafitte, Père Honoré, Madame Ducasse. Arlette should be and is not.
- *Which relationship carries it?* Coralie carries it, twice. Lesca and her Coralie pay in 13. Maylis and hers pay in 19, on eleven stairs. The relationship the premise promises, between the two women, pays least. The one that pays best was not the one the book was selling: Maylis and Jeannot.
- *Does the reader care who lives?* Yes. After "And six. Bugger off." the man in the cellar is worth a village, and room twelve hurts.

## 3. The ten faults, worst first

**1. Maylis is sealed twice, by her word and by the narration, so the trade at the centre of the book is not believed.** (8, 9, 12)
"'Your Saturday. The square, and the sash. That's what I want for the night.'" and, next morning, "'Is it here?' … 'No,' she said."
She has had one day of being obeyed. On that she gives away her night at the gate and leaves Coralie "thirty metres from the patch". Nothing in 1 to 7 shows her hungry for the chair; in 2020 she let Marie-Jo's telephone ring. The want is then disposed of in one sentence: "Maylis had meant to wake in that woman's bed, where the sash was, and leave a van on a dyke with nobody in it."
*Do:* go behind "Either" three times before chapter 5, as chapter 3 does once at the winch. Show the answer formed and the mouth refusing it, with what it costs her in the body. Make the thing she wants over there one she has been refused on the page here: the pawl, ordered and fitted in an afternoon over her own L. When she gives the want up in 12, give it a scene.

**2. The doubles' relationship is a committee, and the theft of a life is defused the night it is tried.** (5, 8, 10, 11)
"'First business is how this works … I make it three points.'" and "Lesca had a minute for forty houses. She had none for this, and gave none."
The best horror in the book is hypothetical: "'I'd wash the cups with it while you told her what I wanted for supper.'" It is spoken, not lived. Lesca's answer is withheld, which is the scene being declined.
*Do:* write the missing Sunday. Lesca posts her letter at first light on the sixth, still in the body, with a hall of kernels to be counted. Chapter 11 opens on Monday night. Give her that Sunday: breakfast with a Coralie who gets answers, the café, and Arlette with her ponies still standing on the grazing that Lesca's own signature dug out. Put Maylis in the right hand for all of it, watching herself preferred. Then let Lesca defend it in 11.

**3. After chapter 1 the marriage we are to fear for is drawn only by what it lacks.** (5, 7, 9, 12)
"'I've waited a long time to be asked for one thing in this house.'" and "'There's a key, and a Trafic, and Guy, and then there's me, as a distance.'"
The eggs and the oven door in chapter 1 are a marriage. Every scene between them after it is Coralie asking and not being answered. By 9 the reader wants her on the Bayonne floor. Crouch frightens you for a marriage by showing what is good in it.
*Do:* one scene between 3 and 7 of what Coralie gets from this woman that she would not get from the one who answers. Maylis's idiom is doing a thing unasked and saying nothing; nineteen bags at a back door is the right kind and comes ten chapters late.

**4. Lesca's wrong of this year is paid by the other woman, and the last accounting leaves out the five words.** (15, 18, 19)
"'Go to the tonne first.'" against "'A woman is dead of that, and it still isn't what I did. Don't hand me your sum to sit behind.'"
In 15 "Maylis's right hand moved first. It went across to the belt." Left alone she was reaching for the key. Lesca's instruction turned her. In 19 Lesca claims eight rings and two minutes, omits the instruction, and takes the lecturing position while a mother with a pram of silt tells Maylis "You had the key". She owns the five words to her own Coralie in 18, who "asked nothing about the gate". Within the chapter she has her pillow and an empty wardrobe.
*Do:* have it said in the steel room, by either of them. Let Coralie ask about the gate. Move the pillow out of the chapter that removes her from office, or attach a price to it that has room twelve in it.
A slip in the same place: at eleven on Saturday night Lesca tells Coralie "They carried forty-one out. Madame Etcheto drowned in her bed", four hours before the meeting in 19 at which Maylis tells her.

**5. The pillow is a meter, and the mayor's Coralie has no act of her own.** (4, 9, 18, 19)
"Coralie carried her pillow back across the landing and laid it on the wall side of the bed." and "the car door shut on a pillow."
In each village the same object crosses the same four steps to record whether the heroine spoke. On Maylis's side Coralie escapes this, because the envelope behind the fruit bowl has been hers since March. On Lesca's side she is given a want in one speech ("'I didn't want those keys'") the scene before she rules.
*Do:* put the mayor-side want in motion by 8 or 13: something this Coralie is already doing about the keys. Then her return in 18 is a choice between two things she wants, as the other's leaving is.

**6. The father is a stick, never a man.** (1, 6, 7, 12, 14, 15, 16, 19)
"'Your father turned it with one hand.'" "'Your father would have had that count in his head.'" "'you've his key and none of his sense'". "'Your father slept on that platform with the pawl down and never rang me once.'"
The book's account of why Maylis cannot say what she wants runs through him: his key, his L, his line on the pier, "the commune's girl". Every man in the village beats her with him. Neither daughter has one memory of him that is not a work habit, and when Lesca passes him among the dead "She did not stop."
*Do:* one memory in 1 to 3 of him as her father. Make her stop at his chair in 15, or make not stopping cost a line.

**7. One voice for everyone who has waited, and tags run past their strength.** (throughout)
Coralie: "'Sixteen years, and I've had one straight answer out of you'". Marie-Jo: "'I waited six years for one.'" Maïté: "'I've had nothing from you for twelve Decembers'". Arlette: "'I'm not asking why once … I don't want telling.'" Larrère: "'I've sat here since 2015 … I've never once asked her.'"
Four women and a man define themselves by a count of years and a thing unsaid, in the same short reproach that ends on a noun. The village becomes a chorus speaking the book's subject. Beside it the tags: "Here" in five chapters, two fingers on a wrist nine times, "I'll be brief", "A gate is a door in a bank", "priced".
*Do:* take the count of years from Marie-Jo and Arlette. Give one of them a grievance that is not about being unanswered, and let Arlette be the one person here who says too much. Halve "Here"; it must still be new in 16.

**8. The other village's people are thrown away.** (8, 17)
"'Kévin's on the bank with the loudspeakers, in the coat you gave him when he came out of prison.'"
That clause is the sharpest measure in the book of the difference between the two women. One looked away for four winters "for nothing". The other signs things, and the boy went to prison. It is dropped in passing in 17. In 8 Maylis stands beside that Kévin with a rake and learns only that he rakes badly.
*Do:* put the prison in the rake scene in 8, so that Maylis learns what the woman who answers costs the people she herself protects.

**9. The best character cliffhanger is dissolved in two lines.** (6 into 7)
"Coralie stood on the fourth one, waiting to be answered." Then: "'I'll see to the range first,' Maylis said. She saw to it until Coralie's light was out."
"More afraid of her own stairs than she had been of the winch" is the most that is ever said about Maylis and that bed. The next chapter spends it on a kitchen range and moves to a dressing.
*Do:* stay at the foot of the stairs. Let her be the one who does not go up, and know it, before the light goes out.

**10. Lesca's turn is written in code.** (11, 13)
"*Nil.*" and "She knew the answer. In the morning the cross would be Tastet's."
Between resolving to take another woman's life in 4 and getting up off Inguma's back in 15, the mayor has two private moments. One is a stock sheet whose meaning has to be reconstructed: saving the man over there removes nothing from her own December. The other is a sentence about tape. Her change is the hinge of the climax and the reader has not watched it happen.
*Do:* after "Each of them was where she had left it", one plain sentence of what she understands. In 13, say that she means not to come back.

## 4. Five things that must not be touched

1. **The telephone on the charger** (13): "Outgoing. Castagnet J., mobile. Not answered. Two fifty-one." through "'Because you'd have told me.'" and "'I wanted you in the house.'"
2. **"Either" on the platform with one tooth taken** (14), and its name when it comes: "'That was Either, with the river in it.'" (19).
3. **Kévin's hand**, all three times: the loader bucket, "'Five'" in the yard, "'I wanted six, Maylis.'"
4. **Madame Etcheto entire**: the care plan, the sandbags counted aloud, "'I'll not sleep in a chair'", the hatbox on the wardrobe, and her alive at the end of the front row in the other square.
5. **Jeannot on the telephone at five** ("'And six. Bugger off.'") and **letter forty-two** ("'It's four sides, and it doesn't thank you.'"), with the shovel after it.
