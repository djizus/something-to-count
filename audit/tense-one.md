## Chapter 00

**00-1, line 13, kind 6.** "was raised" can be read as the gate going up now, in the scene, before she has touched the handle.

> It was raised by hand, from a platform of boards beside it.

> It could only be raised by hand, from a platform of boards beside it.

## Chapter 01

**01-1, line 13, kind 3.** "had never had to": two "had" in a row.

> She dug graves and lifted sacks of cement for a living, but she had never had to lift a sack off her own chest.

> She dug graves and lifted sacks of cement for a living, but she had never lifted a sack off her own chest.

**01-2, line 47, kind 1.**

> She lay awake and thought about the smell instead.

> She stayed awake and thought about the smell instead.

**01-3, line 73, kind 3.** The sentence before is in the simple past ("came up"). "So had" jumps to the past perfect and the reader has to supply "come up to her shoulder".

> So had the stone.

> So did the stone.

**01-4, line 89, kind 4.** "wouldn't run" can be a refusal, a condition or the future. "The L cut" also reads for a moment as subject and verb.

> The L cut in the stone wouldn't run.

> The L that was cut in the stone couldn't run.

**01-5, line 111, kind 2.** "You put". Jeannot's list moves between present ("That gate lets water through both ways") and past, so this can be read as a present.

> You put the gate up, and my water ran out through it, down into the river.

> You raised the gate, and my water ran out through it, down into the river.

**01-6, line 115, kind 6.** "had that gate raised" reads as a past perfect in the wrong order, and as if Larrère raised it himself.

> 'He had that gate raised to let my water out.

> 'He told you to raise that gate to let my water out.

**01-7, line 155, kind 6.** "Jeannot's" reads as a possessive before it reads as "Jeannot has".

> That's forty-one Jeannot's paid for.

> That's forty-one Jeannot has paid for.

**01-8, line 181, kind 1.**

> She lay down on the side by the wall and was asleep before the light was off.

> She got into bed on the side by the wall and was asleep before the light was off.

**01-9, line 181, kind 1.**

> Then she lay on her back and looked at the top of the wardrobe.

> Then she was lying on her back, looking at the top of the wardrobe.

## Chapter 02

> In eighteen years the only thing that square had ever waited for her to do was move the tractor.

> In eighteen years the only thing that square had ever waited for her to do was to move the tractor.

**02-2, line 7, kind 3.** "had always had".

**02-3, line 7, kind 6.** "had the shed unlocked" reads as a past perfect in the wrong order, or as somebody unlocking it for her.

> By the time she had the shed unlocked, Kévin was standing at its door.

> By the time she had unlocked the shed, Kévin was standing at its door.

**02-4, line 73, kind 1.**

**02-5, line 117, kind 1.**

**02-6, line 125, kind 2.** "left" opens the sentence as a verb with "That" for a subject, then runs straight into "what".

**02-7, line 135, kind 1.** Not "It was still where…": there "still" would read as "even now".

## Chapter 03

**03-3, line 63, kind 1.**

> Lesca lay in the middle of the bed with her left hand closed, as if those fingers were still in it.

> Lesca was lying in the middle of the bed with her left hand closed, as if those fingers were still in it.

**03-4, line 137, kind 1.**

> But that morning she had lain in a bed with rain falling on the roof.

> But that morning she had been in a bed with rain falling on the roof.

**03-5, line 143, kind 2.** "Now she read": "Now" pulls "read" towards the present, and the sentence before has "had read".

> Now she read those lines with a finger under each.

> Now she went down those lines with a finger under each.

**03-6, line 159, kind 3.** "hadn't had". This is the line the chapter turns on.

> That village hadn't had its flood yet.

> The flood hadn't come to that village yet.

**03-7, line 161, kind 3.** A simple past for 2014 inside the scene. The chapter has just said the two years run day for day, so this 8 December can be taken for the one that is coming.

> On 8 December the river reached the line.

> On 8 December 2014 the river had reached the line.

**03-8, line 169, kind 6.** "fell" says the day is over. It is thirteen days ahead.

> This year the 11th fell on a Friday, thirteen days away.

> This year the 11th was a Friday, thirteen days away.

**03-9, line 169, kind 5.** "tell" comes straight after "had rung Lesca" and looks like a missing "told". Its "would" is twelve words back.

> Larrère would ring Maylis, as he had rung Lesca, and tell her to keep the gate shut.

> Larrère would ring Maylis and tell her to keep the gate shut. He had rung Lesca and told her the same.

**03-10, line 213, kind 1.**

> Lesca left her own door open and lay down in the middle of the bed.

> Lesca left her own door open and got into the middle of the bed.

**03-11, line 219, kind 2.** "She read", alone in a short sentence, with a present-tense sentence straight after it.

> She read it over.

> She looked it over.

**03-12, line 219, kind 3.** A present among pasts. The other rules of the council are given in the past ("Any member could speak for three minutes on any item").

> A motion is supposed to say what it is for.

> A motion was supposed to say what it was for.

> Maylis had kept still for the village's sake, not for this.

**05-5, line 213, kind 2.** "left her bare palm" reads as the left palm. It is the right hand, and the sentence before has "the left foot".

> The dish towel fell off, which left her bare palm on the wood, cut and all.

> The dish towel fell off. Her bare palm was on the wood, cut and all.

**05-6, line 225, kind 2.** "nobody left", one sentence after "a left hand" and in the same sentence as "Her right hand".

> Her right hand was still locked around the post, though there was nobody left to hold on against.

> Her right hand was still locked around the post, though there was nobody to hold on against any more.

## Looked at and left

- "They lay a place for her at their table" (02, line 111): present of "to lay".
- "laid" (01 line 111; 03 lines 51, 89, 141, 199; 04 line 11): past of "to lay", cannot be misread.
- "lie", "lie still", "lying" (01, 02, 03, 04): base forms and "-ing" forms. If "kept still" replaces "lay still", Kévin's "Lie still under it" (02, line 35) no longer has its echo in the narration. "Keep still under it" would restore it.
- "put", "shut", "let", "cut", "hit", "set", "hurt" after "she", "he", "it" or a name: the subject settles them.
- "She stood in front of the top line with her keys in her hand and read his name through" (03, line 81): "stood" settles it. If every past "read" is to go, "and went through his name to the last letter".
- Lesca's forecasts in the present ("On Tuesday the 8th that river is at the red line", "On the night of Friday the 11th that river comes through the bank"): she says them the same way each time, and Larrère's answer shows he takes them for the future.
- The motions ("That the mayor remain seated", "I propose that the stone be given a verb").
