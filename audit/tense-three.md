# Verbs that read as the wrong tense: chapters 12 to 17

Read: `manuscript/12.md` to `17.md`, in full, one verb at a time. Nothing in the manuscript was changed.

Kinds: 1 "lay" or "lain" as the past of "lie", 2 a past form that looks like a present or like another word, 3 a tense that changes with no visible reason, 4 "would" that reads two ways, 5 a bare verb that looks like a missing ending, 6 any other verb read twice.

There is no "lain" and no "had had" in these six chapters. The motion in chapter 16 ("That the council ask the prefect to remove the mayor") is left as it stands. "Madame Castagnet will lay the flowers" (15) is the base form of "to lay" and is not listed.

The number after each entry's chapter is the line in the file. A sentence quoted from the middle of a speech is given without speech marks.

## Chapter 12 (9)

**12.1 (kind 4, line 33).**
"'They say they'd come,' the woman said."
"They'd come" is "they would come" and also "they had come", because "come" is both forms. Nothing in the sentence settles it.
Correction: "'They say they would come,' the woman said."

**12.2 (kind 3, line 51).**
"It has leaked before, with the river over the line, and it held."
"Has leaked" and "held" are two tenses for the same occasions.
Correction: "It has leaked before, with the river over the line, and it has held."

**12.3 (kind 4, line 61).**
"'I'd be up that lane every night of the year if she'd let me,' Larrère said."
Two "'d" in one sentence. "She'd let" is "she would let" and also "she had let", because "let" is both forms.
Correction: "'I would be up that lane every night of the year if she let me,' Larrère said."

**12.4 (kind 1, line 135).**
"On her other side, far below, the flood fields lay under a few centimetres of rainwater."
Correction: "On her other side, far below, the flood fields were under a few centimetres of rainwater."

**12.5 (kind 4, line 161).**
"I waited two weeks for water on it, and you'd put the river in my cellar up to the trapdoor."
After the past "I waited", "you'd put" reads as "you had put": the river is already in the cellar.
Correction: "I waited two weeks for water on it, and you would put the river in my cellar up to the trapdoor."

**12.6 (kind 6, line 257).**
"Kévin could turn the handle while she went back down for Jeannot."
"She went back down" reads as something she does. It is only a plan she is weighing, and she drops it in the next sentence.
Correction: "She could go back down for Jeannot and leave Kévin to turn the handle."

**12.7 (kind 6, line 257).**
"She wasn't going to have him turn the handle that sent him to prison."
"Sent" reads as done. In this village Kévin has not been to prison; in the other one he has, which makes the slip easy.
Correction: "She wasn't going to have him turn the handle that would send him to prison."

**12.8 (kind 6, line 299).**
"Then she came down and locked the padlock on the platform behind her, as she had every night for eighteen years."
"As she had every night" reads first as "had" for owning something, with the thing missing.
Correction: "Then she came down and locked the padlock on the platform behind her, as she had done every night for eighteen years."

**12.9 (kind 4, line 303).**
"He would argue about every hour of it."
A habit of his, or what he will do when she claims the night. It is the second.
Correction: "He was going to argue about every hour of it."

## Chapter 13 (4)

**13.1 (kind 6, line 5).**
"One chair there was empty, with a card laid in front of it, and the name on the card was hers."
"There was" reads as "there was a chair", and the sentence has to be started again.
Correction: "One chair in the hall was empty, with a card laid in front of it, and the name on the card was hers."

**13.2 (kind 2, line 55).**
"The van stopped where the track left the bank, with its front wheels on the slope."
"Left" as a verb, in a chapter that says "the left hand" eight times, four lines before and four lines after.
Correction: "The van stopped where the track went down off the bank, with its front wheels on the slope."

**13.3 (kind 2, line 87).**
"She knew what that cost."
"That cost" reads as a noun ("that cost"), which leaves the sentence without a verb. As a verb it is spelled like its present, and the price has not been paid yet.
Correction: "She knew what that would cost her."

**13.4 (kind 1, line 109).**
"On the edge of the stage a duck lay in white paper, with its feet sticking out."
Correction: "On the edge of the stage there was a duck in white paper, with its feet sticking out."

## Chapter 14 (14)

**14.1 (kind 1, line 7).**
"Maylis's left hand lay open on the steering wheel."
The sentence before ends "what she had left behind", so "left" the verb and "left" the side also stand one word apart. The correction to "lay" does not remove that.
Correction: "Maylis's left hand was open on the steering wheel."

**14.2 (kind 6, line 67).**
"She also knew what an open gate did four minutes later at the other end of this track."
The paragraph before uses "would" for what the gate will do ("what they would buy", "would go out", "would stay dry"). "Did" reads as if the gate had been opened.
Correction: "She also knew what an open gate would do four minutes later at the other end of this track."

**14.3 (kind 6, line 75).**
"If she turned that handle, Jeannot drowned and her hand had done it. If she went on, whatever the river did behind her was the river's doing."
"Jeannot drowned" and "her hand had done it" read as facts. In the other village they are facts, so a reader can believe for a moment that it has happened here.
Correction: "If she turned that handle, Jeannot would drown and her hand would have done it. If she went on, whatever the river did behind her would be the river's doing."

**14.4 (kind 2, line 97).**
"Something gave in her side."
"Gave in" reads as "to give in", to surrender.
Correction: "Something broke in her side."

**14.5 (kind 2, line 113).**
"He came after her and fell behind."
In mud, at night, "fell" reads as a fall.
Correction: "He came after her but couldn't keep up."

**14.6 (kind 2, line 129).**
"She was already running along the top of the bank, towards the lights that were left."
"Left" reads as the side. Eight lines on, the Port is "on her left".
Correction: "She was already running along the top of the bank, towards the lights that were still on."

**14.7 (kind 2, line 143).**
"Whatever had given in her side at the trapdoor now let her have a quarter of a breath at a time, so she breathed fast."
The same verb as 14.4.
Correction: "Whatever had broken in her side at the trapdoor now let her have a quarter of a breath at a time, so she breathed fast."

**14.8 (kind 2, line 213).**
"At the top she stopped, looked along the corridor at the chairs that were left, and counted them."
The chairs stand along the left side of the corridor ("She said the left side, front to back"), so "the chairs that were left" reads as the chairs on the left.
Correction: "At the top she stopped, looked along the corridor at the chairs that still had people in them, and counted them."

**14.9 (kind 2, line 263).**
"'I put that handle in letter thirty,' Jeannot said."
"I put" is spelled the same in the present, and "put a handle in a letter" has to be read twice by itself. The correction keeps the words of chapter 17 ("You told me twice to put it in forty-two. It's in.").
Correction: "'That handle is in letter thirty,' Jeannot said."

**14.10 (kind 2, line 317).**
"It pressed against the place that had given in her side and held it, and for the first time since the trapdoor she got half a breath."
The same verb as 14.4.
Correction: "It pressed against the broken place in her side and held it, and for the first time since the trapdoor she got half a breath."

**14.11 (kind 1, line 319).**
"The bed had been stripped down to the mattress, which had floated off the frame and now lay across the room."
"Was across the room" would mean on the far side of it, so the correction keeps "lying".
Correction: "The bed had been stripped down to the mattress, which had floated off the frame and was now lying across the room."

**14.12 (kind 1, line 335).**
"The whole of it had come in, frame and glass, and now lay under the water."
Correction: "The whole of it had come in, frame and glass, and was now under the water."

**14.13 and 14.14 (kind 1, twice, line 341).**
"She lay on her back with her hands outside the blanket, the way she lay to say her words."
Correction: "She was on her back with her hands outside the blanket, as she always was when she said her words."

## Chapter 15 (3)

**15.1 (kind 6, line 21).**
"'She's had her table laid since 7.'"
"She's had" is "she has had", followed by a second participle.
Correction: "'Her table has been laid since 7.'"

**15.2 (kind 3, line 57).**
"On the night of the 11th it stood ninety centimetres over the red line. Water was coming through the old bank at the Port. Behind that bank were forty houses and a care home with forty-two people in their beds. Eight of the forty-two were sitting a few metres from her knees."
The first three sentences are 2014, as her sheet tells it. The fourth is the square in 2026. All four are in the same tense, so "Water was coming through the old bank" reads as happening during the ceremony. The correction uses the form Jeannot's letter already has in chapter 17 ("It says that…").
Correction: "It said that on the night of the 11th the river stood ninety centimetres over the red line and water was coming through the old bank at the Port. It said that behind that bank were forty houses and a care home with forty-two people in their beds. Eight of the forty-two were now sitting a few metres from her knees."

**15.3 (kind 6, line 117).**
"'Jean Castagnet shut the gates of my field behind him,' Arlette said."
It reads as one occasion, perhaps the night of the flood. The next sentence shows it was his habit.
Correction: "'Jean Castagnet always shut the gates of my field behind him,' Arlette said."

## Chapter 16 (3)

**16.1 (kind 1, line 5).**
"I said my words, and I lay there waiting for it until it was light."
With "I", nothing tells the past of "lie" from the present of "lay".
Correction: "I said my words, and I waited for it in bed until it was light."

**16.2 (kind 2, line 29).**
"I wanted that cut under his name, with yours under it."
"That cut" reads as a noun, and the book has a cut already, on the palm.
Correction: "I wanted that word cut under his name, with yours under it."

**16.3 (kind 6, line 65).**
"She had handed her own back across this table in a plastic bag."
"Handed her own back" reads as her back, the part of the body.
Correction: "She had handed back her own across this table in a plastic bag."

## Chapter 17 (6)

**17.1 (kind 1, line 103).**
"The shopping pad lay on the table."
Correction: "The shopping pad was on the table."

**17.2 (kind 6, line 139).**
"In the kitchen she got out the first-aid box she kept from work."
"Kept from work" reads as "kept away from work".
Correction: "In the kitchen she got out the first-aid box she had brought home from work."

**17.3 (kind 3, line 139).**
"Her hand stayed on it a little longer than a bandage needs."
The one present in a paragraph of pasts.
Correction: "Her hand stayed on it a little longer than the bandage needed."

**17.4 (kind 5, line 141).**
"Maylis heard her cross the landing to the spare room and come out again."
"Come" stands seven words from "heard her" and looks like "came" with the wrong ending.
Correction: "Maylis heard her cross the landing to the spare room. Then she heard her come out again."

**17.5 (kind 1, line 145).**
"That night Maylis lay down on her back in the middle of the bed and waited for Inguma."
Correction: "That night Maylis got into the middle of the bed, on her back, and waited for Inguma."

**17.6 (kind 1, line 147).**
"She lay still under it."
Correction: "She stayed still under it."

## Looked at and left as they stand (not counted)

- **"read" after a quotation**: "the prefect read" and "she read" twice (15), "the deputy mayor read" (16). The subject is one person each time, so the present would be "reads". In chapter 15 "she read" for the typed sheets against "she said" for what she adds is the turn of the scene, and no form of "read" removes the spelling.
- **"I rang his phone, and I let it ring once"** (15). "I let" stands between "rang" and "opened".
- **"Each of them put her card in"** (17). It stands between "took", "held", "closed" and "laid".
- **"I told you I'd come if you asked"** (12). The "if" settles it inside the sentence.
- **"Nobody else in this village has ever opened one before I knocked"** (12). The tenses differ, but the sentence is clear.
- **"Eighteen turns would lift it off the stone…"** (12). Three "would" in a row with one reading: what the handle does if she goes on.
- **"wouldn't" for a refusal**: "She wouldn't ask him to" (12), "she wouldn't sleep in a chair" and "I wouldn't wake her" (17). Each has its time stated. A reader whose first sense of "would" is the conditional may still take the last one as "I would never wake her"; if these are to change, the plain form is "refused to".
- **"The bank rose above the yard wall"** (12), **"its lights left the road"** (12), **"Its headlights left the glass doors"** (14), **"seven chairs left with people in them"** (14). Read correctly at the first pass.
- **"Bayonne's written"** (17). "Has written"; the envelope is on the table.
- **Presents for a general truth**: "the way warmth goes out of a chair", "the kind that hangs in a kitchen", "the kind you turn by hand", "the way you carry a child" (14). "The way" and "the kind" announce them.
- ***Left*** on Maïté's stone (16) and the cards ***left*** and ***right*** (17) are deliberate.
