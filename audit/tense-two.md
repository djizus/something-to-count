Kind 1 is complete: every "lay" and "lain" that is the past of "lie" is listed. Chapter 7 has seven of them in 106 lines, because Maylis spends it in bed and on the kitchen floor.

Three faults repeat across the six chapters and are worth fixing as classes:

- **"read" as a past tense** (seven times). It is spelled like its present and said differently, so it stops the eye even after "she" or "he". Listed wherever it stands as the first verb of its sentence.
- **"left" as a verb in a book that says "the left hand" on every page** (four times). Listed only where a left hand, a right wrist or a left cheek is within a few lines.
- **"'d" before a verb that does not show whether it means "had" or "would"** ("I'd never set foot", "you'd come", "you'd read", "If I'd asked, I'd have had"). Five times, all in speech. Writing the word out fixes each one.

Line numbers are the line in the chapter file.

## Chapter 6 (15)

**1. Line 15, kind 6.** A verb that says the thing happened when it was only dictated. She answers "I haven't resigned" four lines later.

> First came her name and her job, then that she resigned from the job and gave up the key, with effect from.

Corrected: First came her name and her job. Then came *I resign from the job and give up the key, with effect from*.

**2. Line 37, kind 2.** "let" is a past participle here ("water that was let out") and looks like a present with "Water" as its subject.

> 'Water let out of the complainant's duck hut on Monday the 23rd.

Corrected: 'The complainant's duck hut was drained on Monday the 23rd.

**3. Line 69, kind 5.** "stay" hangs on a "would" that is nine words back, behind "had done". It reads as "stayed" with the ending missing.

> So she would do what Lesca had done to her, and stay sitting.

Corrected: So she decided to do what Lesca had done to her. She was going to stay sitting.

**4. Line 71, kind 1.**

> At 11 she lay down on her back and said the words quietly to the top of the wardrobe.

Corrected: At 11 she got into bed on her back and said the words quietly to the top of the wardrobe.

**5. Line 73, kind 4.** It reads first as a habit: what Coralie used to wake up beside. It is the next morning seen from the night before.

> Coralie would wake up beside a body with its eyes open and nobody in it.

Corrected: Coralie was going to wake up beside a body with its eyes open and nobody in it.

**6. Line 83, kind 1.**

> The right hand lay on the blanket in a fist, and a fist was how it stayed.

Corrected: The right hand was on the blanket in a fist, and a fist was how it stayed.

**7. Line 105, kind 1.**

> Maylis lay on her back beside her, with her eyes open.

Corrected: Maylis was lying on her back beside her, with her eyes open.

**8. Line 125, kind 2.** "left" as a verb, about a hand, in a scene that names "the left hand" and "Lesca's right hand" throughout.

> Lesca's hand came away and left Maylis the mouth.

Corrected: Lesca's hand came away and let Maylis have the mouth.

**9. Line 143, kind 2.** "read" twice. The second one is a habit, not an event.

> She read it the way she read a job sheet, for what the job would need.

Corrected: She went through it the way she went through a job sheet, for what the job would need.

**10. Line 165, kind 1.**

> When the door had shut she lay down on her back on the mayor's floor, beside the filing cupboard, with her arms by her sides.

Corrected: When the door had shut she got down on the mayor's floor, on her back, beside the filing cupboard, with her arms by her sides.

**11. Line 181, kind 6.** A habit written as one event. It reads as "she was at work and she rang the doctor". The next paragraph says "She didn't ring it".

> At work she rang the doctor for any pulse under 40, without asking anybody first.

Corrected: At work she always rang the doctor for any pulse under 40, without asking anybody first.

**12. Line 207, kind 3.** A present among pasts.

> Then he went on, like a man who has found a door unlocked.

Corrected: Then he went on, like a man who had found a door unlocked.

**13. Line 211, kind 2.** "They put" is the same in the present.

> They put their pens down to watch.

Corrected: They laid their pens down to watch.

**14. Line 227, kind 2.** "saw" reads as "looked at" until "to her gate" arrives.

> 'I saw Madame Castagnet to her gate myself.

Corrected: 'I walked Madame Castagnet to her gate myself.

**15. Line 247, kind 2.** "wound" for a dial, with "right around" straight after "The right hand".

> The right hand took the dial, wound it right around and back to nothing, and the bell rang.

Corrected: The right hand took the dial, turned it all the way around and back to nothing, and the bell rang.

## Chapter 7 (10)

**16. Line 13, kind 1.**

> The pad lay on the blanket.

Corrected: The pad was on the blanket.

**17. Line 23, kind 4.** The sentence before it has a "wouldn't" that means the future. This one means a decision. It also has "she" and "her" for two women.

> Until she could, she wouldn't ask her back to lie beside that.

Corrected: Until she could, she wasn't going to ask Coralie back to lie beside that.

**18. Line 35, kind 1.**

> She lay down on her left side at the edge of the bed with her knees up, so that Inguma would have nowhere to sit.

Corrected: She got into bed on her left side, at the edge, with her knees up, so that Inguma would have nowhere to sit.

**19. Line 39, kind 1.**

> On Thursday night she took a blanket down to the kitchen and lay on the floor between the table and the stove.

Corrected: On Thursday night she took a blanket down to the kitchen and got down on the floor between the table and the stove.

**20. Line 41, kind 1.**

> When it had gone she lay on the floor and took a breath.

Corrected: When it had gone she stayed on the floor and took a breath.

**21. Line 49, kind 2.** "read" as a speech tag.

> 'Friday the 4th, in the morning,' he read out.

Corrected: 'Friday the 4th, in the morning,' he said, reading off the pad.

**22. Line 59, kind 1.**

> That night she lay down in her own bed, on her back.

**23. Line 59, kind 1.**

Corrected: Then she let it go and kept still.

**24. Line 75, kind 2.** "read", with an object that is not something one reads.

**25. Line 79, kind 1.**

Corrected: Last night Maylis had kept still.

## Chapter 8 (7)

> All Maylis had to do was keep the mouth shut until it came, so that Lesca couldn't give it anything else to count.

Corrected: Maylis only had to keep the mouth shut until it came, so that Lesca couldn't give it anything else to count.

**27. Line 57, kind 1.**

> The duck lay on the edge of the stage in white paper, with its feet sticking out.

Corrected: The duck was on the edge of the stage in white paper, with its feet sticking out.

**28. Line 91, kind 2.** A plural subject with "put".

> The Lassalle sisters put their free hands flat on their card.

Corrected: The Lassalle sisters pressed their free hands flat on their card.

**29. Line 127, kind 1.**

> The tablecloths lay down again, one after another, all the way back to the door.

Corrected: The tablecloths came down again, one after another, all the way back to the door.

**30. Line 147, kind 4.** "you'd come" can be "you had come" or "you would come".

> 'You said to ask, and you'd come back across,' Lesca said.

Corrected: 'You said to ask, and you would come back across,' Lesca said.

**31. Line 159, kind 2.** "had left" reads as "had gone away", two lines after "the right wrist" and eight before "the left hand".

> The wrist was all she had left to pull with.

Corrected: The wrist was all she still had to pull with.

**32. Line 175, kind 3.** "you'd had" is a "had had".

> Over the soup you told me you'd had Jeannot Castagnet on your mind every day for twelve years.

Corrected: Over the soup you told me Jeannot Castagnet had been on your mind every day for twelve years.

## Chapter 10 (6)

**36. Line 5, kind 1.**

> Now it lay along the corridor, brown, from the door past twelve and eleven.

Corrected: Now it was all along the corridor, brown, from the door past twelve and eleven.

**37. Line 9, kind 1.** After "had taken", "lay" also reads as a second participle that is wrong.

> Each of these had taken its litre and lay there as fat as a pillow.

Corrected: Each of these had taken its litre and was now as fat as a pillow.

**38. Line 9, kind 3.** Seven "had" in nine short sentences: two that mean "owned" and five that mean "earlier". The first earlier one does the work.

> The care home had no sandbags. It had towels. Coralie had rolled all the bath towels in the cupboard and packed them along the bottom of the door. On top she had laid the pads that went under a resident at night. A pad was made to hold a litre. Coralie had never seen one fail. Each of these had taken its litre and lay there as fat as a pillow. Bixente had stood the yellow sign in the middle of it all.

Corrected: The care home had no sandbags. It had towels. Coralie had rolled all the bath towels in the cupboard and packed them along the bottom of the door. On top were the pads that went under a resident at night. A pad was made to hold a litre. Coralie had never seen one fail. Each of these had taken its litre and was now as fat as a pillow. Bixente's yellow sign stood in the middle of it all.

**39. Line 17, kind 1.**

> Madame Etcheto lay against her two pillows, facing her wardrobe.

Corrected: Madame Etcheto was lying against her two pillows, facing her wardrobe.

**40. Line 57, kind 1.**

> That night Maylis lay still under Inguma, and then she was in the chair.

Corrected: That night Maylis kept still under Inguma, and then she was in the chair.

**41. Line 65, kind 4.** Two "would" that mean "she was sure of it", followed in the next sentence by one that means a condition. "would have come" reads first as something that did not happen.

> The ducks would have come down onto it, so he would be in the hut, in the cellar under the trapdoor, wearing the long rubber boots he hunted in.

Corrected: The ducks must have come down onto it, so he must be in the hut, in the cellar under the trapdoor, wearing the long rubber boots he hunted in.

The next sentence ("If she opened the gate, the river would cover that trapdoor.") is then the only "would" in the paragraph and stays.

## Chapter 11 (12)

**42. Line 5, kind 4.** "I'd never set" can be "I had never set" or "I would never set". She means a vow. The next paragraph says she has been in the building before, so the reader has to go back.

> 'I said I'd never set foot in here,' she said.

Corrected: 'I said I would never set foot in here,' she said.

**43. Line 41, kind 2.**

> He read it to her off his sheet, from the anthem to the photograph.

Corrected: He told it to her off his sheet, from the anthem to the photograph.

**44. Line 45, kind 2.** "left", three sentences after "the left cheek" and "stands on the left".

> He left the kiss on it.

Corrected: He didn't take the kiss off.

**45. Line 51, kind 6.** A habit written as one event. The reader cannot tell whether he swept it once, this morning, or keeps it swept.

> A doorstep was all that the State's contractor had left of his house, and he swept it.

Corrected: A doorstep was all that the State's contractor had left of his house, and he kept it swept.

**46. Line 111, kind 2.** "read" straight after a quotation.

> She read it in a flat voice.

Corrected: She said it in a flat voice.

**47. Line 113, kind 2.** "left her coat" reads as "went away from her coat". Nothing has said she was taking it off.

> Lesca left her coat half off.

Corrected: Lesca stopped with her coat half off.

**48. Line 115, kind 1.**

> I finished my shift at 7, and you came in at 9 in your wet clothes and lay down on this bed with your boots on and your hand wrapped in a dish towel.

Corrected: I finished my shift at 7, and you came in at 9 in your wet clothes and got onto this bed with your boots on and your hand wrapped in a dish towel.

**49. Line 133, kind 4.** "you'd read" can be "you had read them" or "you would read them", a habit.

> The shifts were on the fridge door, and you'd read them.

Corrected: The shifts were on the fridge door, and you had read them.

**50. Line 133, kind 3.** The first "'d" is "had" and the second is "would", and "have had" follows.

> If I'd asked, I'd have had the answer in the house with us, at every meal.

Corrected: If I had asked, the answer would have been in the house with us, at every meal.

**51. Line 167, kind 2.**

> She read the two sentences on the third sheet again.

Corrected: She looked at the two sentences on the third sheet again.

**52. Line 169, kind 1.**

> At 10 she lay down the way her mother had told her to, on her side with her knees up.

Corrected: At 10 she got into bed the way her mother had told her to, on her side with her knees up.

**53. Line 177, kind 4.** It can be a guess about now, the future, or a condition. She knows it is raining there.

> Over there it would be raining on forty houses with their shutters closed.

Corrected: Over there it was raining on forty houses with their shutters closed.

## Read and left alone

- **"lay" as the present or base form of "to lay"**: Maïté's "I'll lay the flowers" and "After I lay them" (6, line 127). "laid" is not listed anywhere: it cannot be read as another tense.
- **Complaints told in the present** by a speaker who is listing wrongs: Jeannot in 6 (line 47), 8 (line 77) and 10 (line 107), Larrère in 6 (line 227, "this morning she laughs in the minute"). The device is the same each time and the dates in each speech place the events.
- **The present for a rule that is always true**: "what happens to the one Inguma can't take home" (6, line 223), "the kind that hangs in a kitchen" (10, line 59).
- **"would" with a time word beside it**: "In ten days she would be on that platform" (6, line 67), "Inguma would come for Lesca tonight" (8, line 15), "Castagnet would be at the end of the track tonight" (11, line 31).
- **"put", "let", "shut", "set", "hit" and "cut" after one named person or "he" or "she"**, where the present would end in "s".
- **A bare verb after "saw", "heard", "felt" or "watched"** ("Maylis saw the paper tablecloths lift", "heard the pillow go down").
- **The motion** in 6, line 245 ("that on the 12th the prefect speak alone") and the broken-off speech of Maïté and Lesca.
- **The recap in 7, line 79**: four "had" in four sentences, each with its own day beside it. Only "had lain" is listed.
