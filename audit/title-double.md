# The title

## 1. Is *Either* the right title?

No. It names the book's thesis word, not its world. On a list of forty a stranger expects a slim, abstract novel of ideas, half of Kierkegaard's title, and nothing of a village, a river or a joke.

## 2. The nine, ranked

1. *Something to Count*. The book's true motif: leaves, corn, rings of a phone, fingers, votes, heads in a hall. Light on the cover, grave by the end.
2. *The Keyholder*. Concrete, a job and a burden, true of both women. A little thriller-plain.
3. *Somebody Has to Stack the Chairs*. The funniest and most memorable, and it is the village's voice. Long, and it promises only the comedy.
4. *The Gate Stays Shut*
5. *Forty-One*
6. *The Other One*
7. *Either*
8. *The Second Shovel*
9. *Both*. As abstract as *Either*, and it is the climax's own word, so it tells a reader of the book where the night goes.

## 3. Two of my own

- *Across the Landing*. Four steps between two bedrooms and the crossing between two villages in one phrase, domestic enough to smile at and sad once you know who sleeps where.
- *Apologies Received*. The committee voice that carries the comedy, which is also what the mayor says to decline her seat among the dead.

## 4. My one choice

*Something to Count*.
