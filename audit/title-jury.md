# The title

## 1. Is *Either* the right title?

No. It is the book's spine once you have read it, but cold, in a list of forty, it reads as an abstract one-word literary title with a nod to Kierkegaard: no village, no river, no joke, and nothing to picture or repeat to another judge.

## 2. The nine, best first

1. ***Something to Count*** — the book's own mechanism and its habit of mind: leaves, stars, corn, pulses, fingers, heads. Light on the cover, heavy by the last page, and it gives nothing away.
2. ***The Keyholder*** — plain, sayable, and it names the burden ("You had the key"). Slightly thriller-shelf, and it has no smile in it.
3. ***Somebody Has to Stack the Chairs*** — the most memorable and the truest to the tone, comic and after-the-disaster at once. Long, and it risks reading as whimsy.
4. *Either*
5. *The Gate Stays Shut*
6. *The Other One*
7. *The Second Shovel*
8. *Both*
9. ***Forty-One*** — a bare number means nothing to a stranger, and to a reader it is the ending: the head count that comes up one short.

## 3. Two of my own

- ***The Question of the Handle*** — the mayor's line through a car window ("It isn't settled, and you're not to worry about it") is the book's first joke, and by the end the handle nobody turned is the whole moral question.
- ***Put It in Forty-Two*** — the running joke about a crank's registered letters, which becomes the one letter that is not a complaint; funny first, then not, and no spoiler. Its weakness is the echo of another famous forty-two.

## 4. My one choice

***Something to Count.***
