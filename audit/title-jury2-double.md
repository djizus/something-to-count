# The title

**Is *Either* right?** No: among forty prize entries, it would suggest an abstract novel about choice or alternative lives, giving little sense of this book’s physical work, comic village or dangerous evasions.

**The nine, ranked**

1. *The Keyholder* — An ordinary job becomes an accusation; possession, authority and responsibility are all in the word.
2. *Something to Count* — Invites the uncanny while accommodating bingo, nursing, council votes and the terrible arithmetic of rescue.
3. *The Gate Stays Shut* — A comic bureaucratic pronouncement that becomes frightening without changing a syllable.
4. *Somebody Has to Stack the Chairs*
5. *The Other One*
6. *The Second Shovel*
7. *Both*
8. *Either*
9. *Forty-One* — Meaningful afterwards, anonymous beforehand; it also directs attention towards the final casualty count.

**Two alternatives**

*One Hand.* The same words encompass practical labour, the absurd struggle between two occupants of a body, and the frightening price of letting somebody else live your life.

*The Spare Room.* An ordinary domestic arrangement becomes the measure of two possible lives, allowing a modest comic beginning to deepen into estrangement without advertising the outcome.

**One choice: *The Keyholder*.** It makes the book’s recurring question concrete: what does having the means to act oblige you to do?
