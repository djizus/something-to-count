# The title

## 1. Is *Either* right?

No: among forty prize entries, an unread *Either* suggests an abstract novel about alternative lives or indecision, with little promise of this book's village comedy, physical work or culpability.

## 2. Ranking

1. **Something to Count.** An ordinary task with an unsettling purpose; it accommodates the jokes, the protective rituals and the counting of survivors.
2. **The Gate Stays Shut.** A memorable instruction whose authority becomes increasingly sinister; it makes refusal sound like an action.
3. **The Keyholder.** Places responsibility with a person and a job, while leaving open what the key permits or obliges her to do.
4. **Somebody Has to Stack the Chairs**
5. **The Second Shovel**
6. **The Other One**
7. **Forty-One**
8. **Either**
9. **Both.** Even less distinctive than *Either* in a prize list; it promises reconciliation or completeness that the book painfully denies.

## 3. Two more

**The Question of the Handle.** Larrère's evasive official language sounds comic on a cover, while the neglected handle makes that evasion consequential.

**The Member's Time.** The council's recurring phrase begins as a joke about procedure and comes to mean who is allowed to speak about responsibility.

## 4. One choice

**Something to Count.** It is easy to say, belongs to the novel's everyday vocabulary and changes its emotional meaning as the reader proceeds, without disclosing the outcome.
