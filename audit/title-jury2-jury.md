## 1. Is *Either* right?

No: among forty prize entries, an unread *Either* promises an abstract novel about alternatives or indecision, while concealing the village, the labour and the strange visitor that make this one distinctive.

## 2. Ranking

1. *Something to Count*. A small, almost homely task that becomes a defence against terror; counting also gives the comedy its eventual human cost.
2. *The Gate Stays Shut*. Memorable spoken authority, with danger behind it. Slightly more suggestive of a thriller than the whole novel warrants.
3. *Somebody Has to Stack the Chairs*. The village speaks. Funny, weary and capable of becoming devastating; its length is the price.
4. *The Keyholder*
5. *The Second Shovel*
6. *The Other One*
7. *Either*
8. *Both*
9. *Forty-One*. An unexplained number on the cover, and explaining it reveals the ending's arithmetic; it acquires its force too late.

## 3. Two alternatives

*The Member's Time*. The council's repeated phrase starts as petty procedural comedy and comes to sound like a person's allotted life.

*A Lot of Steel for One Chair*. Maylis's observation introduces the impossible with practical amusement, while the solitary chair carries the book's later struggle over who gets to occupy a life.

## 4. One choice

*Something to Count*. It invites curiosity without promising a different genre, carries the local supernatural practice, and leaves the reader to discover what counting cannot protect.
