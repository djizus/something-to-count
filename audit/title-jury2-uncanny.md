# The title

## 1. Is *Either* right?

No: among forty prize entries, an unread *Either* would suggest an abstract novel about alternative lives or romantic indecision, giving little sense of this book's village, humour or bodily dread.

## 2. Ranking

1. *Something to Count*. An ordinary task with an unsettling purpose; it holds together the counting charm, bingo, care work and responsibility for individual lives.
2. *The Gate Stays Shut*. Concrete, memorable and menacing; an official instruction becomes a dangerous refusal.
3. *The Keyholder*. Gives the overlooked worker her proper importance and makes possession of the key sound like a burden.
4. *The Second Shovel*
5. *Somebody Has to Stack the Chairs*
6. *The Other One*
7. *Forty-One*
8. *Either*
9. *Both*. Even less distinctive than *Either*; it suggests reconciliation where the book makes wanting everything costly.

## 3. Two alternatives

*It Rang Once*. The opening's last sentence sounds innocuous until a single ring becomes a measure of what somebody chose not to do.

*Leaves in the Bed*. The leaves can first be dismissed as a village worker bringing her job home, then become evidence that something has followed her there.

## 4. One choice

*Something to Count*. It invites curiosity without announcing a double or a disaster, and its small comic promise grows frightening as the things counted become people.
