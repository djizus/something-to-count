## Is *Either* right?

No: among forty prize entries, *Either* would suggest an abstract novel about alternatives or an indecisive relationship, giving a new reader little sense of this book's village comedy, physical work or gathering danger.

## The nine, ranked

1. *The Gate Stays Shut*. An order with a comic bureaucrat behind it and a catastrophe ahead; its meaning darkens as the book does.
2. *Something to Count*. Fits the folk creature, bingo, council votes and human losses; ordinary words acquire an unsettling weight.
3. *Somebody Has to Stack the Chairs*. The most distinctive village voice, with duty surviving public disgrace; slightly long, and the danger is harder to hear.
4. *The Second Shovel*.
5. *The Keyholder*.
6. *Forty-One*.
7. *The Other One*.
8. *Either*.
9. *Both*. Even less distinctive than *Either*; sounds conciliatory when the novel makes reconciliation costly and incomplete.

## Two others

*The Member's Time*. The council's comic formula also names the limited time a person has to act, without disclosing what anyone chooses.

*Apologies Received*. Lesca's administrative phrase is funny in its disproportionate setting and carries the colder suggestion of guilt being formally acknowledged without being repaired.

## One choice

*The Gate Stays Shut*. It gives the reader an object, a voice and a question before the first page, and remains troubling after the last.
