# The title: the comic village read

## 1. Is *Either* the right title?

No. It is the book's key word, but only once you have read it. On a list of forty, a stranger sees an abstract, slightly philosophical novel about a choice (Kierkegaard, or a love triangle), with no village, no water and no joke in it, and cannot be sure how to say it aloud.

## 2. The nine, ranked

1. ***The Gate Stays Shut*.** The book's refrain: an order through a car window, a vote, a joke at an oven door, and then the thing that kills. It promises a village, a small tyrant and a disaster, and it reads differently after the last page.
2. ***Somebody Has to Stack the Chairs*.** The tradition's own tone, funny and then not. It is long, and it undersells the flood.
3. ***The Keyholder*.** Easy to say, and it becomes the charge against her: "You had the key." It has no comedy in it, and it sounds like a thriller.
4. *Something to Count*
5. *Either*
6. *The Other One*
7. *Forty-One*
8. *The Second Shovel*
9. ***Both*.** It has the faults of *Either* without its history in the book, and nobody could ask for it in a shop.

## 3. Two of my own

- ***Staff Not to Join In*.** The care plan's line is the best joke in the book, and it names the book's real offence, which is standing by.
- ***The Question of the Handle*.** It is Larrère's formula for never deciding anything ("It isn't settled, and you're not to worry about it"), and the handle is the moral question as well as the faulty part.

## 4. My one choice

***The Gate Stays Shut*.**
