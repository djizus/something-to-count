# Two titles

## 1. Before reading

- *Something to Count*: a quiet, wry book with a puzzle in it, perhaps about sleeplessness or a small ritual; the risk is a faint air of a children's counting book.
- *Count for Something*: an earnest novel about an overlooked person who wants to matter, with a consoling ending.

## 2. After reading

- *Something to Count*: the work given to the thing on the chest to keep it away, and then every tally in the book, from grains of corn to rings of a phone to heads in a hall. Idiomatic, with no bad second meaning.
- *Count for Something*: the village's girl, whose name is not on her own raincoat, wanting to matter, and one life weighed against forty houses. The pun on counting is real, but the phrase reads as a clipped fragment or a command, and it states a moral.

## 3. Funny and then not, giving least away

*Something to Count*. It is light on the cover and darkens by itself. *Count for Something* promises a vindication the book refuses to give.

## 4. My choice

*Something to Count*. Sure: 4 out of 5.
