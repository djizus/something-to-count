# Two titles

1. **Before reading**

*Count for Something* promises an overlooked person's struggle to matter, but among forty prize entries its familiarity risks suggesting an uplifting story.

*Something to Count* suggests a quieter, stranger book about a ritual or an ordinary task whose importance the reader will discover.

2. **After reading**

*Count for Something* joins the literal counting of fingers, votes and survivors to the wish that work and choices should matter, though its idiomatic English carries a hopeful promise the ending complicates.

*Something to Count* names Inguma's assigned work and, more painfully, the survivors and losses left to count, with a secondary suggestion of distraction from unbearable knowledge; it is idiomatic, deliberately modest English.

3. **Tone and concealment**

*Something to Count* suits the movement from comic counting games to Coralie's forty-one. It gives away neither the double nor the flood, and its apparent smallness becomes painful. *Count for Something* announces the moral question earlier and sounds more encouraging than this book feels.

4. **Choice**

*Something to Count*. **4/5.**
