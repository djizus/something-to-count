1. Before reading

*Count for Something* would suggest, among forty prize entries, an earnest novel about an overlooked person's struggle to matter, with a faint promise of uplift.

*Something to Count* would suggest a quieter, stranger book about small particulars, possibly comic, whose subject the title withholds.

2. After reading

*Count for Something* means that Maylis wants her work and choices to matter, while its second meaning asks whose life enters her reckoning; the English is idiomatic, but the promise of vindication fits the ending poorly.

*Something to Count* names the work given to Inguma and, more painfully, the people counted into safety or missing from it; both meanings work, and the English is natural, though "something" acquires a disturbing impersonality beside Coralie's named residents.

3. Tone and disclosure

*Something to Count* better suits a book that is funny and then is not. A task that sounds almost trivial becomes forty-one survivors and an empty chair. It reveals neither the double nor the flood, and makes no advance claim that the protagonist will earn her worth.

4. Choice

*Something to Count*. Confidence: **4/5**.
