1. **Before reading**

*Count for Something* promises a novel about overlooked lives acquiring importance, with a faintly improving tone that could disappear among forty prize entries.

*Something to Count* promises an understated, possibly comic novel in which an ordinary preoccupation conceals a stranger story.

2. **After reading**

*Count for Something* asks whether work, rescue and confession confer worth, while its numerical second meaning makes the saved lives and the single death bear on that question; it is idiomatic, though the bare phrase can sound exhortatory.

*Something to Count* names the task given to Inguma, then acquires a harsher second meaning when the quantities become people and Coralie reaches forty-one; it is natural English, and its modest wording gains weight retrospectively.

3. **Fit and concealment**

*Something to Count* better accommodates the movement from a nightmare's counting chores to a missing resident. It gives away little on the cover. *Count for Something* announces the moral argument before the comedy has drawn us in.

4. **Choice: Something to Count. Confidence: 4/5.**
