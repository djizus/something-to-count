# Two titles

## Before reading

*Something to Count* suggests a quiet, possibly eccentric novel about someone who needs a small occupation; among forty prize entries, its withheld subject invites curiosity.

*Count for Something* suggests a novel about finding personal worth or making a difference, with the encouraging tone of a slogan.

## After reading

*Something to Count* names the work given to Inguma and gathers fingers, votes, letters and the forty-one survivors into an ordinary phrase whose practical comfort becomes terrible; its modest sound concealing fatal arithmetic is a good second resonance, and the English is natural.

*Count for Something* means both to matter and, by implication, to have one's counting serve a purpose, but its idiomatic imperative sounds exhortatory and its numerical reading is less natural, encouraging a reassuring moral the ending refuses.

## Fit and choice

*Something to Count* suits the village's comic preoccupations before revealing what counting costs, and gives less away about the moral destination.

**Choice: *Something to Count*. Confidence: 5/5.**
