# Two titles: the comic village read

## 1. Before reading

- ***Count for Something*.** An earnest, uplifting novel about a small person who wants to matter. It could pass for a motivational memoir.
- ***Something to Count*.** A small, dry, odd novel, perhaps about insomnia or tedium, and probably comic.

## 2. After reading

- ***Count for Something*.** The village's girl, whose name is not even on her raincoat, wants to matter. Its second meaning is the pun on Inguma's counting, and on whether one man counts against forty houses. The pun is good, but the idiom states the moral. It is correct English, though with no subject it can be misread as an order.
- ***Something to Count*.** It is what you give the demon to keep it off: leaves, stars, corn. Then it is the heads in the hall, stopping at forty-one. That second meaning is good and unforced. It is idiomatic, if quiet.

## 3. Funny and then not, and least given away

*Something to Count*. It begins as the joke and ends as the roll-call, and it tells a stranger nothing about either.

## 4. My choice

***Something to Count*.** Sureness: 4.
