# Report of the fluent reader

## 1. Ease

Read twice in X:

- "…pulling leaves off the drain covers before the drains noticed."
- "On the bench under the third tree somebody had left a child's glove, palm up, filling."
- "Wherever she started, she raked her way back to it."
- "Somebody had put it up straight."
- "Rain on the roof: not hers, there had been none on her own for two weeks."
- "One hand for, one against."
- "Deferred."
- "Any member could speak for three minutes on any item, a rule Lesca had made in her first month as mayor, for Larrère, who had been mayor before her."
- "She came down the last steps, … let herself in and went to the cupboard, where the flood file for 2014 stood between school meals and street lighting."

Read twice in Y:

- "The only things that still obeyed her were her eyes."
- "Maylis was the village's only outdoor worker, so she spent it in the square…" ("it" is the Tuesday of the sentence before.)
- "It had been needed for Larrère, who had been mayor before her."

Read twice in both: "An answer went past Lesca on its way to the mouth."

Y reads more easily: three stops against nine.

## 2. Voice

X. In Y one person tells both chapters, and I know whose head I am in only because the names say so. In X the telling changes with the head.

- Chapter 1, a woman who lifts things for a living: "A lot of steel for one chair."
- Chapter 3, a woman who runs meetings: "She took the room the way she took a meeting, one item at a time."

## 3. Sentences that read as wrong

X:

- The child's glove, "palm up, filling." Decorated. The glove does nothing afterwards, and "filling" needs a second look to become rain.
- "…before the drains noticed." Forced. Drains do not notice.
- "Deferred." A label on a moment I had already understood.
- "One hand for, one against." Explains the sentence before it.
- "Between her and the wall was Coralie, warm from the bed…" Turned around for no reason.
- "…and put her question before she sat down." "Asked" is the word.
- The three-minute rule sentence quoted above. Three facts joined by commas.

Y:

- "Its back was all she could see." Explains what was plain. The thing has just turned its back to her.
- "The only things that still obeyed her were her eyes." Turned around, and twice as long as it needs to be.
- "She read it over." Plain already. She has just unfolded the sheet.
- "It had been needed for Larrère…" Stiff.

## 4. The differences

Keep:

1. X: "A lot of steel for one chair." Short, funny, and hers.
2. X: "They had hydraulic as two words." It pays back the timer that cut Larrère off in the middle of that word. Y loses the joke.
3. Y: "Maylis was stacking chairs. Coralie took the top one out of her hands." In X ("…the village festival, stacking chairs. She took the top one out of Maylis's hands.") I could not tell at first who was stacking.

Change back:

1. Y: "The only things that still obeyed her were her eyes." Back to X: "Only her eyes still worked." It is on the first page.
2. X: "On the bench under the third tree somebody had left a child's glove, palm up, filling." Back to Y, which does not have it.
3. X: "Deferred. The spare room was four steps away across the landing, where Coralie had carried her pillow in the February after the flood, and where the pillow had stayed." Back to Y, which drops the first word and makes two sentences of the rest.

## 5. My choice

Y, 3 out of 5. Y never stopped me for more than a moment and X stopped me nine times, but X's short lines cost me nothing to read and I would want about six of them put back into Y.
