# Report r2: the fluent reader

## 1. Ease

**Read twice in both versions**

- 'By then it was no use to Lesca.' ('It' is the jaw.)
- 'An answer went past Lesca on its way to the mouth.'

**Read twice in X only**

- 'The only things that still obeyed her were her eyes.'
- 'It had been needed for Larrère, who had been mayor before her.'

**Read twice in Y only**

- 'pulling leaves off the drain covers before the drains noticed.'
- 'On the bench under the third tree somebody had left a child's glove, palm up, filling.'
- 'Larrère was at the wheel with the engine running, where he spent his mornings until the town hall opened at 2.'
- 'Rain on the roof: not hers, there had been none on her own for two weeks.'
- 'One hand for, one against.'
- 'Deferred.'
- 'The spare room was four steps away across the landing, where Coralie had carried her pillow in the February after the flood, and where the pillow had stayed.'
- 'Any member could speak for three minutes on any item, a rule Lesca had made in her first month as mayor, for Larrère, who had been mayor before her.'
- 'They had hydraulic as two words.'

X reads more easily: two stops of its own, against nine in Y.

## 2. Voice

Y. In X one plain narrator tells both chapters and only the facts say whose head it is. In Y the telling does it:

- Maylis: 'Maylis would have pulled it up in October.'
- Lesca: 'She took the room the way she took a meeting, one item at a time.'

## 3. Sentences that read as wrong

**Decorated (Y)**

- The child's glove. Nothing comes of it.
- 'before the drains noticed.' A joke I had to translate.
- 'where the card in the door said BACK IN FIVE MINUTES and had gone brown at the corners.' It delays the stone.

**Joined to another it has nothing to do with (Y)**

- 'She raked left-handed, because of the rib.' It now follows the glove. In X it opens the paragraph about the rib.
- '…the floodgate down in the river bank, where the handle had stuck and thrown her against the rail.' The accident is hidden in a 'where'.
- The Larrère, spare-room and three-minute-rule sentences above. X gives each fact its own sentence.

**Turned around for no reason (Y)**

- 'Between her and the wall was Coralie…'
- 'Since 2018 she hadn't spoken to Lesca directly…'
- 'Since the January after the flood Lesca had looked after…'
- 'For the last item, the stone, Marie-Jo stood…'
- 'On her desk she opened it…'

**Forced (Y)**

- 'put her question before she sat down.' X's 'asked' is the word.

**Explains what was plain**

- X: 'Its back was all she could see.' The line before says so.
- X: 'She read it over.'
- X: 'She kept looking at it.'
- Y: 'One hand for, one against.'

## 4. The differences

**Keep (all Y)**

1. 'She took the room the way she took a meeting, one item at a time. The paper lampshade: hers.' With its echo, 'Clock radio: 06:20. Pillows: one.'
2. 'Wherever she started, she raked her way back to it.'
3. 'Maylis would have pulled it up in October.'

Close behind: 'Only her eyes still worked.', 'A lot of steel for one chair.', 'It took her half a minute.'

**Change back (all Y)**

1. 'On the bench under the third tree somebody had left a child's glove, palm up, filling.' The rib line goes back with it.
2. 'Any member could speak for three minutes on any item, a rule Lesca had made… for Larrère, who had been mayor before her.'
3. 'Larrère was at the wheel with the engine running, where he spent his mornings…'

## 5. My choice

X, sure at 3 out of 5: X never sent me back up the page for a sentence that was showing off or carrying two facts at once, though I would want Y's short voice lines carried into it.
