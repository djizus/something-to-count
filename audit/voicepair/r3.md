# Voice pair p1: the fluent reader

## 1. Ease

Read twice in X:

- "…pulling leaves off the drain covers before the drains noticed."
- "Eighteen days made five." (Five what?)
- "On the bench under the third tree somebody had left a child's glove, palm up, filling."
- "Somebody had put it up straight."
- "Rain on the roof: not hers, there had been none on her own for two weeks."
- "One hand for, one against."
- "Deferred."
- "Any member could speak for three minutes on any item, a rule Lesca had made in her first month as mayor, for Larrère, who had been mayor before her."
- "…and put her question before she sat down."
- "They had hydraulic as two words."

Read twice in Y:

- "That was twelve years ago, in June 2014, when the village council had rented the barn to a team from a university."
- "It had been needed for Larrère, who had been mayor before her."

Read twice in both: "By then it was no use to Lesca." and "…she had woken up over there with a mouth."

Y reads more easily: two stops against ten. Six of X's ten cost half a second. Four are real: the glove, the drains, the two hands, hydraulic.

## 2. Voice

X. In Y the same careful person tells both chapters, and only the facts tell me whose head I am in. In X the telling does it:

- Maylis: "A lot of steel for one chair."
- Lesca: "The paper lampshade: hers."

"Maylis would have pulled it up in October" and "Clock radio: 06:20. Pillows: one." do the same work.

## 3. Sentences that read as wrong

In X:

- "…before the drains noticed." Decorated. Drains do not notice.
- "…a child's glove, palm up, filling." Decorated, and it belongs to nothing around it. It also pushes "She raked left-handed, because of the rib" away from the Monday that explains the rib.
- "One hand for, one against." Explains what the sentence before had shown.
- "They had hydraulic as two words." A joke I had to work out, so I did not smile.
- "…the floodgate down in the river bank, where the handle had stuck and thrown her against the rail." Two things joined: it reads as if the bank had the handle.
- "Since 2018 she hadn't spoken to Lesca directly…" and "Since the January after the flood Lesca had looked after its roof…" Turned around for no reason.
- "…put her question…" Forced. The word is "asked".

In Y:

- "Its back was all she could see." Explains what was plain. It had turned its back one line earlier.
- "The only things that still obeyed her were her eyes." The long way round for "Only her eyes still worked."
- "…where the leaves parted she could see grey steel underneath…" "She could see" comes three times in seven lines.
- "It had been needed for Larrère…" Forced.
- "She read it over." Plain without it.

## 4. The differences

Keep:

1. X: "A lot of steel for one chair."
2. X: "The paper lampshade: hers. The top of the wardrobe: hers." The whole list, down to "Pillows: one." It is the easiest paragraph in chapter 3 and the one that tells me a mayor is counting.
3. Y: "The handle had stuck and thrown her against the rail." Its own sentence, with the rib sentence directly in front of it.

Change back:

1. X: "On the bench under the third tree somebody had left a child's glove, palm up, filling."
2. X: "One hand for, one against."
3. Y: "Its back was all she could see."

Y's other splits are also worth carrying into X: the alarm ("Then an alarm had rung a long way off."), the pillow on the landing, the file taken down from the cupboard, the chairs at the festival.

## 5. My choice

X, 3 out of 5. Its short dry lines pull me down the page the way Coben's do, and its hard sentences are few enough to cut, while Y is smooth but nobody is telling it.
