# Report r4: the juror, folder p2

## 1. Ease

Read twice in X:

- "Maylis was the village's only outdoor worker, so she spent it in the square…" ("it" reaches back to Tuesday.)
- "It had been needed for Larrère, who had been mayor before her."

Read twice in Y:

- "On the bench under the third tree somebody had left a child's glove, palm up, filling."
- "Rain on the roof: not hers, there had been none on her own for two weeks."
- "On Monday the mayor, Guy Larrère, had rung and told her to raise the floodgate down in the river bank, where the handle had stuck and thrown her against the rail." (It reads as if the handle stuck before he rang.)
- "…Coralie had found her behind the tent on the last night of the village festival, stacking chairs. She took the top one out of Maylis's hands." (Who stacks, who takes.)
- "The spare room was four steps away across the landing, where Coralie had carried her pillow in the February after the flood, and where the pillow had stayed."

X reads more easily, by a small margin. Every Y stumble is a join or an ornament.

## 2. Voice

Y. In X one even hand tells both chapters, and only the facts say whose head it is. In Y the teller of chapter 1 weighs materials and other people's workmanship, and the teller of chapter 3 keeps minutes.

- Chapter 1, Y: "A lot of steel for one chair."
- Chapter 3, Y: "Clock radio: 06:20. Pillows: one."

One warning. Chapter 3 uses the minuting four times. Over a book it becomes a tic unless it is rationed.

## 3. Sentences that read as wrong

- Y: "She took the room the way she took a meeting, one item at a time." Explains what the next five sentences show.
- Y: the child's glove, quoted above. Decorated. It belongs to an author, not to a woman with a rake, who would have moved it.
- Y: "…pulling leaves off the drain covers before the drains noticed." A joke laid on top of the work.
- Y: "Deferred." The device again, at the one moment that should be left bare.
- Y: "Between her and the wall was Coralie, warm from the bed…" Turned around.
- Y: "Since 2018 she hadn't spoken to Lesca directly…" and "Since the January after the flood Lesca had looked after its roof…" Turned around for no reason.
- Y: "For the last item, the stone, Marie-Jo stood the timer…" X's "The last item was the stone." sounds more like an agenda.
- Y: "…shook it out, and a few crumbs of brown leaf fell on the floor…" Joined. The crumbs are a clue and want their own sentence.
- X: "Its back was all she could see. It was covered in overlapping brown leaves, and where the leaves parted she could see grey steel…" "She could see" twice.
- X: "She kept looking at it." Flat.

## 4. The differences

Keep (all Y):

1. "Grey granite, as high as her shoulder. Somebody had put it up straight." A gravedigger checks the setting before she reads the name.
2. "One hand for, one against." Paid off when the closed hand goes up at the vote.
3. "Wherever she started, she raked her way back to it."

Change back (all Y):

1. "She took the room the way she took a meeting, one item at a time." Put back X's "The room could have been her own." and let the list follow it.
2. "…told her to raise the floodgate down in the river bank, where the handle had stuck and thrown her against the rail."
3. "Deferred. The spare room was four steps away across the landing, where Coralie had carried her pillow…, and where the pillow had stayed."

## 5. Choice

Y, sure at 4. It is the only version in which two different people tell the two chapters, and what it costs in ease is a handful of joins and ornaments that can be taken out without touching the voice.
