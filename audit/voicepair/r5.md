# Juror report: p1, X against Y

## 1. Ease

Read twice in X:

- "Somebody had put it up straight."
- "Two trailers a week came off that square. Eighteen days made five." (five what)
- "On the bench under the third tree somebody had left a child's glove, palm up, filling."
- "On Monday the mayor, Guy Larrère, had rung and told her to raise the floodgate down in the river bank, where the handle had stuck and thrown her against the rail." (it reads as if the handle stuck before he rang)
- "Rain on the roof: not hers, there had been none on her own for two weeks."
- "They had hydraulic as two words." (a second read, but it pays: the timer cut the word in half)

Read twice in Y:

- "The only things that still obeyed her were her eyes."
- "That was twelve years ago, in June 2014, when the village council had rented the barn to a team from a university."
- "It had been needed for Larrère, who had been mayor before her."

Y reads more easily, by a small margin. Nothing in it stops me, and nothing makes me look up.

## 2. Voice

X. In Y both chapters are told by the same even hand, and only the facts say whose head it is. In X the two heads sort the world differently. Maylis weighs things as labour and materials:

- "A lot of steel for one chair."

Lesca rules on things as agenda items and votes:

- "One hand for, one against."

"Deferred." and "Pillows: two." do the same for Lesca. Chapter 3 gains more than chapter 1: there the voice runs through the whole first scene and comes back at the vote.

## 3. Sentences that read as wrong

- X: "pulling leaves off the drain covers before the drains noticed." Forced wit, and not how Maylis jokes elsewhere.
- X: the child's glove, "palm up, filling." Decorated, tied to nothing around it. Maylis clears that square. She would pick it up.
- X: the floodgate sentence joined with "where". A phone call and an accident, made into one place.
- X: "She took the room the way she took a meeting, one item at a time." It explains the list that follows.
- X: "Since 2018 she hadn't spoken to Lesca directly". Turned around for no gain.
- Y: "Its back was all she could see." The sentence before has it turn its back to her.
- Y: "She kept looking at it." It reports what X shows.
- Y: "She read it over." Plain already.
- Both: "the only form she had for it was a motion, the kind the council voted on." The last six words gloss a word nobody needed glossed.

## 4. The differences

Keep:

1. X: "A lot of steel for one chair."
2. X: "Wherever she started, she raked her way back to it."
3. X: "One hand for, one against."

Close behind, all X: "It took her half a minute." (against Larrère's three), and "Lesca had stayed sitting." standing alone, since the motion is built on it.

Change back:

1. X: "On the bench under the third tree somebody had left a child's glove, palm up, filling." Y has nothing here, and nothing is right.
2. X: "…raise the floodgate down in the river bank, where the handle had stuck and thrown her against the rail." Y's order is better: the rib, the call, then the handle in its own sentence.
3. X: "…before the drains noticed." Y's "raking leaves off the drain covers under the plane trees" will do.

If Y were the base, the first three to change would be "The only things that still obeyed her were her eyes", "Its back was all she could see" and "It had been needed for Larrère".

## 5. Choice

X, sure at 4. Y is a clean account of what happened; X is two women each telling it her own way, and its faults are three or four ornaments in one paragraph of chapter 1 that can be cut without touching the voice.
