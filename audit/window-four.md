# Window four: chapters 13 to 17

Checked against `state.md` parts 1 to 4 and against the rest of the window. Chapters 09, 11 and 12 were read whole, because the window opens in the middle of their night. Chapters 00, 02, 03, 04, 05, 06, 08 and 10 were opened only to check single facts.

Counts: People 1 · Facts 1 · Time 3 · World 4 · The telling 1. Three entries in `state.md` are wrong about this window. Three things are owed.

What was checked and holds: every weekday against its date (Saturday 12, Sunday 13, Monday 14, Tuesday 15 December); the clock from 02:52 to 04:16 in chapter 14, minute by minute, including the ten minutes given at 2:55, the six left at the dip and the sixteen the bank held; the distances on the track and the bank (six hundred, four hundred, two hundred, "a kilometre and two hundred metres"); which side the river is on each time she drives or runs the bank; the hands in chapter 13, every one of them; the rings counted in chapters 12, 13 and 14; every sentence spoken in the steel room in chapters 13 and 17 against the rule that an untrue one will not come out; the key from belt to Jeannot and back; the phone from the seat of the van to her chest pocket; the list and the torch between Nadine and Coralie; the council's arithmetic (thirteen members, eleven for, one abstaining, one in the chair); the speech, the confession and the texts, word for word against chapters 11 and 12; the clock radio (it leaves with Coralie, and that night Maylis reads 02:54 off her phone); the umbrella on Monday against the rule that rain follows a night in the chair.

The count of forty-one closes, but only one way: Kévin's three runs carry five, five and nine, which leaves twenty-two for the minibus, and since "he didn't stop at twelve" on its second run, its first carried nine or fewer. Nothing says otherwise.

## 1. People

**P1. Maylis has one arm, then two hands.**
Chapter 14: "With only one arm to push with, she put her left hand on the left handle and her hip against the back of the seat." Chapter 17 holds her to it twice: "'You had the one arm.'" and "It was a morning's work for two arms. She had one."
Against chapter 14, later the same hour: "so Nadine brought the second chair up beside the first and the two went across the yard side by side, with Maylis between the handles."; "Maylis watched him do it. Her own hands were on a chair."; "Maylis had Madame Poey's chair by the handles."; "took the handles out of her hands without asking."
Mend, chapter 14, the later places: "Her own hand was on a chair."; "by its handle"; "took the chair from her without asking"; and for the sisters, "with Nadine on one and Maylis on the other".

## 2. Facts

**F1. The form behind the fruit bowl.**
Chapter 17: "Coralie reached over to the fruit bowl and took a new envelope from behind it, from the place where her form had stood since March."
Against chapter 04, Saturday 28 November: "she reached behind the fruit bowl and took out an envelope that had been standing there since March. She put it in her bag." And chapter 08: "'I've had the form since March. I posted it today, and I posted it for me.'"
The place has been empty for two weeks. "Had stood since March" says the form was there until now.
Mend, chapter 17: "from the place where her form used to stand".

## 3. Time

**T1. Three hours in Bayonne.**
Chapter 15: "It was nearly 6 on Saturday morning when Inguma put her down." Then Coralie: "'I came in at 2:55,' Coralie said. 'Your eyes were open, so I rang, the way I said I would. Bayonne has had you for three hours and found nothing.'"
Against `bayonne` (twenty minutes, chapter 09 and chapter 17). Rung at 2:55, an ambulance is twenty minutes out and twenty back. The hospital has had her from about 3:35 at the soonest, so for less than two and a half hours by nearly 6.
Mend, chapter 15: "for two hours".

**T2. At 3 the baby had not yet been carried out.**
Chapter 17, Madame Darrigrand: "'At 3 Sarthou carried my baby out over his head, and you were a kilometre away, fetching a man out of a dry hole.'"
Against chapter 14: "She was two hundred metres short of the bank when it broke, at 3:11." and Sarthou, with the baby still over his head: "'3:11. I looked at my watch. That is for the file.'" No water was in the street to carry a child over until 3:11. And when he does carry it, Maylis is not a kilometre away but beside him: "He went past her up the street into the dark, with the baby over his head".
Maylis corrects the distance in her head and lets the hour stand, so the hour reads as meant to be right.
Mend, chapter 17: put the hour on her and not on the baby. "'At 3 you were a kilometre away, fetching a man out of a dry hole. Sarthou carried my baby out over his head.'"

**T3. "On Sunday" for a night that was Monday's.**
Chapter 13: "On Sunday, standing in the steel room, Lesca had tried to call it the tide, and the word hadn't come."
Against chapter 09, where Sunday 6 December ends with "That night Maylis didn't push. Then she was in the chair." The chair comes at 2:51, and the calendar in `state.md` puts it on Mon 7 Dec, before dawn. Everywhere else the book says "night" when it names a 2:51 by the evening before ("On Friday night she got back in").
Mend, chapter 13: "On Sunday night".

## 4. The world

**W1. Kévin's van points both ways.**
Chapter 14: "Two vehicles had their headlights pointed at the glass doors." Next paragraph: "The minibus was parked with its nose to the doors and water over its step. Beside it Kévin had backed up his van, opened the rear doors and put two mattresses on the floor."
A van backed up to the doors has its headlights pointing out of the yard. The chapter then leans on those headlights three more times. When the minibus leaves: "Now there was only one van to light the corridor." When he comes back: "Then his headlights came down the hill again and across the glass." followed by "He backed up to the doorstep and got out." And at 4, standing at the back of the van with a wheelchair "half up on the back of his van": "Then he held his left hand out in front of him, in his own headlights, with the fingers spread." A man at his own rear doors is not in his own headlights, whichever way the van faces.
Everything else in the yard fits a van that came in nose first ("The headlights were in her eyes. It was fourteen metres to the back of the van.").
Mend, chapter 14, three places: "Beside it Kévin had driven his van in"; "He drove up to the doorstep and got out."; and for the count, "in the light his headlights threw back off the glass". If he is to stay backed up instead, the same three lights have to become his rear lights.

**W2. Headlights that were switched off.**
Chapter 12: "She switched off the headlights." They are not touched again that night.
Against chapter 13. Lesca starts the van: "She dropped the phone in the lap, reached across with the left hand and turned the key. The engine started." Then: "The headlights swung out across the flood fields and down the wheel tracks. At the far end a round reflector shone red." And later: "At the end of the headlights the reflector was red, and it was getting no nearer."
Mend, chapter 13: after "The engine started." add "She put the lights on."

**W3. Sarthou walks into the gap.**
Chapter 14. Maylis has come down the bank at the gap, beside the last house: "Sarthou himself was in the street in his cape, up to his waist in water, coming up from the lower houses." He tells her: "'I have done every door down to thirty-one.'" Then: "He went past her up the street into the dark, with the baby over his head, shouting the number of the next house."
He has come up to her from the lower houses. Past her there is only his own house and the gap. The next house, the church road and the baby's parents are all back the way he came.
Mend, chapter 14: "He turned and went back down the street into the dark, with the baby over his head, shouting the number of the next house."

**W4. The water is at her ribs and level with the bed.**
Chapter 14: "At the bottom the water came up to her waist, then to her ribs." At the door: "This time it opened. The water on the other side was level with the water on this side." Inside: "The water was level with the bed." and "Coralie laid the torch on the pillow." Then: "She stood in the doorway with the river up to her ribs and looked at the top of the wardrobe."
One level of water cannot be at a standing woman's ribs and also at the mattress of a bed a woman of 94 sleeps in.
The ribs carry the half breath, so the bed should change. Mend, chapter 14: "The water was level with the bed, which had been wound up as high as it went." If the bed is to stay low, both "ribs" become "hips" and the half breath goes.

## 5. The telling

**G1. Lesca sees behind her.**
Chapter 15, in Lesca's head, with Lesca facing the square: "Behind Lesca the deputy mayor had his own copy of the speech out, with a finger under the second line of the third sheet. He turned the sheet over to see whether there was more on the back."
Two paragraphs earlier she learns that she can turn her head ("She could turn her head."). She is not said to do it.
Mend, chapter 15: "She turned her head. Behind her the deputy mayor had his own copy of the speech out".

## Entries in `state.md` that are wrong about this window

- `coralie-m` says she holds "Bayonne's letter". Chapter 17 leaves it on the kitchen table: "She laid it beside the pad." and "She turned the envelope around on the table." She is never seen to pick it up.
- `marie-jo-m` says she "had the hall open at 3:30". Chapter 17 says "'I had the coffee pot on in here at 3:30 on Saturday morning, and I counted them in'". The hall was taking people before that: at about 3:18 in chapter 14 Kévin says "'I've done one trip. Five people. Bixente's done one.'" The entry should say the coffee pot.
- Calendar, L, Sat 12 Dec: "Bayonne from about 3 to 6". See T1. Twenty minutes each way from a call at 2:55 makes it about 3:35.

## Owed

Planted in the window, not paid, and not in part 4:

- **The second call at the dip** (chapter 14). "The phone on the seat began to ring again. The screen said *Castagnet, house*." His order had been "touch nothing until I ring again". He rings again six minutes later and she leaves it on the seat. What he rang to say is never learned, and on Sunday he says "'That gate went up without a word from me'". If this is meant to sit under `larrere-m-reckoning`, the entry should name it.
- **What gave in her side** (chapter 14). "Something gave in her side. She heard it go." After Coralie's warning in chapter 07 ("Push on it and it goes through into the lung"), and with a quarter of a breath for the rest of the night, it gets three turns of bandage in chapter 17 and a grave to dig. Nobody names it. Part 4 has only `pink`.
- **The minibus after its second run** (chapter 14). "The minibus backed out of the yard, pushing a wave in front of it, and turned up the hill." Chapter 12 set it to go up and come "back for more", seven minutes each way. It is not seen again, and half an hour later "'There isn't another trip,' Coralie said" with nine people for one van.
