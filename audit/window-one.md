# Window one: chapters 00 to 04

Checked against `state.md` parts 1 to 4 and against the rest of the window. Chapters 05, 06, 07, 10, 12, 15 and 17 were opened only to check single facts.

Counts: People 0 · Facts 2 · Time 2 · World 2 · The telling 1.

What was checked and holds: every date against its weekday (24, 25, 27, 28, 29 November; Friday 11 December; Saint Catherine on the 25th; All Saints four weeks before the 29th); every countdown (eighteen, seventeen, fifteen, thirteen days); the eighteen days of rain; twelve, eleven and sixteen years wherever they are used; the hands in chapters 02, 03 and 04; Lesca's sentence in chapters 02 and 03, word for word; the file lines against chapter 06; the letter numbers; the pulse figures; every sentence spoken in the steel room against the rule that an untrue one will not come out.

## 1. People

None.

## 2. Facts

**F1. Bread at the café on a Monday.**
Chapter 04: "She went to the café at 10 for bread, which on Sundays the café sold from a crate on the counter."
Against chapter 05, Monday 30 November, the dealer: "'The bread went at 11.'"
If the café sells bread only on Sundays, there is none to go at 11 on a Monday.
Mend, chapter 04: cut "on Sundays". Chapter 17 (a Sunday, bread in its crate) still stands.

**F2. The date on the stone is never on the stone.**
Chapter 01, the picture: "Under hers was a date. That she could read: 12 DECEMBER. A year came after it, but that part of the picture wouldn't stay in her head." Chapter 04 confirms it is cut there: "The 12th was the morning they got him out."
Against chapter 03, where Lesca goes round to the front and reads it: "The top line was JEAN CASTAGNET, who had been Jean in the town hall's files and Jeannot at the café. Under him, in the same capitals, was MAYLIS LESCA. There was frost in the bottom of each letter." Larrère at the table: "It has a man's name and a woman's name and nothing between them." Chapter 06 and chapter 15 ("The two names on it") also show two lines and no date.
The whole countdown hangs on a date that the stone, each time it is looked at, does not carry. `state.md` has noticed: `stone` says the picture "adds" it.
Mend, chapter 03: one sentence after "MAYLIS LESCA.", such as "Under that was the date."

## 3. Time

**T1. Coralie's alarm.**
Chapter 01: "But Coralie had to be up before 6, so Maylis didn't ask."
Against chapter 03, an early-shift morning (chapter 04: "She had the early shift to get to"), where the alarm rings as the clock radio "said 06:00"; and chapter 04, "At 6 on Monday morning she woke up", with Coralie only just down in chapter 05.
Mend, chapter 01: "up at 6".

**T2. The lines of the file.**
Chapter 03: "Every morning she had gone down to the old gate" and "written down what she saw, one line a day."
Against, same chapter: "On 8 December 2014 the river had reached the line. Three lines later she came to the last one in her own handwriting." / "*11 December 2014, 22:00. Over the line. Rising.*"
Three lines after the 8th are the 9th, 10th and 11th, so the 11th has one line and it was written at 10 at night, not in the morning. The same passage also counts the other way: the 24th to the 28th is "Five lines later", which is five lines counting the first, where the 8th to the 11th is three lines not counting it.
Mend, chapter 03: "Every morning" to "Every day", and "Five lines later" to "Four lines later".

## 4. The world

**W1. What Madame Etcheto gives Inguma to count.**
Chapter 02: "'You'll count the stars,' the voice said. 'The ones behind the cloud as well. Then the grass on the low fields, each blade. Then the sand on the coast, from Bayonne to the border. You're not to come till you've finished.'" and "'Till 3,' Coralie said."
Against chapter 03, Arlette: "For Inguma she had the grass on the village field. She gave it that, blade by blade, and it was counting till daylight. Madame Etcheto, down in the Port, gives it the stars." and "Stars, in December, under cloud. It needs more than that. A great many things, and small ones."
The grass of one field holds it till daylight; the stars, the grass of all the low fields and the sand of the coast hold it only till 3. Arlette also says Etcheto gives it the stars and nothing else. Every later count (the gravel, the roadsides, the corn) works on Arlette's rule, that more things take longer.
Mend, chapter 02: cut "Then the grass on the low fields, each blade. Then the sand on the coast, from Bayonne to the border." Then, further down, "which was the stars behind the cloud or the grass" needs a second thing to choose between, such as "the stars she could see or the ones behind the cloud".

**W2. How long a push lasts.**
Chapter 01: "She pushed up against the thing with all the breath she had, which wasn't much." / "The thing pressed down just as hard." / "At 2:54 the little finger of her right hand moved against the blanket." / "The thing got down off her and left the way it had come".
Chapter 04, Lesca: "You pushed it off, the first nights. I felt you do it."
Against chapter 07: "Inguma pressed down just as hard. Maylis pushed until the church clock struck 4, when Inguma got down and left the way it had come." and chapter 12: "It was an hour and nine minutes short of 4."
The same push, in the same words, ends after three minutes in the window and cannot end before 4 afterwards. The first night can be read as different because nobody was under it with her, but chapter 04 has her pushing it off on nights when Lesca was. No sentence gives the rule, and `state.md` (`inguma`) records both lengths side by side.
Mend, chapter 04: one clause in Lesca's line that states it, such as "With me under it, it stays till 4." Chapter 01 can then stand as the night it had nobody to carry.

## 5. The telling

**P1. Maylis sees behind her.**
Chapter 04: "Marie-Jo stayed on the bottom step with her bucket and watched the pot all the way to the fourth house."
The chapter is in Maylis's head, and she is walking away from the church with her back to it.
Mend, chapter 04: "Maylis felt her watch the pot all the way to the fourth house", or cut the sentence. Slight.

## Entries in `state.md` that are wrong about this window

- `counts`: "Etcheto's stars: till 3". Chapter 02 gives her the stars, the grass and the sand (W1).
- `stone`: cites chapter 03 for the date. Chapter 03 shows no date (F2).
- `inguma`: "gone by 2:54 on the first night" and "stays till 4 when she pushes". On the first night she pushed (W2).
- `rain-2014`: "eighteen days (ch00, ch03, ch15)". In the window the count is in chapter 00 and chapter 04 ("on the eighteenth day of rain"); chapter 03 has only the start, the night before the 24th.
- `box`: "door with a rubber seal and no handle (ch01, ch02, ch05)". The door is in chapter 04: "It had rubber around its edge and no handle."

## Owed

- **The leaf crumbs.** Chapter 01: "A few crumbs of brown leaf fell on the floor, as they had most nights for twelve years." Coralie puts them down to the plane trees. Nobody corrects her and they are never seen again. It is the same thread as `sat-on-me`, which `state.md` already lists as dropped, and it is the only proof in the book that Inguma had been coming to that bed all along.
- **Thirty-one.** Chapter 04: "I gave that word up when I was thirty-one". It is the only age either woman is given, and no chapter gives a birth year or a present age, so the reader cannot work out that thirty-one was 2014.
