# Window three: chapters 9 to 12

Checked against `state.md` parts 1 to 4 and against the window itself. Other chapters were opened only to confirm single facts.

The window is clean on the things that usually break. Every weekday matches its date. Every clock time follows the one before it. The hands are right in every scene (Lesca's left and Maylis's right at the kitchen table in 9; Maylis working left-handed all through 12; Lesca's scar on the right in 11). The quoted texts (the petition, the pad, the three messages, the speech line, the call list, the file's times) come back word for word. What follows is everything that does not hold, and most of it is small.

## 1. People

**1.1 — ch12. Jeannot knows an order he was not there to hear.** (minor)

> 'If that phone rings on your dashboard while you're standing here, he'll have your job for it, and I won't write you a letter.'

Larrère gave the order about the phone on the town hall steps, to Maylis alone (ch12: "Keep your phone on the dashboard, charged, and touch nothing until I ring."). All Jeannot is told on the bank is: "'I've been told to stay by the gate. And keep it shut.'" Nothing tells him that Larrère will ring, or where the phone is.

Mend, in Maylis's line on the bank at 5: "I've been told to stay by the gate, by the phone. And keep it shut." Jeannot's line at the trapdoor can then stand.

## 2. Facts

**2.1 — ch9. Bordenave's entry has lost a clause.** (minor)

> 'Monday 30 November. She says the mayor will tell her on Tuesday the 8th, at this door, that the gate stays shut, and that people in the Port know they live by a river.'

In ch5 he is told to take it down slowly, and what he is given has three parts: "That the river's at the line, that the gate stays shut, and that people in the Port know they live by a river." The part that has gone is the first thing Larrère has just said through his window ("They tell me the river's at the line").

Mend, in ch9: "…at this door, that the river's at the line, that the gate stays shut, and that people in the Port know they live by a river."

## 3. Time

**3.1 — ch9. The tide on Tuesday against the tide on Friday.**

> At 3 that afternoon Maylis was up on the bank. The tide had brought the river to her father's line.

against, in the same chapter:

> 'Open,' Maylis said. 'On Friday at 2, on the falling tide, with nobody on the fields, by your order.'

and the petition ("on Friday the 11th, in daylight, on the falling tide"), which repeats ch4 and ch5 ("on that Friday afternoon … while the tide is going out").

High water comes about fifty minutes later each day. A tide that is at its top at 3 on Tuesday is at its top at about 5:30 on Friday, so at 2 on Friday it is still coming in. Chapter 12 agrees with the later hour, not with the petition: "By 5 Maylis was up on the bank … The river was over the red line … just under the boards of the platform."

The text never states the fifty minutes, so only a reader who knows tides will catch it. A reader on that coast will.

Mend, in ch9, the Tuesday sentence only: "At 3 that afternoon Maylis was up on the bank. The river had come up to her father's line." With no tide named on Tuesday, no hour of high water is fixed before Friday, and the falling tide of Friday afternoon is left unchallenged. The state's "at the line at 3 pm" stays true.

## 4. The world

**4.1 — ch12 into ch13. The headlights are switched off and then shine.**

> She switched off the headlights. On her right the river went by, close under the top of the bank, with a sound like a crowd far away.

That is at 10. Nothing switches them on again in ch12. In ch13, at 02:52, Lesca only turns the key ("reached across with the left hand and turned the key. The engine started."), and then: "The headlights swung out across the flood fields and down the wheel tracks. At the far end a round reflector shone red." Chapter 13 needs them again at 02:53 ("At the end of the headlights the reflector was red").

Mend, in ch13, since ch12 uses the dark: "…turned the key, then the lights. The engine started." The left hand is the right one for it.

**4.2 — ch12. The lamp on the platform is lit before it exists.** (minor; an unlit light, not two facts at odds)

> Three. Over Inguma's back she could see the lamp on the platform and the handle where she had left it.

This is the first the book says of a lamp there. After dark Kévin works on the same platform "with a torch in his teeth". At 11, with the van's lights already off, Maylis unlocks the box and fits the handle with no light named at all. Chapters 13 and 14 then lean on the lamp throughout.

Mend, in ch12, at 11: "She got out and went to the platform, under its lamp."

## 5. The telling

**5.1 — ch10, first scene. Neither woman is in the building.**

> A pad was made to hold a litre. Coralie had never seen one fail.

The rest of ch10 is in Maylis's head, and this scene is in Coralie's, at the care home, at 10 at night. Elsewhere the book leaves the two heads only at a bedside where one of the two bodies is lying (ch6, "At home, in Maylis's bedroom, it was raining. Coralie hadn't gone in to work."; ch8, "In Lesca's own bedroom the clock radio said 23:55."). This is the one scene with neither of them there, and Maylis is given only one sentence of it afterwards ("The yard came in under our back door tonight").

It reads as chosen, and it is listed because it is the only one of its kind. No small change mends it. If the rule is meant to be strict, the scene has to be told by Coralie at the kitchen table.

**5.2 — ch12. Boots seen through a van door.** (minor)

> It stopped beside hers, door to door, and Jeannot rolled his window down. He was wearing his rubber boots.

Maylis is in her seat and he is in his. She cannot see his feet. A little further on she can ("Jeannot came up the steps onto the platform in his rubber boots").

Mend, in ch12: cut the first sentence about the boots.

## Owed

Planted in this window, and not in part 4 as paid or as left open on purpose:

- **The three-column page (ch9).** "Coralie tore the chart off the pad, folded it in four and put it in the pocket of her dressing gown." Part 1 of the state has her holding it. It is never taken out again in chapters 13 to 17. It goes the way of `torn-page`.
- **Darrigrand's step (ch11).** "All I ask is that nobody puts a glass down on my step." The reception is heard once from a distance in ch16 (a firework from the bank). The step and the man do not come back.
- **"I've written it down. Nil." (ch9).** Said in the steel room, so under `truth-rule` there is a written sum somewhere in Lesca's house or office. It is never seen. Kin to `motion-sheet`.
- **Hontaube (ch12).** "From 1 January you won't need a form. You'll belong to us," and the form she promises to post that day. The state files this under `maylis-file (ch17)` as left open on purpose, but chapter 17 never names Hontaube, the agreement or the form. On the page it is planted once and dropped, which is not the same as left open.

## The state against this window

- `maylis-file` cites ch17 for "Hontaube from 1 January". The only place M's Hontaube appears is ch12. See above.
- The M calendar puts "the 2014 night received" on Thursday 10 December. By the text it comes after 2:51 on the Friday ("Now Maylis was back under her own lampshade … The clock radio said 04:57 … It was Friday morning."). Nothing in the book depends on it.
- Nothing else in parts 1 to 4 disagrees with chapters 9 to 12.
