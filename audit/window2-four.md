# Window four: chapters 13 to 17

Read against `state.md` parts 1 to 4, then against chapters 0 to 12, which I read through once to check each fact in the window where it was first fixed.

Part 5 was not read. Its four headings were visible when I looked for where part 4 ends, and two of them name things in this window (the gutter, the hour of the river reading). Both are listed below because the text bears them out, but they are not independent finds.

Five contradictions, none of them in the plot. The night of 12 December closes on its own clock, and so do the counts.

## 1. People

**One, slight.**

**Chapter 17.** "Maylis had the card from her raincoat in her hand. *left*."

She is in bed: "That night Maylis got into the middle of the bed, on her back, and waited for Inguma." The card lives in the raincoat's chest pocket (2: "She put the card in the chest pocket of her raincoat"; 14: "She pushed the card back in"; `maylis`, On her). Chapter 13 fixed that she arrives in the steel room as she is in the waking world: "Maylis faced her in the yellow raincoat, with rain on its shoulders and mud up to the ankles of her boots." On 27 November, with the card in the coat, she had nothing in her hand in the chair (2). So the card can only be in her hand in the steel room if she took it to bed, and the book does not say she did.

Mend, chapter 17: "That night Maylis got into the middle of the bed, on her back, with the card from her raincoat in her hand, and waited for Inguma."

## 2. Facts

**Two.**

**Chapter 15.** "the red line on the pillar of the old gate, which she had read at 8 every morning."

Chapter 3 has the readings as "one line a day", and the only one that carries an hour is the last: "*11 December 2014, 22:00. Over the line. Rising.*" (`rain-2014`). If she read at 8 every morning, the single line for the 11th would be a morning line. Nothing else in the book gives the 2014 readings an hour.

Mend, chapter 15: "which she had read every day."

**Chapter 14, slight.** "Through the hole Maylis could see the foot of the bank, a metre away, with clay running down it."

Room twelve is "the last room at that end", the end under the bank. Chapter 12 puts a yard at that same end, between the building and the bank: "The door opened onto the yard, at the end of the building nearest the bank. The bank rose above the yard wall." Chapter 10 gives that yard a drain, and in chapter 12 Coralie stands in it in the light of the back door. A yard with a wall cannot lie between the end of the building and a bank whose foot is a metre from a window at that end. It holds only if the yard covers half of that end and the bank comes in close on the other half, which nothing says.

Mend, chapter 14: "Through the hole Maylis could see the yard wall, and the bank above it, with clay running down it."

## 3. Time

**One. This is the worst of the five.**

**Chapter 16.** "'That back gutter's blocked again. Have a look at it before you go.'" and "The gutter was packed solid. Lesca cleared it by hand, dropping the rotten leaves past Maïté's shoulder in black handfuls."

Fourteen days earlier, on 28 November, she did that job at that house: "Lesca set the tile back in its row and cleared the leaves out of the gutter along the roof" (3). The stone village has had no rain in that time or for two weeks before it: "No rain had fallen on her own roof for two weeks" (3), frost on 2 December (6), "Her own roof was dry" on the 11th (11), dry and windy on the 12th (15); the calendar in `state.md` has no wet day for that village. A gutter cleared a fortnight ago in a dry month is not packed solid with rotten leaves, and nobody can tell that a gutter is blocked until water runs in it.

Mend, chapter 3: cut "and cleared the leaves out of the gutter along the roof", so that on 28 November she only sets the tile. Nothing leans on the clause. What Maylis is given of that afternoon in chapter 4 is "a roof tile in her hand". Chapter 16 then stands as written: "again" reaches back to other years, and the leaves are the autumn's.

## 4. The world

**One, slight.**

**Chapter 13.** "Her father was sitting at the end of the second row, with his watch on the paper tablecloth beside his card. He didn't look up from his numbers. She didn't stop. The rows went on and on, and behind her the voice went on calling her name."

When Lesca arrives, the steel room is behind her and the tables run away from it "on further than she could see", with "the voice at the far end". Chapter 9 puts her father at the end of the second row nearest the steel room: Maylis reaches him first on her way out to the empty chair. So Lesca, with the voice behind her, is walking back towards the steel room, and passes him where the row stops. Past the end of a row the rows cannot go on and on; what lies there is the steel room, "open on its fourth side". If instead she is walking away from the steel room, the voice is in front of her and her father is not on her way.

Mend, chapter 13: cut "The rows went on and on, and" so that it reads "She didn't stop. Behind her the voice went on calling her name."

## state.md against the window

Four entries are looser than the text. None changes a finding above.

- `coralie-rain`, "Left the house on Sunday 13 Dec at about 9 pm". Nine is when she arrives: "Coralie came in at 9 that evening." She leaves after the talk at the table, the packing and the strapping.
- `lesca`, "On her: … the card *right*". The card is not on her. It is kept in the steel room: "Lesca was holding the one from the shelf" (17), and the shelf is under the panel (2).
- `care-home`, "Kévin's van: five a trip". True of the first two. The third takes nine: "He took the next one from Jeannot and the one after from Maylis. There were seven chairs left with people in them, and two mattresses", then "'They'll have to sit up against each other.'"
- `right-hand` in part 4, "After the 12th the book does not say what the hand can do." The fear planted in chapter 9 was that Lesca would keep the hand. Chapter 14 answers that at 2:53: "Maylis had her body back", and "Maylis took her hand off the brake." Chapter 17 has her "holding the pad in both hands". What stays unsaid is only the grip.

## Checked and found to agree

Listed because these are where a contradiction was most likely.

- **The clock from 2:51 to 4:16.** 2:52 on the dashboard, eight rings, 2:53; the patch at 2:55 and ten minutes given; off the bank at 02:57; six minutes left at the dip, which is 2:59; the break at 3:11, sixteen minutes after 2:55; the gate at 3:20; 03:31; 4:00; 4:10; 04:16. "At 3" at the trapdoor (17, twice) is two or three minutes early by this clock and reads as a round figure in speech.
- **The run.** Twelve minutes from the dip to the break cover 800 metres on foot and 400 in Jeannot's van, about twice the pace of the timed walk in chapter 7.
- **Forty-one.** Kévin's three trips carry five, five and nine. That leaves twenty-two for the minibus's two trips, the second of them over twelve. Marie-Jo's "three times" agrees.
- **The vote.** Thirteen sitting: the deputy mayor in the chair, Larrère abstaining, eleven hands.
- **Hands.** In the van Lesca has the legs, the left hand and the mouth; the right hand takes the wheel, the eyes and the brake. Wherever a later sentence names Maylis's hand it is the left, or both for a thing of no weight.
- **Sides.** River on the right facing the gate from the Port (12), on the left running back (14). The village van faces the hut in the dip, so Kévin's chain goes on its back and it comes out backwards (17).
- **The clock radio.** Coralie packs "the clock she woke up to", and that night Maylis reads 02:54 off her phone.
- **Days and dates.** Saturday 12, Sunday 13, Monday 14, Tuesday 15 December 2026 all agree. Rain on Monday follows the meeting of Sunday night, as `water` has it.

## Owed

Planted in the window and not in part 4, paid or open.

- **The medal** (15). "Whether the prefect might have the medal back, until things are clearer." She hands it over. Nothing says what "clearer" comes to. It could sit under `removal`.
- **Arlette's kitchen** (15). "They'll be in my kitchen tomorrow morning with this, the whole of the Port. … They can take it up your steps." Sunday 13 and Monday 14 are not shown in the stone village, so neither the kitchen nor the steps is seen. Looks left open on purpose.
- **The words at the grave** (17). "I've had them written out for me, and I'm not sure they're a prayer." "They'll do." The burial lies after the last page. The calendar notes it; part 4 does not. Plainly left open.
- **The camera** (15). A television camera is on her when she says it, and the cameraman looks at her over the top of it. The book shows what the village does with the sentence and not what goes out beyond it. Probably meant; it could sit under `removal`.
