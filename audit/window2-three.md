## 1. People

**1.1 The phone is in a hand that was busy two minutes before (chapter 12 against chapter 13).**

Chapter 12: "02:49. She was awake. She laid her left hand on the steering wheel and touched each finger in turn. She counted five." Then, at 2:51: "In her hand, the phone began to ring." and "The phone went on ringing in a hand she couldn't close."

Chapter 13, one minute later: "The phone was ringing in the left hand." and "The right hand moved before anything else did. It was Maylis's. It took hold of the steering wheel at the top."

At 02:49 the left hand is spread on the wheel and the right is doing the touching, so neither holds the phone. At 2:51 it is in the left with no word of her taking it up again, and from then on she cannot move.

Mend, in chapter 12: after "She counted five." add "Then she took the phone up again in that hand."

## 2. Facts

**2.1 The stone village's secretary is in at 9, in a town hall she opens at 2 (chapter 11 against chapter 6).**

Chapter 11, at "9 on Friday morning": "Behind her the secretary had the visitors' book open and her pen ready."

Chapter 6, the same town hall nine days earlier: "That left Maylis alone in the town hall until 2." and "At 2 the secretary opened the town hall." State key `townhall` records both halves without saying which holds.

Mend, in chapter 11: "Behind her the secretary, who had come in early all that week for the ceremony, had the visitors' book open and her pen ready."

**2.2 A spare bed in a home with forty-two beds and forty-two people in them (chapter 10 against chapters 9 and 12).**

Chapter 10: "'I'd like you at the front tonight,' Coralie said. 'There's a bed made up in three, and it's dry.'"

Chapter 9: "But what I'd have had is a river over forty-two beds." Chapter 12: "There are forty-two people in the care home, right under that bank, and most of them can't walk." State key `port`: forty-two beds. Chapter 14 counts forty-one in the hall and one in room twelve. No bed is free.

Mend, in chapter 10: "I've had a folding bed put up in three, and it's dry."

**2.3 The sandbags are tied, then have open ends (chapter 12 against itself).**

"'What's that, six months?' He tied the neck of a bag."

"Maylis laid the bags across the bottom of the door with their open ends folded underneath, the way Sarthou laid his."

Mend, in the second sentence: "with their necks folded underneath".

## 3. Time

**3.1 The river is at the boards ten hours before the height that puts it there (chapter 12 against chapters 0, 10 and 14, and against itself).**

Chapter 12, at 5 pm: "The river was over the red line. It had gone the colour of the sky, and it was very wide. It went past just under the boards of the platform."

Chapter 12, at about 6:30 pm: "Then he saw her face, and the river going by at the height of the boards."

Chapter 12, after 10 pm: "On her right the river went by, close under the top of the bank, with a sound like a crowd far away."

Chapter 12, at about 6 pm: "'It'll be ninety over the line by 3, Jeannot.'" Chapter 14, at 2:53: "Pau says the river is ninety centimetres over the line and rising."

Chapter 0 gives the place the river reaches at that height. In 2014, at the minute of the phone call: "When she climbed onto the platform and pointed her torch at the water, it was going past just under the boards, close enough to touch." Chapter 10 puts the 2014 river at that minute at "ninety centimetres over the red line and rising", and chapter 4 says the two rivers run "day for day".

So "just under the boards" is ninety over the line. At 5 pm the river has only just passed the line (state calendar, Friday 11: "over the line by 5 pm"), and it has ninety centimetres still to rise. It cannot be at the same place at both hours.

Inside chapter 12 the three readings also run the wrong way. The platform stands above the top of the bank ("Jeannot came up the steps onto the platform"). A river "at the height of the boards" at 6:30 is higher than one "close under the top of the bank" after 10, on a night when it is rising to its peak.

Mend, in chapter 12 only, two phrases:

- 5 pm: "It went past a metre under the boards of the platform."
- 6:30 pm: "Then he saw her face, and how far the river had come up under the boards."

The 10 pm sentence can stand. The order is then a metre under the boards, nearer, close under the top of the bank, and at 3 just under the boards, as in chapter 0.

A wider point for whoever holds chapter 0: state key `port` has "a clay bank as high as a house" and key `father` has the red line "at the height of the Port's doorsteps". Ninety centimetres over the doorsteps is not the top of a bank as high as a house. Chapter 12 inherits that; it does not start it.

## 4. The world

**4.1 Room twelve's window is a metre from the bank, across a yard (chapter 12 against chapter 14).**

Chapter 12: "The door opened onto the yard, at the end of the building nearest the bank." and "The bank rose above the yard wall." Chapter 10 puts a drain in that yard, and in chapter 12 Coralie stands in it "looking up at the bank".

Chapter 14, in room twelve, "The last room at that end": "Through the hole Maylis could see the foot of the bank, a metre away, with clay running down it."

A yard with a wall and a drain lies between that end of the building and the bank. The foot of the bank cannot be a metre from a window in the same end. This is the least sure entry in the report: it holds only if the window of twelve is in the end wall, which "the water from the gap was breaking against it as high as the windowsills" suggests.

Mend, in chapter 14: "the foot of the bank, across the yard, with clay running down it."

The motion is last seen on that table in chapter 3. Nothing in chapter 11 says Coralie has read it.

**Calendar, rain village, Sunday 6 December.** The last column has only "Lesca in her until breakfast". Chapter 9 has a meeting that night: "That night Maylis didn't push. Then she was in the chair.
