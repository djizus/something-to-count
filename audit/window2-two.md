# Continuity check, window two: chapters 05 to 08

Read against `state.md` parts 1 to 4. Other chapters were opened only to check single facts.

One thing to know before the list. Part 5 was not read, but its four headings were seen while finding where part 4 ends. The second heading names chapter 6 and a count given at 11 pm. World item 1 below was worked out from the text, but it was not found blind.

Counts: People 1, Facts 1, Time none, World 2, The telling none. One state entry is wrong about this window.

## 1. People

**1.1 Coralie's job (chapter 5). Slight.**

> 'The husband,' Josiane said. 'She lives with a nurse, and that's to throw us off.'

Against the same chapter, forty lines on:

> 'And who do I say told me?' Coralie said. 'I'm a care assistant, and you cut roadsides.'

and chapter 12: "I'm a care assistant, Maylis." In chapter 8 a nurse is what Coralie wanted to be and is not ("a night nurse called Ithurbide ... I wanted to be her").

If Josiane is meant to be loose with the word, leave it. If not, change Josiane's line in 5: "She lives with a woman from the care home, and that's to throw us off."

## 2. Facts

**2.1 The file has no centimetres in it (chapter 7). State key `file-form`.**

> 'Fifty-five under,' Lesca said out loud, to the right hand. 'My file has the same.'

Against chapter 3, where every line of the file that is quoted has the same form and no figure:

> *28 November 2014. Under the line. Rain.*

> *11 December 2014, 22:00. Over the line. Rising.*

Chapter 5's "seventy under the line" does not name the file, so it can stand as what she remembers. Only the sentence in 7 claims the figure is written down.

Smallest change, in 7: "'Fifty-five under,' Lesca said out loud, to the right hand. 'I read the same, that day.'"

## 3. Time

None. Every date agrees with its day. The pulse chart runs down without a rise (64 at 6, 58 at 10, 48 then 47 by 3, 36 at 18:40, 34 at 7). The council fits between 6:30 and the stroke of 7. The two dry days fall on the two nights she pushed.

## 4. The world

**4.1 A count given at 11 pm waits until 6 to start (chapter 6). State keys `chair`, `fetch`.**

> At 11, on her back in bed, she said the words quietly to the top of the wardrobe. She gave Inguma every metre of roadside she had cut in this village since 2008, to count before it came to take her home. She worked out that the count would take it one working day: eight hours from 6, when Lesca woke up.

and, the same night:

> That night, when the warmth beside her in the chair got up to go, she stayed sitting.

Everywhere else a count takes hold when it is given, and Inguma goes off to do it:

- Chapter 2, the same act at the same hour (words to the top of the wardrobe at bedtime) is meant to keep it away at 2:51: "'That will do for one night.'" And of Madame Etcheto's stars: "'Till 3,' Coralie said. 'Then it comes anyway'".
- Chapter 8: "The tablecloths came down again, one after another, all the way back to the door."
- Chapter 9: "It stopped where it had landed and waited for its work."

Here the count is given at 11 pm and nothing happens for seven hours. Inguma still comes at 2:51 and sits on her, or she would not reach the chair. Then the thirteen hours run from 6 ("It was five hours late" at the stroke of 7). Had the count begun when she gave it, Inguma would have been busy until noon: no chair that night, no crossing, and no five hours.

Smallest change, in 6, two sentences. At 11 she only chooses the count and works out the eight hours. She gives it at 6:20 in the mayor's bedroom, when Inguma comes to fetch her, which is how Lesca does it in 5 ("That's a day") and in 7 ("By 6:20 Lesca had sent Inguma off to count the lake again"). One line after "She was the one driving" does it. The pad still says 2, and 7 pm is still five hours late by the pad. `fetch` would then read "Roadsides: 06:20 to 19:00".

Seen outside the window, for whoever has chapter 9: "Each would keep it busy till morning." The roadsides are known by then to take thirteen hours; from 2:52 that is until about 4 pm.

**4.2 The empty body lasts twice as long in the stone village (chapter 8). State key `empty-body`. Not a strict contradiction; one of rate.**

> In Lesca's own bedroom the clock radio said 23:55. Her mother had sat beside the bed since noon with her coat on. Coralie was asleep across the landing with her door open.

and then "'That's till morning,' Madame Etcheto told Coralie", which leaves that body empty from 6 on Saturday to breakfast on Sunday, about twenty-six hours (chapter 9: "Since 6 yesterday"; chapter 11: "from noon on Saturday till breakfast on Sunday").

Against chapter 6, where the same body in the other village is close to death after thirteen:

> It had been 64 at 6. At 10 it was 58, and it hadn't once gone up.

> The last line on the chart said 36. Coralie sat on the edge of the bed with her phone in her hand. The doctor's number was on the screen, with her thumb over it.

and chapter 7: "'Your pulse was 34 when I rang him,'". The chart falls faster each hour: about one and a half beats an hour in the morning, three by evening. On that chart Lesca's body has no pulse left well before Sunday morning, and it had already done fifteen hours on Monday the 30th. What the book gives it is "colder each time" (9) and "colder than on the Monday" (11), and the Coralie who rang Arlette after twenty minutes on 28 November is asleep.

No figure is given for Lesca's body, so the two can stand side by side. But the reader has been taught by the chart what thirteen hours costs.

Smallest change, in 8: one sentence in Arlette's scene that gives this body a count and says it has stopped falling (for instance that Coralie took the wrist at 10 and wrote the number she had written at 6). If Lesca is meant to be near death that night, then the same sentence should say so with a number, and chapter 9 should show it.

## 5. The telling

None. Chapters 5 and 7 stay in Maylis's head. Chapter 6 cuts to Coralie at the bedside and chapter 8 to Arlette at the other bedside; chapter 10 does the same, so it is the book's habit. No village is named or described as the other one.

## State entries wrong about this window

**`file` (part 4, "6, 17").** The entry treats one file as open from chapter 6 to the end. There are two.

The file of chapter 6 is Jeannot's complaint about his drained hut:

> 'Complaint of Castagnet, Jean, against persons unknown,' Sarthou said.

> 'Either is not a person. I cannot summon Either to the station.' He wrote, then put his cap on. 'Then this file contains one name, and it is the name of the person who turned the handle.'

It is withdrawn inside the window. Chapter 6: "Friday. You'll have your complaint back." Chapter 8: "'You've had your complaint back,' he said."

The file of chapter 17 is a new one, about the night of the 12th: "'There is a file,' Sarthou said. ... 'The keyholder was under an order to remain at the gate. At 2:57 she left it.'"

So `file` should cite 17 only, and the complaint belongs under Paid (6→8).
