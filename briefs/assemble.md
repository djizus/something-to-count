# Brief: assembling the revised book

A third round of critics called the book a 7 with a 9 inside it. `revision3.md` is the plan that answers them: the swap becomes the middle of the book. You are one of two writers building the chapters from it.

## Before you start

Read `PLAN.md` (the bar), `revision3.md` in full, the sections of `bible.md` it says changed, and `audit/comedy.md` (the method and the tone rules). Skim the reports in `audit/round3-*.md` so that you know what each change is for, and read section 4 of each closely: what must not be touched.

- The chapters the plan works from are in `manuscript-v3/`. The plan names their scenes by chapter and letter, in the order of the section breaks: 05b is the second scene of chapter 5.
- The new scenes are in `drafts/v4/`.
- You write the new chapters into `manuscript/NN.md`, numbered as the plan numbers them.

## For each chapter that is yours, in order

1. Lay out the scenes as section 1 of the plan lists them: keep, move, cut to a line, new.
2. Where the plan says **keep whole**, the text stands, apart from the line pass. Where it gives a length, cut to it, and cut what the plan's section 3 names first.
3. Set each new scene in its place and make the joins. A join is something that happens, not a sentence of the narrator's.
4. Apply the line pass in section 3 of the plan to the whole chapter: the woman named in the first sentence, Inguma named once it has been named, the way in told in full only where the plan says, fingers counted only where someone sees it, each habit of speech with its one owner, the named secretary and deputy, the glosses, half the figures in chapters 1 to 6, half the negated verbs.
5. Nothing is staged twice. If a thing was played in the other woman's chapter, yours gives it a line.
6. The chapter ends where the plan says it ends, on that event, with nothing after it.
7. Stay within ten per cent of the word target.

## Hold to

- One register to a scene. Do not smooth a comic scene and a dark one into an even tone.
- Follow the calendar in the bible. The flood of 2014 is the night of Thursday 11 to Friday 12 December.
- **Mend the clock.** The last pass cut "had" and broke the tense, so that earlier events read as present ones. Wherever the story steps back in time, the pluperfect returns. Check every kept passage for it.
- Hold to the plan's line pass: no noun withheld, few numbers, each person with an idiom of their own, pace that follows the scene, no recap before a payoff, no feeling announced flat, and every chapter ending on its turn.
- Section 10 of the bible. Neither woman is softened.
- A stranger must be able to read it at speed: plain sentences where something has to be understood, and no sentence that needs reading twice.

## Rules

- Load the `stop-slop` skill and audit each chapter before the next.
- British spelling. Nothing you write mentions any writing tool, model, vendor or session. Write only your chapters. Do not edit `manuscript-v3/`, the drafts, the bible or the plan, and do not run git. Leave `state.md` alone; it is rebuilt afterwards.
- If the plan is wrong or silent on something you need, decide, and list it.
- Reply when all your chapters are written: one line a chapter with its word count and anything you decided that the plan did not.
