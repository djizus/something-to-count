# Brief: the book moves to the Basque Country

JC has decided the setting. It is no longer the Landes. The village is in the French Basque Country.

This lands while you are redesigning the figure (`briefs/figure.md`). Do both together: the figure now comes from Basque tradition, and the retrofit list carries the setting changes as well.

## What should survive

The lower Adour's left bank is Basque Country (Labourd, in the Pyrénées-Atlantiques), and it has flood meadows, banks, gates and glass eels as the right bank does. So the river, the flood, the gate, the winch, the bank, the Port quarter and the eels can stay where they are. Move the village across the river, not across the map. Check each of these against what is true on that bank, and change only what is false there.

## What changes

1. **Names.** The village, the neighbouring commune, and every person. Basque villages have Basque names and most families in them have Basque surnames. Choose them with care for how they read in English: a reader has to tell eight people apart. Decide the protagonist's first name and surname afresh; the device stands (first name where nothing happened, surname where she became mayor). Write a table of every old name and its new one.
2. **The figure.** Research what Basque tradition says comes to sleepers in the night and sits on them. Start with Inguma, also called Mauma, and the words people said against it. Verify before you use any of it. A named night-spirit with a charm against it is a better base for the monster JC asked for than the one we had. Lesca's mother's four rules become what a Basque grandmother would have said.
3. **Institutions and towns.** The department, the prefect and where he sits, the sub-prefecture, the town where people go for a doctor, a flat, a roofer or a prison, the brigade. Each mention in the chapters has to be found and replaced.
4. **Customs.** The fête, the games, the music, the hunting, the crops, the church. Keep what is true on the Basque bank (the firework bull is), replace what belongs to the Landes, and say which is which. If night duck-hunting from a sunken cabin is not done there, say so and propose what replaces the hunter's reason to be out in the meadows on a flood night. That one is structural: the book needs a man alone, at night, in a low place, in a white van.
5. **Language.** A few Basque words where English has none, clear from context the first time, as with the French ones. No glossary and no tourism. No politics unless a villager would bring it up in passing.
