# Changes to the bible, from JC

These replace the instruction to cut events B and C.

1. **Lesca made several choices, not one.** The gate is the first. As mayor he then made others, including the lease of the common barthe to the gravel company from event C, and one or two more of your choosing. Each is defensible by the numbers and each costs one particular person. Together they are what "he achieved a lot and it cost a lot" means, and they are what Rémi envies: every decision in the village became his.
2. **The gate stays the only secret.** It is the withheld event and the late reveal. The later choices are public, the village argues about them, and they can appear in the glimpses and be misread. Keep them from diluting the one image the reader carries, the reflector.
3. **Event B** can go, or lend a piece to one of the later choices.
4. **Rémi's side.** Say what the absence of each choice looks like in his village, so the callbacks hold.
5. **Titles.** JC likes none of the five. Replace them with ten new ones that go in different directions from each other.

Everything else you were told stands: the both-refuse ending is the ending, The Good Place joins the neighbours, and the text never names the trolley problem or any philosopher. The cap rises to 4,000 words.

## Second round

6. **Learning the dream.** The middle of the book is two men learning to work a dream, and it should use real lucid-dreaming practice: checks made by day (counting fingers, reading a line twice, since print does not hold still in a dream), a diary kept at the bedside, waking in the night to go back under, and entering the dream awake through the paralysis, which is how practitioners do it. Turn "each learns to stay under the weight" into a ladder: what each skill lets a man do in the room that he could not do before, and what it costs him by day. Each learns from a different source in his own village, at village scale. Speech still does not pass. Add the nearest dream-craft works to the neighbours.
7. **Rémi's twelve years.** Lesca now has four dated choices and Rémi has none. A village where nothing happened still had twelve years in it. Give Rémi three dated moments of his own: things he did, refused or let pass. They are small, they are his, and they are what Lesca would get.

The cap rises to 4,400 words.

## Third round

8. **The protagonist is a woman.** This is not a change of pronoun. Rework section 1 and everything that follows from it, in both worlds.
   - Choose her name. The device stands: her first name where nothing happened, her surname where she became mayor. Rename both labels everywhere, including in the glimpses, the leaks and the shape.
   - Decide her household on purpose: who she lives with, and whether that person is a man or a woman. She has no children in either world. That is a fact about her and not a wound, and the village has opinions on it.
   - Say how the village treats a woman who holds the outdoor job and the sluice key, and a woman mayor who took four hard decisions. Show it in what people do, in passing. The book is not about that.
   - Revisit every tie that changes: the widow, the mother, the partner, the men who give instructions through car windows.
9. **Rules for writing her.** Add these to the bible as rules for writers.
   - She is never described by her body, her looks or how men see her. No mirror.
   - No backstory of assault and no longing for a child.
   - Her choices keep their arithmetic and their ambition. She rang once and opened. She is no softer, no sorrier and no more excused than a man would be written.
   - She is allowed to be funny, petty and wrong.
   - She has at least two ties with other women that are not about a man.
   - The figure on her chest is weight, steel and tobacco. Nothing about it is sexual, in image or in implication.

The cap rises to 4,800 words.
