# Brief: the steel room pays

The blind pair chose the last version again (`audit/pair19/`). One reader then named the thing that decides it against Blake Crouch (`audit/pair19/first.md`, section 7): "The box with two lamps is the most Crouch thing in the book, and it stays a doorway. Nobody comes back from the university, why the rain follows her is left at 'I don't know why it comes', and the card rides in her chest pocket to the last page without being used." Earlier readers said the same: "the reader who came for the box gets folklore"; "two objects are carried to the last page without my knowing what they are for".

The box asked one question in June 2014, and the book already holds the answer. Nobody says it, and nothing is done with the two cards. Four small changes. You are the only writer. Everything follows `briefs/line.md` and JC's notes on reading (nobody she knows is "a man" or "a woman"; one comparison to a sentence; nothing to decode; no line built to be quoted). No new rule, no new person, and nobody comes back from the university.

1. **Chapter 3.** When Lesca understands where she has been, she has the answer to what the woman from the university asked in chapter 1 ("whether, somewhere, you also see the other one"): somewhere is Peyrebarthe. One line, hers.

2. **Chapter 4, the first meeting.** Both lamps on the panel are lit, and each of them saw only one. One of them says so, and it can be funny: they were asked whether she also saw the other one, and she is looking at her.

3. **Chapter 9, Sunday night.** When Lesca says the river comes with her, she also says how, in one line, by the thing they already know about that chair: what one of them carries in her head comes through it to the other. The rib came that way. So does the flood. Cut "I don't know why it comes" wherever it is still said after this.

4. **Chapter 17, the last meeting.** The two cards are in that room: *left* in one hand, *right* in the other, in the same handwriting, the t crossed low. After "Don't come again" and "I won't", they do the one thing the box was built for and nobody came back to collect: they put the two cards together into the one envelope on the shelf and close it. The lamps go out, one and then the other. Then Inguma goes out over the sill, and neither of them sits down.
