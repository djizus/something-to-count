# Brief: drafting the last chapters

The process changed. Chapters are no longer drafted twice and merged into one even voice. Each scene now has one register, and the dark writer drafts every remaining chapter in full. That is you.

## Before you start

Read these again, since all of them changed: `PLAN.md` (the bar, and who writes), `bible.md` sections 4, 5, 6, 10 and 11, `outline.md` chapters 19 to 25 and the tracks, `state.md`, and `briefs/stakes.md`. Then read `manuscript/19.md` and `20.md`, which were revised, and any other merged chapter you have not read since the creature was put in.

`manuscript/` and the bible are the only canon. Your own earlier drafts are not. Your old draft of chapter 21 was written before the ending changed and is discarded.

## What to write

Chapters 21, 22, 23, 24 and 25, in order, each into `drafts/NN-dark.md`. Write each one in full, to the outline: everything under Happens, Figure, Rain, Plants and Pays, with the cast it lists, ending on the event it names, within ten per cent of its word target. Keep your own five chapters consistent with one another.

- Third person, past tense, following the woman whose chapter it is. One is Maylis and the other is Lesca.
- `audit/comedy.md` has a tone map. Where a scene is marked comic, write what happens in it plainly and briefly; another writer will play it. Where it is marked held or dark, it is yours, and from the dusk of chapter 21 nearly everything is.
- Nothing arrives in a dream before the other one has lived it.

## The bar for these five

A reader of Blake Crouch has to be unable to stop. That is your job more than anyone's.

- Something is at risk in every scene, and the reader knows what. Put people behind the bank by name, not "forty houses".
- The clock is always visible: the height of the river, the minutes the bank has, the time on a screen.
- Each chapter ends on something that happens and that makes the next one necessary. Chapters get shorter as the outline says. Sentences may too.
- Chapter 23 is the book. Tell it in order, plainly, close to her body and her watch. She means to do both: fetch him and come back to the gate. The reader must believe she might manage it, and must feel each minute it costs. The death is told in a few lines, by the count in the hall, and nobody says what it means.
- Neither woman is softened or excused, and neither is punished by the narrator.
- No explanation of the creature, the room or the hall. No dream furniture.
- No office words, no tallies offered as jokes and no running lines in a dark scene.

## Rules

- Load the `stop-slop` skill before you write, and audit each chapter against it before you go on to the next.
- Follow section 10 of the bible to the letter. British spelling.
- Nothing you write into this directory mentions any writing tool, model, vendor or session. Do not edit anything but your five drafts, and do not run git.
- If the outline or the bible is wrong or silent on something you need, decide, and list it in your reply.
- Reply when all five are written, in one line a chapter: the word count and what you decided that the outline did not. Then the one thing you are least sure of.
