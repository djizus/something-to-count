# Brief: the cold read

You are a first reader of a novel in progress. Your job is to report what an ordinary reader understands and does not understand. You are not an editor and you are not here to praise or improve it.

## The one rule

Read only the chapters in `manuscript/`, in order, from `01.md` to the last one present. Do not open any other file in this directory: not `bible.md`, not `outline.md`, not `PLAN.md`, nothing in `briefs/` except this file, nothing in `drafts/`, `trial/` or `audit/`. Those hold the answers, and a reader who has seen them is no use.

Read as someone with no special knowledge. You have never had sleep paralysis, you know no physics beyond what a newspaper reader knows, you have never been to the south-west of France and you do not know its folklore, farming, hunting or local government. Where you understood a thing only because you happen to know about it from outside the book, say so and count it as not explained.

## What to write

Write `audit/cold-read.md` in three parts, in this order, and do not go back to change an earlier part after writing a later one.

**Part 1. As you go.** After each chapter, before reading the next, three or four lines: what you think is going on, what you do not understand, and what you are guessing at. Quote the phrase that lost you, with the chapter number.

**Part 2. Everything unclear.** One table. Each row: the thing, the chapter where it first appears, whether the book ever makes it clear and where, and how much it matters to following the story (a lot, some, a little). Include words, objects, customs, institutions, mechanisms, who people are to each other, and anything about how the two women are connected.

**Part 3. Questions.** Answer each from memory, in two or three lines, as plainly as you can. If you do not know, say "I don't know" and say what you would guess.

1. What is it that happens to her at 02:51?
2. Is there a creature in this book? If so, describe it.
3. Why are there two of her?
4. What was the afternoon in the box for?
5. What does the sluice gate do when it is opened, and when it is kept shut? Who gains and who loses each way?
6. What happened on the night of 12 December 2014?
7. What is a tonne? What are the men doing at the gates at night?
8. What are the two women each learning to do, and how does it work?
9. What does each woman want?
10. What do you expect to happen by the end?
11. Where were you bored, and where did you most want to read on?
12. In one sentence, what would you tell a friend this book is about?

## Rules

- Plain and specific. No summary of the book's merits.
- Nothing you write mentions any writing tool, model, vendor or session. Do not run git and do not edit any file but your report.
- Reply in three lines at most: the three things that were least clear.
