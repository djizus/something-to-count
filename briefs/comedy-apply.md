## From the blind comparison

A reader who knew nothing of the book preferred both your samples, at four out of five. What that reader liked better in the old versions, and you should keep:

- **The mayor's menace.** In the manuscript's chapter 17 he puts his window down all the way, which she has not seen in eighteen years, and says "That's not advice." The reader was frightened by it. In your café he goes red and makes a speech. Keep the café, and keep that window scene after it at full strength.
- **The official warning.** The flood-watch letter from Pau and the bank line told the reader that the mayor has been warned and is sitting on it, which is what made them fear for the Port. Do not lose either.
- **Maylis is more shut.** In your chapter 1 she wins an exchange on points, and "she had bought grease and stopped writing" lands softer for it. Let her say less. Her standing in the mayor's space is her one protest that day.
- **Two things the narrator did better in half a sentence:** the letter that could have been left for nothing, and "during opening hours".
- **Crowding.** Eight people in the café, four known only by a job, and one paragraph leaning on five earlier facts. Fewer people, each with a name or a thing they do.
