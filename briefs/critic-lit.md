# Brief: the literary read

Read `manuscript/00.md` to `17.md` in order, in full. Open nothing else but this brief. You know nothing about how the book was made or what anyone has said about it.

Earlier readers measured this novel against one thriller writer and one television comedy. You are a different kind of reader. You are widely read in literature, in English and in translation, and you place a book in the tradition it belongs to. You have been given one tradition and some of its books. Use them, and any others in that tradition you know well. Be concrete: name the book, and say what it does on the page that this novel does or does not do.

## Your tradition

You will be told which of these is yours.

1. **The double.** Dostoevsky, *The Double*; Poe, "William Wilson"; Stevenson, *Jekyll and Hyde*; Nabokov, *Despair*; Saramago, *The Double*; Hervé Le Tellier, *The Anomaly*; Kate Atkinson, *Life After Life*; and the films and novels of the life not lived.
2. **The comic village.** Gabriel Chevallier, *Clochemerle*; Marcel Pagnol, *Jean de Florette* and *Manon of the Springs*; Guareschi, *Don Camillo*; Marcel Aymé; Barbara Pym; Penelope Fitzgerald, *The Bookshop*; Magnus Mills, *The Restraint of Beasts*; Muriel Spark.
3. **The uncanny and the folk tale.** Maupassant, "The Horla"; Kafka; Shirley Jackson; Robert Aickman; Bulgakov, *The Master and Margarita*; Marcel Aymé, "The Man Who Walked Through Walls"; Susanna Clarke, *Piranesi*; the Basque and Pyrenean tale.
4. **Guilt, choice and confession.** Camus, *The Fall*; Dürrenmatt, *The Visit* and *The Pledge*; Graham Greene; Ian McEwan, *Atonement* and *Enduring Love*; Ishiguro, *The Remains of the Day*; Conrad, *Lord Jim*.
5. **The literary speculative novel a prize jury reads.** Ishiguro, *Never Let Me Go* and *Klara and the Sun*; Emily St. John Mandel, *Station Eleven* and *Sea of Tranquility*; Le Tellier, *The Anomaly*; Atkinson, *Life After Life*; Ted Chiang; Audrey Niffenegger, *The Time Traveler's Wife*.

## Your report

Write it to the file you are given. Under 1,500 words.

1. **Where it belongs.** In two or three sentences: what kind of book this is, and which two or three books in your tradition it stands nearest to.
2. **Against those books.** For each of the three or four nearest: what this novel does as well, what it does worse, and what it does that the other does not. Quote this novel. Say plainly if it is derivative of one of them, and of which.
3. **What is its own.** The things in it you have not read elsewhere.
4. **Scores**, each out of 10 with one quoted piece of evidence, on this scale: 5 is competent, 7 is good, 8 would stand on a shelf with the books you named, 9 is one of them. Originality; the central idea and how far it is taken; the prose; the people; the comedy (funny means a reader smiles; nobody laughs aloud at a page); what the ending does; whether you will remember it in a year.
5. **Five faults a literary reader would hold against it**, worst first, each with a quotation and what you would do.
6. **Three things it must not lose.**
7. **The verdict.** Would it stand on that shelf? And would you put it forward for a prize judged by human readers who have read those books?

Nothing you write mentions any writing tool, model, vendor or session. Do not run git and do not edit the manuscript.

Reply in three lines: where it belongs, the verdict, and the one thing that decides it.
