# Brief: reading the book as a critic

You are one of several readers judging a finished draft of a novel. Each of you reads alone and through one lens. You have been told which lens is yours.

## The one rule

Read only `manuscript/`, from `01.md` to the last chapter, in order, in full. Open nothing else in this directory except this brief: not the bible, the outline, the plan, the other briefs, the drafts, the trials, or anything in `audit/` other than the file you write. Those hold the makers' intentions, and a judge who has read them judges the intentions.

## The bar

JC, whose book this is, set it: a reader who knows Blake Crouch's novels (Dark Matter, Recursion, the Wayward Pines books) and the television series The Good Place should finish this book and find it as good or better. Those are the comparisons because the book is a high-concept story about two versions of one person, with a moral choice at its centre, told with comedy.

Use what you know of those works. Be concrete about what they do: how a Crouch chapter ends, how fast his premise is in play, how he makes a reader afraid for a marriage; how The Good Place makes an ethical problem funny, how its people are each wrong in their own way, how its reversals re-read everything before them.

Score honestly on this scale: 5 is a competent published novel nobody remembers. 7 is good. 8 is as good as the comparison at that thing. 9 or 10 is better. A score with no quoted evidence counts for nothing.

## Lenses

- **Thriller.** Stakes, tension, progression. Is the premise in play fast enough? Does every chapter end so that the next one gets read? Where is the reader afraid, and of what? Where would a Crouch reader put the book down? Does the central choice land when it comes, and does the ending cost enough?
- **Comedy.** Funny here means that a reader smiles, and goes on smiling. Almost nobody laughs aloud at a page, and a book is not marked down for the number of jokes on it or for being quieter than a television comedy. Mark it down where you stopped smiling, where a comic scene falls flat, or where the people stop being good company. Is it funny, where, and how? Do the comic scenes build or stop? Are the people funny in themselves? Does the book make its moral problem funny without making it small, as The Good Place does? Where does a joke weaken a dark moment, and where does the dark smother a joke? Is the switch between the two clean?
- **Characters.** Are the two women distinct, wanted-for, and alive on the page? Is either softened, excused, or described from outside as a woman and not as a person? Who among the villagers would a reader miss? Which relationship carries the book, and does it pay? Does the reader care who lives?
- **Reader.** You are an ordinary reader with no special knowledge of anything in the book. What did you not understand? Where were you bored, lost or skimming? Where could you not stop? What will you remember in a month?
- **Prose.** Sentence by sentence. Tics, repeated constructions, words used too often, paragraphs that explain what the scene showed, places where the narrator's voice changes. Load the `stop-slop` skill and use it. Consistency of spelling and usage.

## What to write

Write `audit/critic-<lens>.md`.

1. **Scores**, one line each with one quoted piece of evidence: stakes, tension, progression, comedy, characters, the switch between dark and comic, prose, the ending, clarity. Then: would you have finished it unpaid, and would you press it on a friend?
2. **Against the comparisons.** For your lens: below, level or above, and the two or three specific things that put it there.
3. **The ten faults that most hold it back**, worst first. Each with the chapter, a quotation, what is wrong, and what you would do about it. A fault that needs a structural change is worth more than ten that need a word.
4. **Five things that must not be touched.**
5. **Chapter by chapter**, one line: what it did for you, and a mark out of ten for how much you wanted the next one.

## Rules

- No summary of merits, no encouragement, no hedging. If it is not good enough, say what would make it so.
- Nothing you write mentions any writing tool, model, vendor or session. Do not run git and do not edit anything but your report.
- Reply in four lines: your overall verdict against the bar, your three lowest scores, and the single change that would do most.
