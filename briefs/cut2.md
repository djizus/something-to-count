# Brief: the middle, cut

Nine blind readers preferred this book's opening to the openings of two published thrillers of the kind JC measures it against, and readers call it level with them from chapter 10 on. What is left is between: "I would have to tell them to push through the Tuesday of chapter 9." "Chapters 6 to 9 are work." "The same contest is replayed in new rooms: hand against hand on the stair post, the timer, the jaw at the bar, the mouth in the kitchen. Each scene is good. Together they are a holding pattern." "Not until chapters 5 to 9 read as easily as chapter 14."

The last passes added what the middle lacked: a body at risk, the river at a door, the turn played. Now it carries too much. This pass cuts. You are the only writer, in chapters 5 to 10 (`05.md` to `10.md`). Read `audit/round38-thriller.md`, `-reader.md` and `-prose.md` section 3 for those chapters first, and `audit/pair20/third.md`.

**Take 2,200 words out of chapters 5 to 10**, and no fewer than 1,800. The book must stay above 41,500 words.

## What goes

- **A contest for the body that repeats an earlier one.** The book needs the stair post in 5, the jaw at the bar in 8 and the wrist in the kitchen in 9. Any other tug of hand against hand, jaw against jaw, is a line or goes.
- **Paper.** The council in 6 keeps the minute of silence and the laugh, Larrère with no bell and "timed like an egg", "I want that", "I go in at the front, Guy", and the vote in one beat. The complaint, the resignation letter and Kévin's sheet are each explained once in the book and in one sentence. The association's vote in 9 is Jeannot's three points and his hand going up.
- **Anything told that an earlier scene showed**, and any sentence that says how a rule works.
- **Readings of the river** that nobody acts on, and dates the reader does not need to place the scene.
- **Setting-up and getting-there**: arriving, crossing the square, sitting down, who is in the room, unless it is funny.

## What stays whole

Everything in section 4 of those reports. And: the stairs and "Are you coming up?"; Coralie's chart with the pulse going down, and the thumb over the doctor's number; the mayor signing letters on the floor; what the rib can do, and the pink she rinses away; the two dry days and the cross on Bordenave's pad; the trapdoor; "You're the water" and the hall mishearing it; Arlette at the bedside; the landing and "I don't get into a bed over somebody who can't speak"; the kitchen chart and "what I'd have had is a river over forty-two beds"; the tide that will not come out; the rule for Friday; the words that do not keep it off; the card; the corridor outside room twelve; Lesca's night and the nine rings.

## How

Cut whole sentences and whole exchanges. Do not compress a scene into a summary: a scene is played or it is gone. Where a cut needs a join, write the join in one plain line. Everything follows `briefs/line.md` and JC's notes on reading.

Nothing you write mentions any writing tool, model, vendor or session. Do not run git.

Reply in three lines: words cut from each chapter, and the three largest things you took out.
