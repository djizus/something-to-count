# Brief: Maylis goes after something from page 4

The blind pair chose the last version again (`audit/pair18/`), and a cold reader was first afraid for a named person on page 4: Maylis, her name on a stone, eighteen days. The same reader then said what still holds the book under Blake Crouch (`audit/pair18/third.md`, section 7):

"For the first half, the person who wants something and goes after it is the visitor. A Crouch lead wants one thing by page 10 and runs at it. Here that is Lesca. Maylis's acts until then are refusals… Before that the clock is a date on a pad, and I waited for it more than I feared it."

Her flaw stays exactly as it is: she cannot say which she wants, to anybody, and "Either" is still what comes out of her mouth. But a woman who cannot say can still do. She now has eighteen days and her own name on a stone. **From page 4 she is trying to find out what that stone is before the 12th, and every step of chapter 2 is a step she takes.** You are the only writer, in chapters 1, 2 and 5.

Everything follows `briefs/line.md` and JC's notes on reading: nobody she knows is "a man" or "a woman"; one comparison to a sentence; nothing the reader has to decode; no line built to be quoted. No new scene, no new rule, no new person.

## Chapter 1

After the stone and the count of days, one line of decision, in her own head and in her own idiom, the way she takes on a job: she has eighteen days to find out whose stone that is. She tells nobody.

## Chapter 2

The scenes are the right ones and stay in their order. What changes is why she is in each one. At present things come to her. Now she goes to them.

- **Kévin.** She goes to him because the whole village knows he has had the thing on his chest twice. She wants to know how he got out from under it. She does not say why she is asking.
- **Madame Etcheto.** She asks to help Coralie with the lights-out round, or finds a reason to be in that corridor, because an old woman in room twelve talks to the thing on her wardrobe every night, and Maylis wants to hear what she says to it.
- **The envelope and the steel room.** She takes the envelope out of the drawer as a test, and goes down to the barn because that is where the smell comes from. She lies still under the thing on purpose that night, counting her fingers, to see where it takes her.
- Each of those is one or two sentences of intent at the head of a scene that already exists. The count of days comes back where she is alone.
- When her mouth says "Come back across", she went looking and something answered.

## Chapter 5

She lets Lesca drive on the Monday. At present that happens to her. Make it a thing she decides, for her own reason, and is wrong about: she would rather the village heard it from the one who signs things. One line, as she lies still under the weight. By the stairs she knows what it cost.
