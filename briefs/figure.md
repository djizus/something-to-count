# Brief: the figure

JC read the chapters so far and did not understand what the weight on her chest was. He is right. Two instructions stacked, "it is never explained" and "nothing about it is sexual", and between them the figure lost its body. It is now a sensation with three repeated markers (thirty-five kilos, cold steel, old tobacco), it is routine by chapter 3, and nobody on Maylis's side ever names it. JC wants a presence: more of a monster.

Read `manuscript/01.md` to `manuscript/14.md` for how it stands, then do three things.

## 1. Redesign it in `bible.md`

Rewrite "The figure" in section 5 and the last rule in section 10.

- **It is a thing in the room.** She sees it, or part of it, and she sees or hears it come before it is on her. Start from what people with sleep paralysis report (a presence by the door or the foot of the bed, a shape that approaches, breathing, footsteps, a weight that climbs on) and from what the Landes and Gascony say of the sarramauca. Research both. Then make it this book's own, tied to the steel room and the tobacco barn it smells of.
- **A reader who has never had sleep paralysis must understand, on the first page, that something is in the room with her and is holding her down.**
- **It has intent.** It guards the line between the two lives, and it does things that show it: what it does when she reaches for what is the other one's, what it does when she uses something that leaked, how it follows her into the day. Give it a ladder across the book, as the leaks and the craft have one.
- **Decide whether there is one of it or two.** One creature holding both women at the same minute is worth considering.
- **Both villages have the word for it early.** Lesca gets it from her mother in chapter 4. Find where Maylis gets it by chapter 3 at the latest, from someone with a reason to know, such as the old people at the home.
- **It still has a case.** By the end the reader should be able to see what it was keeping apart and why, without anyone saying so. When both women get up from the chair, it has nothing left to hold.
- **What stays.** It does not speak. Nobody explains it. Nothing about it is sexual, in image or implication: no hands on her, nothing between her legs, nothing that reads as a man in her bed. It can be frightening without any of that.
- **Kill the tic.** The three markers are no longer a formula. Say what a writer may repeat and how often.

## 2. Write the retrofit list

In a new file, `briefs/figure-retrofit.md`, go chapter by chapter through 1 to 14 and say what changes: which passage, what the figure now does there, and what is cut. Keep each change as small as the redesign allows. Do not rewrite scenes that have nothing to do with it. Chapters 1 and 2 matter most, since they are what a stranger reads first.

## 3. Carry it through the outline

In `outline.md`, add one line to each chapter from 15 to 25 saying what the figure does in it, following your ladder. Chapter 25 already ends with it not coming.
