# Brief: apply the grammar and error review

Three proofreaders read the finished book for anything that is wrong and anything that is right but reads as wrong: `audit/grammar-seven.md` (chapters 00 to 06), `audit/grammar-eight.md` (07 to 12) and `audit/grammar-nine.md` (13 to 17). Thirty-three places left after two rounds of corrections, each with the sentence quoted and a corrected sentence. Apply them.

- **Apply every correction for your chapters.** Find each sentence by its words. If it is no longer there, skip it.
- **Use the proofreader's corrected sentence** unless it changes what happens, changes a fact, or reads worse than the original. In that case write your own correction that answers the same complaint.
- **Inguma is "it", always.** Where a "she" or "her" could be taken for the creature, name the woman. Where two women are in a scene and a pronoun could be either, name the one meant.
- **Do not touch** deliberate fragments, Maïté's broken sentences or Sarthou's uncontracted ones, and do not change any line of speech beyond what a correction asks.
- **Who is speaking.** JC stopped at an exchange in chapter 2 where two people spoke with no names and a "she" could have been anybody: "'Does it work?' / 'Till 3. Then it comes, and she rings for us.'" Go through every exchange in your chapters. In a run of speeches with no speaker named, a reader must know who says each line; name the speaker at least every third line, with a plain "X said". Where a speech uses "she", "he" or "it" for somebody not speaking, the reader must know who or what it is; if there is any doubt, use the name. These runs have four or more speeches in a row with no name: chapter 1 (after "It's the rib"), chapter 4 (five places), chapter 11, chapter 12 (the call to Hontaube, ten lines; Larrère at his window; the trapdoor; Kévin on the platform), chapter 14 (the call to Coralie; the trapdoor), chapter 17 (the priest).
- Change nothing else. Keep the plain style: short sentences and common words (`briefs/line.md`).

Nothing you write mentions any writing tool, model, vendor or session. Do not run git.

Reply in three lines: how many corrections you applied, which you changed or skipped and why, and the word count before and after.
