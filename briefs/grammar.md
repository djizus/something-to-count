# Brief: the grammar and error review

The book is finished and about to be submitted. JC, who reads English fluently as a second language, stopped at "At midnight Maylis was walked up her own stairs" and asked whether it was a typo. It was correct English, and it read as a mistake. That is the standard for this review: **anything that is wrong, and anything that is right but would make a fluent reader think it is wrong.**

Read your chapters of `manuscript/` in full, slowly, one sentence at a time. This is a proofreader's read. Do not judge the story, the style or the jokes.

List every instance of:

1. **A grammar error**: agreement, tense, a wrong preposition, a wrong article, a sentence with no main verb where one is needed, a dangling clause.
2. **A typo**: a misspelling, a missing word, a word twice, a wrong word that sounds like the right one.
3. **Punctuation**: a quotation mark that opens and does not close, a speech that changes speaker without a new paragraph, a comma that changes the meaning, a missing full stop.
4. **Correct but reads as a mistake**: an unusual passive ("was walked"), a verb used in a rare sense, a word order a reader would stumble on, an idiom that is regional or old.
5. **A pronoun with an unclear owner.** Above all near Inguma: the creature is always "it", never "she" or "he". List any sentence where "she" or "her" could be taken for Inguma, and any "it" that could be taken for something else. Also any "she" or "her" where two women are in the scene and a reader cannot tell at once which one is meant.
6. **Inconsistency**: British spelling is the rule (colour, metre, towards, grey); a word spelled two ways; a number or a time written two ways (clock times are in figures: "2:51", "at 5"; counts are in words); a name spelled two ways; a capital used in one place and not in another.
7. **A tense slip**: the book is in the past tense; an event before the scene takes "had"; list any place that breaks this without a reason.

For each: the chapter, the sentence quoted exactly as it stands, the kind, and the corrected sentence written out in full. The correction changes as little as possible and keeps the plain style: short sentences, common words.

Do not list a sentence fragment that is clearly deliberate ("Nothing." "Five."), dialogue that is deliberately broken (Maïté breaks off; Sarthou does not contract), or anything you merely would have written differently.

Write `audit/grammar-<your name>.md`. Give the count for each chapter. If a chapter is clean, say so. Do not pad.

Nothing you write mentions any writing tool, model, vendor or session. Do not run git and do not edit the manuscript.

Reply in two lines: the total, and how many of each kind.
