# Brief: what a cold reader could not follow

A reader who had seen nothing but `manuscript/01.md` to `15.md` wrote `audit/cold-read.md`. Read it in full, Part 2 above all. It is the evidence for this brief. This is a third input to the task you are on (`briefs/figure.md`, `briefs/basque.md`): the same retrofit list carries all three.

The rule from JC: readers are not stupid, and they must not be asked to work out niche things. "Never explained" in the bible was meant for the figure's nature and the room. It was never meant to cover how a gate works or what an experiment was for.

## Must be made clear, and where

For each, decide the plain sentence or the small scene that does it, who says or sees it, and in which chapter. Put the decision in the bible and the change in `briefs/figure-retrofit.md`. One clear statement each, early, attached to something happening. No lecture and no character who exists to explain.

1. **What the afternoon in the box was for, and why it made two of her.** The reader got the furniture and never the purpose. A newspaper reader knows the cat that is alive and dead until someone looks. The team must have told the council or the woman something in plain words in 2014, and the village must have a version of it. That version reaches the reader by chapter 2 at the latest. It should also account for why Dr Kessler called her "the friend".
2. **What the gate does in a flood, and why opening it saved the houses.** The reader pieced it together by chapter 10 and still was not sure. Before chapter 5 is over the reader must know, as a fact of the place: with the river over the red line the bank at the Port is carrying it, opening the gate gives the river the meadows to spread into and takes it off the houses, and anything out in the meadows goes under. Also what the red line marks, and that the river is tidal this far up.
3. **Why a hide wants water standing at it.** One clause, the first time the hunter complains.
4. **That netting glass eels is against the law, and what the padlock is on.** In chapter 3, where both first appear.
5. **Why the thing has sat on the mayor for twelve years and comes to the other only now.** This belongs to your redesign of the figure.

## The hole

The reader's biggest: why one village had the rain of 2014 and the other did not, and why the same rain then falls on the other exactly twelve years later, day for day. Hold this one. The reviewer is putting a proposal to JC and will send you the answer. Do not design around it yet.

## Left alone on purpose

The missing verb on the stone, what she told the priest, the order of events on the flood night, Sarthou's tin, the suitcase, why the hunter will not name Larrère, and what the hall is. These are withheld, the reader felt them as questions, and that is right. Check only that each one is paid by the outline.

## Wearing thin

The reader stopped reading vote counts and sums of euros as numbers by chapter 6, and found "I'll be brief", "still in the box", the second envelope scene in chapter 9, the budget in chapter 12 and the marquee in chapter 6 slow. In the outline for 16 to 25, ration them: say how many votes and sums a chapter may carry and how often each running line may return. For 1 to 15, list in the retrofit the cuts that cost nothing.
