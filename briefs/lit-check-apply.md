# Brief: apply the cold read

Three cold readers read the book after the last pass: `audit/litcheck-reader.md`, `audit/litcheck-ab.md`, `audit/litcheck-continuity.md`. Apply the corrections below and nothing else. Find each sentence by its words. Keep the plain style of `briefs/line.md`. No correction may state a rule of the dream in the narrator's voice.

Writer one has chapters 00 to 08. Writer two has chapters 09 to 17.

## Part 2 of each report (errors)

Apply every correction in part 2 of all three reports for your chapters, with these decisions:

## Part 1 (places a reader went back): the ones to mend

**Chapter 1**
- "'It's my water. One." becomes "'One. It's my water." so that the number comes first, as it does everywhere else. If "It" could then be taken for the hut, name the water's place instead.
- "She closed her hand around nothing and turned it for the oven door." → "She faced the oven door, closed her hand around nothing and turned it."

**Chapter 2**
- Cut "There was still the barn, where the smell came from."
- "It means she's not afraid of it." → "The words mean she's not afraid of it."

**Chapter 3**
- First sentence: "Lesca lay on her back in a bed that wasn't hers and listened to the rain on the roof."

**Chapter 4**
- All three readers did not know why "Either" stuck. Put back the plain sentence that was there: after "It had always come when she needed it." the text reads "It had never been true, and it wasn't true now. She did want one of them, badly." (replacing "Tonight she wanted one of the two, badly."). Nobody says it is a rule.
- "She had written that word on the shopping pad" → "She had written Either on the shopping pad".
- "showed each of them its back" → "turned its back on each of them as it passed".
- Check the hands against chapter 3: one reader found the left hand closed in chapter 3 and the right hand closed in a fist here. If they disagree, make chapter 3 agree with the rest of the book (the right hand is the owner's).

**Chapter 5**
- "Maylis had lain still for the village's sake." → "Maylis had lain still for the village's sake, not for this."
- "from the bottom of the first finger to the heel of the hand" → "from the base of the forefinger to the bottom of the palm".
- "'That's a day,' Lesca said to the right hand." → "to Maylis's right hand."

**Chapter 6**
- "'For the minutes.'" after the secretary's "You were the keyholder, Madame le maire": give it its speaker.
- "At home it was raining. Coralie hadn't gone in to work." → "At home, in Maylis's bedroom, it was raining."
- "\"A man can no longer.\" The timer rang there." → the cut-off sentence ends on a dash, not a full stop, and "without Pau watching" becomes "without the prefecture watching".

**Chapter 7**
- "'Twenty-five,' Lesca said." → "'Twenty-five minutes,' Lesca said."

**Chapter 8**
- "'I'd have you tomorrow." → "'Ask me tomorrow and I'd have you." Chapter 9's "I said I'd have you tomorrow" stays.

**Chapter 9**
- "That was my first night on my back. It was her first night in, and it started raining." → "That was my first night on my back, and it started raining."
- "One night. That's all I have to keep you out for." → "Friday is the night the river comes through. That's the one night I have to keep you out for."
- "Inguma came in over the step and climbed into the chair." → add "and waited".

**Chapter 11**
- "'I've watched you up there eleven Decembers. You never had to.'" → "You never needed to before.'"

**Chapter 12**
- "From the step Maylis could see the whole length of the building." → "Through the open door Maylis could see down the whole length of the building."
- "'It doesn't have enough seats for that.'" → add "Maylis said".
- "'She had a van at the end of the track,' Maylis said. 'It wasn't yours.'" → "'Not yours. His.'" if "his" is clear there; otherwise name Jeannot.
- "'I'd do them, Maylis.'": leave it if "six months" is in the line before; otherwise "'I'd do the six months, Maylis.'"

**Chapter 13**
- The long sentence in the hall made all three readers start again. It becomes: "In front of her the tables ran in three rows under the lights, out past the place where the back wall of the village hall should have been and on further than she could see. At every place there was a bingo card and a small dish of corn, and one of the village's dead sat over them with their head down. Nearest were the ones she had known. **Chapter 14**

**Chapter 15**

**Chapter 17**
