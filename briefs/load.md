# Brief: the load

Two blind readers chose this version over the last one, clearly, because of the turn. Then both, and all five readers of round 24, said what still keeps it under Blake Crouch: **ease**. Clarity is 5.5 to 6 in every report. "Crouch gives me one rule and lets me read fast. This one made me read carefully." "The rules are the hardest sentences in the book." JC said the same in his own words: tough to follow.

So this pass removes load. A system that the reader has to carry earns its place or it goes. You are the only writer on this pass. Read `audit/round24-prose.md` fault 1, `-reader.md` faults 2 to 4, `-thriller.md` faults 1 to 3 and 6, `-characters.md` fault 1, and `audit/pair2/first.md` and `second.md`.

## 1. The rules, said once, in the plainest sentences in the book

A reader of ten must be able to repeat each one. Each is said once where it is first needed, by a person, and used afterwards without being said again. The narrator never restates one.

In chapter 4 Lesca's meeting has **three points**:

1. In the chair, what one of them knows, the other gets.
2. If one stays sitting when the other wakes, she wakes in the other's body and drives it. The owner keeps her right hand.
3. Inguma takes the visitor home when it has finished counting what it was given.

Found in that room, not listed: standing up, they can only say what is true.

From Madame Etcheto in chapter 2, in her voice, and recalled once at the card that ends chapter 8: the one it cannot take home dies in her own bed.

From chapter 8: every night Lesca crosses, the river rises by one day of her flood.

That is all. **Cut:**
- "A place that is laid is filled by morning, by her or by another", everywhere. The death in room twelve has two causes the reader can see, the river Lesca brought and the gate Maylis left, and it does not need a third. "Twelve. One, two." in chapter 14 stays as what Lesca hears: who has arrived. No sentence makes it a trade. In 15 the old woman's "It was you" is about the river.
- Every sentence that explains a rule at the moment it is used: "Eight rings had been long enough for her night to come through… and for the dead to lay her a place", "So that's what it fetches me home for", "'Two. When one of us wakes, she wakes at home…'" and the others the reports quote.
- Half the counting. Inguma is set to count by Madame Etcheto in 2, by Lesca once in 5 where the reader watches, by Madame Etcheto at bingo, and by Maylis in the van. Cut the other jobs, or it is a servant. A creature anybody can send away is not frightening.

## 2. The turn gets its own scene, and it changes the plan

Chapter 8. At present the turn shares a scene with "Both" and the table of the dead, and the sentence that carries it cannot be finished by its speaker.

- Give it a scene to itself, or the first half of one, before anything else is discussed. Maylis says it in one sentence a stranger understands: the river only rises when you come. Lesca fails to say the tide. Then Lesca says the rule herself, whole and plain: every night I cross, your river rises one day of mine. And what Inguma is: it has been taking her home to keep her flood out of this village.
- It changes what Maylis does. From here her plan is to keep Lesca out on Friday night. She cannot push, so she will not lie down: upright, awake, in the van. Say that plan once, here, in her words, so that the van in 11 is the contest the reader has been promised.
- "Both" and "there is no both" come after, and shorter.

## 3. The van is that contest

Chapters 11 and 12. At present Inguma lands and she has lost in a sentence, and then two women fight for eight rings over something they both want.

- When it lands on the roof she fights it. Play that: the rings of the phone in her hand are the clock, and she holds it off for the first of them with the rib she has. Then the rib goes and Lesca is in.
- **The fight is not about the road. Maylis wants her out, because every minute she stays is the river. Lesca wants to stay.** One plain line says so as it starts. Lesca has the mouth and uses it: she can get him out, she knows where the dip is. Maylis takes the jaw for three words: you're the water.
- Then the words with no "Either" after them, the five fingers, and Lesca lets go. Put back the two things the last version did better (`manuscript-v23/12.md`): "The hand locked on the brake was the hand that had held the stair post against her", and the pause in the middle of the words, "Lesca waited for the other word, the one this mouth put after everything. It didn't come." No explanation after either.
- Her going is spoken, not narrated: a line from her, then the five words.
- In the hall she sees her own card, which the reader last saw at the end of chapter 8, and knows what it is.
