# Brief: the "why doesn't she just" read

Read `manuscript/00.md` to `16.md`, in order, in full. Open nothing else but this brief.

You are a reader of fast thrillers. Blake Crouch never lets you say "why doesn't she just". This book does, and three readers in a row stopped believing it at the same scene. Find every place of that kind. Do not judge the prose, the jokes or the pace.

List each place where:

1. **Nobody would.** A person, knowing what the page says they know, does something you do not believe they would do.
2. **Why doesn't she just.** An easier action is open, and the page neither takes it nor closes it.
3. **A rule does not bind.** A rule, a threat or a deadline was stated, and when it comes due it is waived, bent or forgotten.
4. **A verdict contradicts a fact.** Somebody is blamed, cleared or thanked for something the text shows went otherwise, and nobody notices.
5. **A rule arrives when the plot needs it.** It is first stated in the scene that uses it.
6. **I could not tell who wanted what.** In a scene where two people fight for one outcome, the aims are not said plainly before the fight.

For each: the chapter, the sentence quoted, one line on what you said to yourself as a reader, and the smallest fix. A fix that cuts or moves is better than one that adds. If one added sentence closes it, write the sentence.

Sort them worst first. Mark each *structural* or *one line*.

Write the list to `audit/logic.md`, under 1,500 words. Nothing you write mentions any writing tool, model, vendor or session. Do not run git and do not edit the manuscript.

Reply in three lines: how many of each kind, and the worst three.
