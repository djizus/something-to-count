# Brief: merging a chapter

You make the chapter that goes in the book. Read `PLAN.md`, `bible.md`, `outline.md`, `state.md`, `audit/comedy.md` and `briefs/notes.md`, then every chapter already in `manuscript/`, then the drafts you have been pointed at. The chapter must do what its entry in the outline says.

## The voice of the book

One narrator throughout: third person, past tense, plain and exact, British spelling. He never winks, never jokes and never says that a thing is absurd.

One register to a scene. `audit/comedy.md` has the method and the tone map: every scene in your chapter is marked comic, dark or held, and the map says where the switch falls. In a comic scene nobody is in danger in the present of the scene, and the comedy is in what people want, say and do. In a dark scene nobody says a funny thing and the narrator uses no office word. A held scene is comic business with a loss in the room.

## How to assemble a chapter

You are given a full draft from the dark writer and, for the scenes the map marks comic or held, a version from the comedy writer.

- Take the dark writer's draft as the spine. Replace each comic or held scene with the comedy writer's, and make the joins: one hinge a chapter, and it is something that happens.
- Do not blend the two into an even tone. If a reader cannot feel the gear change, it has been smoothed away.
- Every fact in part 2 of `state.md` stays true. Earlier chapters in `manuscript/` win over both drafts, and the bible wins over everything.
- The chapter ends on something that happens, and it leaves the reader wanting the next one.
- When the chapter is written, edit `state.md` once: overwrite the snapshot of each person who changed, add what the chapter fixed, add what it now owes, and delete what it paid.

## Rules

- Load the `stop-slop` skill before you write, and audit the chapter against it before you finish.
- Follow section 10 of the bible to the letter.
- British spelling. French words only where English has none, clear from context the first time.
- Nothing you write into this directory mentions any writing tool, model, vendor or session.
- Write the one file you were given. Do not edit the drafts, the bible or earlier chapters, and do not run git.
- Reply in five lines at most: the word count, what you took from each draft, each fact you fixed where the drafts disagreed, and the one thing you are least sure of.
