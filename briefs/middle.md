# Brief: a new middle, designed as a thriller and written fresh

You are a story doctor brought in on a novel that eleven rounds of readers have judged the same way: the opening holds, chapters 11 to 18 are level with Blake Crouch or above him, and **chapters 6 to 10 are where every reader stops**. Nine revisions have cut, moved and patched those chapters. None has changed the verdict. So they are to be designed again from nothing and written fresh, in one hand.

Read `manuscript/00.md` to `18.md` in full. Then `bible.md`, `state.md`, and what the last three rounds say about the middle: `audit/round9-*.md`, `audit/round10-*.md`, `audit/round11-*.md`, the faults and section 4 of each.

## What the readers say is wrong with 6 to 10

- Nothing is at risk for Maylis but a job and a pension. The largest stake in the book, a woman losing her body and her bed to her other self, "is never made" to bite.
- The plan to stop the flood is stated three times in nearly the same words and does not change. The day the river reaches the line is told in summary.
- The same ground is walked once from each side. Rides in the other village open on the same sentence.
- Chapters end on paper, a vote or a tableau. Nothing has got worse by the last line.
- Who has which hand and mouth has to be worked out.

## What to design

Write `middle.md`: five chapters, numbered 6 to 10, about 2,000 words each, that take the book from the end of chapter 5 to the start of chapter 11 as it stands.

For each chapter:

1. **Whose it is, the day, and where the river stands** on the gauge at its start and end.
2. **What she is trying to do**, in one sentence a reader could repeat, said in the first hundred words.
3. **Who or what stops her**, with a face. Larrère has a hold on Maylis and uses it. Lesca is a danger to Maylis, not a correspondent.
4. **A reversal**: something the reader did not expect, that changes what she is trying to do.
5. **The last line**, which is a danger that has just got nearer. The next chapter in that village picks it up at once.
6. **Scene by scene**, what happens, in order. Each scene is played, not reported. A ride in the other body is told from the side with most to lose, and each sentence with an act in it names who acts.

Across the five:

- The takeover escalates to a point where Maylis could lose her body for good, and the reader believes it. Coralie is in the room for the worst of it.
- At least one attempt to stop the flood in daylight is fought for on the page and lost to a person, not to a form.
- The river reaches the red line on the page.
- The comedy is situation: a woman acting in the wrong village with the wrong habits, in front of people. One comic scene in each chapter, which leaves the reader more worried than it found them.
- Nothing from chapters 0 to 5 is retold, and the night of 2014 is not told again before chapter 11.

## Constraints

- **The hand-off.** List what chapter 11 onward needs to be true at its start: who knows what, where each woman's plan stands, what has been said between them, the state of both marriages, every object and line that the last third pays. Your five chapters must deliver exactly that. Where a small change to chapter 11 or later would free the middle, name it.
- **The three rules of the crossing stand**, and no others.
- **Keep the lines the readers quote with love** from the present 6 to 10, each in a scene that earns it. List them and where they now fall.
- Section 10 of the bible stands.

## Rules

- Under 3,000 words. Plain, concrete, no sales language.
- Do not edit `manuscript/`. Do not run git. Nothing you write mentions any writing tool, model, vendor or session.
- Reply in six lines: each chapter's aim and last line, in a phrase each, and the one change you need after chapter 10.
