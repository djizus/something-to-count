Thirteen blind pairs and thirty-four panel rounds end on the same sentence: the book is level with its comparisons from chapter 9 and below them before it, because "from chapter 4 to chapter 8 I was learning rules. Meanwhile the flood is a date on a pad. Crouch has someone in danger by page 10." "That friend stops in chapter 7."

## 1. Chapter 6: her own body is dying while she is away

The rule is Madame Etcheto's: the one Inguma cannot take home dies in her own bed. On Wednesday Maylis is in the other village for thirteen hours, and at home Coralie sits by a body with its eyes open. At present that is one cut-away and nobody is afraid.

- **The pulse falls.** Coralie counts it every half hour and writes it down, and each time it is lower. Give the numbers. The reader sees the chart twice more, briefly, between scenes in the other village: once in the morning, once in the afternoon. By the last one her thumb is on the doctor's number and the reader knows what a doctor will find.
- **Maylis knows.** In the mayor's body she can feel that something at home is going, and she knows the rule. From the middle of the day she is trying to get back and cannot: Inguma is away counting what Lesca gave it. The mayor signing letters flat on the office floor while she tells Inguma to hurry is already here and already funny. Keep it, and let it be frightening as well.
- **She gets home with almost nothing left.** Coralie has rung the doctor, or is one second from it. The price she pays that evening, the spare room, now has a reason the reader has felt.
- The council keeps its best beats and loses whatever length this needs.

## 2. Chapters 5, 7 and 8: the river is at somebody's door

Now the river is a number read off a post. Each time, somebody acts on it or somebody's house shows it.

- **Chapter 5.** The patch on the bank is dark across the width of a door: that is here. Add what a person who lives under it sees that day.
- **Chapter 7.** After the two dry days and the Saturday it comes back: water where it was not on Friday. A cellar in the Port, the drain in the care home's back yard, the line across the road. One of the people who signed nothing yet is standing in it.
- **Chapter 8.** Sarthou has water at his doorstep: that is here. By the vote on Tuesday the Port is doing things about the river with its own hands: sand, a pump, furniture going upstairs. The reader should be able to see the village losing a little every day that Lesca crosses.
- Take out, to pay for it, readings of the river that nobody acts on, and nights that are told and not played.
