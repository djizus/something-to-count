# Brief: the clock keeps running from chapter 7 to chapter 10

The new middle won twice, blind, and both times clearly (`audit/pair14/`, `audit/pair15/`). Readers now say the book is level with The Good Place and level with Blake Crouch from chapter 9, and that one thing is left: "Between the council in 6 and the van in 11, nothing is closing on the heroine on the page." "The pulse chart is the only such clock in those four chapters." "Crouch puts a body at risk in every chapter."

Chapter 6 showed what staying costs: the body left at home runs down. This pass lets that clock go on running in chapters 7, 8 and 10, on the woman who keeps staying, and lets the woman she stays in pay as well. No new rule: it is the same one, seen again. You are the only writer. Everything follows `briefs/line.md` and JC's notes (nobody she knows is "a man" or "a woman"; one comparison to a sentence; nothing to decode; no line built to be quoted).

## 1. Chapter 7: Lesca is spending her life for a bed, and the reader watches the bill

She is in from 6 on Saturday morning until Sunday. Over there her body lies in her own bed with its eyes open.

- **Two short cut-aways to that bedroom**, as chapter 6 cut to Coralie: Arlette beside the bed, in her own way of talking, with no chart and no nursing. What a mother notices: the hand colder than at noon, the breath she has to bend down to hear. One in the afternoon, during the walk to the trapdoor or before bingo. One late, while Lesca is on the landing asking for a pillow.
- Nothing in Maylis's village knows this. The reader does. So when Coralie says "I'd have you tomorrow", the reader knows what tomorrow is costing.

## 2. Chapter 8: it is closing on Maylis too

Each time Lesca has stayed, Maylis has got her body back a little later and a little worse.

- **On Sunday morning, when Lesca has gone, the right hand is slow.** Coralie is a nurse and tests it the way a nurse does: squeeze my fingers. It is weaker than it was on Monday, and she says so, as a fact about a hand. Then what follows from it, said once, plainly, by Coralie or by Maylis: if she stays the whole of Friday night, there may be no hand to come back to.
- That is what "a whole life with one hand" was already afraid of. Now it has started. Do not explain it further.
- By Wednesday it has not come all the way back. One line where she drops something, or signs her name and the L is not her father's.

## 3. Chapter 10, the first half

On the Friday in her own village Lesca is not well, and people who have known her for eleven years can see it: one line each from two of them, in their own voices, while the rehearsal goes on. She goes anyway.

## 4. In the van, chapter 12, one line

The right hand that locks on the brake is the weak one, and it holds.
