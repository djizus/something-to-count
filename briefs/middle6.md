The last three passes on the middle each won their blind test (`audit/pair14/`, `pair15/`, `pair16/`). Readers now say the book is level with The Good Place and level with Blake Crouch from chapter 9, and that one sentence still holds it under him: "From chapter 7 to chapter 10 the danger is a date on a calendar or a body in the other village. Two things the book already has can be in the room. You are the only writer. Everything follows `briefs/line.md` and JC's notes (nobody she knows is "a man" or "a woman"; one comparison to a sentence; nothing to decode; no line built to be quoted). No new rule and no new person.

## 1. Chapter 7: the rib can kill her, and a nurse says so

Maylis keeps Lesca out for two nights by pushing a weight off her chest with a cracked rib. At present that costs her breath. It should cost more, and the reader should know it before she does it.

- **Before the first night, Coralie tells her what a cracked rib does if it is pushed against.** She has seen it at work: the rib goes through, into the lung. She says it as a nurse says it, with what the patient looked like. She thinks she is talking about lifting sacks.
- **Maylis pushes anyway, both nights.** On the second, on the kitchen floor, something is wrong afterwards that was not wrong the night before: what she tastes, what she coughs into the sink, how far down a breath goes. She hides it from Coralie.
- **That is why she cannot push on Friday the 4th**, and why she cannot push again all week. It is no longer that the rib "gives her one breath". She has been told what the next push does. Mend the later lines that explain it differently, in 8 and 11.
- In the van in 11, when she pushes for those six rings, the reader knows what she is spending. Chapter 13 already has "whatever had given in her side"; it now has a cause.

## 2. Thursday night: the river is in room twelve's corridor

The reader fears for Coralie and for Madame Etcheto, and until 3 on Saturday morning neither of them is ever wet.

- **One cut-away, on the night of Thursday the 10th**, where the others are (Coralie at the bedside in 6, Arlette at the bedside in 7). The care home, the bank end, after lights-out. Water has come under the back door and is lying along the corridor outside room twelve. Coralie and Bixente deal with it with what a care home has. Madame Etcheto is awake and has an opinion. Coralie asks her to sleep at the front and is refused: "That's my door."
- It is short, it is in Coralie's head, and it can be funny. Nobody in it knows what Friday is. The reader does.
- Put it where chapter 8 ends or where chapter 9 begins, whichever keeps "LESCA, MAYLIS." as the last line of 8.
- Chapter 11's message at 10 at night, "Etcheto back in 12. Walked there on my arm", now lands on a corridor the reader has stood in.
