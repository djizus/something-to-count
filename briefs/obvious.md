# Brief: the sentence that says the obvious

JC opened the book and stopped at its second sentence: "She tried her right hand first, because she was right-handed." His word for it was stupid, and he is right. It gives a reason for something that needs none.

Read `manuscript/00.md` to `16.md` in order, as a reader with no patience for being told what he can see. List every sentence of that kind:

1. **A reason nobody needed.** "…because she was right-handed." "He kept the cap in his hands, because he was waiting for the rest of it."
2. **A fact the page has just shown**, said again as a statement.
3. **A thing a person could not fail to know**, told to the reader as news: what a hand is for, that rain is wet, that a door she has just opened is open.
4. **A gloss on a plain word or a plain act.**
5. **A sentence that would make a reader say "obviously".**

Leave alone a reason that is a joke ("At seven there was soup, because soup had been ticked"), a reason that tells the reader something he could not know, and anything a person says aloud in their own voice.

Read chapters 00 to 03 hardest. Those are the pages he reads first.

Write `audit/obvious.md`: for each one the chapter, the sentence quoted exactly as it stands in the file, and the fix. The fix is nearly always to cut the clause or the sentence; where a cut needs a join, write the joined sentence in full. No limit on how many, and no padding: if a chapter has none, say none.

Nothing you write mentions any writing tool, model, vendor or session. Do not run git and do not edit the manuscript.

Reply in two lines: how many, and the chapter with the most.
