# Brief: the first five pages

Three blind readers were given the first 1,300 words of this book beside the first 1,300 words of a published thriller of the kind JC measures it against, unlabelled (`audit/h2h/first.md`, `second.md`, `third.md`). All three chose to go on reading this one, slightly, and all three judged its writing the better of the two, sentence by sentence. They also said what the other opening does better, and they agree on it. Mend those things, in `manuscript/00.md` and the first two sections of `manuscript/01.md`, and nowhere else.

Everything follows `briefs/line.md` and JC's notes on reading (nobody she knows is "a man" or "a woman"; one comparison to a sentence; nothing to decode; nothing obvious; no line built to be quoted).

1. **Two people talk, early, and it is warm.** "Let two people talk for a full page… the reader needs warmth and air between the strange things." At 2:54, when Coralie takes the pulse, they have five lines. Give them about a dozen, half asleep, in which Coralie is funny the way a nurse is funny at 3 in the morning and Maylis is dry back. It ends where it ends now: what Maylis wanted, and didn't ask. The reader should like both of them before the stone.

2. **The sentences are not all one length.** "X's are nearly all the same length and shape, and by the third section that flatness shows." Where the page is calm, let a sentence carry a whole movement from its start to its end, the way a person tells it, with "when", "while", "so", "until", "which". Where something is happening, keep it short. Do not join two unrelated facts with ", and"; that is the fault JC named. Read each paragraph aloud.

3. **"Whose stone it was."** All three stopped there: her own name is on it. What she has eighteen days to find out is what the stone is, and why her name is cut into it.

4. **The site plan goes.** The paragraph that lays out the bank, the patch, the gate, the track, the fields and the tide was read twice by all three, and "ends on drainage instead of on a line". Keep only what the next scene needs: she keeps the key to the gate, Larrère told her to raise it on Monday, and the handle threw her against the rail. The rest is learned later, when the gate is worked.

5. **"She was scared."** in the cold open: a reader called it explaining what he had understood. The clay moving under her hand has said it. Cut it there. Leave the same sentence in the flood night, where it is hers.

6. **Each section ends on a pull**, not on a description.

The two files together end within 150 words of their present length. Nothing you write mentions any writing tool, model, vendor or session. Do not run git.

Reply in three lines: the new dialogue's first and last line, the sentence you put in place of "whose stone it was", and the word count before and after.
