# Brief: the square does not let the stone go slack

Nine blind readers in three runs were given this book's opening beside the opening of a published thriller of the kind JC measures it against (`audit/h2h/`, `audit/h2h2/`, `audit/h2h3/`). All nine chose to go on with this book, and all nine judged its writing the better. Two runs said the same thing about what it still does worse:

- "Y's last scene, the leaves and the postman, is funny but lets the eighteen days go slack."
- "The last page of village business spends the tension that the stone bought."
- "Stay in one scene with one question, and stop on an act."

One change, in `manuscript/01.md`, in the scene in the square (from "It rained all day Tuesday" to Jeannot's "Then that goes in forty-two as well"). Everything follows `briefs/line.md` and JC's notes on reading.

- **The stone is in the scene.** In her picture it stands in the space where the mayor parks, and the mayor's car is parked there now, with him in it. She is raking leaves beside it. Let that be felt: where she looks, what she measures (she measures everything), how many days. Two or three short touches through the scene, in her own head, not one paragraph.
- **The scene keeps its comedy and loses a sixth of its length.** The postman, the gap in the window, "The town hall opens at 2", "That's me", "I'll be brief", Jeannot's numbered points and "Put it in forty-two" all stay. Cut from what is between them.
- **It ends on an act**, hers, that has to do with the stone and the eighteen days, after Jeannot has driven off. Something she does, small and exact. Not a thought.

Change nothing else in the chapter. Nothing you write mentions any writing tool, model, vendor or session. Do not run git.

Reply in two lines: the act the scene now ends on, and the word count of the scene before and after.
