# Brief: continuity and proof of the final text

The book has been through many passes by several hands. Read `manuscript/00.md` to `16.md` in order, in full, and find what those passes left behind. Do not judge the story or the style. Open nothing else but this brief.

## 1. Continuity

Build the calendar as you read, in both villages, and check every statement against it.

- **Dates and weekdays.** In 2026, 24 November is a Tuesday and 11 December is a Friday. Every date with a weekday, every "yesterday", "on Monday", "two nights ago", "for eleven years", "twelve years", "sixteen years", every age and every count of days.
- **Times.** The order of clock times inside each night. 2:51 is the minute throughout.
- **The river.** Every level given, in order, in each village. It must rise, stand for the two dry days, and catch up as the text says.
- **Numbers.** Forty houses, forty-two beds, forty-one, nineteen turns, twenty-five minutes and ten, the letters numbered to 42, the eight graves, and anything else counted more than once.
- **Who knows what.** Nobody acts on something they have not been told yet. Nobody is surprised by something they already know.
- **Which village.** In each scene: whose village, who is mayor there, what job each person has there, who is alive there.
- **Whose hand.** In scenes where one woman drives the other's body: the owner keeps her right hand. Any sentence that gives the wrong hand to the wrong woman.
- **Objects.** The key, the pad, the timer, the card and the envelope, the pillow, the phone, the two vans, the folder: where each one is each time it appears.
- **Things that were cut** but are still referred to, and things referred to before they are introduced.

## 2. Proof

- Spelling that is not consistent (the book is in British spelling), and any word used in two forms.
- Dates and clock times written in words. They should be in figures: "11 December 2014", "2:51 a.m.", "at 5 p.m.". Counts stay in words.
- Any of these words, which the book no longer uses: winch, sluice, pawl, gauge, embankment, blind (for the duck hut), hatch, waders, meadows, dyke.
- Quotation marks that open and do not close, or two speeches run together.
- A word repeated by accident, a missing word, a sentence that does not parse.
- A name spelled two ways, or a person given a name in one place and only a role in another.

## Report

Write `audit/proof.md`. For each finding: the chapter, the sentence quoted exactly, what is wrong, and the smallest correction, written out. Sort section 1 by how much a reader would notice. If a kind has no findings, say so. Do not pad.

Nothing you write mentions any writing tool, model, vendor or session. Do not run git and do not edit the manuscript.

Reply in three lines: how many of each kind, and the three worst.
