# Brief: the read for quirks

JC is reading the book and stopped at three more lines in its first two pages:

- "It hit the floor like a full sack dropped off a trailer, then crossed the room with a dry sound, like dead leaves being dragged." → "repetition of like"
- "'A hundred and twelve,' she said. 'What is it?'" → "I don't understand the 112 there?" (It was her pulse. Nothing said so.)
- "A woman drove past all of them to the end of the road and got out in the rain." → "this sentence feels wrong, it feels impersonal yet personal. I think this is really a writing quirk, not sure how to describe it."

He is describing a narrator who stands outside a person and calls her "a woman", and in the next line reports what she feels. A human writer picks one. Those three are mended. Find the rest.

Load the `stop-slop` skill and use it in detect-only mode as one of your instruments. Read `manuscript/00.md` to `16.md` in order, in full. Read chapters 00 to 04 hardest; those are the pages he is on.

List every line of these kinds:

1. **Outside and inside at once.** The narrator calls somebody "a man", "a woman", "the mayor", "the keyholder", "the other one", or holds a name back, when the person whose head we are in knows perfectly well who it is. "A man had just sworn at her in six points, and he was alive" is Jeannot, and she knows it. Leave only the places where she truly does not know yet who she is looking at.
2. **A line the reader has to decode.** A number, a gesture, a nickname or a reference whose meaning is not on the page at that moment. The test: would a reader say "I don't understand that"?
3. **Comparisons.** Two in one sentence. One straight after another. A comparison that says less than the plain thing would. A comparison a thousand books have used.
4. **The camera.** A paragraph that describes a place from nowhere, before anybody is in it, or lists what is in a room in a row.
5. **Anything else that reads as machine-made** and that a person telling this story would not write: a sentence that balances two halves for the sake of it, a short sentence added only for a beat, a paragraph that ends on a line built to be quoted, a thing described by what it was not, the same construction three times running, a word nobody says. Quote it and say what the tell is.

For each: the chapter, the sentence quoted exactly as it stands, the kind, and the correction written out in full, in the plainest words. A correction that cuts is better than one that adds. Do not flag speech unless it is kind 2.

Write `audit/quirks.md`. No limit and no padding. Sort each chapter in reading order.

Nothing you write mentions any writing tool, model, vendor or session; describe a tell as what a reader hears, not by where it comes from. Do not run git and do not edit the manuscript.

Reply in three lines: how many of each kind, and the chapter with the most.
