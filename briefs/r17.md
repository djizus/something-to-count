# Brief: three jobs after round seventeen

Round seventeen (`audit/round17-*.md`) put the book at 7.2 against a bar of 8, "below, by a little". The voice is now the fluent one in `briefs/style.md` and `briefs/style-words.md`: read both, and keep to them. Five readers agree on what is left. It is split into three jobs on separate chapters. Do yours, in place in `manuscript/`, and touch no other chapter. Read the whole manuscript first, and the faults in all five reports that name your chapters.

## Job A: chapter 8, the day Maylis spends as mayor

Three readers: "the reversal day is a sketch", "the return crossing is a synopsis", "Maylis's one day in the life she did not get is told from inside the other woman."

Rewrite chapter 8 so that the day is played at full length from Maylis's side: she is in Lesca's body, in Lesca's village, in the life she wanted, with Lesca fighting her for the right hand. Four or five stations, in public, each worse than the last, as chapter 6 does for Lesca. She should want it, enjoy part of it, and be frightened by the end. The misunderstanding that chapter 6 starts about who visits the Castagnet house is picked up here and gets its laugh. About 2,800 words. It ends on a danger the next chapter in that village answers on the page.

## Job B: chapters 13, 14 and 15, the night

- **13.** "The heroine's plan for the night is to wait, and the second pair of hands is sent away without a thought." Maylis acts before the night: she tries the thing a reader would try, and it fails for a reason on the page. Kévin and his van are kept in play or their absence is paid for. She does not doubt what the book has already proved.
- **14.** "The takeover the book has built since chapter 4 is settled in eight rings, by a paragraph of costing." The fight for the hand carries the decision. Cut every restatement of the dilemma: the reader has it.
- **15.** "The evacuation has no one in danger on the page." Someone the reader loves is in danger where the reader can see it, before the count. The timestamps and "Forty-one" stay.
- In all three, cut each line where the narrator explains a pay-off the reader has just had.

## Job C: chapters 2, 7, 9, 10, 12, 16 and 18

- **Endings.** "Three chapter endings are answered off the page." Chapters 7, 9 and 10 each end on a hook, and the next chapter in that village opens by answering it, in scene.
- **Rules.** After chapter 5 nobody recites how the crossing works. Cut each recital in 9, 10 and 12; the reader has seen it done.
- **Chapter 2.** The premise is announced "in a voice that knows more than Lesca can". Give it to her as what she works out, in her own head, plainly.
- **Chapters 16 and 18.** "Two receiving lines": villagers come up one at a time, deliver a verdict and leave. Keep the two encounters that cost most in each and fold the rest into them. Chapter 18 opens going forwards, not by repairing the calendar or explaining the verdict.
- Cut each line where the narrator explains a pay-off.

## For all three

Fluent and plain, by the style brief. Every sentence reads once. The flood defence is the embankment or the bank. Keep the book between 45,000 and 48,000 words. Nothing you write mentions any writing tool, model, vendor or session. Do not run git. Reply with one line a chapter: its word count and what changed.
