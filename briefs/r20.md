# Brief: round twenty, the hinge and the cuts

Five readers and a sixth who read only for plot logic agree on what holds the book one point under its bar. Their reports are `audit/round20-thriller.md`, `-comedy.md`, `-characters.md`, `-reader.md`, `-prose.md` and `audit/logic.md`. Read section 3 of each for your chapters, and section 4 of each, which nobody touches.

The class of fault is one: **the book makes a reader say "why doesn't she just", and then tells him what he has already seen.** Everything below closes that. The decisions are made. Do not reopen them.

The voice is `briefs/style.md`, `briefs/style-words.md` and `briefs/rhythm.md`. Read them first. Edit in place in `manuscript/`. Chapter numbers below are the present ones.

## Rules for every chapter

1. **Captions go.** If an action or a line of speech has just shown the feeling, the sentence that names it is cut: "It hurt to hear.", "She found that she could.", "It was the right answer, and Maylis had not been the one to give it." A stated want stays only where it gives a fact no action gives. The prose reader counted about sixty.
2. **A callback is not explained.** Cut "the way it had buzzed on the dashboard of a tractor", "no timer rang", "This time the mayor had no car around him", "He meant the medal", and the rest of comedy fault 7.
3. **One explanation per thing.** The pawl, the blind, the chair and each rule are explained at first use and never again. After chapter 1 it is "the blind". No chapter opens on a recap longer than one clause.
4. **Names.** Larrère and Jeannot after first entrance in a chapter, unless the formality is the joke. "She knew that…" stays twice in the book (1 and 12). The withheld name ("It was…") stays only where the delay is the point.
5. Add nothing that is not listed here. Each chapter ends no longer than it is now unless its entry says so. The book stays above 43,000 words.
6. Nothing you write mentions any writing tool, model, vendor or session. Do not run git.

Give him one sentence about Maïté that is not about a file.

  - `08b.md`: the breakfast fight, the shift change, the river at the line, the petition, the vote, the note, the steel room, the hall of the dead. ## Chapters 09 to 12

- **9.** The way in is three sentences: the chair's arms were cold, Lesca was not in it, and the night came to Maylis anyway. Then "A phone rang beside Lesca's bed in the dark." Cut from the night everything chapter 00 already gave, and keep what is new: Larrère's call and the clock behind it, Coralie's car in the yard, the reflector, the four minutes, the sole of the wader. Cut "Nobody in that village knew she had looked down the track first."
- **10.** Open the rehearsal at "The front row belongs to the care home." Cut the two sentences that repeat the handle turning. "Wednesday night" names one night in the book; fix the other.
- **11. Five o'clock is hers.** This is the decision of the book and at present she is blackmailed into it. Rewrite from Jeannot's "Shut, or open?" to "'Either,' Maylis said."
  - Cut "This was the moment Lesca's plan had needed… The only thing missing was the order."
  - What she weighs, in plain sentences, is this. She can open it now. Then the river is on the meadows by dark on her word alone, against a vote and an order, with the bank still standing and her own patch in it, the one she built in April with a wheelbarrow. If the patch holds, it is her flood. Or she can open it at three, when the water is coming through and nobody can say she chose. At three it would be the river's doing. At five it was hers.
  - The folder with Kévin's name is in the car, and it is one clause. It is what she will tell herself afterwards. It does not decide her.
  - She says "Either" to a direct question from a man standing on her planks, the way she said it to six men. He says "Then it's shut" and goes down before she can take it back. What her waiting costs is him.
  - Cut "She had been ordered to stay by the gate… She found that she could."
  - **The café before Hontaube:** she had tried the café first; the nine had watched Jeannot's hand go up, and a vote was a vote.
  - **At the hatch**, Jeannot shuts off her only proof himself: he will not be given Lafitte's pad, because Larrère has said that sentence at every high water since 2008.
  - **Kévin** does not offer to sit on the hatch; cut the offer and "She wanted to say yes." "He wouldn't for me either. I'm in three of his letters" closes the hatch. She will not put the boy at the handle the sheet is about, and that is said in one sentence.
  - **One o'clock.** One short paragraph closes "fetch him now": he had not come up for her in daylight with the gate shut and his cellar dry, and nothing she could say at one would be different until there was water at his hatch.
  - **Why Inguma finds her in a van, awake:** it came at that minute for the woman who made the call, and tonight she was the one at the gate with the phone.
- **12.** Before the fight for the van, one plain sentence for each aim: Lesca is driving to the blind, and Maylis is fighting to stay at the gate, for Coralie. In the hall, after "I saw your van. I rang once", one beat more before her knees bend: she waits for him to look up, he marks a second card, the caller says her name again. Cut "and a third sheet to put right". The voice goes on calling her name as she walks. The place stays laid.

## Chapters 13 to 16
