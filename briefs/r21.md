# Brief: round twenty-one

The last pass moved the book from 7.1 to 7.3, its best mark, by closing "why doesn't she just" and cutting what the reader already had. Five new readers have read the result: `audit/round21-thriller.md`, `-comedy.md`, `-characters.md`, `-reader.md`, `-prose.md`. Read section 3 of each for your chapters, and section 4 of each, which nobody touches. Chapters now run 00 to 17.

What is left is four things. The first five chapters load before they move. The middle repeats itself. The choice at the dip is a sum. Lesca gets off lightly. The decisions below are made. Do not reopen them.

The voice is `briefs/style.md`, `briefs/style-words.md` and `briefs/rhythm.md`. Edit in place in `manuscript/`.

## Rules for every chapter

1. **Tell each event once**, from the woman it costs more. Where a later chapter replays an earlier one, keep only what is new.
2. **No caption after a beat, and none before it.** Prose fault 4 lists nine; cut eight. Keep the two sentences in 13 that say what each woman is fighting for.
3. **The narrator contracts** (didn't, couldn't, wasn't, hadn't) everywhere. People who speak formally, Sarthou and Père Honoré, do not.
4. **Half the "looked at" go**, where the next line shows what was seen. Fewer sentences open with "Then". A scene opens on speech or action unless the hour is the threat.
5. **Two people do not use one template.** "I'm asking…" belongs to Coralie; "Don't give me…" belongs to Coralie. Take them from Arlette, Jeannot, Maïté and Lesca. At least three of the women who end a scene by correcting a heroine lose the capping line.
6. "Rang", never "phoned". "Around", never "round". "Drives" for what the one in the chair does to the other, never "rides".
7. Each chapter ends no longer than it is now unless its entry says so. The book stays above 43,000 words.
8. Nothing you write mentions any writing tool, model, vendor or session. Do not run git.

## Chapters 14 to 17

- **14.** Beside the winch at 02:55, with the handle level with her eye: two sentences of why she takes the van down. At five that morning she let a phone ring nine times and heard the man alive, and she thought she could do better than the woman who rang once.
  - **The dip is the choice, and it is the longest beat in the chapter.** Cut "Coralie had a list, a minibus, Kévin's van and ten minutes' warning." She knows what six minutes at the winch buys: the bank end dry, room twelve dry, Coralie dry. She knows what it does four minutes later to a bolted hatch. She takes three steps back towards the lamp over the winch. Then she turns and goes on. About two hundred words. Nothing comforts her.
  - While she is moving, no "because" and fewer ", and". Cut the second "Here". Jeannot at the door is the click count, the knee and number six. Cut the recap of the two of them laughing at door twelve.
- **15.** Cut the narrator's version of "nothing on the sheet for this" and the sentence that explains the confession before it comes.
