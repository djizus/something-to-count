The voice is `briefs/style.md`, `briefs/style-words.md` and `briefs/rhythm.md`. Edit in place in `manuscript/`. Chapter numbers are the present ones; files are renumbered afterwards.

## Rules for every chapter

1. **The narrator does not explain.** No sentence says what a scene has just shown, what it is about to show, or what a rule is at the moment the rule is used. The narrator never knows the future. The prose reader marked 28 captions; keep one only where it turns the scene.
2. **A refrain is used three times**: set, turned, paid. Halve "care home", "forty houses", "twelve years", "two fingers", "ninety", "nine minutes to three", "in daylight". After a person's second appearance in the book, one name.
3. **One owner for each way of speaking.** The year count in front ("Sixteen years, and…") belongs to Coralie and Larrère. "Which do I put?" belongs to Lafitte. "I'll number it" is never announced; Jeannot just counts. Arlette and Maïté lose the finished retort and speak plainly. The gendarme keeps the uncontracted sentence; the priest loses it.
4. **The rules of the crossing are two, and the reader can hold them:** the one who stays in the chair drives the one who gets up; Inguma fetches her home when it has finished counting. The one who stays simply decides to stay. Cut alarms, shifts and rotas as the reason for who drives.
5. Half the scenes that open on a quoted line and two sentences of back-fill open on place or action instead. "The window went up" closes a scene twice in the book.
6. "Gendarme" and "gendarmes", never "police". "Rang". "Around". "Drives".
7. The book stays above 41,000 words.
8. Nothing you write mentions any writing tool, model, vendor or session. Do not run git.

## Chapters 06 to 09: the same fight is not played four times

- **6.** One section, the café at noon, is told from inside Lesca: she is looking at Jean Castagnet alive on a stool. At the kitchen table she sends Inguma off again, aloud and plainly, once, so the reader has seen it done before bingo night.
- **7.** One sentence says what a complaint with her name in it costs her, and she says her threat to Jeannot in plain words. The line about verges at the speed of the village tractor moves here, to the floor of the mayor's office at half past two.
- **8.**
  - **The walk to the hatch is a scene:** Lesca with the watch, Maylis in the right hand, the hatch under their boots. Twenty-five minutes on foot, ten for the bank. It is the sum the book runs on and it has been a linking paragraph.
  - **The bar is rebuilt around what the hall can see.** A customer at the urn. The cup overflows. Somebody reads the clenched teeth as a tooth, or as the Castagnet business, and says so. The numbers run through the struggle. Madame Etcheto finishing the words is the topper, and it lands on a room that has been laughing. Name Inguma as what lifts the tablecloths. Cut the narrator's notes on the rule.
  - One line after the two nights of pushing: the gauge kept to the file anyway.
  - On the landing Coralie says what Bayonne is to her: the night sister called Ithurbide, thirty beds, a torch and a list. She asks the one who answers what Jean Castagnet is to her, and gets no answer. The section ends on the pillow going down in the spare room.
- **9.** The wrestle at the kitchen table gets a page, Coralie has a line in it, and her choice between the two gets its beat: what she sees in the hand over the mouth, one look, one line, then the wrist. In the steel room **Maylis says both, and Lesca tells her from experience that there is no both**: ten minutes against twenty-five. In place of "So who holds my hand down then?", a plain sentence of what Lesca will do on Friday and who could stop her. Madame Darrigrand has two lines at the petition.

## Chapters 10 to 13

- **10.** Cut "She wouldn't hire the woman who had rung him once and then opened the gate."
- **12. Shorter by a quarter: no more than 3,700 words.** The café is two sentences again. Jeannot refuses twice, not three times: the van-window exchange is its two practical lines.
  - **Five o'clock.** "Shut, or open?" The river goes by. "'Either,' Maylis said." "Then it's shut." He is down the steps. Then, while she lifts the handle off, two sentences: at three it would be the river's doing, and at five it would have been hers. Cut the three paragraphs before the word, and "the reason she would give herself afterwards". At 01:12, cut the sentence that says she is waiting for the river to decide. Cut "She was scared."
- **13. The fight is about who drives, not about where.**
  - Sitting on Inguma is Lesca's own idea. One sentence, before she sits, says what it buys: Inguma cannot get up to fetch her, and she keeps the body. Nothing says what it costs.
  - Lesca drives for the blind, to get the man out herself this time. Maylis is not against the blind. She is against being driven. That is the handbrake.
  - **Maylis puts her out.** She takes the jaw and says the words herself, "Inguma, enauk hire beldur", for the first time in her life without "Either" after them, and she gives it something short to count: the fingers of her right hand. Inguma gets up to do its work.
  - Lesca has the arms of the chair and could hold on. One sentence names what she sees: the hand on the brake is the hand that held the stair post against her, and the reflector is no nearer than it was twelve years ago. She lets go. On the way out: "Go to the blind first."
  - In the hall her name is on a card, because she held Inguma down for eight rings. She learns that there, and so does the reader. Cut "and that counted". **The dead play bingo**: one of them calls a line early, something is on the edge of the stage as a prize, and there are two more details of that kind. She resists once before she gets up and walks.
