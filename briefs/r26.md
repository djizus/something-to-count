# Brief: round twenty-six

Round 26 is the best yet, 7.40, and a fourth blind pair chose this version. Four of five readers would press it on a friend, each with the same warning: "it gets going in chapter 4." The reports are `audit/round26-thriller.md`, `-comedy.md`, `-characters.md`, `-reader.md`, `-prose.md`; read section 2 and 3 of each for your chapters, and section 4, which nobody touches.

Four things are left, and each was named by three readers or more. The decisions are made.

The voice is `briefs/style.md`, `briefs/style-words.md` and `briefs/rhythm.md`. JC's standard for comedy: funny means the reader smiles. Nobody is asked for bigger jokes.

## A. Chapter 3 never goes backwards (writer one)

All five reports: chapter 2 ends on "'Come back across,' her mouth said. Maylis hadn't said it", and chapter 3 opens "Four nights earlier" and takes 3,400 words to get back to that hand. "This is where a reader stops." And Lesca "enters the book as a deduction".

- **Open chapter 3 at that moment, from the other side.** Rain on a roof, a bed with two pillows, a mouth that is stiff and is hers to use. Lesca finds out where she is by being there. Nothing is reasoned out lying down.
- Then the chapter goes forward only: the morning after, in her own village. The committee, the timer and the verb for the stone, the house with two doors, the file read line by line until the handwriting turns into typing, "It hadn't had it yet", the motion. Re-date what needs it. What she lived before that night comes as a clause inside a forward scene, or goes.
- The chapter keeps its best lines and loses its file voice: scenes, not entries. No longer than it is now.

## B. The rule about the truth is found by Maylis failing to say "Either" (writer one)

Comedy fault 2, characters fault 5, thriller fault 2: "the truth room catches one woman only", it "does Lesca's honesty for her", the reversal is "proved by an oracle".

- **Chapter 4.** The rule is discovered when Lesca asks Maylis a straight question, standing, and "Either" will not come out of her. It is not true: she does want one of them. That is how they learn that in that room a woman can only say what is true. Then Lesca tries her ready answer about the bank and the winch and loses it the same way. Take out the "Either" that Maylis now says whole in that room.
- **Chapter 8, Sunday night. Lesca says it herself.** She is a woman of files: on Sunday she has been through the dates, and every night she crossed, the river rose, and the two nights she was kept out, it stood. She opens the meeting with it, unasked. One speaker, a silence, a room that has to hear it. Maylis said it at a bar and was laughed at; here it is said by the one it condemns. The room is not what proves it.
- Then the rule a reader can use, once, in plain words: **if Lesca is in this village at nine minutes to three on Friday night, the bank goes at eleven minutes past, as it did in hers. If she is not, it holds.**
- Maylis: don't come on Friday. Lesca tries to say she won't, and that is where the room catches her, as now.
- **Chapters 11 and 12 use that one rule and no other.** When Lesca gets in, the night is coming, whatever happens after. Putting her out does not stop it, and one plain line says so. Cut "long enough for her night to come through" and anything else that gives the water a second cause.
- The nights of Inguma in chapters 7 and 8 are "the same night six times". Play two in full and give the others a line each.

## C. Maylis is good company from page one (writer two)

Characters section 2 and faults 1 and 7, comedy fault 4: "the book's lead is its least vivid person for five chapters"; her first ten spoken lines are refusals; "no reason to want Maylis to keep Coralie"; "Maylis has a dry eye and the book takes it away when she is alone."

Her flaw stays. She still cannot say which. But:

- **She has a dry eye, and the reader gets it from the first page.** She prices and measures everything she looks at: a morning's work, four barrow-loads, twenty minutes with the tractor. Give her that in her own head in chapters 1, 2 and 5, and wherever she is alone in 7, 11 and 13. It replaces sentences in which the narrator says what she wants; twenty-seven of those are quoted in characters fault 7, and most go.
- **She loves by doing.** In chapters 1 and 2, two things she has done for Coralie without being asked and without saying so, that Coralie has noticed. In chapter 1 Coralie laughs at something Maylis says. The reader must want this marriage kept before anybody comes for it.
- Chapter 1: Jeannot and Larrère are not a puzzle to decode before the reader knows what a gate is; say who each is in a clause when he appears.

## D. Chapter 6 has an errand and a body at risk (writer two)

Thriller faults 5 and 6, reader fault 3: "Maylis crosses for too little, at a price she knows." Two readers: between chapters 6 and 10 "the danger on the page is a vote, a file and a folder".

- **She crosses to read the night she is going to face.** In the mayor's office is the file of the flood, and she reads the page for the hour: where it came through, how long the bank had, what the gendarmes typed about the blind. She wants a day as the woman who signs things as well, and that stays.
- **Cut once, in the middle of the council, to her own bedroom**: her body with its eyes open, Coralie with two fingers on the wrist every half hour and a thumb over the doctor's number. She is hours overdue, and the one it cannot take home dies in her own bed. Then back to Larrère with no bell.
- The council is no longer than it is now; pay for the cut-away out of the first half of his speech.
