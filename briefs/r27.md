## 1. The turn is revealed once, played once, and confirmed in five lines

Now it is told at the platform in narration, shouted at the bar, and then read into a meeting as a list of dates. "Told three times and never played." "In a Blake Crouch novel or in The Good Place that discovery wrecks the next chapter. Here it changes the plan to staying awake in a van."

- **Chapter 7, the platform.** Evidence only: yesterday the river was a metre under the red line, today it is fifty-five. Cut the two sentences in which the narrator draws the conclusion. She has nobody to say it to and no mouth to say it with; keep that.
- **Chapter 7, the bar. This is where the reader learns it**, from her mouth: "You're the water." The hall mishears it, as now. Lesca hears it. Nothing else explains it there.
- **Chapter 8, Sunday morning, the kitchen. This is where it is played, in front of Coralie.** Coralie heard three words at a bar. She is a nurse and she wants it like a chart: what, since when, how do you know. When Maylis has her own mouth back she tells her, in whole sentences, without being asked twice. It is the first straight answer she has given Coralie since "You". Coralie takes the dates the way she takes a pulse, and then she turns to the face she said she would have tomorrow and asks it one question. Lesca answers her with the tide. Coralie does not have her tomorrow after that: the woman she liked better is the river over forty-two beds, thirty metres from her shift. Her choice between the two, the hand she pins, comes out of this and not out of a scuffle. This scene changes what all three of them want, and the rest of the chapter shows it.
- **Chapter 8, Sunday night, the steel room: five lines.** Lesca tries the tide again, standing, and it will not come out. Then the rule, as now: only the last night counts; if she is in this village at 2:51 on Friday night the bank goes, and if she is not, it holds. Cut the list of dates she recites.
- **Wednesday** keeps "Don't come on Friday", what she cannot say, the van, and "there is no both".
- **Chapters 11 and 12** do not explain the rule again. "You're the water" comes back once, in the van.

## 2. The fight in the van has a name

"I do not know what the fight in the van is for." "Lesca's reason for coming on Friday changes between chapters without a scene."

- **Lesca has one reason she says and one that is true, and they are the same in chapters 8, 10 and 12.** She says she is coming to get the man out this time. The true one, which is all she can say standing in the steel room, is that she is coming to stay.
- **In the van, Lesca means to keep this body, and Maylis means to have it back.** One plain line says so where it starts. Neither disputes the road. The river is already coming; cut "every minute she stayed was the river" and anything like it.
- What Lesca risks by staying is on the card the reader saw: the one Inguma cannot take home dies in her own bed. One line reminds us before she sits.
- The rest stands: the words with no "Either", the five fingers, what Lesca sees, "Go to the hut first".
- **Chapter 13.** She was warned about the dip and waded it at dusk. She takes the van into it knowing, and the text says so in a sentence.
