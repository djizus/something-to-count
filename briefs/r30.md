## 1. No rule is listed. Each is learned from something that happens

The five rules stay as they are. What changes is how the reader gets them: once, at the moment it happens to somebody, in a line that person says or sees. Never as a numbered point, never in an agenda, never twice.

- **Stay sitting and you wake in her body; the owner keeps her right hand.** Chapter 3 already shows both: Lesca wakes in the wrong bed, and the right hand is not hers. That is where the reader learns it. In chapter 4 each of them says what happened to her, in a line, and they have it.
- **Inguma takes the visitor home when it has finished counting.** Madame Etcheto gives the words and the counting in chapter 2. In chapter 5 the reader watches Lesca send it off, and watches it come back for her on the stairs.
- **The one it cannot take home dies, and plays bingo.** Madame Etcheto, chapter 2. The card at the end of chapter 8 recalls it without a word.
- **Standing in the steel room, only the truth comes out.** Chapter 4: "Either" will not come out of Maylis. Then Lesca loses her ready answer the same way.
- **If Lesca is in this village at 2:51 on Friday night, the bank goes. If she is not, it holds.** Chapter 8, as now.

**Chapter 4 is still a meeting with nobody in the chair**, and still funny as one. But its items are not rules. They are: who are you; what happened to your village and when; what do we do on Monday. Cut "There are three points, and I'll say them once" and the points. Cut every later sentence, in any chapter, that says one of the five again. After this pass a reader should not be able to find a rule stated in two places.
