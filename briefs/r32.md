# Brief: one rule fewer

Ten blind pairs in, every reader says the book is "below, not by much", "level from chapter 9", and that one thing decides it: the number of rules a reader holds through chapters 4 to 8. One counted eight. Making them clear has been done. This pass makes them fewer, by taking one out whole. It is a test: the result is read blind against the present version, and the better one is kept.

**The rule that goes: "standing up in the steel room, a woman can only say what is true."** Readers have called it an oracle and said it does Lesca's honesty for her. Without it, she chooses what to say, and that is worse for her and better for the book.

You are the only writer. Everything follows `briefs/line.md`. Change only what this rule touches.

- **Chapter 4.** Nobody finds a rule about truth. Cut "Either" failing to come out, "Tell me it's Tuesday" and Lesca losing her ready answer; cut it from the three points if it is there. When Maylis asks why the flood would come now, Lesca gives her ready answer, the bank nobody has looked after and the handle that sticks, and Maylis does not believe it. Neither of them knows the true one.
- **Chapter 8, Sunday night.** Lesca told Coralie it was the tide, across a kitchen table. Now it is only the two of them, and Maylis has the chart. Lesca stops lying because there is nobody left to lie to, and says it herself: she brings it. Then the rule for Friday, as now.
- **Chapter 8, Wednesday.** "Don't come on Friday." Lesca does not fail to promise. She says what she is going to do: she is coming, and she is coming to stay. Said by choice, to the woman's face.
- **Chapters 10 and 12.** She says to her mother and in her office that she is going to get the man out this time. In the steel room on Friday she says the other thing, because Maylis is the one person she has stopped lying to. Cut "Standing up, it was the only answer that would come out" and anything like it.
- **Chapter 16.** "Don't come again." "I won't." Nothing certifies it. Inguma goes out over the sill, and neither of them sits down: that is the proof. "I don't know", about which she turned the handle for, stays, as a thing she says and not a thing a room allows.
- Anywhere else the rule is mentioned or leaned on, including "This isn't your kitchen", cut or mend it.

The book ends this pass above 41,900 words. Nothing you write mentions any writing tool, model, vendor or session. Do not run git.

Reply in four lines: what changed in each chapter and the word count before and after.
