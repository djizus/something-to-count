# Brief: no page read twice

Every reader now says the book is level with the comparisons from chapter 9 and just under before it, and that the distance is ease: "An 8 is a book I read as easily as Dark Matter." Five blind readers of the present version each listed the pages they had to read twice (`audit/pair10/`, `audit/pair11/`). They are the same few pages. This pass mends those pages and nothing else. You are the only writer. Everything follows `briefs/line.md`.

The test for each: a reader who has never seen the book reads the page once, at speed, and knows who is where and what has just happened.

## The pages

1. **Chapter 3, the first page** (four of five readers). "Whose bed, which Coralie, which eleven years." The first three sentences of the chapter must say, in this order and in plain words: this is the other Maylis Lesca, the one whose village had the flood and made her mayor, and the book calls her Lesca. She has just woken in Maylis's bed, in Maylis's body, beside Maylis's Coralie. In her own house Coralie has slept across the landing for eleven years. Then the scene. Do not open on "It was her own bedroom".

2. **Chapter 3, the file** (two readers). "2014 dates laid over this year's." Say it outright before the dates: her file is from 2014; this year the days of the week fall on the same dates; so each line of it can be read as this year's. Then "Tuesday had been the 24th" is easy.

3. **Chapter 6, the tied vote** (four readers). Maylis is driving Lesca's body, so tonight the right hand is Lesca's. When the vote comes, say whose hand goes up for what, one hand to a sentence, and then say in a plain line how the count ends and whose vote decided it.

4. **Chapter 7, the end of bingo** (three readers). "Maylis had won, then Maylis had lost." Two plain sentences, each at its moment. With the jaw shut, Lesca cannot send Inguma away, so it will take her home: that is Maylis winning. Then Madame Etcheto says the words for her and gives it corn to count until morning, so it goes, and Lesca stays the night: that is Maylis losing.

5. **Chapter 8, the rule for Friday** (three readers). "Your river made up two days in an afternoon, so only the last night counts" is the muddiest line in the book, and the flood night stands on it. Lesca says it in short steps: keeping her out for a night or two changes nothing, because the river catches up the day she is back. One night matters. If she is in this village at 2:51 on Friday night, the bank goes, as hers did. If she is not, it holds.

6. **Chapter 12, the hall** (two readers). Chapter 2 said the one who will not be taken home dies. She is in the hall, and then she is alive in a hospital corridor. Say why, in the hall, in one or two lines: her place was laid because she held on for a ring; she let go before she sat down in it; so Inguma takes her home after all, by way of the hall. One more ring and she would have sat.

7. **Chapter 1, Jeannot's water** (one reader). What raising the gate did to his water: the fields held a hand of rain that the ducks came down to; the river was low; when the gate went up, that water ran out into the river. Three short steps.

8. **Chapter 4**: "It's Saturday" is said at about 3 on Sunday morning. Mend the day. **Chapter 8**, Sunday night: "Now I've got both" sits just before Maylis's "Both" means something else. Reword it.
