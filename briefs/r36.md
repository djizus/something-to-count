## Writer one: chapters 7, 8 and 11

**1. Chapter 7 becomes two chapters, and the first ends on the trapdoor.** Write them as `07.md` and `07b.md`; they are renumbered afterwards.

- `07.md`: the two nights, the cross on Bordenave's pad, the Saturday, the walk. **On the platform Maylis does the sum where the reader can watch**: the cross on the pad, the dry road on Friday, forty-five centimetres made up in a day, and the first night she lay on her back was the first night it rained. She has it, and so does the reader, in plain sentences. She has no mouth to say it with. The chapter ends with her standing on Jeannot's trapdoor inside the woman who is timing the walk to save him, and who is the reason he needs saving.
- `07b.md`: bingo night, the bedside in the other village, the landing. Now the reader knows what "You're the water" means when she gets it out, and the hall does not. Nothing in the scene explains it.
- In chapter 8 the chart is Coralie testing what the reader already holds. Trim Maylis's recital to what Coralie asks for.

**2. Somebody tries the words.** The book's own lore says the words keep the thing off a sleeper until it has finished counting. Maylis knows them, and she has one night to get through. Between Tuesday and Thursday she tries it: at 2:51 she says the words and gives it a long count. It comes down and sits on her anyway, and Lesca is in the chair. In the steel room she finds out why, from Lesca, in a line: the count only decides when it takes the visitor home. It does not keep it off. 2:51 is Lesca's minute, and it comes for her wherever Maylis is. So the van is the last thing left and not the only idea. One short scene in chapter 8.

**3. Chapter 11.** Cut Kévin's "The thing that sits on you doesn't need you asleep. I was wide awake, both times." The roof bending in should be a shock.

Chapters 7, 7b, 8 and 11 together end no more than 400 words longer.

## Writer two: chapters 1, 2, 4 and 10

**4. Chapter 1: the stone has a date on it.** A reader is first afraid for somebody at the end of chapter 3; Crouch does it in ten pages. The picture Maylis wakes with is a stone with her name cut into it. Under the names there is a date, and she can read it: 12 December. The year is in the part of the picture she cannot hold. She digs the graves in this village. She knows what a name and a date on a stone mean, and she counts: eighteen days.

- She tells nobody. In chapter 2 the count is in her head once or twice, as a number of days, where she is alone.
- In chapter 4, when the two meet, Lesca tells her what the stone is: it stands in front of her town hall, and it is for a night, not a grave. For one breath that is a relief. Then Lesca says what happened on that night.
- A few lines in each place. Do not explain it further.

**5. Chapter 10: Lesca chooses, and the reader watches her do it.** At present the decision to cross on Friday is a paragraph of recollection. Her mother has told her how to keep the thing off: on her side, knees up, so that it has nowhere to sit. On Friday night Lesca lies down on her side, with the light showing from the open door across the landing. Then she turns onto her back. That movement is the scene. Nothing explains it afterwards. Cut the paragraph it replaces.

Chapters 1, 2, 4 and 10 together end no more than 250 words longer.
