# Brief: mend every place a reader went back

JC said the book was tough to follow where Harlan Coben and Blake Crouch are not. Two cold readers have now read it as he does, fluent and not native, and listed every place they had to go back up the page: `audit/reread-one.md` (50 places) and `audit/reread-two.md` (48). Each entry quotes the line, says what went wrong and gives the smallest change. Apply them.

- **Apply every entry for your chapters, from both lists.** Find each sentence by its words. Where both lists flag the same line, mend it once so that both complaints are answered. If a line is no longer there, skip it.
- **Use the reader's change, or a plainer one.** The mended line must be readable once, at speed, by somebody reading in a second language.
- **Do not tell the reader an outcome before the scene reaches it.** If a suggested change does that, find another way to make the line clear.
- **A word the reader would have to look up is replaced** by the plain word, or the sentence says the thing plainly.
- **Where the reader lost track of who is speaking, whose head it is, which village, or whose hand**, name the person or the place in that sentence.
- Change nothing else. Do not add scenes, rules or explanations beyond what an entry needs. Each chapter stays within three per cent of its length.

Everything follows `briefs/line.md` and JC's notes on reading: nobody she knows is "a man" or "a woman"; one comparison to a sentence; nothing obvious; no line built to be quoted.

Nothing you write mentions any writing tool, model, vendor or session. Do not run git.

Reply in three lines: how many entries you applied, how many you skipped and why, and the word count before and after.
