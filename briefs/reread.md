# Brief: every place you went back

Read `manuscript/00.md` to `17.md` in order, in full, at the speed you read a thriller. Open nothing else but this brief.

You are a fluent reader whose first language is not English. You read Harlan Coben and Blake Crouch in English without ever going back up the page. Each time this book makes you go back, stop and note it. Do not judge the story.

Note every place where you:

1. read a sentence or a paragraph twice to understand it;
2. went back to an earlier page to check a fact, a rule, a name or a date;
3. did not know for a moment who was speaking, whose head you were in, which village you were in, or whose hand was doing what;
4. met a word you would have to look up;
5. understood only later what a line had meant.

Write `audit/reread-<your name>.md`. For each: the chapter, the sentence quoted exactly as it stands, which of the five it was, what you thought it meant and what it turned out to mean, and the smallest change that would have let you read it once. The change must make the line plainer. It must not tell the reader an outcome before the scene reaches it.

Give the total, and the count for each chapter. If a chapter has none, say none. Do not pad, and do not list a thing you read once and merely disliked.

Nothing you write mentions any writing tool, model, vendor or session. Do not run git and do not edit the manuscript.

Reply in two lines: the total, and the three chapters with the most.
