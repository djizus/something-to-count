# Brief: the revision plan

The book is complete at 25 chapters and five critics have read it cold, each through one lens. Their reports are in `audit/critic-thriller.md`, `critic-comedy.md`, `critic-characters.md`, `critic-reader.md` and `critic-prose.md`. Read all five in full before anything else. They agree with each other, and the reviewer agrees with them.

**The verdict.** Chapters 19 to 24 are at the bar JC set, a reader of Blake Crouch and The Good Place. Chapters 1 to 13 are not, and that reader stops before chapter 8. "Give it until chapter 8" is the report.

Your job is the plan that fixes it: `revision.md`, a new outline that says, chapter by chapter, what stays, what moves, what is cut and what is written new, precisely enough that three writers can execute it without asking. You also update `bible.md` where a rule changes. You do not rewrite chapters.

## What the plan must achieve

These are requirements. How is yours.

1. **The reader has the premise, the danger and the date by the end of chapter 3.** The premise is said once in plain words by a person, in twenty words or so, and again later when it matters. The reader is given the calendar of 2014 in chapter 2 (the rain from 24 November, the readings, the twelfth), so that every reading on the other gauge is a countdown the reader can do and the women cannot.
2. **Both women know there are two of them by a third of the way in, and one knows the flood is coming there soon after.** At present those are chapters 9 and 14. The chapters this frees go to the temptation, the public slips and the plan to stay.
3. **Nothing is staged twice.** An event is played once, in the village where it costs most. The other woman gets a line of it, and does the wrong thing with it in front of people.
4. **The wrong thing in front of people is the engine.** A woman acting in her own village on the facts of the other is this book's own comedy and its own dread. Give each woman several more of these, in public, with consequences that pile up and that the village keeps count of.
5. **Every chapter ends on its turn.** Run the day first and end on the night and what it delivers, or on the thing that makes the next chapter necessary. No chapter walks on to a quiet beat.
6. **The way in is told in full three times and nowhere else.** After that it is a clause. The count of fingers stays only where somebody sees it or it turns something.
7. **There is something warm to lose.** One scene in the first three chapters in which Maylis and Coralie are good together and funny with it, and one remembered in Lesca's house. The reader has to want the pillow to cross the landing.
8. **The people behind the bank are met early**, Madame Etcheto with Maylis in a scene of her own before chapter 10. Kévin stays in the book to the last chapter, in the water on the last night, and "I'd fly" is paid.
9. **The rule of the chair is stated once as a finding, in the notebook's own form**: what happens to the one who stays, to the one she goes with, and to the bed she leaves. Each woman weighs what she would take as well as what she would leave.
10. **The two women have one scene.** On the last night the creature is in the chair, so for the first time both are standing, either side of it. Each sees the other once. The five words are said aloud by one to the other. Both still refuse, and Maylis's refusal has a reason the reader can see.
11. **The last night is three times its present length and stays in the water.** Keep every timestamp to 03:11 and "Forty-one" as the last word. Someone we are with goes down the dark corridor.
12. **The sender gets the bill.** Lesca says once, to someone, what she fears about the date the rain began there. After the last night one more thing crosses, and she stands at the stone knowing what her five words did, a metre from the old woman who is alive in her village.
13. **The man who gave the order is faced, on foot, in both villages.** The firework bull is lit and seen. Lesca's Coralie is paid on the landing. Maylis's door stays unheard.
14. **People sound like themselves.** One owner for each habit of speech. Lesca talks like a woman who won two elections; her minute on the forty houses is heard in full once, early. The secretary and the first deputy have names.
15. **A stranger can follow it at speed.** The place is laid out once, from above, early. Each office word is glossed once in passing. Each chapter names its woman in its first sentence. The creature is called Inguma once it has been named. Half the figures in the first six chapters go.
16. **The book stays between 55,000 and 60,000 words**, and may have fewer chapters than 25.

## What must not be touched

The critics' lists, section 4 of each report. Among them: the two opening sentences, the cards, the loto, the mairie counter, the chrysanthemums, Coralie in chapter 20, the ledger of Received and Removed, the flood night in 19 with the nine rings, the sandbags and the words in 21 and 22, the timestamps of 23, the kitchen clock, the gutter and the grave.

## What `revision.md` contains

1. **The new chapter list**, each with: whose it is, the date, a word target, the scenes in order, and for each scene one of *keep* (old chapter and where), *move* (from where), *cut to a line*, or *new*, with who writes it: the dark writer or the comedy writer. Then the last thing that happens in it.
2. **A list of every new scene**, with what happens, who is in it, what it must plant or pay, its register (comic, dark or held) and its length.
3. **The cuts**, by old chapter.
4. **Changes to the bible**, made in `bible.md` and listed.
5. **The tracks**, redone for the new order.
6. **A check against JC's five** (stakes, humour, interesting characters, plot and progression, intrigues) and against the sixteen requirements above, one line each.
7. **Five title candidates.** JC liked none so far. The critics' reports quote what readers remember.

## Rules

- Load the `stop-slop` skill and audit the plan. Under 6,000 words.
- Nothing you write mentions any writing tool, model, vendor or session. Do not run git and do not edit `manuscript/`.
- Reply in eight lines at most: the new chapter count and length, where the premise, the two-of-us and the flood-is-coming now fall, the new scenes in a phrase each, and anything you could not settle.
