# Brief: the third revision plan

A third round of fresh critics read the book: `audit/round3-thriller.md`, `round3-comedy.md`, `round3-characters.md`, `round3-reader.md`, `round3-prose.md`. Read all five in full.

**The verdict.** "A 7 with a 9 inside it." Chapters 14 to 22 are at the bar or over it, and the last page is above it. Chapters 5 to 13 are not, and every critic says the same thing in different words: the book has one of the best ideas in the genre, two versions of one woman loose in each other's lives, and it reaches that idea at the halfway mark and uses it for two mornings. "Crouch would have built the middle on that room."

Three rounds have moved the good part forward a few chapters at a time. This plan does it once and properly. Write `revision3.md` in the same form as `revision2.md`, working from the current `manuscript/`, and update `bible.md`.

## What this plan must achieve

1. **The swap is the middle of the book.** The first time one of them wakes in the other's body is no later than chapter 6. From there to the last night, every chapter has one of them in the wrong village for part of a day, or facing in public what the other did in hers. What now arrives as nightly deliveries and leaks (the signature, the nets, the weal, the list of slips) becomes what a woman finds she has done, said or signed while the other had her. The chapters that only receive and write things down go.
2. **The two women have scenes together, and they talk.** Not one meeting of thirty lines. From the middle of the book the room is where they stand either side of the chair and argue, as one stubborn woman argues with herself: each knows the other's evasions, and neither can lie to the other. It is funny and it is dangerous. At least three such scenes before the last night, which is the fight. Change the rule in the bible to allow it, and say what it costs.
3. **Each woman has an aim a reader can repeat, in every chapter.** Lesca's does not change four times: she is going over to stop what she started, and it escalates. Maylis has one of her own from the midpoint, and she knows a flood is coming long before the night before. Each chapter makes plain what its woman is trying to do next.
4. **The decisive acts are on the page, from inside.** Lesca's letting go is played in her own chapter. The hall with its empty chair is the third way out, it is offered to her there, and she refuses it too. Maylis chooses at the gate in words: asked all book what she wants and answering "Either", she says "Both", and goes. The rut costs her minutes; it does not make the choice.
5. **The endings cost.** What the confession does to Lesca is shown: the square, the prefect, the council, the medal. Chapter 25 does not settle every account in Maylis's favour, and Larrère does not carry the blame for both women. Of the two Coralies, one is paid in full and one is lost or leaves. No ending on a door nobody listens for.
6. **Larrère's threat is used with force**, on the page, for longer than a clause.
7. **The rule that makes the second flood hers either holds against the book's own dates or goes.** Check it against every date and reading in the text and say which.
8. **Five rules at most, said aloud.** Kévin recites them as a man with a forum behind him, or Madame Etcheto does, and they are funny. The findings in ledger language go. A reader must be able to say how the crossing works after hearing it once.
9. **Chapter 1 has the hook and the premise inside 600 words**, and stops explaining France. Glosses are few and never a second narrator.
10. **Every chapter ends on a turn**: a threat, an act or a question. The critics list the ones that close.
11. **The clock of the prose is mended.** The last pass cut "had" and broke the tense: earlier events now read as present ones. Wherever the story steps back, the pluperfect returns. This is the first item of the line pass.
12. **The line pass.** Ten people do not share one wit: give each an idiom of their own, and let people be surprised out of their habit as well as into it. The two leads are funny in themselves: Maylis short and wrong, Lesca long and certain. One pace does not serve calm and fear alike. The narrator does not keep score, recap before a payoff, or announce a feeling flat. "For some time", the stock gestures, the habit clauses and the year counts go.
13. **55,000 to 62,000 words.** The new middle is paid for out of chapters 5 to 13.

## What must not be touched

Section 4 of each round-three report. Chapter 22 from the bank going to "Forty-one", the eggs and the oven door, Madame Etcheto and what she gives it to count, the counter, the chrysanthemums, Coralie's call log, the nine rings, "You rang once", the confession at the microphone, Kévin's five fingers, the gutter and the grave.
