# Brief: the fourth plan, which is a repair and not a rebuild

A fourth round of fresh critics read the book: `audit/round4-thriller.md`, `round4-comedy.md`, `round4-characters.md`, `round4-reader.md`, `round4-prose.md`. Read all five in full.

**Where it stands.** Four rounds, three rebuilds. The people, the comedy, the switch between dark and comic and the ending now score 7 to 8 from every reader, and chapters 15 to 19 are called as good as Crouch. Two things have not moved, and one has got worse:

- **Clarity is at 4 to 5.** Each rebuild added machinery. The reader now has to work out the rules of the chair from riddles, who is driving which body from a sum at the head of each chapter, and what "the other one" refers to (62 times, changing each chapter).
- **The middle stops the clock.** After the warnings fail on 3 December, neither woman tries anything about the flood until a telephone call a week later. Chapters 10 to 14 take stock.
- **The two women never finish with each other**, and at the central choice a reader cannot tell who is choosing.

So do not rebuild again. The order of chapters stands. This plan changes as little as will fix those three, in place. Write `revision4.md`, short, and update `bible.md`.

## What the plan must contain

1. **The rules card.** Three rules at most for how the crossing works, each one sentence a child could repeat. Cut every other rule from the book, or fold it into these. Say who states them (someone who understands them, once, early, in plain words) and where each is restated just before it matters. The riddles and the forum hearsay go. If a rule cannot be said plainly, the book does not need it.
2. **Who is driving.** One plain sentence at the head of every chapter in which a woman is in the wrong body or has a passenger: who is where. And the narrator's own device does the rest: the two are Maylis and Lesca, by name, everywhere. "The other one" stays only inside a character's head or mouth where she would say it, and never changes meaning within a chapter.
3. **The middle gets a clock and a plan that fails.** For each of chapters 10 to 14, one thing a woman tries about the flood, on the page, in public: something done, not said. Each fails for a reason the reader can see, and each failure brings the night nearer or makes it worse. Larrère is what most of them fail against. The gauge is read in every one. Cut what only takes stock or retells: Lesca's Monday is told once, not six times. Every chapter in this stretch ends on a danger that has just got nearer, and the next chapter in that village picks it up.
4. **The impostor has teeth.** Once in the middle, Lesca does not give the body back when she should, and something in Maylis's life is changed by it that cannot be undone. Coralie has to choose between them, in a scene, and wants something of her own when she does.
5. **The central choice is plain.** In chapters 18 and 19 the reader always knows whose hand it is and what she is choosing between. Maylis's "Both" and Lesca's letting go are each a decision the reader watches being made, in sentences with no code in them.
6. **The two women finish with each other.** After the last night, one more meeting. Maylis tells her to her face what the five words cost. What Lesca does with that is what she carries to the stone. The chair, the hall and Inguma are each accounted for in that scene, so that a reader can say what the hall was.
7. **The opening.** The reader must know by the end of chapter 2, not chapter 4, that a flood is coming to the other village, even if neither woman does. And nobody in chapter 1 or 2 explains France to another French person.
8. **Coralie's leaving** takes more than a page and is believed.
9. **The line pass, as a list an editor can apply chapter by chapter.** No private shorthand: any sentence about the mechanics or a plan is rewritten until it can be read once. No scene's meaning stated after it. "She meant to" as the only way of pointing forward: vary or cut. The numbers that stay are 02:51, nineteen, forty houses, forty-two, forty-one, ten against twenty-five, and the gauge; every other figure is cut or made vague. Signature phrases are used once. Sentence openings and the trailing "with" clause are varied. Chapter endings do not repeat one shape. No glossary in anyone's mouth.

## What must not be touched

Section 4 of each round-four report, and everything in chapters 15 to 19 that the critics praise.

## Form

- Under 3,500 words. For each chapter: what changes, in a few lines, and "nothing but the line pass" where that is true. Then the few new scenes, with their writer and length. Then the rules card and the line-pass list, written so that they can be handed to an editor as they are.
- The book stays between 55,000 and 60,000 words. What the middle gains, it pays for with what it stops retelling.
- Load the `stop-slop` skill. Do not run git and do not edit `manuscript/`. Nothing you write mentions any writing tool, model, vendor or session.
- Reply in six lines at most: the three rules, what is tried in each of chapters 10 to 14, the new scenes, and anything you could not settle.
