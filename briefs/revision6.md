# Brief: the sixth plan, four repairs

A sixth round of critics read the cut book (17 chapters, 45,000 words): `audit/round6-*.md`. Read all five.

**The cut worked.** The sentence "hold on until chapter 15" is gone. Readers now cannot stop from chapter 10 of 17, one read chapters 10 to 14 in a sitting, and stakes and tension rose. What is left is narrower, and every critic names the same places:

- **Chapter 2 is where a Crouch reader stops.** Three critics say so in those words.
- **Chapters 7 to 9 lose the river.** A day in the other village is one move made three times, and "the whole of the campaign against the flood between chapter 7 and chapter 12" is one exchange.
- **Clarity is at 5.** At a swap the reader has to work out who is driving, who told the knees to bend, and on the eve of the night what each woman means to do.
- **Lesca's ending is paid inside twelve hours, off the page, and the reader is never let inside Maylis** at the moments she decides.

Write `revision6.md`, under 2,000 words, for editors to apply in place. No rebuild and no reordering.

1. **Chapter 2 comes down to 1,600 words or fewer and ends on the flood.** The mirrored opening, forty letters against twelve, the stone, her minute with the calendar of 2014, and then the rain she woke with and the date. By the last line of chapter 2 the reader knows a flood is coming to the other village. Say what is cut and what moves to chapter 4.
2. **Chapters 7 to 9 keep the river in sight and carry a campaign.** In every scene someone reads the gauge, or the rain, the patch or the date is in the room. Two or three things are tried about the flood, each done and not said, each failing against something the reader can see. `manuscript-v5/` chapters 10 to 14 held attempts that the cut removed (the call to Pau, the timed walk, the order for the winch part): bring back the best, at a third of their length. The days in the other village do not repeat one move: say what is different about each.
3. **A reader always knows who is where and who is doing what.** For every passage with two wills in one body, the actor is named in each sentence that has an act in it: Maylis shut the hand, Lesca opened it. At the head of chapters 12 and 13, one plain sentence each: what Maylis means to do tonight, and what Lesca means to do. List the sentences the critics could not read at once, with the plain version.
4. **The endings.** What the confession costs Lesca is on the page before anything is given back to her, and what is given back waits or costs. At "Have Friday, I'll have yours", at the gate and at the grave, the narration goes inside Maylis for a few lines: what she wants, in her own terms.
