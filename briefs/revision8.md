# Brief: the eighth plan

Round eight: `audit/round8-*.md`. Read all five. The three rules worked for the thriller reader, who now scores stakes 8, tension 7, comedy 8, characters 8, the switch 9, prose 8 and the ending 9, and says: "below Crouch from chapter 5 to chapter 10, level in 1 to 4 and 11 to 13, above in 14 to 17. Net, just under the bar." That is the nearest the book has been. What is left is specific. Write `revision8.md`, under 2,500 words, for editors to apply in place and for one writer to play the scenes it names.

1. **The first page.** Without a warning a Crouch reader still stops in chapter 1, which "spends its opening on a winch" and "asks for more than a first chapter can ask". Give the book a cold open of 250 to 350 words, before chapter 1: the night of 12 December 2014 at the gate, from the platform. The rain, the bank moving under a hand, ten minutes, a telephone taken out, and it rings once. It does not say whose number, or what stood at the end of the track; chapter 11 keeps that. Its last words can be the book's title. Then chapter 1 is trimmed so that it asks less: say what goes.
2. **The middle is played, not reported.** Critics: "half the swap is missing", "the shared body is reported, not played", "the farce is kept from the reader", "the daylight plan is dropped without a fight". The cut took scenes that were doing this work. From `manuscript-v5/`, bring back the played scenes of each woman's day in the other's village that the cut reduced to summary (the council under the timer, the car window and the road signs, the bar at the loto), each at the length it needs, and cut the narrator's summaries of them. The plan to stop the flood by daylight is fought for and lost on the page.
3. **Order at the two best cliffhangers.** Chapter 12 ends on the telephone ringing in her hand, and chapter 13 then spends thirteen hundred words on a rehearsal. The next page after that telephone is the steel room. And the single ring is told in chapter 10, by the call log, a chapter before chapter 11 shows it: reorder or redate so that the reader sees the ring before anyone reads it off a screen, without touching the words of either scene.
4. **Chapters 15 and 16 are scenes.** "The three hardest speeches are given as synopsis", and chapter 16 "settles five accounts in summary". Play them. Somebody who is not compromised accuses Lesca to her face. Larrère does not take weight that is hers.
5. **The two women's own ending comes after both endings have happened**, not before, and Inguma is still there for the confession.
6. **What a reader still cannot follow**, each with its plain replacement: the premise sentence in chapter 2 that is built from two negatives; which way the gate works, said the same in chapters 1 and 3; "price" and the other private idioms; the paragraph of the choice in chapter 14, which is "the muddiest in the book" and must be the clearest; pronouns where two share a body; the three names for two women. The narrator stops briefing the reader before each move, stops reciting the rules after chapter 5, and stops captioning echoes.
7. **Open doors.** The hall as a way out that nobody tries; the threat on Lesca's side that is never paid; the thing in chapter 7 that never goes off in chapter 12. For each: fire it or remove it.
