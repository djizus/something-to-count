Every critic now gives the same one warning: **chapters 7 to 11 ask for patience.** "9 and 11 move paper." "On Wednesday the same six men gathered on the bank at the same winch." "At the two-thirds mark the premise is switched off by the heroine herself."

Write `revision9.md`, under 1,800 words, for editors to apply in place.

1. **Chapters 7 to 11 lose a third of their length and every scene in which paper moves and nothing else does.** Keep what the critics quote with approval from those chapters, the played scenes of a woman in the wrong village, and each thing tried against the flood once. A meeting, a form or a sitting that repeats an earlier one goes. No scene in this stretch may end where it began: after each, the night is nearer, or somebody has lost something. Say, scene by scene, what stays, what goes and what each remaining scene changes.
2. **The premise is not switched off.** Where Maylis stops crossing of her own accord in the middle, either Lesca will not let her, or it costs her at once.
3. **The stakes are hers before chapter 12.** For seven chapters what Maylis stands to lose is a job. Coralie behind that bank, and the bed, are hers to lose from the first ride: one line in each chapter of the middle keeps it in front of her.
4. **The last unclear things**, each with its plain replacement: the heading of the cold open, which names the wrong date for a night that began on the eleventh; the ledger scene ending "*Nil.*", which two critics could not read and which must say in plain words that going over would add to what she carries and take nothing away; who has which hand in each passage of two in one body; any other sentence a critic quotes as unreadable.
5. **One cadence for every speaker**: three villagers get a way of talking that is not dry understatement. Say who and how, with two lines each as a guide.
