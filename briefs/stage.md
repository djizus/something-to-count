# Brief: the turn, staged

A third blind pair chose this version over the last. Round 25 then said the same thing in four reports (`audit/round25-thriller.md` fault 2, `-comedy.md` fault 1, `-reader.md` faults 6 and 9, and section 2 of the thriller and comedy reports):

- "The reversal is the right size and is not staged. Maylis has the evidence on a Saturday, writes Lesca an invitation on the Tuesday and announces the discovery on the Wednesday night."
- "It is said in two sentences in chapter 8 and nobody reacts to the second."
- "Bingo night: I do not know what the shut teeth are for."

The evidence is already on the page on Saturday the fifth, at the gauge, and that same evening the whole village is in one hall with both women in one body. Play the turn there. You are the only writer on this pass. Chapters 7 and 8, and the few lines elsewhere that depend on them.

## Chapter 7

**On the platform, Saturday afternoon, she works it out.** "Yesterday morning that iron had said a metre" is the evidence. Give her the conclusion in two plain sentences, in her head: the river stood still for the two nights she kept Lesca out, and it is raining now because Lesca is back. She has nobody to say it to and no mouth to say it with, and the one who has her mouth is the flood. The walk to the hatch follows as now.

**At the bar, the reader knows what the shut teeth are for.** One sentence as the scene opens: if Lesca cannot say the words when Inguma comes, it takes her home, and the river stops. That is what Maylis is holding the jaw for.

**She says it in front of everyone.** When the teeth come apart she takes the mouth for three words, at the bar, with the hall looking up from its cards: "You're the water." The hall makes of it what the hall would: toothache, the Castagnet business, a woman who wants water with coffee all over the bar. Josiane has a theory. Bordenave asks how to put it. Only two people in the room understand it. Lesca does, and the reader sees it reach her: the left hand stops. Madame Etcheto half does.

**Then the old woman finishes the prayer for the village's girl**, as now, and sends Inguma to count the maize until morning. One plain clause says what went with the night: another day of the river.

The landing is untouched. Lesca goes up those stairs having been told what she is.

## Chapter 8

**Sunday night, in the steel room, standing, the room confirms it.** This is the first time they are both on their feet after the bar, so it happens now and not on Wednesday. Lesca gives the answer she has had since the bar: the tide. It will not come out. They both hear that. Then she says the rule whole, as she does now: every night I cross, I bring a day of my flood with me, and Inguma has taken me home every time to keep it out of your village. **Somebody reacts to that sentence.** It is the largest thing said in the book, and Maylis has just learned that the thing she has pushed off her chest for a week was guarding her village. Give it a beat and a line, hers.

**From Sunday, Maylis is trying to keep her out, and the week is her failing at it.** The wrestle at the kitchen table, the vote, the rib that gives her one breath on Monday: each is now a try that fails, and the reader should feel them as that.

**The note is not an invitation.** She knows by Tuesday that Lesca is the river, so she does not write to hand her the gate. The sheet she writes is her own job for Friday night, the way she writes any job: the key, the winch, Larrère, the van, the hatch at twenty-five minutes, and Coralie, thirty metres from the patch. Coralie still reads it and still says "And then there's me, as a distance." Cut "Paid." Fix chapter 9 to match: on Friday morning she does not cross out a handover; she changes her own plan.

**Wednesday the ninth** keeps what is left: don't come on Friday; Lesca cannot say she won't; the plan to sit up in the van; both, and there is no both; the card.

## Elsewhere
