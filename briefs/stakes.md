# Brief: the stakes

JC read the flood night and found it weak: one man lost for forty houses does not land hard. He is right, and the text causes it. It keeps saying "forty houses", which is property. Then the ending confirms it: when the bank breaks in the other village nobody dies and carpets dry on the pavement. He has asked for two changes. Both use what the book already has.

His list of what makes a book good, for you to hold the outline against: stakes, humour, interesting characters, plot and progression, intrigues.

## A. Who was behind the bank in 2014

On the night of 12 December 2014 Coralie was on night duty at the care home, as a care assistant, below the level of the river, with forty-two residents in their beds, most of whom cannot walk. Lesca knew where she was.

- The sum Lesca gave the council and has repeated for eleven years is forty houses and a home against one man. The sum she made on the platform had one woman in it. The book never lets her say which decided her.
- This does not excuse her. She rang once and did not wait for the second ring. A private reason makes the public arithmetic a thing she has hidden behind.
- Coralie has seen the call log, as before. She also knows where she was standing that night. That is what she has not said for twelve years, and it changes what she says in chapter 20 and why she has never asked.
- From here on the text puts people in the scale. Who sleeps behind the shutters, who is in the beds. "Forty houses" stays as Lesca's phrase, the one she uses in council.

## B. The last night costs a life

In Maylis's village on the night of Friday 11 December 2026, Coralie is on night duty at the home, as the chapters already have it. Maylis walks to the tonne. She means to get him out and come back to the gate. The bank goes at the Port while she is on the track, and the home is evacuated in the dark.

- Someone the reader knows dies there. Not Coralie, and not Jeannot.
- The likeliest is Madame Etcheto, who is ninety-four and says the words to Inguma every night while the staff wait at her door. If it is her, she needs to be seen before she is lost. Chapter 7 has a resident who wins a duck at the loto and asks whether there will be one next year, and Coralie tells her there will. That can be her, by name. Find one more place.
- Tell it plainly and briefly. No melodrama, and nobody says what it means.
- So each woman's choice has cost one person. One opened the gate on a man. The other walked away from it and a woman died behind the bank. The sentence that crossed, "Go to the tonne first", was sent to save a man, and it is what put Maylis on the track. That is the fifth leak, and it is the creature's case.
- Maylis digs the graves in Peyrebarthe. Chapter 1 says she has dug seven that year and reads the stone as an eighth.
