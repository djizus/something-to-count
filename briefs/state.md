# Brief: the narrative state

A long story drifts in three ways: facts change between chapters, people act on knowledge they do not have yet, and things are planted and never paid. Re-reading the earlier chapters catches the first. It does not reliably catch the other two. What catches them is a short, typed record that is edited after every chapter and never rewritten from scratch.

You are building that record for a novel with twenty chapters merged out of twenty-five. Read `bible.md`, `outline.md` and `briefs/notes.md`, then `manuscript/01.md` to the last chapter present, in order.

## Write `state.md`

Four parts. Every entry has a short key in backticks so that later edits can name it.

**1. People.** One entry per recurring person per village, as they stand at the end of the last merged chapter. Each is a snapshot that gets overwritten, not a history: where they are, what they want now, what they know, what they have on them or in their keeping, and what state they are in. "What they know" matters most in this book. Be exact about who knows what about the van and the call, about the two women, about the creature, and about each other's secrets, and give the chapter where they learned it. For the two women, list what each has received from the other so far.

**2. Fixed by the text.** Facts the chapters have set that are not in the bible or the outline and that a later chapter could get wrong: numbers, dates, the gauge reading on each date in each village, which rib, which hand, how a thing is signed, what a room contains, exact words that have been quoted or written down, who telephoned whom and when. One line each, with the chapter.

**3. Owed.** Everything the book has planted and not yet paid: an object shown, a question asked, a promise, a threat, a running line with a pay-off due. One line each, with the chapter where it was planted and the chapter where the outline pays it, or "nowhere yet". An entry is deleted when it is paid.

**4. Contradictions.** Anything in chapters 1 to 20 that disagrees with another chapter, with the bible, or with what a person could know at that point. Quote both places. Sort them as facts, timeline, knowledge, or threads. Chapters 1 to 15 were revised in one fast pass yesterday, so look hardest at the seams that pass left: a thing called by its old name, a town that moved, a scene that still assumes what was cut.

## Rules

- Under 3,000 words. A state that is as long as the book is no use.
- Do not edit the manuscript, the bible or the outline. List what should change and where.
- Nothing you write mentions any writing tool, model, vendor or session. Do not run git.
- Reply in five lines at most: how many contradictions of each kind, the three worst, and how many things are owed with nowhere yet to be paid.
