# Brief: verbs that read as the wrong tense

JC reads English fluently as a second language. He stopped at "Lesca lay on her back in a bed that wasn't hers and listened to the rain on the roof" and asked whether the tenses were wrong. They were right: "lay" is the past of "lie". But it looks like the present of "to lay", so the sentence seemed to change tense in the middle. That is the standard for this read: **a verb that is correct and that a fluent reader could take for another tense, or for another word.**

Read your chapters of `manuscript/` in full, slowly, one verb at a time. Do not judge the story, the style or the jokes. The book is told in the past tense.

List every instance of:

1. **"lay" and "lain" as the past of "lie".** Every one, for a person or a thing ("She lay still", "had lain", "The pad lay on the table"). Do not list "lay" where it is the present or the base form of "to lay" ("They lay a place for her", "will lay the flowers").
2. **A past form that looks like a present or like another word.** A past tense spelled like its present, where the subject does not settle it ("they put", "you read", "I let", "they shut", "we cut", "'…,' she read"). A past form that is also a common noun or another verb ("wound" for a window or a watch, "found", "saw", "fell", "rose", "ground", "bore", "left" beside the direction left, "lit", "wind"). List one only if a reader could really take it the wrong way where it stands.
3. **A tense that changes inside a sentence or between two sentences with no reason a reader can see**: a present among pasts, a simple past where the event came before the scene and needs "had", a "had" that goes on for a whole paragraph after the first one has done its work, a "had had".
4. **"would" that could be read two ways**: a habit in the past ("he would come up that lane"), the future seen from the past ("Inguma would come for Lesca tonight"), or a condition. List it when the reader cannot tell which at once.
5. **A bare verb that looks like a missing "s" or a missing "ed"** outside a formal motion. The motions ("That the mayor remain seated") are deliberate and stay.
6. **Any other verb form you had to read twice** to place in time: who did it, and whether it is happening now, happened before, or is only expected.

For each: the chapter, the sentence quoted exactly as it stands, the kind, and the corrected sentence written out in full. The correction uses the plainest form that cannot be misread:

- for a person, "was lying", "stayed still", "kept still", "didn't move", "got into bed", "got down on the floor", as the sentence needs;
- for a thing, "was" or "were" ("The pad was on the table");
- otherwise the simplest verb that says the same thing.

Keep the plain style: short sentences, common words, no two sentences joined by ", and" unless they are parts of one action. Do not change what happens. Do not list speech that is deliberately broken (Maïté breaks off; Sarthou does not contract).

Write `audit/tense-<your name>.md`. Give the count for each chapter and for each kind. Do not pad: for kinds 2 to 6, list only what could really stop a reader.

Nothing you write mentions any writing tool, model, vendor or session. Do not run git and do not edit the manuscript.

Reply in two lines: the total, and how many of each kind.
