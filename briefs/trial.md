# Brief: the voice trial

Read `PLAN.md` and `bible.md` first. Two writers are each writing the same opening chapter in a different tone, and JC will pick one. You have been told which tone is yours. Do not read the other writer's file.

## The chapter

Beat 1 of section 7 in the bible: the protagonist wakes pinned, having seen her own name cut in stone. Later that day she signs for the forty-first registered letter and hears the box joke. It rains.

- 2,000 to 2,500 words, in British spelling.
- Third person, following her. Tense is your choice.
- This is the chapter a stranger reads before deciding whether the book is worth anything. The first page has to make them read the second. No prologue.
- Do not explain the experiment, the split, the other one or the figure. The reader gets what she gets: a weight, a picture of a stone, a day at work, a joke she has heard for twelve years.
- Introduce no more than four villagers, and let each be recognisable by what they do, not by a description.
- French words only where English has none (barthe, tonne, mairie), and clear from context the first time.
- End the chapter on something that happens, not on a thought.

## The two tones

**Deadpan.** The narrator reports the village's procedures and grudges with a straight face and exact detail, as a council minute would if it could notice things. The humour comes from situation, precision and understatement. No jokes, no puns, no winks, and the narrator never remarks that anything is absurd. The fear in the waking scene is real and is not played for laughs. Underneath, this is a woman nobody asks.

**Dark.** The same events with no comedy. Stay close to her and her day: the weight, the rib, the cold, the work, the people who speak to her through car windows. The village is pressure. Nothing is softened and nothing is heightened.

## Rules

- Load the `stop-slop` skill before you write, and audit the chapter against it before you finish.
- Concrete over abstract. Every paragraph has something in it that could be photographed or overheard.
- No dream-sequence furniture: no mist, no echoing voices, no falling. The waking scene is a body that will not move in a known room.
- The text never names the trolley problem or any philosopher.
- Follow the bible's rules for writing her, to the letter.
- Nothing you write into this directory mentions any writing tool, model, vendor or session.
- Write your one file only: `trial/deadpan.md` or `trial/dark.md`. Do not run git and do not edit the bible. If the bible is wrong or silent on something you need, decide, and list it in your reply.
- Reply in three lines at most: the word count, what you decided that the bible did not, and the one thing you are least sure of.
