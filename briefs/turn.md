# Brief: the turn

Two readers compared this version with the one from three rounds ago, blind, and both chose this one. Both then said the same thing about it, and so did the thriller reader of round 23 (`audit/round23-thriller.md`, fault 1; `audit/pair/first.md` and `second.md`, section 7):

- "Nobody tries to cut the wire." If the flood comes across the link, why do they not stop?
- "Nothing pulls the floor out. A Crouch novel and The Good Place each turn into a different story at least once. This one pays exactly what it promised."

The book already owns the turn and gives it away as an agenda item in chapter 4. This pass takes it back and plays it. You are the only writer on this pass, in every chapter it touches, so that it is one thread in one hand. Chapters run 00 to 16.

## What is true, and when the reader learns it

**The river comes only because Lesca comes.** Every night the two of them share the chair, a day of her 2014 goes across. Inguma has been fetching her home each time because it guards that border. The monster has a case, and it was right.

Nobody knows this in chapter 4. The reader gets it at the same moment the women do, in chapter 8, and from there the book is a different story: the woman who came to warn the village is the flood, and she knows it, and on Friday she comes anyway.

## The thread, chapter by chapter

**4. Nobody has the answer.** Maylis asks why a flood would come now, after a dry 2014. Lesca begins the answer she has ready, aloud, so the reader hears what it is: the neglected bank, Larrère, the winch and three letters in the minutes. It dies in her mouth. She finds she cannot say it, and says so, and that becomes a point of the meeting as plain as the others: **standing in that room, a woman can only say what is true.** She does not know the true answer, and that she can say.
- Cut "The flood is the biggest thing I have… one day of my file at a time", "And I get your river", "I want somebody to blame" and "Nobody was to blame for a dry one."
- Maylis says: then stop coming. Lesca cannot: it has sat on her every night for twelve years and she has never once kept it off. Keeping out of the chair is something only Maylis can do, by pushing, and her rib decides how long.

**7. She cuts the wire, and it works.** Stage the two nights as a short scene: Maylis on her side through the rib, or on the kitchen floor, pushing, awake until four. She does it for her bed and for Coralie, not for the river. On the Friday morning, the fourth, she stands at the gauge with the figure from Lesca's file in her head, seventy under the line. The gauge has not moved since Wednesday, and it has not rained for two days.
- In the café Lafitte has the pad, with what "the mayor in her" said the river would read. It is wrong for the first time. Somebody enjoys that.
- That night the rib gives. On Saturday morning Inguma comes, the arms of the chair are warm, and by Saturday noon it is raining. On Saturday the fifth the gauge reads fifty-five under: it has caught up with the file in a day.
- Cut "The river gauge kept to the file anyway."
- Maylis sees it. She says nothing to anyone yet. The reader may be ahead of her.

**8. The vote, and then the turn.**
- At the vote Jeannot has the pad on his side: she said seventy for the Friday and it read a metre, so he goes by the gauge. Keeping Lesca out for two nights is what cost Maylis the village's belief. Nobody says that; it is there.
- **In the steel room, standing, Maylis puts it to her:** the river stood still for the two nights you were kept out, and it rained the day you came back. Lesca tries to say it is the tide. She cannot say it. They both hear that she cannot. "You got my rib," Maylis says. "And I get your river."
- Then Maylis says: don't come on Friday. Lesca tries to say she won't. She cannot say that either. ("I can't stand here and say I'll go" is already hers; this is where it bites.)
- Maylis says both; Lesca says there is no both, as now. The scene keeps its present length by losing procedure.
- The card with "LESCA, MAYLIS." still ends the chapter.

**10. She knows, and she goes.** In her own village Lesca can lie to herself, and does, in the way she always has: the count comes out for her. The river is over the line already. Somebody should be at that gate who has done the night before. One or two lines. When she tells her mother she is going to sit on it, the reader knows what she is bringing with her.

**11. Staying out of the chair is now the stake.** At five, after "Either" and "Then it's shut", the two sentences are: if the gate went up now and the other woman never came, the flood on the meadows was Maylis's alone; at three, if it came, it would be the river's doing. In the van at night she is not hoping: if Inguma does not get her into that chair before the tide turns at four, nobody crosses and the bank holds. She has no push left in the rib, so she stays upright and awake and counts her fingers. At nine minutes to three it lands on the roof, and she has lost.

**12. The van.** Maylis says it to her once, in the fight: you brought it. Before Lesca's five words Maylis never says where she will drive, only that she will drive herself. So "Go to the blind first" still gives her the one thing she did not have: the order to do it in. One plain sentence, where the fight starts, says what each is fighting for. Lesca lets go directly after the word that does not come, the "Either" this mouth has always put after everything, and one plain sentence says that is why. Eight rings were long enough for her night to come through; say that in place of whatever now explains "long enough".

**13.** The bank goes at eleven minutes past three, the minute in her file. One clause.

**15.** To the living Madame Etcheto, Lesca says the order of it in one line: the place was laid because she held on for eight rings; who filled it was settled on a track, by another woman. "It was you" stays.

**16. They cut it for good.** "It was your river" is now a charge with twelve chapters behind it, and Lesca takes it. She adds the one thing she has not owned: she sent Maylis down the track to do what she had not done herself, and it was her own night she was mending. Then Maylis says: don't come again. Lesca says she won't. **This time she can say it.** Inguma goes out over the sill. Neither sits. Nothing else is added to the scene; cut procedure to make the room.

## Two repairs on the way

- **3.** Keep the order of the chapter. Cut about 800 words from the committee, the roof and the soup, keeping "It reads that she opened him", Maïté's verb cut off by the bell, and "It hadn't had it yet". Break the paragraph that begins "On Thursday Lesca went home" into two short staged scenes: the chair and the card; then the file open on the desk, read line by line until the handwriting turns into typing. A reader had to read that page twice.
- **5.** When Lesca first stands Maylis on the platform, one line of speech says what the gate does and why the river on the meadows is not the river in the forty houses.
