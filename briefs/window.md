# Brief: a continuity check on one window of the book

A reader who checks a whole novel for contradictions misses more of them the longer the book is. So the book is checked in four windows of about 10,000 words, each against the same record of what the whole book has fixed.

You are given one window. Read `state.md` parts 1 to 4 first (people, fixed facts, the calendar for each village, what is owed). Do not read part 5. Then read the chapters of your window in `manuscript/`, slowly. You may open any other chapter to check a single fact, but your report is about your window.

Check every sentence of your window against the state and against the rest of the window. List every place where two things cannot both be true, under these five heads:

1. **People.** A person acts against what they want or know at that point; knows a thing before they could have learned it, or has forgotten a thing they were told; has a thing on them they gave away, or lacks one they are carrying; is in two places; has the wrong age, job, relation, injury or hand. In this book the hands matter: in Maylis's body Maylis keeps the right hand and the visitor has the rest, and in Lesca's body it is the other way round.
2. **Facts.** A number, a count, a distance, a price, a name, a spelling of a name, a colour, what a room or a vehicle contains, the wording of something quoted or written down that comes back in different words.
3. **Time.** A date against its day of the week; a clock time against the one before it; how long a thing took against the times given; the order of two events; how many days or nights since something; the weather and the level of the river on that date in that village.
4. **The world.** How the crossing, the chair, the steel room, Inguma and its counting, and the hall have been seen to work, against what happens in your window. Also plain physical sense: where a vehicle is pointing, which side the river is on, whether a light was switched on before it shines, whether a door was left open or shut.
5. **The telling.** Whose head the chapter is in; a thing the narrator states that the person whose head it is cannot know; a village named or described as the other one.

For each: the chapter, the sentence quoted exactly, what it disagrees with (the state entry by its key, or the other sentence quoted with its chapter), and the smallest change that mends it, saying which place should change. If an entry in `state.md` is itself wrong about your window, say so and quote the text.

Then, under "Owed": anything planted in your window that `state.md` part 4 does not list as paid or as deliberately left open.

Do not list a thing that is only odd, a matter of taste, or a question the book plainly leaves open on purpose. Do not pad. If a head has nothing under it, say none.

Write `audit/window-<your name>.md`. Nothing you write mentions any writing tool, model, vendor or session. Do not run git and do not edit the manuscript.

Reply in two lines: the count under each head, and the worst one.
