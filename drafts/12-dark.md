# 12

On Thursday at six Lesca wrote the night down with the notebook over her face, and for the first time she did not know what heading to put on an item.

*Night of Wednesday 2 to Thursday 3 December. Before moving. Went in awake, 04:28. Item delivered twice, complete.*

*1. A kitchen, mine, with a different oilcloth. M-J. Labat at the table with the electoral code open. She reads out that an employee of a commune cannot sit on its council. She wants me third on a list of fifteen, against G. Larrère. February 2020.*

*2. The seat of the mower. A telephone going in my pocket at four, and again at five. I let it ring. Six o'clock goes by.*

*3. K. Labat counting notes onto a dashboard, tens and twenties, two hundred. One finger pushes them back. Then again, a fifty, pushed back the same way.*

She had gone in awake on three nights since Monday, and had delivered the seventh of February each time. Nothing had come back to say that it was received. What came back was this.

She read the three down. In February 2020 she had filed her own list at the sub-prefecture on the first morning it was open, with fifteen names typed and the receipt stamped by nine. She had never let a date go by in her life. The other one had sat on a mower and listened to a telephone until a list closed.

So Guy Larrère was mayor over there. That was who signed his post in front of a parking space with no stone in it.

She looked longest at the first entry. Marie-Jo was sitting in that kitchen with her coat on the back of the chair. She had not been in Lesca's kitchen since June 2018. Over there she had walked in in 2020 and asked for something, and on Saturday night she had stood at a trestle counting coins and said that it was the one thing she had ever asked.

Coralie had left for the home. Her door was open and her bed made. Lesca counted her fingers and got up. She had been getting up at four for five nights, and at six she was more tired than when she lay down.

* * *

She went into the café at noon because she had not eaten and there were six hours to the budget.

The agenda for the finance committee was pinned to the mairie door, and the café had read it. Line 7472 was the Natura grant, sixty thousand euros, for renewal. Four men were at the table by the stove with the cards face down, and Kévin Labat was at the counter in the yellow waterproof, with a coffee, on his dinner hour.

'Sixty thousand a year,' one of the four was saying. 'That's the school bus and the changing rooms. I'm only saying what it bought.'

'And what it cost. A boy from here in Mont-de-Marsan for six months, over a bucket of worms.'

'Four hundred euros a kilo, your worms.'

'She could have had a word with him. Her father would have had a word. You don't put a camera on your own.'

'There were five of them at it. She couldn't have a word with a hood.'

'She was always hard.'

They did not lower their voices when she came in. They had been saying it since 2022, and it was understood that the mayor could hear and that the boy could hear, and that it was said to neither. The café did not argue about Jean Castagnet. It stood for a minute on the twelfth with its caps off, and that was settled. It argued about the cameras. Kévin looked into his cup.

She asked for a coffee and a sandwich and stood at the far end of the zinc. It was where the hand had lain. She had it in the notebook from the Friday before, a left hand flat on this counter and a man in waders beside it with a glass of white. The owner wiped the place with a cloth and set her cup on it. No glass stood there.

The church clock struck twelve. She touched off the left hand and then the right, on the zinc, and made ten.

'All back, Madame le maire?' said one of the four.

'Ten.'

'The box was honest, then.'

The table laughed, as far as it dared. Kévin did not. He turned his cup round in its saucer.

'It was my face on the film,' he said, to the table. 'I looked up. Nobody made me look up.'

'You've her jacket for it.'

'I've a wage for it.'

He paid and went past her to the door. She had the third entry in front of her as plainly as the price list. There were notes on a dashboard, and a finger.

'Kévin. In 2022. Did you ever think of offering me money? To leave a gate be.'

He stopped with his hand on the door. The four men had gone quiet.

'I thought of it,' he said. 'Two hundred a night. I had it worked out. Then the poles went up, and there was nobody to offer it to. You can't put two hundred euros on a camera.'

'I'd have pushed it back.'

'You'd have filmed me holding it out,' Kévin said. He did not say it with any heat. He said it as he had said that it was a good picture.

Somewhere he had counted it onto a dashboard and had it pushed back, and nobody had telephoned the men in green. He had not been to Mont-de-Marsan there. He drove to Irun on the night tides with two blue boxes. And the woman who let him was not called hard for it at any counter.

She left the sandwich on the counter with one bite out of it.

* * *

The finance committee sat at half past six in the council room, seven members and the secretary, with the radiators on.

The preliminary budget for 2027 ran to four pages. The secretary read each line of the operating section aloud, with its number and its figure, and the chair asked whether there were observations. It was Lesca's procedure. She had brought it in in 2015, when it was found that under her predecessor the lines had been voted in blocks of forty.

'Line 60612, energy and electricity, forty-one thousand two hundred.'

'Observations?'

There were none. The secretary read on. Her voice was level and the room was warm. Line 6135 was the hire of the marquee. Line 6156 was maintenance, and under it the motors on the three gates, four thousand two hundred. Lesca had her pen on the margin beside each figure as it went by. She heard 6156. She did not hear the next. The member on her left moved the water jug away from her elbow, quietly, and nobody at the table looked at anybody else.

The church clock woke her. It was striking seven, and she owed it her hands.

Her chin was on her chest and her pen had run off the page onto the table. She brought her head up. By the clock over the door she had been gone eleven minutes, in a lit room, in the chair, and nothing had come to her in them at all. Six faces were turned to her, and none of them had its hand raised. The secretary was writing. The first thing she did was count, the left and then the right, and she made ten with them all watching.

'What line are we at?'

'Line 6232, festivities and ceremonies,' the secretary said. She did not look up. 'As amended.'

'Amended by whom?'

'By me,' Guy Larrère said.

He had his hands folded on the table. There was no timer in front of him. It had not been set.

'I asked for the floor at nine minutes to seven,' he said. 'The chair made no objection. I moved that six thousand six hundred go to the bull and three thousand to the fête in July, in place of nine thousand and six hundred. I was brief. I was under a minute, and the secretary will confirm it. Then I asked whether the chair wished to put it. The chair made no objection to that either. So it was put.'

'By a member.'

'By the senior member present, the chair being unable. It's in the code.' He looked round the table. 'We didn't care to wake you, Madame le maire. We thought you were still in the box.'

Two of them smiled at their papers. The one who had always voted with her was looking at the radiator.

'The count?'

'Five for, one against,' the secretary said. 'The chair present and not voting.'

In eleven years nothing had been carried in that room that Lesca had not put. She knew the article he meant. It did not cover a chair who was asleep, and she could have had the vote struck out in the time it took to say so.

She looked at the page. Someone had ruled through her two figures in pencil and written the others beside them. Three thousand euros hired a band for two nights and a firm to light a bull that worked. It took five minutes off the display on the twelfth, and the bar would not notice five minutes.

It was also the only thing that had passed between the mairie and Marie-Jo Labat in eight years that Marie-Jo would have asked for.

'Let it stand,' she said.

'Madame le maire,' said the one by the radiator, low. 'You've only to say.'

'I've said. Line 6247.'

Larrère unfolded his hands. He had come to that table for eleven years to lose, and he did not seem to know what to do with them.

The committee rose at half past eight, to the minute. She signed the sheet, with the flourish, under a line that recorded her as not voting.

* * *

The allocation went to the president of the fête committee by post, as a rule, on the Monday following. Lesca had the secretary type it that evening and took it herself.

Marie-Jo Labat lived on the terrace, in the second house past the hall, with a porch lamp and two steps up to the door. Lesca had last been inside in June 2018, on the afternoon before the sitting. It was a quarter to nine and freezing. She walked up from the mairie past the hall, which was dark, with the fête's trestles stacked inside it for the fifth. She rang.

The door opened as far as Marie-Jo's shoulder. She had her apron on and a tea towel in her hand, and behind her the kitchen was lit and the wireless going. Lesca could see the end of the table. She had sat at it through the spring of 2015 folding leaflets, and for three years after it with the commune's accounts, and the clipboard still hung from its nail beside the dresser. There was no chair on a step, and no son. She looked at the wall of the house opposite.

'Madame le maire.'

'It's the fête's allocation for 2027. Three thousand.'

'I know what it is. Guy rang at twenty to eight. Five to one, and you asleep.' She did not take the file. 'He says you could have had it struck out.'

'I could.'

'And you let it stand.'

'Yes.'

'Then it's his three thousand, and you've walked it up here as if it was yours.'

Lesca stood with the file held out. The cold came through her shoes. She had the first entry by heart, with the oilcloth and the open code, and the voice that had asked.

'May I come in?'

'No.'

'I want to ask you one thing. If you'd come to my kitchen in 2020 and asked me to stand. Third on your list. And I'd said I'd think about it, and let the date go by. Would you be speaking to me now?'

Marie-Jo looked at her then, directly, for the first time since the dyke. She looked for about as long as it takes to read a figure, and then longer.

'What sort of a question is that? You never let a date go by. You asked me, in March 2015, in that kitchen behind me, and you'd said yes for both of us before I had my coat off.'

'If I had.'

'I'd have gone on at you for six years. I'd have brought it up at every loto.' She folded the tea towel over her arm. 'You don't stop speaking to a person for doing nothing. You stop when they've done something.'

She had almost used the name. Lesca saw it come as far as her mouth and be shut in.

'You count your fingers,' she said, to the wall again. 'Kévin says it's to be sure you're here. Well, you're here. You're on my step at nine at night with Guy Larrère's money.'

She put out her hand and took the file. Then she stepped back into the lit kitchen, and shut the door.
