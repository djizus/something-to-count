Lesca answered him, as she had at that table each December since 2015. Marie-Jo Labat turned the white timer to one minute and stood it between them. The mayor had not once needed more.

'It began to rain on the twenty-fourth of November 2014,' Lesca said, 'and it did not stop. On the third of December the river stood a metre under the red line, which is the height of the doorsteps in the Port. On the eighth it was at the line. On the night of the eleventh it was ninety centimetres over and rising, and that was the nineteenth day of rain. The old bank was coming through beside the last house. I put my hand on it and gave it ten minutes. Behind it were forty houses, and the home, with forty-two people in their beds. Out on the meadows there was one man. A door in a bank is opened from the platform, Guy, and I was the one on the platform. I opened it. The river went into three hundred hectares and came off the bank, and nobody behind it so much as woke.'

The timer still had some seconds to run. Maïté Castagnet kept her eyes on the radiator. It was the sum the mayor had given each year, in the same order, and in all that time nobody at the table had asked to see her working.
