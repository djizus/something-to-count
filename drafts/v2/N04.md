From there she had it all, top to bottom.

Below her the Port went down in three streets to the river, and at the end of the last, under the bank, lay the long roof of the care home. The dyke ran along the foot of it all, between the houses and the Adour, which was tidal this far up and rose against it twice a day.

One door went through the bank, the gate, a little past the last house. Behind the gate the ground dropped by the height of a man to the low meadows, a kilometre across, with one track out to the tonnes. Shut, the gate kept the river leaning on the dyke. Opened on a high river, it let the water lie down in the meadows, lower than any doorstep in the Port.

There were no low meadows.
