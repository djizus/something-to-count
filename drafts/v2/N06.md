Lesca looked at her in the doorway, with her hand on the frame, and for a moment had her in another one.

It was the door of the tobacco barn, on a Thursday in June 2014, at five in the afternoon. The university people had let her out after three hours, paid her and gone back in to their machines. Coralie was sitting on the low wall in the sun with her tunic unbuttoned at the neck. She had come straight off a shift, and she had brought a sandwich in a paper bag.

'They gave me one,' Lesca had said.

'Then this is the second.' Coralie had squinted up at her. 'Well? The woman told me what it was for, while you were in there. One lamp comes on and you see it, and they want to know if there's another of you somewhere who saw the other. So are there two of you now?'

'They opened the door and counted one.'

'That's a pity. I'd have taken both. One for the commune, and one for the washing-up.' She had torn the sandwich across and held out the larger half. 'Sit down, Maylis. You look as if you'd been kept in after school.'

She had sat down. They had eaten it on the wall, and Coralie had kept her shoulder against hers until the bag was empty.

In the doorway Coralie took her hand off the frame.
