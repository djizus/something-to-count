On Monday at a quarter past nine the mayor of Peyrebarthe was raking the drain gratings by the church, in her good coat.

Lesca had left her car where she left it each morning and started across the square with her folder. The commune's rake stood against the church wall, where Kévin Labat had left it on Friday. She had it in her hands before she reached the porch, and she was at the second grating before she felt the weight of it. She raked left-handed, with her right elbow held in against her side. Nothing was wrong with that side.

It had been dry for a fortnight, and there was frost in the slots. She raked the bare iron clean.

The café had its lights on. Four men stood at the window with their cups, and the owner behind them. For eleven years they had seen the mayor cross that square at ten past eight with a folder under her arm. They watched her do the third grating, and one of them went to the door to hear.

Kévin came round from the cemetery side with the blower on his back, in the commune's yellow waterproof, and stopped at the kerb.

'Madame le maire.'

'They block,' Lesca said. She had not been asked. 'If they're left while the planes are still dropping, the water stands in the square and gets in under the mairie door. I've seen it over the step. The council was written to about the fall of that gutter three times and took note of it three times, and somebody has to keep them clear till the leaves are down.'

'What water?'

She looked down at the iron.

'It's not rained since the middle of the month,' Kévin said. 'And I did those on Friday.' He shrugged the blower higher and held out his hand, mildly. 'You gave me the job. If you're doing it, one of us is paid for nothing, and I've tyres to buy.'

She gave him the rake. He stood it against the nearest plane.

'You hold your side when you rake,' he said. 'You didn't on Friday.'

'It's a habit.'

'It's a new one.' He shrugged. 'It's nothing to me. I looked up at a camera nine nights running.'

In the café window a man lifted his cup to her. Guy Larrère was beside him, with his coat on.

She crossed to the mairie and had her key in the door when Kévin called after her. He was coming over the square in the yellow waterproof with her folder held out in front of him.

'You'll want this,' he said. 'It was where my rake was.'
