# N8, chapter 8, comic. Joins: after N7, before 14a. It carries "Thank you, Guy" from 10e, and the ledger speech is not played.

* * *

On Tuesday the council of Peyrebarthe sat at half past six, and Lesca took the chair.

Correspondence came first. Madame Ducasse, the mairie's secretary, read the list. There was the prefecture, about the twelfth, and Hontaube, asking again to open talks, and the firm at Anglet, about the bull.

'And Monsieur Castagnet's letter,' Lesca said. 'It was on top, in the tray. Has that been answered?'

Madame Ducasse held her pen where it was.

'Not since 2014, Madame le maire.'

The table was quiet. Lesca heard it then, and went on, which made it worse.

'I mean the one in the file. I had the file out last week. A letter in a file is a letter received, and the commune had two months to answer it. I'm asking whether it was ever closed.'

'There was nobody to send it to,' Madame Ducasse said.

'I ask that that be minuted,' Guy Larrère said. 'The chair's question, and the answer. They tell me the chair raked the square on Monday, in her coat. I can't have that minuted, since it wasn't done at a sitting. This was.'

'He's within the rule,' Lesca said. 'Minute it.'

On the gates contract he took his three minutes. He spent them as he had at each December sitting, on what a gate is, and the timer rang on him in the middle of the association.

For eleven years she had answered him, and it had taken her under a minute. That evening she had a man in her head who stood at the door of a white van in the first light and told a woman with a chain that her father would have hooked it from the other side.

'Thank you, Guy,' she said.

He looked round the table to see who had heard it. Nobody had thanked him in that room since April 2015.

'You've no reply?'

'No. It was clearly put. I've heard it eleven times, and tonight I've nothing against it.'

'Then I ask that that be minuted as well. The thanks.'

'In what words?' said Madame Ducasse.

'In hers.'

Madame Ducasse wrote that the chair had thanked Monsieur Larrère. He asked for the register before she had blotted it, and read both entries through, twice, with his finger under the lines.
