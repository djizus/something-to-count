# N9, chapter 9, comic. Joins: after the three lines of the night's delivery, before 11a.

* * *

That morning Maylis put her back against the old dyke, at the end of the Port, and walked away from it counting.

She went through Sarthou's gate, past his privet and over his cabbages. She went through the gap into old Lafitte's, and across the Darrigrands' drying green under a line of small clothes. At sixty paces she was in the fourth garden, beside a rabbit hutch, and she stopped and turned round.

That was where it ran. In the night she had stood on a grass bank where these four houses were, with a bench on it.

Sarthou had come out with his shears. The Darrigrands were at their back door, she with the baby on her arm, and Lafitte leaned on his fence.

'You've walked on my cabbages,' Sarthou said.

'The bench goes there.'

'What bench?'

'On the top of the bank. It thanks the State.'

Sarthou looked at his cabbages.

'Is this the commune?' Darrigrand said. 'Are we being bought out?'

'At the State's valuation.'

'Is that good?' said his wife. 'We'd go. It's damp to the windowsills, and she sleeps on the ground floor.' She meant the baby.

'When?'

'In 2016,' Maylis said.

Nobody spoke. It was the second of December 2026, and it was raining on all of them.

'I've no file on a bench,' Sarthou said, 'and I'm not opening one. I've your statement about a telephone. That's all I want with your name on it before the eighteenth.'

Lafitte had the café's score pad out of his coat, and the pencil. He turned to the back of it.

'I'll start it properly,' he said. 'Fingers, counted at the café. A prefect's signature, that Larrère has had round the counter. And now four gardens paced for a bank, and a bench on the gendarme's cabbages.' He looked up. 'Which way does it face?'

'The river.'

He wrote that as well.

Maylis walked back along her own line to the foot of the old dyke. The clay patch on its face had gone dark with the wet. Behind her the Darrigrands were still at their door, in case anybody came with a price.
