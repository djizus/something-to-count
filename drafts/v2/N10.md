That evening the gauge stood a metre five under her father's line.

Maylis went in at twenty-five past four, by Kévin's method. What she was given that night was a walk.

She was going down through the Port on foot, in frost, with a folder under her arm. It was the Port she knew, street for street, and it was in better repair. The shutters were open, and four fronts had new paint.

On the wall of the first house, at the height of her head, there was a plate. It was cast iron, the size of a hand, screwed to the render. A line ran across the middle of it, and under the line was a date. She walked on. There was another by the front door of the home, at the same height, and a third on Sarthou's gatepost. She counted them down to the dyke and made eleven. Each had the same line at the same height, level with her eyes, as if a string had been pulled tight through the whole quarter.

She stopped at the last and read it.

*12 XII 2014. ADOUR.*

The line was where the river had stood. It was above every doorstep in the Port by the height of a woman.

She came out at six with her mother's light on the ceiling, and wrote before she moved. The river had not been near that line in her lifetime. On the pad she put what she took it to be, a flood that had gone to the other village and passed her own.

*12 December 2014. It never came here.*
