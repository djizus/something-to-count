# N11, chapter 10, comic. Joins: as the chapter's first scene, in place of 12a. The narration stays in the room while she sleeps.

* * *

On Thursday the mayor of Peyrebarthe fell asleep in the chair during the budget, and the finance committee, after some discussion, went on without her.

It had sat down at half past nine, four members with Lesca presiding, and Madame Ducasse was reading the draft aloud by chapter and line. At water the mayor asked a question. At telephone her pencil rolled off the table, and she let it go.

Madame Ducasse read two lines more and stopped.

'Madame le maire?' said Monsieur Tastet, the first deputy. He said it as a man tries a door he hopes is locked.

The mayor sat upright with her hands on the draft and her eyes shut. She had been up at four on four mornings running, which the committee did not know.

'Wake her,' said the member on her left.

'You never wake them. They can strike out.'

'That's sleepwalkers. She isn't walking.'

'She's the chair,' Tastet said. 'I'm not laying a hand on the chair.'

Madame Ducasse had the code down from the cupboard. She found the place with her finger.

'A mayor who is absent, suspended or otherwise prevented is replaced by a deputy,' she read, 'in the order of the list.'

'Is she absent?' Guy Larrère said.

'She's there.'

'Suspended?'

'No.'

'Then she's prevented. Plainly. They tell me she keeps late hours now, with her fingers.' He took off his watch and laid it on the table. 'The code gives no time. I'd call four minutes decent.'

They gave her four minutes. The radiator ticked. Madame Ducasse kept her finger in the code, Tastet watched the mayor breathe, and the member on her left moved the water jug out of reach of her elbow.

'Four,' Larrère said. 'Monsieur Tastet, you have the chair.'

'I haven't. She's in it.'

'You have its powers. Go on from telephone.'

Tastet looked at the mayor for some time. Then he nodded to Madame Ducasse, who read telephone, and postage, and came to festivities.

'On that line I've an amendment,' Larrère said.

With nobody awake to time him he took forty seconds. The fête of July went from six hundred euros to three thousand, and the difference came off the bull.

'Is that in order?' said the fourth member. 'With her sat there?'

'It's been put,' Tastet said, unhappily. 'When it's put, you vote. Those in favour.'

Four hands went up, and his own was the last. Madame Ducasse wrote that the chair had not taken part.

'Carried,' Tastet said.

Lesca opened her eyes.

'Carried what?'

Madame Ducasse turned the register round, and the mayor read it for herself. The clock on the wall said twenty-four minutes to eleven. She had been gone twenty-two.

'We didn't like to wake you,' Larrère said.

'The chair was in the room,' Lesca said. 'A chair that is in the room is present. A present chair is not prevented, whatever it is doing, and a vote taken round it is a vote taken at a table with nobody presiding. It's struck out. I'll put the line again as drafted, and I'll speak to it.'

She spoke to it for under a minute, and it carried. Two of the four had found, with the mayor awake, that they agreed with her.

'Go on from festivities,' she said.

Larrère had come to that table for eleven years to lose. For twenty-two minutes that morning he had won. He put his watch back on, and asked that the twenty-two minutes be minuted.
