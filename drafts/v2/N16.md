'It isn't a statement,' Lafitte said. 'It's a list. You can have it read.'

'Counts her fingers on the hour, at this counter and at the loto bar. Signs for a parcel like a prefect, with a line under. Tells the mayor he has three minutes. Paces a bank through four gardens and puts a bench on the gendarme's cabbages. Sits in the mayor's chair with the mayor's pen in her teeth. And a pot of chrysanthemums, bronze, to the wife of a man who was sitting where he's sitting now.'

'That's six in a fortnight,' the owner's wife said.

'It wants a heading. I had Since the Rib.'

'It was before the rib. She's been odd since the box.'

'We're not having the box. We've had the box twelve years.'

'Call it Letters,' Jeannot said, to the bottles. 'I number mine.'

'They're not letters.'

'I don't mind what you call it,' said Sarthou, from the doorway. 'Not a file.'

'Madame le maire,' said the man who was dealing. 'Three minutes, the chair, the pen. She thinks she's the mayor. Put that.'

The table laughed. Lafitte put it to them with his pencil raised. Three hands went up at the cards, and the owner's wife's behind the bread. Jeannot kept his on his glass.

'Carried,' Lafitte said.

He wrote Madame le maire across the top and ruled a line under it.

Maylis read it upside down from where she stood. She had the same words at home, on the back of a shopping pad.
