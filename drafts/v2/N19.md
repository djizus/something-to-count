# N19, chapter 19, comic then dark. Joins: after 21d, before what is kept of 21e.

* * *

Somebody was on the gate platform with a torch in his teeth.

Maylis got out of the van. It was Kévin Labat. He was hauling a net up out of the throat of the sluice, hand over hand, green and fine as stocking, with nothing in it.

'Nobody nets at eighty over,' he said, round the torch. 'It would take the net, and me after it. I'm fetching them in.'

'That platform's locked.'

'The gate to it is. I come over the rail.' He shrugged. 'Four winters I've come over the rail. The two hundred was to save my trousers.'

She had turned her back on that for four winters. Tonight she was under orders to sit and face it.

He dropped the net into a cool box and took the torch out of his mouth.

'Does it know about me? Your box. Tonight.'

'It had a van at the end of the track. It wasn't yours.'

'That's something.' Then he saw her face, and the river going by at the height of the planks. 'What else did it have?'

Every short answer she had was wrong.

'How many does your van seat?'

'Two. And the boxes.'

'Take it down to the home. The back door, in the yard at the dyke end. Coralie's on. The minibus takes fourteen a turn, and she has forty-two.'

'Has it gone red?'

'It's orange.'

'Then nobody's moving anybody.' He stood with his shoulders up, and they stayed up. 'What do I say I've come for?'

'Say I sent it.'

'I've four kilos in those boxes for Irun, and the pump running.'

'Leave it running.'

He looked along the crown to where her headlamps had been pointing, and down the track. He asked her nothing more. He lifted the two boxes over the rail, one and then the other, and strapped them in. The van went off the crown towards the Port with the pump going in the back. She could hear it as far as the home.
