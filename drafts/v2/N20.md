Maylis did not set an alarm. There was nothing left for four o'clock to be the hour of. She meant to be awake when the telephone rang, with it in her hand, and to have the engine started before the voice had finished her name.

She put the seat up straight and held the telephone against her knee, screen upwards. It said 22:41. The river went by under the crown of the bank.

She had been up at four on eighteen mornings. She had slept on a mower, and at a stop line with two bales in the air.

The screen said 23:58. She had not seen it change.

She wound the window down a hand's width, for the cold, and said the gauge to herself, and the bags on Sarthou's step, and her mother's ponies by name as far as the old mare.

Then she had the telephone in her hand, and her eyes shut.
