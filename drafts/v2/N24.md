On Saturday at six Lesca woke on her back, in a dry room, and one more thing had crossed.

It was the village hall, with the strip lights on. Chairs had been set along the trestles in three rows, and people sat in them in blankets with their heads down. A woman in a wet tunic went along the rows with a list on a cord. She touched each one on the shoulder and said a number, and it was Coralie's voice. She came to the end of the third row. The last chair was empty.

'Forty-one,' she said.

Lesca had the number as she had once had forty letters. She had the chair as well, and whose it was, and a door with a brass twelve on it standing open on black water.

She knew what had put a woman on a track at three in the morning. She had said it to her face.

She took the notebook off the blanket and opened it at the page with two columns. Received was full. Under Removed there was the one word. With the edge of the envelope she ruled a third column down the right-hand side, and headed it Delivered.

There was a date at the foot of that page which had stood for three days with no heading. She gave it one.

*Delivered. 24 November, a night and its rain. 12 December, five words: Go to the tonne first.*

She did not rule the column off. At seven she was at her mother's table, and ate what was put in front of her.
