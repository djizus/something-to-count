# N25, chapter 22, comic then dark. Joins: after 24c, before N26.

* * *

Lesca took the ladder down from its two hooks under the lean-to and set it against the eaves at the back. Maïté put one shoe on the bottom rung and a hand on each rail.

The gutter was packed from the corner to the downpipe. She cleared it by hand, by the light from the kitchen window, and dropped it past Maïté's shoulder in black handfuls. It took a long time. They had said what there was indoors, and out here they kept to the gutter.

At ten the band stopped. From the top of the ladder she had the Port under her, and the bank with the marquee lit along it, and the crown black with people from the gate to the last of the four plots.

One match showed on the crest.

The bull went up in white fire from horn to horn. It was a man in a harness under a frame of welded rod, and it put its head down and ran at six thousand people. They opened in front of it and shut behind it, screaming, with their glasses held up out of the sparks. It turned at the bench that thanked the State and came back through them throwing red. A rocket left its spine, burst over the gravel lake and lit the dredger.

'There,' Maïté said, at the foot of the ladder. 'That's the duck gone. Every bird off that water as far as Urt. They'll sit out on the river and not come down to anything.' She shifted her grip. 'A week. He timed it once, after the July one, with a notebook. He had it in a letter.'

'I moved the money for that.'

'You did. I abstained.'

The bull went through them a third time. The crowd had learned it by now, and ran towards it.

A latch lifted at the bottom of the garden.

A man had come up the back lane on foot, with no torch. He had known where the ruts were. He stood at the garden gate with his hand on it and his face turned up to the ladder, and it was Guy Larrère, in the coat he had worn to the stone.

'He comes up each year when it's lit,' Maïté said. She kept her eyes on the rung. 'Four years he came by that lane, on duck nights, to the kitchen. Twelve he's come as far as the gate. I've not opened it.'

Lesca came down the ladder. She crossed the garden with the gutter on her hands and stopped a metre from him. The gate came to his chest, and there was no glass in it.

'From whose telephone did you ring me, Guy?' She gave him no time to begin. 'I had your order in the dark, off the telephone by the bed, and behind your voice there was a clock. I've heard that clock tonight. It's on her wall, over the telephone. For eleven years I've stood at that stone while she asked who was meant to go round the tonnes, and you've stood behind me under a tree. From whose telephone?'

A rocket went up off the dyke. It lit him red, and then white.

'I'll be brief,' he said.

She gave him the time. She gave him his three minutes and more, with no bell on them. Down on the bank the bull burned out a horn at a time, and the crowd groaned and clapped, and the band began again. He had his mouth a little open. He looked past her at the lit kitchen window, and at the woman holding a ladder with nobody on it.

Nothing came.

He took his hand off the gate. He went back down the lane the way he had come up it, in the dark, and she heard him find the ruts.

Lesca hung the ladder on its two hooks and went home by the front.
