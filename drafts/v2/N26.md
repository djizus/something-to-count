Lesca came up the stairs at eleven with rust from the Castagnet gutter on her hands.

Coralie was on the landing in her dressing gown, with her door open behind her and the lamp on in the box room. She had stood in the second row that morning and heard the third sheet read as typed. She had said nothing since.

Lesca spoke first.

'I'm going to tell you a thing, and you'll want to ring Bayonne, and I'd ask you to hear the end of it. You asked who the five words were for. She was in a van last night, at the old gate, with the river at ninety over and the bank coming through. I saw her. She holds her side. I said them to her face, and she went to the tonne first, and she got him out. He was dry. While she was on the track the bank went. It went into the Port, and into the home at the dyke end. I gave it ten minutes once. Hers held sixteen.'

'Don't give me the minutes.'

'Then I'll give you who. They got forty-one out. Room twelve is under the bank. It was Madame Etcheto.'

Coralie's hand went to the frame of her door.

'I wheeled her up this morning,' she said. 'She had the end of the row. She asked for it.'

'I read a speech a metre from her. She was in twelve that night in 2014 as well. I kept the river off her then. Last night I sent it.' Lesca's right hand was shut. 'I wanted you to have it from me, and not through a wall.'

Coralie looked at her for some time. On Thursday she had told her not to say which. She did not ask it now, and she asked nothing about the gate.

'Was she in her bed?'

'Yes.'

'Then she'd said her words.' Coralie took her hand off the frame. 'Is it on the wardrobe?'

'It wasn't last night. I don't think it comes again.'

Coralie went into the box room. The lamp on the shelf went out. She came back with her pillow held against her chest, as she had taken it the other way in February 2015, and carried it across the landing.
