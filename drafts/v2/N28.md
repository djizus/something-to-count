On Saturday morning Lesca walked the shore of the gravel lake with her father's watch in her hand.

The track had gone under with the meadows in 2019. The company's haul road ran along the near shore in its place, from the pier of the gate towards the far heaps, and it passed the spot. She had the spot from the prefecture's plan of 2015. She had put her boots on. She started the watch at the pier and walked as a person walks at night, in mud, without a torch.

On the platform that night she had reckoned the walk in the time it takes to look down a track. The inquiry had reckoned it afterwards with a ruler, and had agreed with her. In twelve years nobody had walked it.

A pickup came along the haul road behind her and slowed. The site manager put his window down. He had a white helmet on the seat beside him and a sheaf of tickets on the dashboard.

'This is a working site,' he said. 'Are you from the mairie?'

'I am.'

'Then you can tell me. Does Monsieur le maire want the weighbridge opened? He's had my tickets, and he's been on the telephone twice. I can have a man in the cabin till twelve.'

'She's had your tickets.'

'Who has?'

'I'm walking to where a tonne stood,' Lesca said. 'A hide, for duck. A concrete one, with a cellar.'

He looked along the shore, and out at the water.

'There's nothing like that on my plan. You'd want a boat.' He let the pickup roll. 'I'll tell the cabin twelve o'clock.'

She walked on. Where the plan put it there was a marker post on the bank, and beyond the post the lake, with a moorhen on it. She stopped the watch.

It had taken twenty-five minutes. She had been right to the minute, with a telephone in her hand and the rain coming sideways.

Over there the track was still a track. On a night that was coming a woman with her hands would stand at the head of it. She would have what Lesca had had, and no watch.

Lesca had held past the alarm on Thursday, with a lamp. If she could hold on the night itself, in that chair, she would have no need to send anything across. She would be standing there. She knew how long it took, and she would walk it.
