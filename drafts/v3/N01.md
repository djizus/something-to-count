At four Maylis went down to drop the gate before the tide came back up.

From the crown of the dyke she had all of it in one look. On her right the Adour went by, brown, and higher than the grass on her left. The gate stood between the two, in the old bank. Beyond it the low meadows ran out flat for a kilometre, with one track across them. Where the track stopped, a hump of concrete showed in the rushes, under a steel hatch. It was Jeannot Castagnet's tonne. He went down a ladder into it at dusk and sat there until morning, below the ground and below the river, waiting for duck.

She let the gate down left-handed, and drove back up to the hall.
