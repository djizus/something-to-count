# N2, chapter 2, comic. Joins: in 02d, after the vote on the money and in place of "Lesca closed the meeting" as far as "had their coats on". Maïté's question comes at the coats, and her tile follows it.

* * *

Lesca took the stone last. The gilding was gone from the letters, and a mason in Bayonne would renew both lines before the twelfth.

'While he's there with his brush,' Guy Larrère said, 'I move that the stone be given a verb. I moved it three times in 2015. It has a man's name and a woman's name and nothing between them. A stranger reads it as a married couple.'

Marie-Jo stood the timer in front of him again.

'The chair has an interest, and doesn't vote,' Lesca said. 'I'll say what was found then, since half this table wasn't at it. Every verb that was tried was right for one line and wrong for the other. It's in the minutes of all three sittings, and I can have them fetched.'

'Saved,' Larrère said.

'Through the chair,' said Marie-Jo. 'Saved whom? It sits under his name. It reads that she saved him.'

'Opened.'

'Opened what? There's a name over it. It reads that she opened him.'

Somebody at the end of the table laughed and made a cough of it.

'Then I leave the committee to find one,' Larrère said. 'There are verbs. It only needs somebody to say which.'

'I have a verb,' Maïté Castagnet said.

She spoke at that table to abstain, and for nothing else. Every head turned. She held her handbag in both hands, and she opened her mouth to give it.

The timer rang.

'That's the member's time,' Madame Ducasse said. 'It goes to the vote.'

It was lost by two hands, as it was lost each time in 2015. Maïté kept hers on her bag. The stone would be gilded as it stood, with two names on it.

Maïté Castagnet waited until the others had their coats on.

'Did you know he was out?' she said.

'No one came to tell him,' Lesca said.

It was not what she was asked. It had been the answer for eleven years.
