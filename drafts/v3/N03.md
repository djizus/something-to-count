On Friday at eight Lesca let herself into the mairie, took the flood file of 2014 down from the cupboard and opened it at the gauge sheets.

They were in her own hand, as she wrote before she had a signature. She was the keyholder that winter, and each morning at eight she read the gauge with a thumbnail against the mark and gave it a line, with her father's L at the end.

*24 November 2014. Rain from the night.*

*27 November 2014. 1 m 40 under. Rain.*

Three hours before, in the chair, she had been given that gauge with rain running down the iron, and the river on it stood a metre forty under the red line.

She opened the notebook beside the file. The registered letter was signed for in rain. Jeannot Castagnet sat in his van with the wipers going. The woman who had her hand wore a hood in each thing the night gave Lesca, and water ran off it. Through her own window the back of the stone was white with frost, and the gravel of the square lay dry.

Madame Ducasse came in with the bread and stopped at the sight of the file.

'It's for the gauge,' Lesca said, before she could be asked. 'The twenty-seventh. I wanted the level and the weather, and who read it. I read it. I'd forgotten I ever made a 4 like that.'

'It rained,' Madame Ducasse said. 'From the Monday. I had the washing on the radiators till Christmas.' She laid the loaf on the blotter. 'Shall I put it back?'

'Leave it out.'

When the door shut Lesca turned the sheets forward. There was a line for each day, and the river came up the gauge in them. The last line in her hand was the eleventh of December, at ten at night. After that sheet the file was typed, by the gendarmerie.

From the twenty-seventh to the eleventh was a fortnight. This year the eleventh fell on a Friday.

She had taken Jeannot Castagnet, alive at the window of a van, to mean a village that the flood had passed by.

That village had not been spared December 2014. It had not had it yet.
