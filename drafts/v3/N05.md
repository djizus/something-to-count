A man was sweeping the first doorstep when the committee came along the crest, and Lesca knew the broom before she knew him.

It was Monsieur Darrigrand. The step was what the State's contractor left of his house in 2016, and he drove over from Urt to do it on the last Saturday of each month.

'Madame le maire.'

'Monsieur Darrigrand. The marquee goes on the plots, as it does each year, and since you're here I'll say what I said to the council. The old bank was coming through at this end every spring. The State would build a new one on its own line or on none, and past the home its line went back from the river, through four houses. It was thirty-six houses and the home against four. Yours was bought at the State's valuation.'

'Thirty-six against four,' he said. He leaned on the broom. 'I've turned that over for ten years, and you can have it back. It's four when you're the one counting. When it's your house it's one, and they knock it down.'

'You were paid.'

'They valued a house with a river at its back. Nobody valued it with a bank at its back, which is what the thirty-six have now. The one two doors along sold last spring for twice mine. It's dry. I paid for that.'

'The valuation was the State's.'

'The vote was yours.' He swept the last of the frost off the step. 'Put your marquee up. All I ask is that nobody stands a glass on it.'

'On what?'

'My step.'

'It's the commune's step.'

'Then the commune can sweep it,' Monsieur Darrigrand said. He put the broom over his shoulder and went down the bank to his car.

Madame Ducasse had written none of it. Marie-Jo Labat had heard it all.
