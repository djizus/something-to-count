Lesca went back in, took the flood file down again and opened it on her desk beside the notebook.

She set the two against each other, as she would have set two readings of one meter. In the file the rain began on the night of the twenty-fourth of November 2014. Over there it began on the night of the twenty-fourth of November of this year. On that night she woke for the first time with forty letters in her head that a dead man had not written. On that night, by the card and by the warmth in the chair, Inguma sat on two breastbones at one minute.

Before that night Inguma had sat on hers alone. For twelve years she had carried the night of the flood, with its nineteen days of rain in front of it, and had told it to one priest. She took it for a thing she had. It was a thing Inguma kept, on her side, under its weight. Then in one week she lay on her back for Inguma, got up at four and went in to meet it, and the night she carried went where the letters came from.

So the river over there was rising by her file, a line a day, behind a bank with forty houses under it. A woman with her hand was reading it each morning with a thumbnail.

Lesca could not yet say how a woman went over. Things went over, and a chair was warm when she came to it. She had until Friday the eleventh to learn the rest. She meant to go over herself and stop it.

She wrote it under the two findings she had, and gave it a number.

*3. It began to rain there on the night Inguma first sat on both of us. I kept that flood on my side for twelve years, and I have let it across. That flood is mine.*
