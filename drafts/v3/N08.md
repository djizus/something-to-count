He left the window where it was. He put two fingers out through the gap, and she gave him back the slip.

A buff folder lay on the passenger seat, with LESCA M. on the tab in Madame Ducasse's capitals. He opened it against the wheel and laid the pink leaf in on top.

'That's your staff file. In eighteen years I've put nothing in it but your father's letter. Hontaube reads it in January and decides what it keeps.' He shut the folder. 'They tell me men have been over the rail of that platform for four winters. On the night tides, with nets. I don't know who, and I've not asked. It's a locked platform.'

'I hold the key to the winch.'

'And to the platform gate. They're on one ring. I gave it to your father.' He looked at the wipers. 'You're on that dyke at each tide. A file can say so, or not.'

The window went up.

At eleven she was on the crown of the dyke for the turn of the tide, and she looked over the low meadows for what he had in them.

The gate was shut. It kept the river off three hundred hectares of grass, which were the association's, and a good share of them his. Halfway out, beside the ditch, stood a hut of breeze blocks under a tin roof. A black pipe ran from it under the track, towards his maize. From June to September a pump ran in that hut. Each pump on the river wore a meter in a grey box, with the water agency's seal on it, and she had read the commune's twice a year since 2008. That hut had no box.

She had walked past it for as long as she had walked past the nets.

It stood a hand above the grass. With the gate shut, it was dry.
