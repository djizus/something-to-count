# N9, chapter 9, comic. Joins: in place of 09d. The pass is told from the café. The last part is hers, and keeps what Kévin says from 09d.

* * *

At two the rain held off, and the café watched Maylis bring the ride-on mower round to the strip of grass in front of the mairie, for the last cut of the year.

Old Lafitte was dealing. She did the first pass along the wall and turned at the plane tree. She did the second.

'She's sat very straight,' the owner's wife said.

On the third pass her chin went down onto her chest. The mower kept its line. It came on along the grass at walking pace, with the blades going and nobody driving it.

'She's asleep,' Lafitte said, and laid his cards face down.

'On the commune's machine?'

'It's not my machine. Who's to wake her?'

'It'll stop at the kerb.'

'It'll stop at the bollard.'

The four at the table got up and came to the window. The mayor's parking space had a cast-iron bollard at each corner, set in concrete. Jeannot Castagnet had driven round the near one for years, and it was in three of his letters.

'If Castagnet were here he'd pay to see this,' the owner said.

'He'd want it in writing that he wasn't driving.'

Kévin Labat came out from behind the hall at a run, with his boots unlaced, shouting. The mower got there first. It rode a hand's height up the iron, and the bollard went slowly over to the angle of a man leaning on a bar, and the engine laboured and held it there. Kévin reached in past her and turned the key.

The café gave it a moment.

'That's the mayor's bollard,' Lafitte said. He turned the score pad over and wrote.

What was said on the grass the café could not hear.

Maylis was awake. For the length of that pass Inguma sat on her breastbone, in daylight, with its back to the mairie wall, and she had her eyes and nothing else. Until that afternoon it had come at night, to a woman lying down.

'Castagnet came to my van at one,' Kévin said. 'He says you know what's under his boards. You saw his nets in that box, and you came out and used them on him. And me.'

'I used your name.'

'A dream doesn't know where a man keeps his nets. I got as high as a gutter. What have you got, Maylis?'

'I don't know yet.'

'Does it know about me?'

'It had you on a mower.'

He looked at the mower, and at the bollard.

'So it works,' he said, and went back to his van.
