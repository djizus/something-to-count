# N10, chapter 9, comic then dark. Joins: as the chapter's last scene, after 09e. It treats Tuesday's statement as taken and completes it, so the lines in 09a and 09c that quote "by telephone" still stand.

* * *

At five Maylis sat at the oilcloth in Sarthou's kitchen, with the dyke filling the window.

'The statement of yesterday is incomplete,' Sarthou said. He had the form squared in front of him and his cap on the table. 'It contains an order, given by telephone, and no person giving it. One question is outstanding. Did the order come from the mayor, or from the president of the association?'

'Either.'

'Either is not a person. I cannot summon Either to the brigade.' 'I ask it a second time, for the record.'

'Whichever he says.'

He wrote. He read it back as he read everything, with nothing in his voice.

'Asked whether the order came from the mayor or from the president, the keyholder states by telephone.' He blotted it. 'Then the one name in this file is yours. I would have preferred another. Sign at the foot.'

She signed, with the tall L.

It was dusk when she came out. From his gate she watched a white van come down off the crown of the dyke and go out along the track to the meadows.

She drove up to the terrace. At the end of the Castagnets' road a car was turning into the lane that ran along the backs of the houses. Its lamps were off. It went down the lane at walking pace and stopped at the fourth gate.

She knew that car. She stood beside its window most mornings, in the rain.

It was the mayor's. It stood in the lane behind the Castagnet house with its lights off, and nobody got out of it while she was passing.
