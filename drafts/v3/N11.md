# N11, chapter 11, comic. Joins: in place of the opening of 11c, as far as "to the public side, and stood at it." The counter scene follows untouched.

* * *

At two on Friday Maylis followed Madame Ducasse, the secretary, into the mairie, and this time she went round the end of the counter on purpose.

She sat down at the mayor's desk. The commune's order forms were in the second drawer, and she knew the drawer. She took a form and wrote on it left-handed, since the right was in its bandage. *One pawl, for the hand winch of the sluice gate. Urgent.*

At the foot was the box for the mayor. She had tried for that signature a week ago and lost it. Today she asked the hand for it, and it came, slower in the left than it wanted to go, *Lesca* in one run and the stroke back under it. She lifted the commune's stamp off its pad and brought it down beside the name.

A thing was ordered, and it had taken four minutes. She sat with the form in front of her and liked it more than she had liked anything since the medal.

'Madame Ducasse,' Guy Larrère said. 'Is this lady in the day book?'

'She's the commune's employee, Monsieur le maire. She has a key.'

'She's at my desk with my stamp. That's a caller. Enter her.'

He came round and drew the form out from under her hand. He read it through, and then read the signature again.

'A pawl,' he said. 'For the association's winch, on the commune's paper, over the mayor's name.' He opened his folder and laid the form in it, on a pink slip. 'I'll keep it with the other. They tell me Hontaube reads a staff file from the back.'

'It sticks at nineteen.'

'What did she come about?' said Madame Ducasse.

'The school roof,' Maylis said.

'Then she can ask for it at the counter,' Larrère said, 'in opening hours.'

She got up out of his chair and went back round the end of the counter, to the public side, and stood at it.
