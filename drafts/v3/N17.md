On Monday at breakfast Coralie gave Maylis back what had been said to her at six, word for word.

'Come back across. I've left you on that landing eleven years.' She said it standing, in her tunic, with the coffee pot in her hand, in the voice she used for reading a dose off a chart. 'That's what I had. I said it over on the stairs, so as to keep it.'

Maylis had heard it the first time. She had heard it from inside her own mouth, lower than she used it, while she tried to get her hand shut.

'Was that you?'

'Either.'

Coralie put the pot down on the shopping pad.

'No. Either gets eggs. I've cooked Either for sixteen years, and I'm not cooking it this morning.' She pulled the pad out from under the pot and wrote on it, pressing. She wrote the sentence out in full. Under it she drew two boxes. Beside one she put You, and beside the other Somebody else. She turned the pad round and laid the pencil across it. 'Tick one.'

Maylis took the pencil in her left hand. Both were true, and there was no third box.

She ticked both.

'That's Either again, in pencil.'

'It's what I've got.'

'Then I'll tell you what I've got. You had my hand. You turned yours over and held on, and I can't think when you last did that. And the whole time your fingers were trying to get out from under mine. I had both. I've held hands for a living since I was nineteen. I know when one's holding and I know when one wants to be let go.' She put a finger on each box. 'So which was you? The holding, or the getting out?'

The getting out was hers. Maylis could hear what that would be, said across a table to a woman in a tunic.

'I was slow coming round,' she said.

'You were wide awake, at a hundred and thirty.' Coralie took her finger off the first box and left it on the second. 'I've been in the box room three nights. I went because you get up at four and write on my shopping. I'd have come back the first morning you said so. Sixteen years I've waited to be asked for one thing in this house. This morning I'm asked, out loud, with my hand held. It's got the wrong number of years in it, and you can't tell me who did the asking.'

'No.'

Coralie waited. She had the early shift to get to, and she put her coat on a sleeve at a time, to give Maylis the time.

Then she tore the page off the pad, folded it and put it in her coat pocket. On her way to the door she reached behind the fruit bowl, lifted out an envelope that had stood there since March, and put it in her bag.
