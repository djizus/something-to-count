She had the pencil in her left hand, and a dozen strokes on the form. Her right hand came out of her pocket, bound as it was, and took the pencil from the left. It turned the form over on the board and wrote across the back, fast, in a script that leaned.

*That the mayor remain seated, and take the other one's bed, and Coralie Mounet in it.*

Maylis read it as it came. It was the hand that had held on to Coralie's fingers at six, and it knew Coralie's surname.

Madame Ducasse had stopped under her umbrella at the foot of the mairie steps. She had filed that employee's chits since 2008, each with the tall L on it, and she watched the pencil to the end of the line.

Maylis took the pencil back in her left hand. Under the motion she wrote her own, large and upright.

*I mean to stay in the chair. That is where the sash is.*
