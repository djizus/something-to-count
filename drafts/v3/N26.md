At half past five, with the light going, Maylis left the telephone on the dashboard as she had been ordered to, shut the van door on it and went down off the crown on foot.

She walked on the grass between the ruts, which stood full to the brim. Her rib let her go at a walk and no faster. At the dip the track was under water from edge to edge, and she went through it to the shins. She looked at her watch there, and again when she came to the Trafic. A kilometre had taken her twenty-five minutes.

The Trafic stood where the track ran out. Beyond it the tonne was a hump in the rushes, with a stovepipe smoking in the rain.

She knocked on the hatch with her left fist.

A bolt went back. The hatch came up the width of a hand and stopped. In the gap there was lamplight, the smell of a paraffin stove, and one eye.

'I told you not to.'

'I walked.'

'I can see how you came. You've put up each bird between here and the ditch.' The eye went to her boots, and past her, along the empty track. 'Who's at the gate?'

'It'll be ninety over by three, Jeannot. The bank goes at the patch. When it does, that gate has to come up, and four minutes after it this hatch is under the river.'

'Ninety.' He said the figure over to himself. 'Pau gives eighty-five for the top of the tide. I had it on the wireless. Who gave you ninety?'

She had it from a woman who had stood at that gate with it.

'I've been given it.'

'You were given what's under my boards. You were given the way to my kitchen and all.' The gap narrowed. 'It's shut. He's told you shut, and with it shut I'm sat in the driest hole in the commune. I'll not come up out of it for a figure. Go back to your gate, Lesca. If it rings on that dashboard and you're stood here, he'll have you for it, and I'll not write you a letter.'

The hatch came down. The bolt went across under it, and after the bolt something heavy was dragged across.

She knocked twice more. The pipe went on smoking.

She walked back with the rain behind her. It was dark by the dip, and she crossed it by the sound of the water.

The telephone lay on the dashboard where she had left it. The screen showed two calls from G. Larrère, at ten to six and at a quarter past, and under them one line.

*Where were you? I ring once more tonight. Be in that van.*

She had been fifty minutes off the gate, and he knew it. A man who would not open could not be carried, and to try him again would cost her the same fifty. She got in behind the wheel and put her hand on the telephone.
