Lesca was standing. The panel was in front of her with both lamps lit. The wall on her left was steel, with its rivets, and the door was shut in it.

Inguma was in the chair.

It filled the seat from arm to arm. The brown leaf hung over the steel on either side, and the rivets ran up its spine to the height of her chest. It had its back to her.

A woman stood on the far side of it, a chair's width away. She wore a yellow waterproof zipped to the neck, with rain on the shoulders and the cuffs turned back once. Her right forearm lay across her side and stayed there. The hand at the end of it was bound across the palm. A key hung at her belt, and her boots were muddy to the ankle.

She was looking at Lesca. Then her eyes went down to Lesca's right hand, which was shut.

Lesca had spoken first in each room she entered since she took the mayor's chair. In this one she opened the hand and turned the palm up. The seam lay across it, white, from the forefinger to the heel. She held it out over Inguma's back, where it could be read.

The woman read it. It took her as long as a gauge takes.

'You rang once,' she said.

It was Lesca's voice, as the answering machine at the mairie gave it back to her.

Lesca had answers. She had given them to a prefect, to a council and to a widow, for twelve years, and each of them was true. She shut the hand on them.

She turned her back to Inguma, took hold of the arms of the chair behind her and sat down.

The leaf gave under her like a sack of husks. Under the leaf was the plate, cold through her T-shirt, with the rivet heads in a row. It bore up. It lifted her until her heels came off the steel floor. She bore down. Inguma had sat on her for twelve years, and she knew to the kilo what it weighed and how hard it pushed. She got her heels to the floor and kept them there. Then she laid her forearms along the arms of the chair, in the leaf, fixed on the left lamp and held.

The woman watched her do it. She had come for that seat as well. She stood square, as she had been taught to stand to a handle, and made no move to pull Lesca off.

Behind her, a long way off, a telephone began to ring.

The woman's head went round to it. Her bound hand went to the key at her belt.

Lesca was sitting, and holding, when the other one turned to a telephone.
