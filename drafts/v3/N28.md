At nine minutes to three Maylis was in the van on the crown of the dyke, with the telephone ringing in her left hand and two people in her right.

Rain ran down the windscreen. The river went by under the bank, close, and louder than at ten. She had turned from the chair to that ringing, and a woman was sitting in the chair when she turned.

She knew what the telephone would tell her. She sent her right hand to her belt, for the key of the handle box. Coralie was behind that bank with a list round her neck, and the gate would take the river off it.

The hand reached the key and closed on it. Then it opened, against her, and went the other way. It crossed in front of her to the steering column and took hold of the ignition. Somebody meant to drive, and from that gate there was one place a van could be driven to in the dark, at the end of a kilometre of track.

She had felt that on Monday at six, from underneath, with Coralie's fingers in it.

The hand turned the key. The engine caught. The van stood in gear, as she had left it, and it jumped a metre towards the gate and stalled. It threw her onto the wheel, and her rib let her have no breath to shout with.

The telephone rang on. She dropped it in her lap and took her right wrist in her left hand. She dragged it down towards the belt. It came as far as her knee. The fingers were set as her own set on a handle, and they belonged to a woman who had turned a winch with the skin off that palm and had not let go of it since. They went back up to the key. Under the bandage the weal opened.

She got her elbow inside the wheel and hauled. The heel of the hand came down on the horn.

It sounded once, out over the meadows, where a man sat in a cellar with his telephone left in his van.

The telephone had rung eight times. She counted them, since counting was what she had.

Then her mouth opened, and she had not told it to.

'Go to the tonne first.'

It was her own voice, lower than she used it, as the commune's answering machine gave it. It asked for nothing.

The hand was hers. It lay on the wheel with the fingers open and nobody in it. Something went out of the cab as the warmth had gone out of a chair, and she was by herself in a stalled van with five words.

The screen in her lap said 02:53. Under the time it said Castagnet, house.

She answered.
