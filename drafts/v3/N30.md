Lesca came up the stairs at eleven with rust from the Castagnet gutter on her hands.

Coralie was on the landing in her dressing gown, with her door open behind her and the lamp lit in the box room.

Lesca spoke first.

'I've one thing more, and it wasn't in the speech. Last night the other one was in a van at the old gate, with the river at ninety over and the bank coming through. I was in her right hand. I let go of it, and I told her with her own mouth to go to the tonne first. She went. While she was on the track the bank went into the Port, and into the home at the dyke end. They carried forty-one out. Room twelve is under the bank. It was Madame Etcheto.'

Coralie's hand went to the frame of her door.

'I wheeled her up this morning. She had the end of the row.'

'I read a metre from her. She was in twelve that night in 2014 as well, and I kept the river off her. Last night I sent it to her.' Lesca's right hand was shut. 'I wanted you to have it from me, and not through a wall.'

'Was she in her bed?'

'Yes.'

Coralie looked at her for some time. She asked nothing about the gate.

'Wash your hands, Madame le maire. There's soup on the ring, and you'll eat it. Don't stand on this landing waiting for me to say something. I've had one thing from you at a microphone and one on the stairs, and I'm taking the night over both.'

'Yes.'

'And you'll lie on your side. Your mother rang.' She had half turned to the box room. 'Go to bed, Maylis.'

She went in. The lamp on the shelf stayed lit.

Lesca washed her hands and ate the soup standing at the ring. When she came up again she lay down on her side, with her knees drawn up, facing the landing.

Coralie's door stood open, and her pillow was still on the far side of the landing.
