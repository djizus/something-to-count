# N31, chapter 8, comic with dread. Joins: after the moved 10d. The first part is Tuesday's day. The part from "That evening" belongs inside 08b, after the first "Minute it." and before the gates contract, and it gives Larrère a third entry to read at the end.

* * *

On Tuesday Lesca had one thing to send to the other village, and she gave it the day.

It was a date. *Friday the eleventh, at night.* She said it under her breath on each hour, and between the hours it got loose. At the church wall she found the rake in her hands again and said it to the gratings, twice, before Kévin Labat took the rake off her for the second morning running.

'Friday the eleventh what?' he said.

'At night.'

He shrugged, and wrote it on the back of his hand in case it was overtime.

She said it on the mairie stairs. Madame Ducasse, a step behind her with the post, asked whether that was for the diary.

That evening, in the sitting, a member for the Port had a question for the chair. The gravel lorries had cut ruts in his lane a hand deep. When did the commune mean to fill them?

'Friday the eleventh, at night,' Lesca said.

'I ask that that be minuted,' Guy Larrère said. 'The chair has given a day and an hour. They tell me she's had it by heart since the morning. If it isn't for the ruts, I take it for the next sitting of this council.'

'The next sitting is the first Tuesday of January.'

'Then what is Friday the eleventh, Madame le maire?'

It was the night a river came over a bank, in a village this table did not have.

'Minute it as he asks,' she said.

Madame Ducasse wrote that the chair, asked about ruts, had named Friday the eleventh of December, at night.
