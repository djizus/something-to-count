She knew the smell. It was the smell of the old tobacco-drying barn at the end of the village, and she had sat in that barn once for three hours and been paid for it.

In June 2014 the council had let the barn to a university team. The team had built a steel room inside it, the size of a garden shed, with one chair and a panel with two lamps.

'One of these two lamps will come on,' Dr Kessler had told her, at the door. 'You'll see which. What we want to know is whether, somewhere, you also see the other.'

Maylis had asked where somewhere was. Dr Kessler had said that was the question.

One lamp had come on. She had written which on a card and sealed the card in an envelope. Then they had opened the door, counted one of her, given her a hundred and eighty euros and a sandwich, and gone back to their university.
