'There's five,' Kévin said. 'They're pinned at the top, and every one's a don't.' He held the telephone up and read them off it.

'Don't push at it. Lie still under it and you're in. Don't be the one still sitting. Whoever wakes first goes home, and if you're still sat there you go home with them, and it isn't your home. Don't count on staying. It fetches you, as soon as it's done counting whatever you gave it. Don't get up in there. And don't leave it the chair.'

'Why not get up?'

'It doesn't say. The pinned ones don't give reasons. A man in Limoges says he got up, and they took his posting rights off him.'

'I pushed at it. The first night.'

'That's one.'

'And she was the one still sitting, this morning.'

'That's two. It's hers, but it was your bed, so it counts.' He scratched his head with the telephone. 'I'll have to put it up. They'll say I invented you.'

'What happens if you do the fourth?'

'Nobody's said. Nobody's done it and come back to type.' He lay down again. 'Don't, is my advice. It's in capitals.'

She meant to do it that night.
