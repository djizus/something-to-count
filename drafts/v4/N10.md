At seven there was soup, since Lesca had ticked it.

Coralie ladled it at the table and sat down across the oilcloth. Lesca had wrapped the right hand in a clean tea towel and knotted it at the wrist, with the fingers out for a spoon.

'What have you done to that?'

'I tried the winch at eight. It stops at nineteen turns and has to have a shoulder, and when it gives, the pawl misses and the handle runs back. It ran back through this fist. That winch was reported in 2011 and twice after. A council took note, which means it wrote down that it had been told. I'd have had a pawl on it inside the month.'

Coralie had put her spoon down to listen.

'That's more than I've had about that winch since they took note,' she said. She picked the spoon up again. 'And Larrère? The home had it by ten that you gave him three minutes.'

'Three is what an item gets. He had used one of them on my father. He has talked down through that gap each Tuesday since he was elected, and no one has put a clock on him. He wound the window right down. I think it was to hear his own first name.'

Coralie laughed, with her head back. In Lesca's kitchen that laugh had stopped in a January.

The right hand turned its spoon over, and the soup went back in the bowl. Lesca filled the spoon again. It went over again.

'It hurts to hold,' she said.

'I'll dress it after.' Coralie drew the pad across. 'And tomorrow? What do you want?'

'The rest of the duck, with the potatoes done in the fat. And you sitting down to it, and not eating at the ring with your coat on.' She watched the box ticked. 'It's eleven years since anybody put that to me across a table and waited.'

'Sixteen,' Coralie said, writing. 'And I put it to you yesterday.'

'There's one thing more, and it isn't for the pad. On Friday night that river will be ninety over the line, and the bank will come through at the patch. Have them out of their beds by two. Dressed, in the corridor, with the minibus at the back door and its tank full. Those who can walk go first, from the front, since that fills a minibus quickest. Then the frames. Then the ones who are carried.'

'On whose order?'

'Mine.'

'You cut the verges, Maylis. The director's at Bayonne, and I'm a care assistant. I can't get forty-two people out of bed on the word of the woman I live with.'

'Then put it on a list. Nobody asks a list for its order.'

Coralie wrote something under the duck.

She stacked the bowls and carried them to the sink. She spoke from there, with her back turned.

'I don't know who you've been today. The café says you think you're the mayor. I've had five answers with one soup, and one of them was an instruction.' She ran the tap. 'I'd have this one again.'

Under the knot of the tea towel the fingers had shut.

Coralie put out the kitchen light and went up. On the fourth stair she stopped, with her hand on the rail.

'Are you coming up?'

Lesca had come for that. She got as far as the foot of the stairs. Then Inguma was on her breastbone, with a back to her and a back to the stairs, and Coralie stood on the fourth one, waiting to be answered.
