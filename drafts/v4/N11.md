At two he had her into the mayor's office and shut the door on Madame Ducasse. Maylis had been in that room to bleed the radiator.

He sat. She stood on the far side of the desk with her right hand in its dressing. He untied the buff folder and laid four things out of it on the blotter.

'A delivery slip. Yesterday, twenty to twelve. A courier asks for somebody from the mairie, and you tell him that you are the mayor, and sign as one. He's from Bordeaux, and he'd know you again.' He touched the second. 'Yesterday, nine o'clock, in the square, in front of the café. You tell the mayor of this commune that he has three minutes, and you call him Guy.' The third was a sheet in his own hand. 'Yesterday, eight o'clock. You put the handle to the association's winch with no order from its president, and you come away with that hand. The association isn't insured for a keyholder who turns its gate to see what happens.' The fourth sheet was blank, and he kept his palm on it. 'And four winters of nets, at a platform you hold the key to. I've written nothing down about that. I can.'

She weighed the four of them. Three were a day old, and none was in her writing.

'Passing yourself off as the holder of a public office goes to the prosecutor at Bayonne,' he said. 'I shan't send it. I've a month of commune left and I'd like it quiet.' He took a sheet of headed paper from the second drawer, turned it to face her and laid his own pen across it. 'Write.'

She took the pen in her left hand.

'I, the undersigned, Lesca Maylis, outdoor employee of the commune of Peyrebarthe and keeper of the key to its sluice.' He gave it to her at the pace of that hand, which was a child's. 'Undersigned is one word. Hereby resign my post, and the said key. With effect from.'

She stopped there.

'Leave it. Leave the width of a date, and sign under. The old way.'

She signed *M. Lesca*. The tall L came out of the wrong hand like a fence post set by a boy. It had taken six lines. She had cut that commune's verges since 2008, and it came to six lines and a gap.

He blotted the sheet and laid it in the folder, on the slip.

'It has no date, so it isn't a letter. It's a sheet of paper that I keep. On the day I date it, you resigned. Nobody dismissed you, so there's no hearing, and Hontaube doesn't take on a woman who left.' He tied the tape. 'You're the one who dates it. With a signature. With that gate, which moves when I say. With the Castagnet house. With the tone you take to me in my own square.'

'It wasn't me.'

'They tell me it had your coat on.' He drew his post towards him. 'Pau wants the road signs counted by Friday. Each panel with the commune's name on it, in the columns. Madame Ducasse has the form.'

He was reading before she had found the door handle with her left hand.
