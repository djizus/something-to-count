# N15, chapter 11, comic with dread. The second argument, on Thursday night. Joins: after N14, as the chapter's last scene.

* * *

That night Lesca was hardly in the chair before she stood up out of it, and Maylis was on her feet already.

Inguma was inside the door. It had come over the sill while they were still turning round, and it waited on the steel floor a few steps off, with a back to each of them.

Maylis had a bandage across her right palm.

'That's yours,' she said, and held it up. 'Monday. You took the handle out of its box to see what nineteen was like.'

'It stopped at nineteen. A council took note of that three times and nobody ever turned it to see. I turned it.'

'With my hand.'

'With the only one I had.' Lesca had a list of her own, and went down it. 'On Wednesday you sat in my chair and let Guy Larrère speak for eleven minutes. Eleven. I made a rule in 2015 so that he should never have four. You thanked him. You said Either to three of my councillors in my mother's kitchen. Tastet has it in his diary as consent, and the prefecture has a copy. You signed my post with your father's L.'

'You told Larrère he'd three minutes. In my square, through his window. He had me in his office on the Tuesday with the door shut. There's a letter in his folder now that says I resign. I wrote it. It hasn't a date on it.'

'He can't do that.'

'He's done it.'

'Then that one is mine, and I'll answer for it. I went over to stop a flood.'

'You told Castagnet he wasn't to go down on the Friday. He's going down on the Friday.'

'I heard him say so.'

'You had soup with her.'

'Yes.'

'She says you answered everything she asked. She liked it.'

'So did I.'

'What do you want?' Maylis said. It was not her question. It had been put to her for sixteen years, and she had never had the use of it.

Lesca answered it. She had waited a long time to be asked.

'Friday. I told your village, and your village said I was changing the subject. So I mean to be at that gate on Friday night myself, in your arms, and do it properly. And I want the bed after it, with her in it. I wrote that down the first morning. I can't stand here and tell you I don't.'

'Have Friday,' Maylis said. 'I'll have yours.'

'My what?'

'Your Saturday. The square, and the sash.'

Lesca began to explain why not, and found it out as she went. To have Friday she had to be the one still sitting when Maylis woke. Then she went home with Maylis, and Maylis was in there too, on that dyke, in the one pair of arms. For Maylis to have the Saturday, Maylis had to be the one still sitting. Then she went home with Lesca, to a dry bed.

'And the van?'

'Stands on the dyke with you laid back in it, and your eyes open, and nobody there.'

'It's one chair,' Maylis said.

'It's one chair. Somebody sits and somebody gets up. It never was an exchange.'

Inguma had come a step nearer the seat.

'On your night,' Maylis said. 'Did you know he was out?'

'No one came to tell him.'

'That's Either in a good coat.'

Lesca had nothing true to put against it, and in that room nothing else would come out of her mouth. Inguma went past her knee and got up into the chair.

'Then I'll sit,' Maylis said. 'It's where the sash is.'
