# N17, chapter 13, comic. The loto from underneath, in three parts round 07e. Part one joins after 07c, at the bar. Part two sits inside 07e, after "If you'd answered me in 2020.", in place of the exchange down to "Maylis rinsed a cup." Arlette's vote and "Who'd have been mayor?" go with it unless they are moved. Part three follows the ham of 07f and ends the chapter.

* * *

Maylis worked the loto from underneath.

The other one had come home with her at six that morning and stayed. On Friday night she had given Inguma every signature she had put to a paper as mayor, and a mayor, it seemed, signed a great deal. At eight in the evening Inguma was still counting, and Maylis stood behind Marie-Jo's bar as a passenger in her own arms.

The other one read the slate.

'The wine is under-priced,' she said, to the first six in the queue. 'At that the fête clears nothing once the ham is paid for. I propose to put it up. Those in favour?'

Nobody had been asked for a show of hands at a bar. Old Lafitte put his up slowly, to see what would happen.

'Against?'

Five hands went up.

'Then it's lost, and the wine stays where it is.' She did not seem to mind. She had put a thing to the vote, and that was what she had wanted.

'It's a bar,' Marie-Jo said, from the door. 'I didn't put you behind it to hold a sitting.'

'It was put,' said Lafitte. 'When it's put, you vote.' He had the score pad out.

Coralie came up the hall with her tray.

'Twelve coffees. Four with nothing in.' She looked at the crates, and then at the face. 'You carried those.'

'Two at a time. It's a rib, it isn't a leg. And I've a thing to say to you about Friday, and another about that landing.'

Maylis shut the jaw on the next word. She held it shut until her ears sang.

'Twelve,' she said. It was all she could get out.

'Which of you was that?' Coralie said. She waited for it, and was given her coffees.

* * *

'I'd have stood,' the mouth said.

Maylis heard it from underneath, in her own voice, a little low. In 2020 she had been on the mower, with the telephone going in her pocket.

Marie-Jo stopped with a pile of coins in her hand.

'I'd have said yes before you had the book shut,' the mouth went on. 'Third on the list, and the key handed in the same week. You'd have had your thirty-eight votes and some over, since half the Port votes for whoever keeps its gate. You'd be mayor. I'd have your accounts in order by now.'

'You were on the mower. I could hear it from the hall.'

'She was.'

'Who was?'

Maylis got the jaw. It took all she had.

'I was thinking,' she said.

Marie-Jo looked from the mouth to the coins. 'That's two answers,' she said. 'I waited five years for one.' She tipped the pile into the box uncounted, which nobody in that hall had seen her do.

* * *

Jeannot Castagnet stood with a card in each hand and the hall clapping him, and the other one looked at him.

Maylis could not get the eyes off him. She had tried all evening. Whenever he moved at his table they went to him, to his ears and his hands and the back of his neck where the collar rubbed, and they stayed. Lafitte had it on the pad twice.

Jeannot felt it. He came down the hall to the bar, and spoke to the hall.

'She's at it again. On Monday she tells me to my face that I'm to stay in my kitchen on the Friday. The eleventh, at night. She forbids it, like a doctor. All week she's looked at me as if I'd not long to go. So I'll say it to the lot of you, while you're sat. One. I've a hand of water, and I waited for it. Two. Nobody in that mairie has ever told me where I may sit. On Friday the eleventh I'm in my tonne. Come down and count me.'

The hall clapped that as well.

'Jean,' the mouth said. 'Don't.'

'It's Jeannot. Jean's for the stone.'

Then Inguma was on her breastbone, at the bar, with its leaves against the urn, for the length of one breath. It had done the signatures. When it got down the eyes were her own, and they went to his boots.

Kévin Labat stood at the end of the trestle with his beer.

'Which of you is that?' he said.
