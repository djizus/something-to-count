Then there was a windscreen with rain on it, and Lesca was in a right arm.

The van stood on the crown of the dyke with its nose to the gate. A telephone was ringing in the other one's left hand. The river went by under the bank, close.

Lesca had the right hand, and she sent it to the ignition. She meant to drive the track herself. It was a kilometre, and twelve years before she had not gone down it. She had the key between finger and thumb while the other one was still coming awake.

The hand came off the key. It went down to the belt, where the key of the handle box hung, and shut on that.

Lesca knew that reach. She had made it on that platform, with a telephone going back into her pocket. A home stood behind that bank with its back door on a yard, and Coralie was in it tonight with a list round her neck. The other one was going to the gate, and she was going for her.

Lesca took the hand back up and turned the key. The van was in gear. It jumped a metre and stalled, and threw the two of them onto the wheel with one cracked rib between them.

Four rings.

The other one took the wrist in her left hand and dragged it down. Lesca set the fingers as she had once set them on a handle with the skin off, and went back up. Under the bandage the weal opened. The heel of the hand came down on the horn, and it sounded once over the meadows.

Six. Seven.

She could hold. She had held with Inguma under her, and she could hold this arm till daylight. For as long as she did, a van would stand on a dyke with two women in one sleeve, and neither would drive a metre of that track. A man sat under a hatch at the end of it. He would be left a second time, and it would take both of them to do it.

Eight.

She had come for rain on the tiles, and a bed with the wall side slept in. She had come for a pillow that was due back across a landing at seven, carried by a woman who liked to be answered. She had come for a man at a counter with a glass of white, who owed her for diesel. She said them over, as the cost of a motion is read out before the vote. Not one of them would drive a van.

She let go.

The mouth did something with it as she went. She heard five words in the other one's voice, lower than its owner used it.

'Go to the tonne first.'

Then she had no hand, and no rain.
