At eight minutes to three on Saturday morning Maylis had her right hand back, and five words in her mouth that she had not put there.

'Go to the tonne first.'

It had been her own voice, lower than she used it. Somebody had gone out of that arm as the warmth goes out of a chair, and had left the words behind. The hand lay open on the wheel, with the bandage wet across the palm. The engine had stalled. The telephone was ringing in her lap, and she had counted eight.

The words told her nothing. She had been to the tonne first. At five that morning she had rung the man and let it ring nine times, until he answered and numbered her faults for her. At dusk she had walked his track against an order, twenty-five minutes on a cracked rib, and he had bolted his hatch on her. Nobody had sent her on either errand. If she went down that track a third time, she would go on those two.

The screen said 02:53. Under the time it said Castagnet, house.

She answered.
