She rang Coralie as she drove.

'It's going at the patch. Ten minutes. Start them now, and don't wait for a siren.'

'Where are you?'

'On the crown. Jeannot's in his tonne, with the hatch bolted. If I open the gate the river's on him in four minutes.'

'Then which do you want?' Somebody behind Coralie was calling a name. 'The gate, or Castagnet? Say which, Maylis. For once.'

'Both.'

The line stayed open, and Coralie said nothing down it.

'Start with the dyke end,' Maylis said, and dropped the telephone on the seat.

The van went off the crown onto the track at 02:57. At six hundred metres the track dipped. The nose went down and stayed down, and the back wheels dug until the van sat across the ruts on its axles.

02:59.

The telephone on the seat began to ring. It said G. Larrère. He had told her once more, and that she was to be in the van.

She was in it. Behind her, six hundred metres back, was the winch, with nineteen turns in it and forty houses under the bank. In front, four hundred on, was a hatch with a man under it. The bank had six of its ten minutes left, and on foot she could reach one of the two. If she went back she turned the handle on him, as the other one had, and the letter in the folder stayed without a date. If she went on, the river had the Port.

She had said both, and it had been a van's answer.

She got out, left the telephone ringing, and went on.
