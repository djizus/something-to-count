Lesca gave her no answer. The clock ticked over the telephone.

'No,' Maïté said. 'I've had nothing from you for twelve Decembers, and you won't give it me in my own kitchen. You've put it to a council as forty houses and a home. Tonight you've put Coralie in. I asked you which.'

Lesca had tried it aloud on Thursday night, on the edge of her bed, both ways.

'I don't know. I had the sum. I had all of it at once, with my hand on the handle, and I can't find which part of it turned.'

Maïté looked at her until she was sure that was the whole of it.

'I had a verb,' she said. 'For the stone. I stood up with it in that council, and your timer rang. It was *left*. He was left out there. I'd have had that cut under his name, and yours under it.'
