'There's one thing more,' Lesca said. 'A fortnight ago I was in her bed for twenty minutes. Her Coralie still slept in it. She took my wrist, and I turned the hand over and held hers. I told her to come back across the landing. I said it with the other one's mouth, and I meant this landing.'

'You asked her.'

'I'm asking you.'

Coralie kept her hand on the frame of her door.

'Wash your hands, Madame le maire. There's soup on the ring, and you'll eat it. Don't stand on this landing waiting for me. I've had one thing from you at a microphone and two on the stairs, and I'm taking an hour over them.' She had half turned to the box room. 'And you'll lie on your side. Go to bed, Maylis.'

Lesca washed her hands and ate the soup standing at the ring. She went up and lay down on her side, with her knees drawn up, facing the door. Across the landing the lamp stayed lit. She heard the church strike the half hour, and midnight.

Coralie came in with the pillow under her arm.
