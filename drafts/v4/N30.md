'Castagnet's told the Port where you went.' Coralie laid both hands flat on the oilcloth. 'I asked you down a telephone which you wanted, with the water at the back door. You said both. I know that word, Maylis. I've cooked it. That was Either, with the river in it.'

Maylis had no shorter word for it than hers.

Coralie took an envelope out of her tunic pocket, slit along the top, and laid it beside the pad.

'Bayonne's written. It came to the home on Friday. The geriatric floor, on nights. I start in January.'

'Stay.'

It was out before Maylis had weighed it.

'That's the first thing you've asked me for,' Coralie said. 'And I heard it on Friday too, at the back door. I said seven. At seven I was in a hall counting heads, and you were in it with me, and nobody was in the house.' Her voice held. 'I've asked you one question for sixteen years and drawn you boxes for it. I'll ask it once more, with no box. What do you want, Maylis?'

'She's to be buried here on Tuesday. I want to dig it myself.'

'Then you'll dig it. With that side. I'll strap it first.'

She fetched the box she kept from the home and strapped the rib where Maylis sat, three turns of the wide bandage, pulled tight. She tucked the end in, and her hand stayed on it a little longer than a bandage needs.

Then she went up. Maylis heard her cross the landing to the box room and come out of it. She heard her on each stair coming down, all eleven, slower than she had gone up. She heard the passage, and the front door, and the car door shut on a pillow.
