# N1, chapter 10, comic. Joins: at the mayor's desk in the afternoon, after the three telephone calls and before the medal is brought from the safe. The council follows.

* * *

Late in the afternoon Maylis asked Madame Ducasse for the flood service at Pau. At home that number was a recording.

Here it was answered at the second ring.

'Flood service.'

'It's the mairie at Peyrebarthe.'

'Madame le maire.' The young man's voice changed. 'Sorry. Yes. What can we do for you?'

'A hand gate, on flood meadows, with a winch. Who can have it opened by day, before a river's over the bank?'

'That's your rule, really. We've had it since 2014. Since your flood, Madame le maire.' He said it as a thing she would be glad to hear. 'A hand gate gets opened on the afternoon ebb, in daylight, once somebody's checked there's nobody out on the meadows. The order comes from the president of the landowners, in writing. Or it comes from the prefect, and that one goes down through the mayor.'

'And the keyholder?'

'She turns it. She doesn't get to decide it. That was sort of the point.' 'It's one page. I can send it over.'

'Send it.'

She put the receiver down. Over here the president and the mayor were two people. At home they were one man in a car, with an undated letter of hers in his folder.

Madame Ducasse stood in the doorway with the register against her chest.

'Which commune is Madame le maire asking for?' she said. 'Ours have had motors since 2016. There isn't a hand gate in Peyrebarthe.'

'A neighbour's,' Maylis said.

Madame Ducasse wrote nothing down. She waited to be told which, and then went to see about the page from Pau.
