The ham had been won an hour before, and Inguma had not come for Lesca. It had every kernel in the hall to count. At midnight Lesca was still driving Maylis's body, and she walked it up the stairs to the landing.

The box room door stood open, as it had since Friday. Coralie sat on the single bed in her tunic, taking off her watch.

'You said to ask, and you'd come back across,' Lesca said, in Maylis's voice. 'I'm asking.'

Maylis went for her own mouth, to say that it was not she who asked. She could not get one word into it.

Coralie came to the door.

'Which of you is that?'

'Lesca. The mayor. She's in the right hand, and she'd stop me if she could.'

'And what do you want?'

'You, in that bed, on the wall side. Mine took her pillow across a landing the winter after the flood, and I let her go. I have come to your door, since I did not go to hers.'

Coralie took the right wrist and laid two fingers on the inside of it. Under them the hand pulled, and she felt it pull.

'That's her,' she said. 'She can't get a word out, can she? I know that silence. I've lived on it.' She kept the fingers where they were. 'You answer. So I'll tell you a thing I've told nobody, since nobody asked. I want the geriatric floor at Bayonne, on nights. I've wanted it since March. I posted it this afternoon, and I posted it for me.'

'Then take it. I'll drive you in.'

Maylis heard her own voice give that in one breath. At the yellow box she had said how far it was to Bayonne. The cracked rib let her have no breath, and she could do nothing with her face.

'I'd have the one who answers,' Coralie said. 'I said so over a soup, and I'll say it on a landing. I'd have you tomorrow.' She let go of the wrist. 'But it's her house. It's her bed. She's in that hand hearing every word, and she hasn't asked me into it. I don't get into a bed over somebody who can't speak. I've lifted too many of them. You'll go down those stairs.'

'She won't ask. You'll wait till the home shuts.'

'Then I'll have waited for the right one.'

Lesca stood where she was. Maylis got the right hand out towards the door, as far as it would go, and that was all she got.

Coralie looked at the hand.

'Whose is that?'

'Hers,' Lesca said.

'Then she can say so in the morning.'

She shut the box room door. Lesca took Maylis's body down the stairs.
