Lesca sat down at the kitchen table with the writing pad. She wrote for the rest of the night, with Maylis's right hand, in a script that leaned.

*To the Prefect, at Pau. The undersigned holds the key to the sluice gate at Peyrebarthe. The Adour has risen at that gate each day since 24 November. By its readings it will pass the mark of the doorsteps on Tuesday, and on the night of Friday 11 December the old bank will come through at the Port. Under the rule of your flood service a hand gate is opened by day, on the afternoon ebb, by order. She asks for that order for Friday 11 December, with the meadows cleared.*

Maylis read each line as it came out of her own fingers. It was her gate and her river, and each sentence was true. It was also a letter over the mayor's head, from an employee whose resignation he held with no date on it.

At the last line Lesca's grip eased, and Maylis had the pen. She had it for as long as one word takes. She could have put a stroke through the sheet. She crossed a t in *gate*, low, where Lesca had left it bare. Then the pen was taken back.

*M. Lesca, keyholder.*

At first light Lesca walked it up to the square and held it in the slot of the yellow box. It dropped on top of Coralie's.

Inguma came for Lesca there, with its leaves against the yellow iron. When it got down, Maylis stood alone at the box with her hand empty.
