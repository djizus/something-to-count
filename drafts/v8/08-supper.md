Supper was soup at the kitchen table, and Coralie gave her orders for it from the doorway with the keys of the home in her hand.

'Madame le maire will sit down. She'll eat that while it's hot. She won't read at the table, and she won't tell me what the prefecture said until the plate's clean.'

Maylis sat down and ate.

There was no shopping pad on that table. Nobody had drawn her a box, and nobody was waiting to hear which she wanted. She had not had so easy a meal since she left her mother's.

Coralie stayed in the doorway and watched the spoon go up and down.

'You sat,' she said.

'You said to.'

'I've said to for eleven years. I say sit, and I'm given the code on mealtimes. I say eat, and I'm told what the council voted. I say lie on your side, and she tells me which side the rule was made for.' She came in as far as the range. 'That's the first order I've given in this house that got where it was going without an argument.'

In the right hand Lesca shut the fingers on the spoon. Maylis opened them and went on eating.

'I run a home,' Coralie said. 'They do as I say down there, and then I come up here to be answered back. Do you know what I'd give for a woman at this table who sat when she was told?'

'No.'

'The box room. I'd carry my pillow back across that landing tonight.' She put the keys down on the oilcloth, beside the bread. 'I don't know who's eating my soup. Her mother says somebody ate her bread this morning who said Either. I'd keep that one in the house.'

Lesca got the right hand as far as the edge of the plate. Maylis laid her left hand across it.

'It's good soup,' Maylis said.
