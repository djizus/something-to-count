A mayor, it turned out, signed a great deal, and at two Inguma had not come.

Lesca waited in the square, under the fourth plane tree, where she could see the Port road. Coralie's shift ended at two. A woman who waits about a square in the commune's waterproof is asked what she is doing, so Lesca had brought a board, and on it the form for the road signs that the mayor wanted counted.

She counted them as she did everything, in full. *Mairie, door, one panel. Church steps, one arrow, to the cemetery.* She wrote with Maylis's left hand, which was the one she had the use of, and each sign took her a line. In her own village those signs would go on saying what they said. Here the commune's name came down in January, and she was listing, for a man in a car, the places it came down from.

At the fourth line Maylis's right hand came out of the waterproof pocket in its bandage. Maylis took the pencil out of the left hand, made four upright strokes in the first column and put a bar through them. She laid the pencil back in the left.

Lesca read the strokes. Their father had counted bales so.

She wrote the fifth sign out in full, with its street. Maylis took the pencil and made a stroke.

Madame Ducasse had stopped at the foot of the mairie steps under her umbrella. She had filed that employee's chits for years, each with the tall L on it. She watched the commune's girl pass a pencil from one hand to the other and back, and write in two scripts on one form, and she let the umbrella drip on her shoes while she did.

A car came round the church and stopped by the yellow post box at the foot of the steps. Coralie got out in her tunic with her bag on her shoulder, saw the waterproof under the fourth tree, and stayed where she was.

Lesca took Maylis's legs across to her.

'It's the hospital,' Coralie said. She had an envelope out of her bag, and one corner of it had gone soft from being carried. 'The geriatric floor at Bayonne, on nights. I filled it in the week they told us the home shuts in June. I've walked it to this box four times and walked it home, to see what you'd say. I'm not asking today.'

Lesca had a great deal to say to that, and opened Maylis's mouth to begin. Maylis took the mouth from her for one sentence.

'It's twenty minutes to Bayonne.'

'Yes,' Coralie said. 'That's what I thought you'd say.' She had seen that mouth open for more than a measurement, and her eyes stayed on it a moment longer.

She held the envelope in the slot by its corner. She let it go, and it dropped into the yellow box by the church.
