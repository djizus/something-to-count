Lesca pushed the lever into gear with the left hand and let the clutch up, and the van went. It ran along the crown in first, past the gate. At the head of the track Lesca put the wheel over. The headlamps swung out across the meadows and down the ruts, and at the far end a round reflector burned red.

Five rings. Six.

She had sat behind that view once, and had reached for a telephone. This time she kept her foot down.

Maylis's right hand came off the belt and knocked the lever out of gear. The van rolled a length and stalled where the track left the crown. It threw the two of them onto the wheel, and the heel of Maylis's right hand came down on the horn, which sounded once over the meadows.

Seven.

* * *

'Lesca, Maylis,' the voice called from the far end, as it gave the numbers.

The place it called her to was laid at the second table, and the chair next to it was taken. Jean Castagnet sat there in two jackets, a card in each hand.

She went to him and stopped at his shoulder. He did not look up from his numbers.

'I saw your van,' she said. 'I rang once.'

He put a kernel on a card.

Her hand was on the back of the empty chair. Her knees bent for it, as far as the seat.
