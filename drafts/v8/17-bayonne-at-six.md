Before six on Saturday Inguma set Lesca down. Behind her, in the hall, the voice called a number, whole and then in its parts.

'Twelve. One, two.'

A chair was pulled in to a table.

The strip lights over her now belonged to a corridor. She lay on a stretcher against its wall, under a blanket with the hospital's name on it, and Coralie's two fingers were on the inside of her wrist.

'I came in at five to three,' Coralie said. 'Your eyes were open, and I rang, as I said I would. Bayonne has had you three hours and found nothing, which I could have told them.' She kept the fingers where they were. 'Lie still, Madame le maire. They'll bring a form to say you're leaving against advice, and you'll sign it. I've got the car. You've a square at eleven.'

Lesca signed it lying down. They drove back with the heater on, and were in the square by half past ten.
