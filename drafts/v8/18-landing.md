Lesca spoke first.

'There's more, and it wasn't in the speech. Last night I was at the old gate over there, in the other one's right arm. I held that arm for eight rings of a telephone. When I let go I said five words with her mouth. Go to the tonne first. She went. While she was on the track the dyke came down into the Port, and into the home at the end of it. They carried forty-one out. Madame Etcheto drowned in her bed, in room twelve.'

Coralie took hold of her door.

'I wheeled her up this morning. She had the end of the row.'

'I read a metre from her. In 2014 I kept the river off that room. Last night I sent it there. I wanted you to have it from me, and not through a wall.'

'Was she in her bed?'

'Yes.'

Coralie asked nothing about the gate.

'There's a second thing,' Lesca said. 'Over there you still slept in that bed. Twice I asked you back across a landing, with the other one's mouth. The first time you asked who I was. The second time you said you'd have me, and you sent me down the stairs, since it was her house.'

'Twice in a week,' Coralie said. 'It's four steps from your door to mine, Madame le maire. They've taken you eleven years. Since you're telling me what you wanted over there, you can have one of mine. I didn't want those keys. They gave me the home because I'd been on that night, and it needed a face. I wanted my tunic back, and earlies, and somebody else's name on the office door. That's eleven years of wanting a thing that wasn't you.'
