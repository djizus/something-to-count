On Monday at nine Lesca laid a telephone on a desk at Bayonne. It had lain in a drawer since the winter of the flood, and its screen was dark.

The lieutenant who had come to the square for the traffic had a form in front of her. She did not say Madame le maire.

'In your own words, Madame Lesca. I'll stop you where I need a time.'

Lesca gave it in order, as she had given it to a council each December, and went on past the place where the council's version ended. The lieutenant stopped her twice. She wanted the hour of the order, and how long the telephone had rung.

'Once.'

'In seconds.'

'I didn't count them,' Lesca said. She had counted everything else that night.

The lieutenant turned the form round. Near the foot was a line with two boxes. *Did the person heard attempt to warn the victim? Yes. No.*

'One ring is an attempt,' Lesca said. 'It's also the whole of one. The form needs a third box.'

'It has two.'

Lesca did not leave boxes empty. She took the pen and ticked *No*. She signed under it with the stroke that came back and crossed itself. The lieutenant put the telephone in a bag, and wrote a number on the bag.
