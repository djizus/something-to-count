At nine minutes to three on Sunday Inguma sat on Maylis once more. She lay still under it and was in the chair with Lesca, and they got up out of it and faced each other.

The fourth wall was steel again. Inguma waited inside the door.

'Forty-one,' Maylis said. 'There were forty-two in the beds. Coralie counted them in the hall, in their blankets, and the one who wasn't there was Madame Etcheto. Room twelve. She was in her bed.'

'I heard her chair pulled in,' Lesca said. 'I'll say mine before you ask for it. Your telephone began to ring at nine minutes to three. You had your arm back at seven minutes to. I held it for eight rings, because I wanted the van, and your bed after it. You were two hundred metres short of the dyke when it went. Those two minutes are your two hundred metres, and they're mine.'

'Two minutes for my bed,' Maylis said. 'She paid for it with hers.'

'Yes.'

'The van in the dip was mine. So was both.' Maylis wiped her face with her sleeve. 'So you were right, that night. Turned first, it keeps them all. The houses, the home, the one in room twelve. I've been in that room now. I'd sign your sum.'

'No. You won't sign it, and I'll say why at length. I was right about the houses. I can show you that on a file, to the centimetre. The file stops at the platform. It doesn't say that I turned the van and put its headlamps down the track. It doesn't say that I knew whose van was at the end of that track. I took out a telephone, and I let it ring once, and I put it in my pocket and took hold of the handle. No dyke made me ring once. If you had turned that winch first on Friday, you'd have been right about the houses too. You'd have drowned him, and you'd be me. You rang nine times. You walked his track at dusk, and you ran it in the night. A woman is dead of that, and it still isn't what I did. Don't hand me your sum to sit behind. I've had my own for twelve years.'

Maylis let that be. She had one question left that was not about either of them.

'Why did it rain? There, that year, and not here. And now here.'

'I don't know. I said it was mine, and you had the dates against me.'

'I don't know either.'

They left it there.

'Yours gives orders,' Maylis said. 'I sat down to her soup when I was told to, and she said she'd carry her pillow back for a woman who did that. Sit down when she tells you.'

'Yours said she'd have the one who answers. She had two fingers on your wrist when she said it. Answer her.'

'And your Saturday?'

'I said it at eleven. Into a microphone, in front of the square, a metre from his widow. The van, the one ring, and a verb for the stone. They've had the medal back since, and the keys.'

'What was the verb?'

'Drowned.'

The dry noise began on the steel floor. Inguma did not come to the chair. It crossed to the door, which opened for it, and went out over the sill, and the door shut behind it on its rubber seal.

The chair was empty between them. Neither of them sat in it.

Maylis woke under her own lampshade, in a bed with one pillow, and the clock read 02:54. On Monday she lay awake at that minute to see, and the top of the wardrobe was bare.
