# The night of the twelfth of December 2014

It was the nineteenth day of rain, and the river had risen above the doorsteps of the houses beside it.

A dyke kept the river off them. The dyke was a wall of clay as high as a house, and forty houses lay under it, each one shuttered, each one full of people asleep. At the end of the row a care home showed one light on its stairs.

A woman drove past them all to where the road ended, and got out in the rain.

Water was coming through the dyke. It ran down the clay in a sheet the width of a door and crossed the road in her headlamps. She laid her hand flat on the dyke beside the water, and the clay moved under her palm.

She had mended that dyke each spring for six years. She gave it ten minutes.

Up on the crown of the dyke there was a sluice gate, and beside the gate a platform, and on the platform a winch. She drove up to it and climbed onto the planks and put her torch on the river. The water went by at the height of her knees, close enough to touch. On her other side the flood meadows lay a long way down, dark and empty, as far as the torch reached.

She took the handle of the winch out of its box and fitted it to the shaft.

Her hand lay on the handle while she counted three breaths, and the handle stayed where it was. She climbed down to the van and turned it on the crown until its headlamps pointed out over the meadows, along the one track that crossed them.

She looked down that track for as long as the wipers took to cross the glass twice.

When she took out her telephone the number was already in it. She pressed it, and held the telephone to her ear, and the rain ran off her hood.

It rang once.
