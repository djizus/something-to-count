# The middle: chapters 6 to 10

## The spine

One fight in three rounds. Lesca takes Maylis's body for a day (6), is answered in kind (8), and comes back to keep it (9). Beaten in daylight by the man she is trying to keep alive, Maylis hires the woman she threw out (10).

The plan to open the gate by day changes at each step: told (6), found to need Larrère twice over (8), written over his head (9), asked for aloud and voted down (10).

Cut: Tuesday's ride, Thursday's meeting and council, the yellow box, Sarthou's microphone, the café minute.

For the writer:

- A ride is told from the passenger: Maylis in 6 and 9, Lesca in 8. The driver is known by what the body says and does.
- Said once, at the head of 6: the visitor has the body; the owner keeps the right hand and can snatch one word.
- Each sentence with an act names who acts. "The right hand" is always the owner's.
- Each count gets a size on a clock, aloud. Each change of day opens on weekday and date.

## 6. Maylis: Monday 30 November, her village, Lesca driving

**River.** A metre and a half under the line at eight; a metre forty by night.

**Aim.** With the one hand left to her, keep the mayor off Guy Larrère and off Coralie until Inguma fetches her.

**Stops her.** Lesca, with Maylis's mouth and legs.

**Reversal.** Coralie likes her. Waiting to be fetched is no longer enough: the wife would have the visitor back.

**Last line.** Inguma has taken Lesca at the foot of the stairs. Coralie, not knowing it, asks the woman who ticked the box: "Are you coming up?"

**Scenes.**

1. *Kitchen, six.* Lesca ticks the supper box and orders the home's director to "my office". Coralie asks for the rest. Lesca opens the mouth to give it; Maylis spends her word: "Either."
2. *The gate, eight.* Lesca tries the winch, to learn whether these arms can raise it alone. Eighteen clicks lift the gate a hand. At nineteen it binds, and wants a shoulder and both arms. The right arm is not hers. The handle runs back through Maylis's fist: the weal.
3. *Larrère's car, nine.* Three minutes. Lesca gives him Tuesday the eighth, the yellow from Pau, and the two sentences he will say. The window goes all the way down. He asks what she said to Maïté. The morning goes in a file.
4. *Café, noon. Comic.* "Still in the box, were you?" Lesca answers it as a question. Lafitte heads the pad. She gets as far as the date and "one man" before Josiane has it for adultery. Jeannot swears to go down on the eleventh because she has said it. Maylis gets the jaw: "Put it in forty-two."
5. *Soup, seven.* Maylis tips the spoon with the right hand, twice. Lesca orders the home out of its beds by two on the eleventh, walkers first. Coralie laughs: five answers with one soup, and she would have this one again.
6. *Stairs, five past nine.* Fifteen hours for a lake; Lesca says so, and that she will bring more. She walks the legs to the stairs. Maylis takes the newel post in the right hand and holds her own body down. The cold comes down the stairs past Coralie.

## 7. Maylis: Tuesday 1 December, her village

**River.** A metre thirty under at first light. A metre twenty at dusk, and the patch on the bank dark to the width of a door.

**Aim.** To have Monday taken back: by the mayor at nine minutes to three, by Larrère at nine.

**Stops her.** Larrère, with a folder. He uses it three times before dark.

**Reversal.** He wants no apology. The sheet is ready, and what frightens him is the Castagnet kitchen, not the forecast. By dusk she means to use the mayor's desk, not undo the mayor's day.

**Last line.** "At six no alarm rang. In the other village the mayor woke, and Maylis went with her, and left her own body beside Coralie with its eyes open."

**Scenes.**

1. *Steel room, 02:51.* Both stand, and Maylis is angry first: the three minutes, the man now sworn to go down, the stairs. "If it hadn't come?" "I'd have gone up." Lesca answers at length and is wrong, then says she is coming to stay. Inguma takes the chair.
2. *Bedroom, six.* Coralie dresses the palm, gets "Either", and knows who she has.
3. *Larrère's office, nine.* He dictates her resignation, left-handed, with a gap for the date: a sheet of paper that he keeps. Under it lies a blank sheet: four winters of nets at her platform, written down, would send Kévin Labat's name to the prosecutor. The gate moves when he says. She keeps away from the Castagnet house. At three she tells Sarthou the association ordered the gate of the 23rd.
4. *Sarthou's kitchen, three. Comic.* The bank fills his window. Mayor or president? "Either." Either is not a person and cannot be summoned. So the one name in Jeannot's complaint is hers, and Hontaube reads it in January.
5. *Café, four.* She does to Jeannot what was done to her: the nets under his duckboards, against his complaint. He leaves his glass.
6. *Dusk.* His van goes out along the track. A car without lamps stops at the fourth back gate.
7. *Night.* The rota gives Coralie a late on Wednesday, so no alarm. Maylis says the words herself and gives Inguma every metre of verge she has cut since 2008: a working day. When the warmth beside her stirs, she keeps her forearms on the steel.

## 8. Lesca: Wednesday 2 December, her village, Maylis driving

**River.** None here: frost, and a gravel lake. Her file for this date in 2014 reads a metre ten under: the other river today.

**Aim.** With the right hand and nothing else, keep the commune's girl from giving away the mayor's Saturday: the speech at the stone, ten days off.

**Stops her.** Tastet with his diary, Larrère at her own table, and Maylis in her mouth.

**Reversal.** Her own rule, made in 2015 so that no keyholder should choose alone in the dark again. Read out at her table, it gives a gate to the president, or to the prefect through the mayor, and over there both are Larrère. Friday will have to be done at night, by her.

**Last line.** Larrère: "She's voted herself onto the platform. A metre from that stone, with a camera on her. I'll have my answer there."

**Scenes.**

1. *Arlette's kitchen, ten past seven.* Coralie orders the mayor up, and Maylis obeys. Tastet asks whether the mayor will speak on the twelfth or stand. Lesca goes for the mouth. "Either." He writes it down as consent. Arlette no longer knows who is eating her bread.
2. *The committee's minute, ten.* Maylis laughs in the silence for Jean Castagnet, at a living man's letter. Maïté walks out, and is not to be kissed.
3. *Soup, six.* Coralie orders; Maylis sits and eats. For a woman who sat when she was told, Coralie would carry her pillow back tonight. Lesca gets the right hand to the edge of the plate. The left covers it.
4. *Council, half past six. Comic.* The medal is laid out and Maylis looks past it at the timer. "I want that." It is in the inventory. She asks who orders a gate, and Madame Ducasse reads the rule. Larrère will be brief. Lesca's hand has the dial; Maylis pins the wrist. Eleven minutes, no bell. The keyholder cannot put a bell on the one man who can order her gate.
5. *The stroke of seven.* The verges are counted: thirteen hours. Inguma sits on the mayor in her chair, before fourteen councillors, and takes Maylis home. Lesca is whole. Tastet moves that the prefect speak alone. Larrère seconds, and she sets the bell on him. "When you stood on that platform, did you—" It rings. Seven and seven, with her own vote.

## 9. Maylis: Wednesday 2 to Sunday 6 December, her village, Lesca driving from Saturday

**River.** A metre ten under on Wednesday. Fifty-five under on Saturday at three, with the dip in the track ankle-deep. Forty under on Sunday.

**Aim.** Never to lie still under Inguma again; and when the mayor is in her anyway, to get her fetched.

**Stops her.** Lesca. And Madame Etcheto, out of kindness.

**Reversal.** At the bar Maylis shuts her own jaw on the words and wins. Across the hall the old woman who taught her them says them for her. Anybody can send Inguma off counting, so the mayor need never be fetched. It will take a second pair of hands.

**Last line.** "Coralie was on earlies all week. Her alarm would go at six on five mornings before Friday, and each time it would wake Maylis first."

**Scenes.**

1. *Bedroom, Wednesday, seven.* Coralie has the doctor's number under her thumb. Thirteen hours. "Where have you been?" "At work." She takes her pillow to the box room, and will come back if she is asked. Two nights Maylis pushes Inguma off. On the third the rib has no push in it, and lying under it without pushing is lying still.
2. *The track, Saturday, three.* Lesca walks the body to the tonne with Maylis's watch. The rib stops her running. She does not knock. Twenty-five minutes, against the ten the bank will have.
3. *The loto bar, eight. Comic.* Marie-Jo: "Still in the box, were you?" The mouth gives each customer a sentence too many and the right hand gives the change. Lafitte asks what Castagnet was given to withdraw a complaint; the hall looks at the bar; Monsieur Daguerre calls "Here." Jeannot wins the ham and answers with the hall seated: on the eleventh he is in his tonne. The cold comes against the trestle. "Inguma, enauk—" Maylis shuts the teeth. Over her duck, Madame Etcheto finishes the sentence and gives it the maize in the hall: till morning.
4. *The landing, midnight.* In Maylis's voice: "You said to ask. I'm asking." "Which of you is that?" "The one who's asking." True word for word, and meant to pass. Coralie comes half across with the pillow, lays two fingers on the right wrist, and feels it pull. She tells the visitor about Bayonne, would have her tomorrow, and sends her down the stairs: it is the other one's bed.
5. *Kitchen.* Lesca writes to the prefect over the mayor's head, left-handed. The right hand crosses a t she left bare. Posted at first light.
6. *Breakfast. The worst.* Lesca answers all that Coralie asks. "I said I was coming to stay. When it comes, I say the words again." The cold comes under the door. "Inguma, enauk—" The right hand goes over the mouth. The left takes that wrist to pull it off. Coralie has one of each in front of her, and holds the left one down on the oilcloth. Round the fingers: "Friday." Inguma sits. Maylis has her mouth. Asked what she says to Bayonne, she gives the distance.

## 10. Maylis: Tuesday 8 December, her village

**River.** A hand under the line at eight. At the line at three. Still there at 02:51 on Wednesday, with the patch weeping across the road.

**Aim.** To ask for the gate herself, out loud, today: open on Friday at two, on the ebb, so that nobody need come and do it in her arms.

**Stops her.** Larrère, presiding from his car. Then Jeannot's hand.

**Reversal.** She says which, for once, and is beaten by the man she is trying to keep alive, with the grudge she gave him. That night she unwinds the strapping and hires the woman she threw out.

**Last line.** Lesca: "She's on nights on Friday. Who holds my hand down then?"

**Scenes.**

1. *Bedroom, six.* Two nights strapped on her side at the edge, and nothing sat. Coralie, at the door: the rota is changed, and she is on nights from Friday.
2. *The pier, three.* The tide lifts the water onto her father's paint. Below, the patch lets a thread across the road to Sarthou's step.
3. *The square.* Larrère, at the café door, says his two sentences. The card table goes quiet. Lafitte reads Monday's entry back off the pad while the window is still down. A petition goes round on a leaf of it.
4. *The association, in the café, four. Comic, then not.* Six members at the card table, the president in his car at the open door. Sarthou requisitions the commune's sand and bills it for his labour. Maylis asks, and it is not Either. Larrère holds the folder to the glass. She says it again. He writes Kévin's name on the blank sheet. Three and three. Castagnet, for the hunters, has the last hand and puts it up for shut: he will not be told what is under his boards and then how to vote.
5. *Kitchen, night.* She writes the handover on the back of the pad, in pencil, from the key on the belt to *paid*. Coralie reads it. She held that woman's hand down on this table, and finds herself in it as a distance. Under the week she draws one box: *Friday 11th, night*.
6. *Steel room, 02:51.* Maylis unwinds the strapping and lies on her back. She opens the sitting. One night: the gate, the winch, the telephone; then home; and for it the mayor's chair when she asks. She says what a life in one hand is. Lesca answers at length and cannot lie: she will not go. "Did you know he was out?" "No one came to tell him." "That's not a no." The wall goes: the dead at their loto. Maylis walks to the one empty place and reads the card: LESCA, MAYLIS. Lesca says whose: the one Inguma cannot fetch is found in her bed and sits there. If she stays it is hers, and she enters it as *nil*. Inguma is in the chair when Lesca asks her question.

## Across the five

- **Where they fall.** The body for good, 9.6, prepared by 6.6 and 9.3. Daylight lost to a person, 10.4. The red line, 10.2.
- **Rules.** The three, and no others. The rib closes the side to Maylis. A count set by somebody else is already in 12 and 13. The place at the table is Madame Etcheto's saying from 3.
- **Neighbours.** *All of Me* shares a body for laughs; here the owner has one hand and the wife holds the other down. *Dark Matter* fools the wife; here, for the width of a landing.

## The hand-off: true at the start of 11

- **Maylis.** Her own since Sunday. Rib strapped, weal closing, key on the belt. The handover is on the back of the pad, unstruck: she lends her arms on Friday and has a sash for it. She has not been told the night.
- **Larrère holds** the undated letter, the sheet with Kévin's name, and her name alone in Sarthou's file. Pau sends him her letter on Wednesday. She saw his car on the first and has not said so.
- **Jeannot.** Forbidden on the Monday, squeezed on the Tuesday; answered at the loto; hand up on the eighth. He will not take her word. "Forty-two" has been said three times.
- **The village.** The pad has its heading; the café says she has a mayor in her. Sarthou has the sand. Madame Etcheto said the words over her duck. The track was walked on Saturday.
- **Coralie.** In the box room since the second, the offer standing. She has met Lesca, sent her down the stairs and held her hand down. Bayonne is posted and told. Nights from Friday, second on a list that sends walkers first. The Friday box is drawn.
- **Lesca.** Seen gone three times: 28 and 30 November, 5 December. Voted onto her platform; not to be kissed. Stopped once at the mouth, so in 12 she means to sit on it. She knows what staying costs.
- **Between them.** One night, paid, against "I'm coming to stay". Inguma has taken the chair three times: 5, 7, 10.

## The one change after 10

Chapter 13, at the car window. If the gate moves before he rings, the letter gets tomorrow's date and the sheet with Kévin Labat's name goes to the prosecutor. The pension lines go. The brake on Friday is then the boy who comes to the home that night with two mattresses.
