"Keep" means the text stands apart from the line pass. A figure after a keep is the length it is cut to. New scenes are N1 to N31, described in section 2 with their writer.

One change is behind most of the others. The two women stop being polite. From chapter 14 each rides in the other's body, by day, for hours, and each wants the other's life until the last night.

**The line pass.**

- No noun is withheld. "The word", "the item", "the other" and any "it" whose noun is more than two sentences back are replaced by the noun. "Drowned" is said where it is meant.
- "Had" by half. Possession for perception stays only where she is held and has her eyes alone. Back-history goes into the mouth of someone arguing, or into an object in the room.
- Three "as a man would" similes stay: the door he hopes is locked, the card he hoped was another, and the sack of maize. Two of the duration kind stay.
- The numbers that stay are 02:51, nineteen, forty houses, forty-two, forty-one, and ten against twenty-five. Letter numbers stay. Everything else is rounded or cut.
- Each gloss comes once in the book, at rest, never on an entrance. The second village inherits the first one's.
- The rib is mentioned only when it changes what she can do. The waders are for chapter 1 and the last line.
- The two Coralies differ by ear. The one who runs the home gives instructions and keeps the title to the end. The other asks questions and has "Don't give me the measurement".
- "I'll not" and "it wants" belong to Jeannot, Arlette, Lafitte and Madame Etcheto. Kévin, Coralie, Bixente and outsiders contract in the ordinary way. Sarthou talks like a report.
- The chorus on Maylis is two voices, Coralie's and Marie-Jo's.
- The tag breaking is left to Jeannot and Larrère. For Maïté and Kévin the sentence that points at it goes.
- Half the "did not" sentences go. Interrupted speech takes one mark throughout, the one chapter 1 uses.
- One form each for maire and for the crown of the bank, taken from chapter 1. Dates in one form. Written words in italics.
- "Wrote before she moved" twice in the book. The way in is a clause after chapter 4. Three chapters end on something written, 6, 12 and 14, and no others.
