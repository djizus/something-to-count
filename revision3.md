# Third revision plan

Twenty-two chapters, 58,400 words. Old scenes are named from the current `manuscript/`, by chapter and letter in the order of the section breaks: 14a is the first scene of chapter 14. "Keep" means the text stands apart from the line pass. A figure after a keep is the length it is cut to. New scenes are N1 to N30, described in section 2 with their writer.

Three changes carry the rest.

- The first crossing is the last line of chapter 5. From chapter 6 to the last night, every chapter has one woman in the other's village, or meeting in public what the other did with her body.
- The two women talk. If both stand up out of the chair they see each other, and neither can lie to the other. They argue three times before the last night.
- Nothing is sent. The medal, the date and the five words rehearsed by day are cut, with the lamps, the alarm at four and the numbered findings. What crosses is a woman.

**The line pass, in this order.**

1. The pluperfect comes back. The first verb of every step back in time takes "had". The year is in the first sentence of the book.
2. Each voice has its own idiom. Sarthou speaks reports and makes no remarks. Madame Ducasse guards the register, and the two of them collide once. Maïté and Arlette are plain. The dry last word is for Coralie and Jeannot. Larrère has his refrains. The woman at Hontaube keeps her trays, and the young man loses half his lines.
3. Maylis weighs, paces and prices what she meets, to the last page, and her answers are short and wrong. Lesca is long and certain, and minutes everything.
4. People are surprised out of their habit as well as into it. Sarthou, Marie-Jo, Tastet and Madame Ducasse each break once.
5. Halve "be minuted", "They tell me", "Your father" and the handbag. Three shrugs. "I'm not asking" twice before 11d and never plainly after. "Sixteen years" in chapters 1, 13 and 22 only.
6. Committees keep the clipped step. The run, the night of 2014 and the square take long sentences.
7. The narrator does not keep score. Two "first" or "only" sentences stay in the book. No recap stands in front of a payoff, and no tag after a beat that has landed.
8. No bulletins of feeling. The fear goes into the body, as at Coralie's fingers in 14a.
9. "For some time", "watched her do it", speech addressed to objects and looks at radiators go. Each gesture has one owner. Half the habit clauses and half the durations go.
10. Glosses come through somebody who needs telling, with a joke on them. The pawl is shown once, in chapter 5, by what it does.
11. The five sentences the prose critic lists are rewritten. One spelling of smelled, and one place for the lettering on the waterproof.
