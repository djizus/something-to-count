# Fourth plan: a repair

The order of the twenty-two chapters stands. Scenes are named from the current `manuscript/`, by chapter and letter. The book goes from 57,300 words to about 56,400.

Three things are fixed, in place. The crossing gets three rules, said plainly. The middle gets one plan, which fails five times. The two women meet once more and finish.

## 1. The rules card

Three rules. Everything else that reads as a rule is cut.

1. **One chair.** When Inguma sits on them at nine minutes to three and they lie still, both women are in the one chair in the steel room. If they stand up, they can see each other and talk, and neither can lie.
2. **Whoever stays goes with her.** When one of them wakes, she goes home. If the other is still sitting, she wakes up inside the first one's body, in the first one's village, and drives it. The owner is still there and can snatch back a hand or a word. The visitor's own body lies in her own bed with its eyes open.
3. **Inguma fetches.** Inguma always comes and takes the visitor home. It stays away only while it is counting what she gave it to count.

**Who states them.** Lesca, in 7c, as the first business of the first meeting. She states motions for a living, and she numbers these one to three, with that morning as the example. Madame Etcheto has already given the half of rule 3 that is hers, in 3d. The narrator says rule 2 flat in 6a, straight after it happens, in ordinary words: who stayed sitting, whose body she woke in, what her own was doing, what brought her back.

**Where each is restated, in one sentence, before it is used.**

- Rule 2: the head line of 8, 10, 13 and 14. In 11e, before the trade. In 17g, before she is held.
- Rule 3: 8a, at the lake. 13, where each kernel put down is one less. 18c, at Arlette's ponies.
- Rule 1: the first line of 11e and of 15a, and the first line of N5.

**Cut.** Kévin's five don'ts, the forum as a source of rules, and the man in Limoges. Kévin keeps the finger count and "I'd fly". "Don't get up", "don't leave it the chair" and "nothing twice" go. Inguma taking the empty chair is what ends a talk, and is shown, not explained. "It never was an exchange" becomes two plain sentences from rule 2: if Maylis stays sitting on the Friday night, she wakes in Lesca's dry bed, and her own body lies in the van with its eyes open and nobody at the gate.

**The hall, in one sentence.** It is the village's dead at their loto, with one place laid for whoever dies that night. The doubled figures go. Lesca's father sits there with his watch, and Jeannot Castagnet in two jackets.

## 2. Who is driving

**The head line.** Every chapter in which one woman is inside the other opens on one sentence in a fixed order: the weather, whose village, whose body, who is driving, and what Inguma was given to count. For 8: "On Monday it rained, and Lesca woke in Maylis's bed and drove Maylis's body, with Maylis awake in the right hand, for as long as Inguma took to count a lake." The same for 10, 13 and 14, and for the cab in 18e.

**Names.** In narration the two are Maylis and Lesca, everywhere. "The other one" stays only in speech and in a thought that is plainly hers, and means one woman for the whole chapter. "The mayor", "the mouth", "the jaw", "its owner" and "the relief" are replaced by the name. Half of the reports from inside the shared body go. Each one that stays has a witness and a line.

## 6. The line pass

To be applied chapter by chapter, in this order.

1. **No shorthand.** Any sentence about the chair, the bodies or a plan must be clear at one reading to somebody who has skipped the chapter before. "Standing", "sit", "was given" and "the relief" are replaced by what they mean.
2. **Names.** Maylis and Lesca in narration. See section 2.
3. **No meaning after the scene.** Cut every sentence that says what a scene meant or that a thing had never happened before. Two "never before" lines stay: the car window in 8 and Marie-Jo's coins in 13.
4. **One telling.** An event is told once, and retold only by the person it costs most, and only if the retelling changes it. Lafitte's pad stays, and Larrère's reading in 15d at three items.
5. **"She meant to."** Keep it in 17g, where the next line breaks it. Elsewhere end the section on the last thing done.
6. **Numbers.** The ones that stay are 02:51, nineteen, forty houses, forty-two, forty-one, ten against twenty-five, the gauge and the clock. Every other figure is cut or made vague. In 16, 17 and 19 no other number is on the page.
7. **Signature phrases once.** Inguma's descent, "lower than she used it", the shut right hand, "glass of white", "tails to the river", "hand on the frame". 02:51 and the two fingers on the wrist stay exact.
8. **Refrains halved.** "I'll be brief" at the four places the comedy report lists. "Through the gap" in chapter 5 only. "Either" is silent from 10 until Coralie's last use of it.
9. **Sentence shape.** Half the "It was" and "She had" openers go, and half the trailing "with" clauses. One paragraph in three ends on something other than a short turn. In 8, 17 and 18 the syntax follows the event, as it does in 19.
10. **Endings.** No two chapters running end on a question about who she is. The endings of 10, 12 and 14 are the river's.
11. **Questions answered.** A question that ends a chapter is answered at the top of the next chapter in that village, if only with an evasion that costs her.
12. **No glossary in a mouth.** Each gloss is one narrator clause.
13. **Idiom by age.** "I'll not" and "I've a" belong to those over sixty. Kévin, Bixente and the clerks speak as people do now. "Telephone" stays for the old, and Jeannot has a radio.
14. **One job simile for each character.**
15. **Three plain sentences of feeling.** What Maylis thinks of the woman who rang once, at the end of 16a's replacement. What Lesca thinks as she lets go, in 18e. What Lesca thinks when Maylis says forty-one, in N5.
16. **Usage.** Letter numbers in words, except *No. 41* and *No. 42*. Dates in words in narration and speech, and in figures in documents. "The waterproof" in 13. Seven days, in 12.
