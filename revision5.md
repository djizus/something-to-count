# Fifth plan: the cut

The book goes from 57,500 words to about 43,200, in seventeen chapters. Present chapters 1 to 14 (35,400 words) become nine chapters (21,400). Present chapters 15 to 22 keep their text and become chapters 10 to 17.

Scenes are named by present chapter and letter. A figure is the length a scene is cut to. No scene is new.

## 5. What does not fit

The brief's figures cannot all be met. Nine chapters of 2,300 words, with chapter 1 at half its length, hold 19,800 at most. With the last eight chapters kept, that is 41,300 words, under the floor of 44,000 and close to the prize's 40,000.

This plan keeps the nine chapters and lets six of them run to between 2,500 and 2,800. It lands 800 short of 44,000. To reach it, restore in this order: the roof in 2f (300), the whole of the counter in 12c (350), Marie-Jo's doorstep in 14e (390). Each is a scene a critic skimmed.

To hold the cap of 2,300, drop in this order: 6c, the nets in 3a, half of 7d and 7e, 10c, 12c, 13e. That gives 40,700.
