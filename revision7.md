# Seventh plan: the mechanism and the first three chapters

For editors, to apply in place. Scenes are those of the current `manuscript/`, by chapter and letter. The book goes from 45,200 words to about 44,000.

## 1. The three rules

1. **One chair.** At nine minutes to three Inguma sits on them both, and if they lie still they are in one chair, where each sees what is in the other's head. If they stand up they can talk, until Inguma sits down in the chair.
2. **The one who wakes first carries the other.** If one is still sitting when the other wakes, she wakes up inside her and does the driving. The owner keeps her right hand.
3. **Inguma fetches.** It brings the visitor home as soon as it has finished counting what it was given. A visitor it cannot fetch dies in her own bed.

Nothing else is a rule. "Neither can lie" goes. Who was awake when Inguma came goes. Counting slows the fetching and does nothing else.

**Who says them.** Madame Etcheto, in 3b, to Maylis, as what her mother had of it, in four short sentences after "It comes to that bed". When it sits on two at once it has them in one seat. The one who wakes first takes the other home in her. It comes to fetch that one back when it has done its counting. If it cannot fetch her, they find her in her bed. Maylis takes the second sleeper to be Coralie.

**Said again, in a line, before each use.**
- Before the first ride, in 3f: she lies still, and somebody is in the seat with her.
- Before the soup and the stairs: Lesca's sitting in 5b, cut to the three sentences above, and Maylis says each back in her own words.
- Before the last night: the two plan sentences in section 4.

**The trade in 8h**, in place of the riddle:

"On the eleventh your telephone rings at seven minutes to three," Lesca says. "It wakes you first, so I ride you, and I'm at your gate in your arms."

"Not if I'm not there to be rung. I'll be in my bed with the telephone off. I'll sleep till you get up for your stone, and then I ride you. Mayor, for the day of the stone."

One line follows from Lesca: her own ride is not for a day. She means to stay.

## 3. The beats changed so that no fourth rule is needed

- **The van.** Maylis is no longer said to keep her body because she was awake. The fight in the cab runs on rule 2, as every ride has. Lesca has the body and the mouth, and means to drive the track. Maylis has the right hand, which is the hand on the gear lever. The van stalls. The five words are Lesca's, said with a mouth that is hers to use.
- **Staying for good.** "Keep the bed afterwards" becomes rule 3. Lesca sits on Inguma so that it cannot fetch her. Her own body then dies, which is why her name is read at the loto of the dead. Letting go is getting up off its back.
- **Arlette's ponies.** They stand. When the count is done Inguma strains under Lesca to fetch her, and she feels it.
- **Maylis's sandbags in the van.** Cut. She cannot count her way out of rule 1.
- **Madame Etcheto's stars.** They stay in the care plan as her own belief. No plan in the book rests on them.

## 4. The eve of the night

- **12, after the pencil.** The chapter opens on Maylis putting her pencil through the handover, as an act, at ten past five on Friday morning. Then, in the scene: "She would be at that gate herself. When it sat on her she would be on her feet in the steel room, between Lesca and the chair, so that nobody was sitting when the telephone woke her."
- **13, after Arlette.** "Tonight Lesca meant to be sitting when the telephone woke Maylis, and to wake at the gate inside her. She meant to sit on Inguma itself, so that it could never fetch her home."

The two collide in the room. Maylis stands in front of the chair. Inguma takes it, as it has three times, and Lesca sits down on Inguma.

The synopsis sentences that now open 12 and 13 are cut.

## 5. The river at the line

New first scene of chapter 10, Maylis's village, Tuesday the eighth, 300. The river is at the red line on the afternoon tide. The yellow sheet is in the mayor's tray. Larrère stops beside her, lowers the window a few centimetres, and says that the gate stays shut and that the Port knows it has a river. He does not hear himself. Lafitte writes it on the pad under "Madame le maire", and nobody at the table laughs. Sarthou goes home for his bags.

Cut the report of it in 10a and the paragraph in 12a.

## 7. Lesca's reckoning

- New last scene of 16, Tuesday at six, 250. The extraordinary sitting. Monsieur Tastet has the chair and the white timer, and gives the suspended mayor three minutes. She uses less than one. Eleven hands go up to ask the prefect for her removal. Larrère abstains, since he was in the room. Her mother is at the back, inside the mairie for the second time. That night Coralie carries the pillow across.
