For editors to apply in place, and for one writer to play the scenes marked **Play**. Scenes are named from the current `manuscript/`, and from `manuscript-v5/` as v5. The book goes from 44,400 words to about 47,100, in a cold open and nineteen chapters. Old 9 is split in two, and old 13 gives its second half to a new chapter 15.

## 1. The first page

**Play: the cold open, 300, dark.** Its only heading is the date, the night of the twelfth of December 2014. A woman, unnamed, is on the platform of a sluice gate in the rain. She lays her hand on the bank and the clay moves under it. She gives it ten minutes. She takes out a telephone and rings a number. It is not said whose, or what her headlamps showed her. The last sentence is "It rang once."

**Chapter 1 then asks less.** It keeps the thing on the wardrobe, the man in the car who will not be the mayor, and the eggs. Out of 1b go the minutes of 2011 and "I was in the room as mayor", the father's four turns, Saint Catherine's and the blower. The first two move to the bank in chapter 3. The village is named in the narrator's voice before "Peyrebarthe said it". 1b comes down from 760 to 450.

**Chapter 8, Maylis's day as mayor.**
- **Play, from v5 10c, 450, comic,** in place of 8d. She takes the rake out of Kévin's hands and does the gratings in the mayor's coat. She signs three letters with her father's L, and Madame Ducasse holds them back. She rings the prefecture on the mayor's telephone and is answered at once: a gate is raised by day on the president's order. The medal is on the blotter. "I want that."

**Chapter 10, Lesca's Saturday.**

**Chapter 11, the daylight plan fought for and lost.**

Asked on what information, she has none she can give. The association votes to keep the gate shut above the line. Jeannot Castagnet votes with them, since shut keeps his tonne dry.

**Chapter 14, Friday.**

## 4. Chapters 17 and 18 are scenes

**Chapter 15, before them.** **Play, 220, dark.** Lesca holds longer. She gets the van as far as the dip before Maylis has it out of gear. In the hall she stops at Jean Castagnet's place. He does not look up. Her knees bend as far as the chair beside him. Then the apologies.

**Chapter 17.**
- 15a goes to chapter 19. **Play in its place, 150.** The threat is paid. Coralie came in at five to three, found her with her eyes open, and rang. Inguma sets Lesca down on a trolley at Bayonne at six, with Coralie's two fingers on her wrist. Behind her a voice calls "Twelve. One, two," and a chair is pulled in. They drive back for eleven.
- **Play, 80.** After the chairs are stacked, Monsieur Darrigrand, with his broom. It was four when she counted his house, and it was one when she counted the man.

**Chapter 18.**
- **Play, in 16b, 120.** Maïté says what Guy did, in plain words. He knew Jean was out. He rang the order from her kitchen and sent nobody, and he let Lesca carry it for twelve years. Lesca will not take the relief. His call does not touch her one ring.
- **Play, 200.** Monday at nine, at Bayonne. A lieutenant, a form, the telephone from the drawer laid on his desk.
- **Play, in 16d, 250,** as speech, in place of the two sentences of report. Lesca tells Coralie that the bank went into the home over there and drowned Madame Etcheto, on her five words. She tells her that she twice asked the other Coralie across a landing, and that the second time that Coralie said she would have her. Coralie's answer has one joke of her own in it, and one thing she wanted in eleven years that was not Lesca.
- 16e stands. Lesca is in the room when the pillow comes.

## 5. The two women's ending

**Play, 600, in place of 17a.** Sunday, at nine minutes to three. Inguma sits on them one last time. They stand, and each tells the other her Saturday.
- Maylis's first words are the count: forty-one, and who was not among them.
- Lesca held her arm for eight rings, and Maylis was two hundred metres short of the bank when it went. Lesca says so before she is asked.
- "So you were right, that night. Turned first, it keeps them all." Lesca refuses it, and the refusal has room.
- Why it rained, asked once. Neither knows.
- One line each about the other's Coralie.
- Lesca: "I said it at eleven."

Then Inguma goes out by the door, and leaves the chair empty. The wardrobe is bare on Monday.

In chapter 19, Larrère's morning comes down to his car in the silt.

**Rules for the pass.**
- Names. In narration, Maylis and Lesca. In Lesca's village people say "Madame le maire", but for Arlette, Marie-Jo on the steps and Coralie's "Go to bed, Maylis". In Maylis's village only Jeannot says "Lesca", in 7 and on the last page.

- One noun for the earthwork, the bank, and "crown" for its top.
- No rule is stated in narration after chapter 5. The sentences that open 6 and 10 keep the weather and the bed and drop the rest.
- No briefing before a move. Cut "meant to" where it closes a section, in 4, 7, 10 to 13.
- No caption on an echo. Cut the eight the prose report lists, and the sentences that explain the hall in 10b.
- The gauge is said in three ways only: under the line, at the line, over the line, with ninety as the one figure.
