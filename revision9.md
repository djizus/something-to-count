## 2. The premise is not switched off

11a now reads so. On Monday night Maylis lies on her side with her knees up. Inguma sits on her all the same. Since it has had two in one seat it comes however she lies. Lesca is in the chair, gives Inguma the stones of the bank to count, and stays sitting when Coralie's alarm goes.

So Tuesday the eighth is a ridden day, and one scene:
- The river is at the line. Larrère says his two sentences through the window to the woman who told him he would, and she is there to hear it. Maylis has the right hand.
- Lafitte writes it on the pad. The café believes her. He writes the Port's request on a leaf of the pad, and it goes in through the gap of the window.
- Lesca puts the proof and the plan to the president with Maylis's mouth. He rings round the association. By evening it is five to two for shut. It was a guess, and a forecast, and it came from the woman who thinks she is the mayor.
- At the counter Jeannot puts his hand up to show how he voted.
- Inguma fetches Lesca at dusk.

The second meeting at the winch is gone.

**The ledger, in 11e.** She rules the two columns and writes the one line under *Received*. Then: "She went down her own December for what going over would take off her. The telephone was there, and the track, and the winch, and the stone with two lines on it. If she stayed over there she would have a living man at a counter as well. She would still have drowned him here. It added, and it took nothing away." *Nil* stays as the last word.

**Who has which hand.** The visitor has everything but the right hand. The mouth is the one thing fought over.
- Each woman has her own sentence. No sentence holds two acts by two women.
- Where a part acts, the part is the subject, and no name is needed: "The right hand came up onto the table and took the pen."
- A name marks the instant the mouth changes hands, and the voice drops when it is Lesca's.
- The winch in 6 becomes three sentences. The pawl missed its tooth and the gate dropped. Maylis let go with the right hand. Lesca caught the handle with the left, and it ran through that fist before it stopped.
- At the loto Lesca talks and Maylis takes the money, with no exceptions.
- Every section that changes village opens on the sky.

## 5. Three ways of talking

**The owner's wife at the café**, who gets a name. She runs on, joins everything with "and", and holds one wrong theory to the last chapter.
- "It's the wife, I said so on the Monday and I'll say it at her funeral, a woman doesn't carry a pot up that road for a man she can see any day at my counter for the price of a white."
- "She's changing the subject, they all do when it's a wife, my sister did it for a year about a roofer and we had floods then as well."

**Kévin Labat**, who says what he thinks as he thinks it, in the speech of his age, and asks.
- "I'd fly. Wouldn't you? Straight up over the church. I'd go and look in my mum's gutter."
- "Does it hurt, being two? Which of you gets the headache?"

**Père Honoré**, who is vain of his numbers, uses no contractions, and is courteous at length.
- "I had nine on Sunday, Madame, and two of those had come in from the rain. I do not complain. I mention it."
- "For the blessing of the bank I am promised six thousand. I have prepared for nine."

The dry last word stays with Coralie, Jeannot and Larrère. Sarthou and Madame Ducasse keep the register between them. "Then" opens no line of the priest's, Madame Etcheto's or Arlette's.
