# The night of the eleventh of December 2014

It had rained for nineteen days. The river was now higher than the doorsteps of the houses beside it.

Only an embankment kept the river away from them. The bank was a wall of clay as high as a house. Forty houses stood below it, with their shutters closed and the people inside asleep. At the end of the row was a care home. One light was on in its stairwell.

A woman drove past all of them to the end of the road and got out in the rain.

Water was coming through the bank. It ran down the clay in a sheet as wide as a door and crossed the road in her headlights. She put her hand flat on the bank beside the water. The clay moved under her palm.

She was scared.

She had repaired that bank every spring for six years, and she knew it well. It would hold for ten more minutes, she thought. Then it would break.

On top of the bank there was a sluice gate. Beside the gate was a platform of wooden planks, and on the platform was a winch. She drove up to it, climbed onto the platform and pointed her flashlight at the river. The water was going past at the height of her knees, close enough to touch. On her other side the flood meadows lay far below. They were dark and empty as far as the flashlight reached.

She took the winch handle out of its box.

She held it for three breaths. She did not fit it to the winch. She climbed back down to the van and turned it around on top of the bank, until its headlights pointed out over the meadows, along the one track that crossed them.

She looked down that track while the wipers crossed the windscreen twice.

Then she took out her phone. The number was already in it. She pressed it and held the phone to her ear. Rain ran off her hood.

It rang once.
